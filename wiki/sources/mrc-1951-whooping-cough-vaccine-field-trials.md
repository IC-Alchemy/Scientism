# MRC 1951 - The prevention of whooping-cough by vaccination (ten controlled field trials)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-10 (loop run 17, queue item 30) from the complete 9-page scanned PDF of BMJ 1951;1(4719):1463-1471 (PMC2069253, file brmedj03552-0003.pdf), downloaded from the PMC PDF endpoint after solving the PMC proof-of-work cookie (SHA-256(challenge+nonce) with 4 leading zeros -> cloudpmc-viewer-pow). Every number below is from that scan, not from any summary. The article is a committee report: analysis and report by W. C. Cockburn, D. G. Evans, and J. Knowelden of the MRC Whooping-cough Immunization Committee (chairman S. P. Bedson; secretary D. G. Evans; A. Bradford Hill among the members). No individual first author exists; not PubMed-indexed.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** ten separate controlled field trials (not one pooled randomized trial; reported together under one uniform plan) in Leeds, Manchester, Tottenham and Edmonton, Wembley, and West Ham, begun between November 1946 and April 1948, completed during 1950; each child followed a mean of 27 months after the third inoculation (range across trials 23-30).
- **Subjects:** children aged 6-18 months at entry whose parents consented after a pamphlet and signed form; 8,927 entered for inoculation (4,515 vaccine / 4,412 control), 7,558 completed the three-dose course (3,801 / 3,757), 848 were lost during follow-up (moves, re-immunization by private doctor, non-pertussis death, uncooperative parents), leaving 6,710 at the end (3,358 / 3,352). Groups checked similar in average age (12.2 months), family size, observation time, breast-feeding history, prior infections, diphtheria immunization, and smallpox vaccination (Table III).
- **Intervention:** three monthly intramuscular inoculations (subcutaneous early in some areas) of one of five whole-cell pertussis vaccine batches - Parke Davis Sauer 087860 (plain), Michigan Dept. of Health D231 (plain), Michigan A236 (alum-precipitated), Glaxo 61 and 174 (plain) - allocated to areas per Table I; doses ranged 30-112 thousand million organisms total by the American count.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES, an "anticatarrhal" vaccine prepared by Burroughs Wellcome containing killed Staphylococcus aureus, Streptococcus pneumoniae, Corynebacterium hofmannii, and Neisseria catarrhalis and **no H. pertussis**, "similar in turbidity to the plain pertussis vaccines" and "dispensed in the same type of bottle." Blinding quote: "Neither the parents nor the observers knew whether a child was in the vaccinated or the unvaccinated group"; parents were told "some of the children would be inoculated with a substance which was not whooping-cough vaccine."
- **Allocation:** randomization by pre-drawn letter sheets - children classified by sex and age band (6-8, 9-11, 12-14, 15-17 months), then assigned in order of consent to letters A, B, C, D in random sequence; two letters meant pertussis vaccine, two meant the sham, deliberately, "so that fieldworkers would be less likely to distinguish the pertussis vaccine from the 'anticatarrhal' vaccine."
- **Endpoint (named exactly):** clinically diagnosed pertussis. Suspicion triggered a report slip or a monthly visit finding; senior nurse-investigator or the medical member of the team made the diagnosis; mothers kept paroxysm counts at the height of disease; postnasal/pernasal swabs attempted in the first two weeks (mean 1.85 per case; swabbed 806/836 = 96.4% of diagnosed cases); severity graded 1-5 (1 = abortive attack with or without occasional paroxysms, bacteriologically confirmed; 2 = fewer than 10 paroxysms/24 h; 3 = 10-20; 4 = more than 20; 5 = complicated by bronchopneumonia, atelectasis, etc.); cough duration in weeks. Exposure to pertussis recorded as "home exposures" (exposure to a sibling in the child's own home) versus "other exposures" (day nurseries, nursery schools, parties, cinemas, buses, outdoor play).
- **Laboratory confirmation:** H. pertussis isolated from 59.8% of swabbed cases (57.7% of all diagnosed cases); the confirmation percentage was similar in both arms in each trial and overall (vaccinated 55.7%, unvaccinated 58.1%). H. parapertussis isolated from 24 cases (10 vaccinated / 14 control); H. bronchisepticus from one.

## Result

- **Overall incidence (all 10 trials):** 149 cases in vaccinated vs 687 in sham; attack rates 1.45 vs 6.72 per 1,000 child-months of observation from the third inoculation; ratio 1:4.6, summarized by the report as "a reduction in the incidence of the disease of 78%." By sex: males 1.38 vs 6.23; females 1.50 vs 7.24 (attack rates higher in females in both arms).
- **Every trial separately** showed a substantially lower attack rate in the vaccinated arm (Table V: per-batch ratios from 1:2.5 up to 1:10.8).
- **Exposure-stratified attack rates:** after a recorded home exposure to pertussis, 18.2% of vaccinated vs 87.3% of sham children developed pertussis (ratio 1:4.8); in only one trial did the sham home-exposure attack rate fall below 75%. After "other exposures" (nursery, school, transport, play): 8.3% vs 38.0% (ratio 1:4.6); children with no recorded exposure: ratio 1:5.0.
- **Severity:** mild courses (grade 1-2) in 73.2% of vaccinated cases vs 24.1% of sham cases; complicated attacks (grade 5) 2 vs 28; no deaths in either arm. Vaccinated cases also had shorter coughs (44.3% under 4 weeks; Table VIII; per-trial severity and duration differences "observed when each trial was considered separately").
- **Batch potency varied widely:** control attack rates were nearly constant across areas (6.48-7.04 per 1,000 child-months; home-exposure sham rates 79.5-90.7%), which the committee used to justify cross-area comparison: Sauer 1:2.5, Glaxo 61 1:3.3, Glaxo 174 1:3.6, Michigan D231 1:7.3, Michigan A236 1:10.8. The two Michigan batches protected best.
- **Duration of protection:** in home exposures the sham attack rate stayed roughly constant over the whole observation period while the vaccinated rate stayed around 20%; for none of the five vaccines separately was there evidence of waning within the 2-3 year observation window (the report's own framing: "within the limits of the small numbers of cases involved").
- **Safety:** plain vaccines produced no severe local or general reactions (some 24-hour fever and 1-2-day redness/swelling); the alum-precipitated batch produced six sterile abscesses. Prompted by then-current reports of convulsions/encephalopathy and provocation poliomyelitis (Byers & Moll 1948; Martin 1950; McCloskey 1950; Geffen 1950; Bradford Hill & Knowelden 1950), all records were scrutinized: no convulsions, and no poliomyelitis within the "arbitrarily chosen period of two months after inoculation."
- **Context - the committee's own earlier failures, recorded in the same paper:** the 1942-44 Oxford trials (McFarlan, Topley, Fisher 1945) found no significant difference (Oxford City 12.5% of 327 vaccinated vs 14.1% of 305 unvaccinated attacked; residential nurseries 55% vs 63%), which is why the new series used American-type strains and included the batch-potency question. Prior external trials cited with attack rates: Doull 1939 (74/479 vs 94/496 exposed), Siegel & Goldberger 1937 (53% vs 58%), Kendrick & Eldering 1939 (34.9% vs 89.4% home-exposed), Sauer 1937, Bell 1941/1948.
- **Non-pertussis coughs after exposure:** of exposures not followed by pertussis, a non-paroxysmal cough occurred in 33.7% of vaccinated vs 34.9% of sham children, with no bacteriological evidence of pertussis in most - the report treats these as not due to H. pertussis.

## Main limitation (as recorded by the report itself)

- 1,369 children entered but never completed inoculation (persistent defaulters, pertussis exposure, moves, unrelated illness or death) and 848 more were lost during follow-up; observations up to loss were retained in the analysis.
- Severity grading rested on mothers' paroxysm diaries - "Parents varied in their ability to keep accurate records" and "no record could be made of the severity of each paroxysm" - though the report notes the blinding made this usable as a relative measure.
- Diagnosis was clinical at bottom; culture failed in ~40% of swabbed cases, and the report does not claim the un-confirmed fraction was all pertussis.
- The two-month poliomyelitis watch window is labeled "arbitrarily chosen" by the report itself.
- Ten trials with five different vaccines in five areas and different epidemic periods: cross-batch potency ranking is indirect (the report says a direct comparison "is not possible... as they were not tested in the same area and at the same time" and defends the indirect one via the near-constant control rates).

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

- **Cross-area potency ranking:** the claim that Michigan batches "proved to be more potent in the field" rests on comparing ratios across trials run in different areas and seasons; the near-constant control attack rates make it plausible, but no vaccine was tested head-to-head in one area - the report says so and still prints the potency ordering.
- **Duration claim:** "no evidence of a waning in the degree of protection" is bounded by the report to the 2-3 year observation, but the summary sentence carries it without restating the bound.
- **Generalization toward practice:** the closing recommendation that British-made Michigan-method vaccines now be field-tested, and the framing of pertussis vaccination as worth assessing as routine prophylaxis, extend a 1946-50 five-area infant result to national practice; the paper does not measure all-cause or overall-health outcomes (it tracked only selected other infections - measles, chicken-pox, bronchopneumonia, diphtheria - with counts reported as group-similarity checks, not as outcome comparisons).
- **Transparency datum worth keeping:** mid-trial, the committee observed that "more cases of pertussis were occurring in children inoculated with vaccines A and C - the 'anticatarrhal' vaccines - than in children with vaccines B and D," plus a reaction-rate difference; its response was to cover the vaccine letter on each record card with a thick label "to ensure... that follow-up observations remained unbiased" rather than to stop the trials or re-randomize. The sham arm therefore continued knowingly while observation continued blinded.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), first row for pertussis.** Exposure = three doses of whole-cell pertussis vaccine under natural community exposure; comparator = a real sham (multi-species killed-bacterial injection with no H. pertussis, indistinguishable in turbidity and packaging); endpoint = clinically diagnosed pertussis with 96% swab coverage, 57.7% culture confirmation, paroxysm-count severity grading, cough duration, and complication counts - disease, not antibody titers; result = 78% incidence reduction, milder and shorter disease in breakthrough cases, constant control rates across areas.
- This is also the vault's first pertussis source of any kind; the cross-cutting ladder gains a fifth pathogen on the GAP 4 intervention rung (pneumococcus-PCV9, rotavirus, HPV, SARS-CoV-2 x2, RSV, influenza, and now pertussis).
- Chain position: the trial measures whether inoculation with killed B. pertussis reduced a defined clinical syndrome under natural exposure with a blinded sham - it does not itself establish the causation chain (isolation, natural-route transmission) for pertussis, which the vault does not yet hold; and its two-arm design says nothing about any indirect (herd) effect.
