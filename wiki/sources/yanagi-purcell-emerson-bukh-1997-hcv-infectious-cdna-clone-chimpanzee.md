# Transcripts from a Single Full-Length cDNA Clone of Hepatitis C Virus Are Infectious

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary infectious molecular-clone paper for HCV
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, 1997
- Authors: Masayuki Yanagi, Robert H. Purcell, Suzanne U. Emerson, and Jens Bukh
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF rendered through Europe PMC/PMC, checked against DOI, PubMed, and PMCID metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Full-length H77 HCV cDNA clone construction, in vitro transcription, direct intrahepatic chimpanzee transfection, serial serum HCV RNA testing, anti-HCV testing, liver-enzyme monitoring, and sequence comparison of recovered genomes |
| Subject / sample | Two chimpanzee experiments are described: one chimpanzee received transcripts from two initial full-length clones, and another received transcripts from a consensus chimeric clone pCV-H77C derived from H77 genotype 1a |
| Exposure or intervention | Laparotomy with injection of transcription mixtures into multiple sites of exposed chimpanzee liver; initial pH21 and pCV-H11 transcripts were tested, then pCV-H77C consensus-chimera transcripts were tested |
| Comparator / control | The first chimpanzee transfection with pH21 and pCV-H11 served as a negative construct/control boundary; weekly baseline/post-inoculation serum testing; sequence comparison of recovered virus with injected transcript sequence |
| Endpoint | Detectable serum HCV RNA by nested RT-PCR, increasing genome titer over time, anti-HCV antibodies, liver enzyme levels, and identity of recovered HCV sequence to the injected clone |
| Result | The initial pH21 and pCV-H11 clones produced no evidence of HCV replication: weekly sera were HCV RNA negative for weeks 1-17 and anti-HCV remained negative. The consensus chimeric clone pCV-H77C produced detectable HCV RNA at week 1, rising from about 10^2 genome equivalents/ml to 10^4 at week 2 and 10^6 by week 8. Sequencing of recovered genomes showed identity to pCV-H77C across tested regions, including unique mutations. |
| Main limitation | This is direct intrahepatic transfection of a chimpanzee with RNA transcripts, not ordinary human transmission. The phenotype required more chimpanzees for careful study, and the experiment does not by itself reconstruct natural route, dose, chronicity, or population transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that RNA transcripts from the consensus H77 pCV-H77C full-length cDNA clone initiated HCV replication in a chimpanzee after direct intrahepatic transfection.
- It can answer that not every full-length H77 clone tested was infectious under the same broad method.
- It can answer that the recovered serum HCV sequence matched the injected pCV-H77C clone sequence in the tested regions.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human acquisition route or dose.
- It cannot prove transfusion transmission by itself.
- It cannot prove all HCV genotypes behave identically.
- It cannot answer vaccine, treatment, or population-risk questions.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This paper is a high-rung molecular-clone infectivity experiment. The leap begins if direct intrahepatic transfection in a chimpanzee is narrated as ordinary human transmission or as a substitute for prospective transfusion, donor-linkage, and clinical-outcome evidence.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: Yanagi et al. 1997 showed that transcripts from one consensus full-length H77 HCV cDNA clone initiated viral replication in a chimpanzee by direct intrahepatic transfection, while two other full-length clones were noninfectious under the tested conditions.
