# Experiments on Intramuscular Inoculation and Feeding Domestic Cats with Rabid Mouse Brains

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary oral-feeding route-boundary paper with intramuscular positive-standard comparison
- Journal/year: *Brazilian Journal of Veterinary Research and Animal Science*, 2007
- Authors: Renata K. Shirakawa, Adriana Cortez, Leonardo J. Richtzenhain, Takuya Itou, Takeo Sakai, and Fumio H. Ito
- Source basis in hand: full-text journal PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental oral feeding and intramuscular positive-standard animal study |
| Subject / sample | Nineteen kittens fed brains from mice infected with PV fixed rabies virus or T-9/95 vampire-bat field isolate; one adult male cat used as positive standard |
| Exposure or intervention | Oral ingestion of infected mouse brains in several dose schedules; positive-standard cat received masseter-muscle inoculation with T-9/95 infected-brain suspension |
| Comparator / control | Intramuscular positive-standard cat rather than sham-fed controls |
| Endpoint | Clinical rabies signs, direct fluorescent antibody test, mouse inoculation test, hemi-nested RT-PCR in multiple tissues, and observation period |
| Result | The masseter-inoculated positive-standard cat developed rabies after a six-day incubation, had eight days of clinical disease, and died; brain, cervical medulla, and salivary gland were dFA positive, brain and cervical medulla were MIT positive, and all tested specimens were RT-PCR positive. None of the cats fed infected brains developed clinical rabies, and all materials from fed cats were negative by dFA and MIT, although several specimens were RT-PCR positive. |
| Main limitation | There were no sham-fed controls, and RT-PCR positivity without disease complicates interpretation. The study tests oral feeding in cats, not bite exposure or parenteral exposure. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that cats fed rabid mouse brains did not develop clinical rabies under the reported conditions.
- It can answer that masseter inoculation produced rabies in the positive-standard cat.
- It can answer that RT-PCR detection in fed cats did not align with clinical disease, dFA, or mouse-inoculation positivity in this experiment.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot refute non-oral rabies transmission.
- It cannot prove that all oral exposures in cats fail.
- It cannot treat RT-PCR positivity as equivalent to infectious disease without the other endpoints.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if oral-feeding resistance is retold as a general failure of rabies-virus causation. The paper supports route and detection-versus-disease boundaries.

## Vault conclusion
Shirakawa et al. 2007 supports a narrow boundary claim: domestic cats fed rabid mouse brains did not develop clinical rabies by dFA/MIT endpoints, while a masseter-inoculated positive-standard cat did.
