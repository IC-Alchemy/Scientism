# Facemasks, Hand Hygiene, and Influenza among Young Adults: A Randomized Intervention Trial

## Source type
- Role in this vault: replication row for GAP 2 (controlled natural-route transmission) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] - the same question Cowling 2009 asked in Hong Kong households, asked instead in a US university residence-hall population with pre-illness mask adoption rather than post-index-case response.
- Journal/year: *PLoS ONE*, 2012;7(1):e29744 (received 29 July 2011, accepted 2 December 2011)
- Authors: Allison E. Aiello, Vanessa Perez, Rebecca M. Coulborn, Brian M. Davis, Monica Uddin, Arnold S. Monto
- Trial registration: NCT00490633 (M-FLU); funding: CDC grant U01 C1000441. This is the second season (2007-08) of the two-year M-FLU study; the first season was reported in Aiello et al., *J Infect Dis* 2010;201:491-498 (reference 2 of this paper).
- Source basis in hand: full open-access article retrieved from Europe PMC fullTextXML (PMC3266257), read end to end including Methods, all numbered tables (Tables 1-4), Discussion, and the supplementary-material descriptions. Narrative/table discrepancies are recorded below.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cluster-randomized intervention trial: 37 residence houses within 5 university residence halls randomized (SAS Proc Plan) to mask+hand hygiene (MH), mask only (M), or control; conducted University of Michigan, 2007-08 season; 6-week intervention (28 January - 14 March 2008, excluding a one-week spring break), begun after campus surveillance laboratory-confirmed influenza was circulating. Statisticians and PIs blinded to arm during analyses; participants unblinded |
| Subject / sample | 1,178 young adults enrolled (eligible if >=18, willing to wear masks, use sanitizer, provide throat swabs when sick, complete weekly online surveys); 1,111 analyzed (94% retention): 349 MH, 392 M, 370 control; mean age 18.95 y (SD 0.9); no significant baseline differences (Table S1). Incident-ILI analysis restricted to the 938/1,111 ILI-free at baseline; influenza analysis used all 1,111 (no baseline lab-confirmed cases) |
| Intervention or exposure | Natural exposure: participants lived normally; no exposure was administered. MH arm: weekly packets of 7 standard ear-loop procedure masks (Kimberly-Clark TECNOL), asked to wear >=6 h/day while in the residence hall, plus 62% ethyl alcohol gel sanitizer (2 oz squeeze + 8 oz pump bottles). M arm: masks only. Reported compliance: masks 5.08 (MH) vs 5.04 (M) h/day; sanitizer 4.49 uses/day (MH) vs 1.29 (M) and 1.51 (C), P<0.001 (Table 1) |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no**. Quote: "The control group did not receive an intervention." No placebo masks or placebo sanitizer; participants knew their arm (single-blind only at the analysis stage). Control households reported sanitizer use comparable to the M arm, and the authors acknowledge: "Due to the inability to blind participants to study interventions, compliance with these interventions must be considered carefully" |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: "The main outcome variables measured at the individual level were time to first ILI and time to PCR-confirmed influenza A/B during the study period." ILI case definition given to participants: "presence of cough and at least one or more of fever/feverishness, chills, or body aches". Laboratory endpoint: throat swab tested by real-time PCR (TaqMan, CDC Influenza Branch primers/probes) for influenza A and B. Analysis: discrete-time survival (proportional-hazards assumption not met), GEE for clustering, intention-to-treat; week-by-week significance threshold stated as P<=0.025 for multiple comparisons |
| Result | 128/938 ILI-free participants (14%) met the ILI definition; 34/128 (27%) were RT-PCR-positive. Cumulative ILI rate ratios (Table 3): M vs control 1.08 (0.86-1.34), P=0.52; MH vs control 0.78 (0.59-1.05), P=0.10 - not significant as randomized. Week-by-week MH vs control RRs: week 3 0.61 (0.37-1.01) P=0.06; week 4 0.48 (0.24-0.94) P=0.03; week 5 0.38 (0.15-0.94) P=0.04; week 6 0.30 (0.09-0.98) P=0.05 (Table 3 labels these "Unadjusted Model"; Table 3 footnote states significance at P<0.05, contradicting the Methods' stated P<=0.025). The abstract and narrative quote a week-6 adjusted RR of 0.25 (0.07-0.87), a "75% reduction", which does not match the table's 0.30 (0.09-0.98); the adjusted week-specific estimates are not tabulated. Laboratory-confirmed influenza cumulative RR (Table 4): MH 0.57 (0.26-1.24), P=0.15; M 0.93 (0.60-1.42), P=0.72 - neither significant; the same figures are labeled "adjusted" in the narrative and "unadjusted" in the table |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors: ILI-negative PCR cases may be other respiratory viruses; masks were worn only in the residence hall ("it is possible that transmission of infection occurred outside of the residential environment"); no hand-hygiene-only arm ("we did not have the funds to include a hand hygiene only group and therefore cannot disentangle the combined effects of masks and hand hygiene"); self-reported compliance; no participant blinding; generalizability limited to similar settings and age groups. Structural: only 34 PCR-confirmed cases across arms - the laboratory-confirmed endpoint was underpowered, as the authors concede for the primary reading ("we only analyzed 34 incident cases of influenza during the study and this limited our statistical power") |

## Table/label inconsistencies (recorded because the vault separates tables from narrative)
- Abstract and Discussion state the maximum ILI reduction as 75% in week 6 with RR 0.25 (0.07-0.87); Table 3's week-6 row is RR 0.30 (0.09-0.98), P=0.05.
- Methods declare week-by-week significance at P<=0.025 (multiple comparisons); Table 3's footnote sets significance at P<0.05. Under the Methods' own threshold, none of the significant week-by-week results (P=0.03, 0.04, 0.05) clears it.
- The narrative labels the Table 4 influenza figures (0.57; 0.92) "adjusted"; the table heading says "Unadjusted Model".

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 15 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]]: a GAP 2 replication row. Cowling 2009 measured randomized natural-route transmission in households after an index case appeared; this trial measures it in a residence-hall population wearing masks before illness, so adherence and timing confounders differ. Both reach the same rung-level result: no statistically significant reduction as randomized, with significance appearing only in secondary or subgroup readings.
- Cross-cutting: extends the natural-route row of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] alongside [[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]]; row in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]].

## What the experiment can answer
- That in a young-adult congregate-living population during a season with confirmed campus influenza circulation, a mask+sanitizer intervention adopted before illness produced no statistically significant cumulative reduction in time to first ILI (RR 0.78, P=0.10) or in time to RT-PCR-confirmed influenza A/B (RR 0.57, P=0.15) versus a no-intervention control, and masks alone produced none at all (RR 1.08 and 0.93).
- That the laboratory-confirmed transmission under natural exposure was measurable (34 PCR-confirmed cases) without any investigator-administered inoculum - an independent second measurement of the same endpoint class Cowling 2009 measured.
- That compliance with a pre-illness mask regime can reach ~5 h/day in this population, and that mask-only adoption at this level did not reduce illness.

## What it cannot answer
- Whether masks or sanitizer reduce transmission under high or enforced compliance; adherence was self-reported and unobservable at all times (authors' own statement).
- Which component of the bundle acts; no hand-hygiene-only arm.
- Route attribution (droplet vs contact vs fomite vs aerosol); the bundle cannot decompose routes.
- Transmission in households, children, elderly, or pandemic strains; single campus, single season, ages 18-19 mostly.
- Any claim about influenza causation; the trial presupposes the virus and measures intervention effects on illness timing.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- Abstract conclusion: "Face masks and hand hygiene combined may reduce the rate of ILI and confirmed influenza in community settings. These non-pharmaceutical measures should be recommended in crowded settings at the start of an influenza pandemic." The first clause rests on results that did not reach statistical significance cumulatively; the week-by-week significance does not meet the Methods' own P<=0.025 threshold. The second clause extrapolates one seasonal, single-campus trial to pandemic policy - the same leap pattern recorded on the Cowling 2009 page (subgroup/secondary finding elevated to headline; pandemic generalization).
- Discussion: "These trends suggest that face masks and hand hygiene should be encouraged during seasonal influenza outbreaks" - a recommendation built on P=0.10 and P=0.15 cumulative results plus post-hoc weekly readings.

## Vault conclusion
Supported as a replication row at the controlled natural-route rung: a second cluster-randomized trial with a named RT-PCR endpoint independently documents measurable natural-route influenza transmission and finds no statistically significant cumulative intervention effect, mirroring Cowling 2009. The vault treats the week-3-to-6 ILI reductions as secondary, threshold-dependent readings, not as the randomized result.
