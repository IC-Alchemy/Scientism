# Recovery of Rubella Virus from Army Recruits

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary acute-rubella virus-recovery and serology paper
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1962
- Authors: P. D. Parkman, E. L. Buescher, and M. S. Artenstein
- Source basis in hand: full-text official journal PDF from *Experimental Biology and Medicine*, checked against DOI and PubMed metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Acute-case throat-washing inoculation into cell cultures, interference assay using ECHO-11 challenge, serial passage, host-range tests, physical-characterization tests, and paired-serum neutralization |
| Subject / sample | Seventy-nine male army recruits hospitalized with febrile exanthems at Fort Dix: 20 definite clinical rubella, 21 probable rubella, 25 scarlet fever, and 13 atypical exanthems |
| Exposure or intervention | Throat washings obtained 24-48 hours after rash onset were inoculated into grivet monkey kidney and other cell cultures; rubella virus was recognized by interference with ECHO-11 cytopathology |
| Comparator / control | Scarlet-fever patients hospitalized at the same time and place; atypical exanthem cases; culture challenge controls with ECHO-11; testing of patient sera for neutralization |
| Endpoint | Interference with ECHO-11 in grivet monkey kidney culture, serial passage, virus titer by interference dose, filterability, heat and ether/chloroform sensitivity, host-range behavior, and rise in neutralizing antibody |
| Result | In the initial experiment, 8 of 10 classical rubella throat washings yielded the interfering agent. In the larger outbreak set, virus was recovered from 16 of 20 definite rubella cases and 14 of 21 probable rubella cases, but from only 1 of 25 scarlet-fever cases and 4 of 13 atypical exanthem cases. Significant neutralizing-antibody increases were reported in 32 of 34 rubella patients tested. The agent propagated in grivet monkey kidney cultures and was filterable, heat-sensitive, and organic-solvent-sensitive. |
| Main limitation | The paper used clinical diagnostic categories and an interference assay rather than direct visible cytopathology for primary recognition. It supports association, recovery, propagation, and serologic response, not ordinary transmission or congenital-rubella causation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a recoverable interfering agent was much more frequent in throat washings from clinically diagnosed rubella cases than in contemporaneous scarlet-fever cases.
- It can answer that the agent propagated in grivet monkey kidney cultures and interfered with subsequent ECHO-11 cytopathology under the reported assay conditions.
- It can answer that paired sera from most rubella patients showed rising neutralizing activity against the recovered agent.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human-to-human transmission.
- It cannot prove congenital rubella syndrome.
- It cannot by itself establish that every rubella-like rash case has the same cause.
- It cannot replace experimental exposure or direct transmission evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The main risk is treating culture interference and serology as the whole causal ladder. The paper supports an acute-case recovery and immune-response rung; it does not by itself settle natural route, dose, clinical sufficiency, or fetal disease.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in Parkman, Buescher, and Artenstein's 1962 Fort Dix series, a rubella-associated agent was recovered from many acute rubella throat washings by grivet-monkey-kidney interference assay and was accompanied by rising neutralizing antibody in most paired sera.
