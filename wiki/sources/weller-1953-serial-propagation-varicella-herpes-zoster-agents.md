# Serial Propagation in Vitro of Agents Producing Inclusion Bodies Derived from Varicella and Herpes Zoster

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary varicella/zoster lesion-fluid culture and serial-propagation paper
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1953
- Author: Thomas H. Weller
- Source basis in hand: full-text official journal PDF from *Experimental Biology and Medicine*, checked against DOI and PubMed metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Vesicle-fluid inoculation into roller-tube human tissue cultures, serial passage by infected tissue suspension, histology, heat test, animal/egg inoculation attempts, and control-culture passage |
| Subject / sample | Vesicle fluids from 11 varicella cases and two herpes zoster cases; human embryonic skin-muscle and foreskin tissue cultures |
| Exposure or intervention | Vesicle-fluid suspensions were inoculated into established roller-tube cultures; subsequent passages used coarsely ground infected tissue, not culture fluid alone |
| Comparator / control | More than 70 control cultures maintained during passage experiments received tissue suspensions from control cultures of preceding passages; tissue suspensions were also inoculated into suckling mice and developing hen's eggs |
| Endpoint | Focal cytopathogenic lesions, swollen/degenerating cells, intranuclear inclusion bodies, serial culture passage, heat inactivation, and lack of overt disease in mice or eggs |
| Result | Varicella vesicle fluids from 6 of 11 cases yielded characteristic focal lesions; two varicella strains were maintained for 10 tissue-culture passages. Two herpes zoster vesicle-fluid specimens yielded similar cytopathogenic agents that were serially propagated. Control cultures did not show focal lesions, and centrifuged culture fluids did not successfully establish passages. |
| Main limitation | This is an in vitro culture and inclusion-body endpoint. It does not prove ordinary chickenpox transmission, zoster latency/reactivation, or clinical sufficiency by itself. Neutralization attempts with convalescent varicella serum had not yet succeeded in this report. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that vesicle fluids from some varicella and zoster cases yielded cytopathogenic agents in human tissue cultures.
- It can answer that selected agents could be serially propagated when infected tissue suspensions were used as passage material.
- It can answer that the focal lesions contained intranuclear inclusions and that control-culture passages did not produce similar lesions in the reported set.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human-to-human chickenpox transmission.
- It cannot prove that zoster is reactivation of latent varicella virus.
- It cannot prove fetal disease, systemic disease, or vaccine efficacy.
- It cannot show that infectious material was freely present in the culture-fluid phase.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The main risk is treating lesion-fluid culture as a full causation and latency proof. This paper supports the culture/propagation rung and a varicella-zoster similarity lead, but it does not collapse chickenpox, shingles, latency, and natural transmission into one endpoint.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in Weller's 1953 experiments, vesicle fluids from varicella and herpes zoster cases yielded similar cytopathogenic agents that could be serially propagated in human tissue cultures using infected tissue suspensions.
