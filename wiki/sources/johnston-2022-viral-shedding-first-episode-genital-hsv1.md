# Viral Shedding One Year Following First-Episode Genital HSV-1 Infection

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary prospective genital HSV-1 shedding paper after first episode
- Journal/year: *JAMA*, 2022
- Authors: C. Johnston and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective cohort |
| Subject / sample | 82 participants with first-episode genital HSV-1 |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural post-first-episode course; oral/genital swabs during two 30-day sessions at 8-12 and 48-52 weeks |
| Comparator / control | Early versus later after onset; primary versus prior/unknown HSV-1 status |
| Endpoint | HSV-1 DNA shedding |
| Result | Genital shedding declined from 12.1% to 7.1% of days over the first year; most shedding was asymptomatic. |
| Main limitation | One-year follow-up, attrition, and PCR endpoint. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that genital HSV-1 shedding declines during the first year after first-episode infection in the sampled cohort.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define lifetime transmission risk or infectiousness of every PCR-positive swab.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating one-year PCR shedding as lifetime transmission probability.

## Vault conclusion
Johnston et al. 2022 supports a genital HSV-1 shedding time-course rung, not full transmission-risk closure.
