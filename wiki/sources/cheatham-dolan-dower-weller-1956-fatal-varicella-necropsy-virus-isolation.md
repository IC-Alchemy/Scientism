# Varicella: Report of Two Fatal Cases with Necropsy, Virus Isolation, and Serologic Studies

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary fatal-varicella pathology and virus-recovery paper
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Pathology*, 1956
- Authors: W. J. Cheatham, T. F. Dolan Jr., J. C. Dower, and Thomas H. Weller
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF rendered through Europe PMC/PMC, checked against PubMed/PMC metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Fatal case reports with clinical history, necropsy, histopathology, tissue-culture virus isolation, passage, fluorescent-antibody identification, animal/egg inoculation, and complement-fixation serology |
| Subject / sample | Two fatal varicella cases: a child with disseminated neuroblastoma treated with antimetabolite therapy and a patient with acute rheumatic fever treated with cortisone |
| Exposure or intervention | Vesicle fluid, blood, lung, and other postmortem tissues were inoculated into roller-tube human embryonic skin-muscle or foreskin cultures; infected tissue was serially passaged |
| Comparator / control | Ground tissues from uninfected cultures were passed similarly; parallel control cultures failed to show the specific degeneration; infected cultures were checked by fluorescent-antibody reaction with acute versus convalescent varicella serum |
| Endpoint | Necropsy lesions with type A intranuclear inclusions, focal cytopathic degeneration in culture, serial passage, fluorescent-antibody staining, complement-fixation antibody, and lack of clinical disease in newborn mice or gross lesions in chick embryos |
| Result | Case 1 yielded virus from blood, vesicle fluid, and lung by tissue-culture techniques; the lung strain was passaged 14 times over 492 days. Case 2 yielded virus from blood and vesicle fluid. Parallel control cultures did not show the specific degeneration. Fluorescent-antibody testing supported varicella identity for the lung isolate. Both cases lacked demonstrable complement-fixing antibody at the tested times. |
| Main limitation | The cases were severely ill and treated with antimetabolite or cortisone therapy, so they are not ordinary uncomplicated chickenpox. Case reports cannot establish general transmission or causation frequency. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that varicella-like virus could be recovered from vesicle fluid, blood, and lung in fatal cases using tissue-culture endpoints.
- It can answer that necropsy lesions in multiple organs contained type A intranuclear inclusions consistent with disseminated varicella pathology.
- It can answer that, in these two severe cases, complement-fixing antibody was not demonstrable at the reported dilutions and time points.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary chickenpox transmission.
- It cannot prove latency or zoster reactivation.
- It cannot generalize severe immunologically unusual fatal cases to all chickenpox.
- It cannot establish that virus isolation from tissue culture alone is sufficient for every clinical attribution claim.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is overgeneralizing from fatal, medically complicated cases. The paper strengthens systemic recovery and pathology rungs for severe varicella, but its endpoints should not be retold as ordinary transmission or latency proof.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in two fatal varicella cases, the authors described disseminated necropsy lesions with intranuclear inclusions and recovered varicella virus by tissue culture from vesicle fluid and blood, plus lung in one case.
