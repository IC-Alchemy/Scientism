# Comparative Studies Between Pathogenesis of Street and Fixed Rabies Infection

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary comparative neuropathology paper for street versus fixed rabies-virus infection
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1967
- Authors: Kaneatsu Miyamoto and Seiichi Matsumoto
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Comparative light- and electron-microscopy neuropathology with brain infectivity testing |
| Subject / sample | Five- or six-day-old mice infected with Komatsukawa street rabies virus or HEP Flury fixed rabies virus |
| Exposure or intervention | Intracerebral inoculation with about 10^7 LD50 street virus or 10^6 LD50 fixed virus |
| Comparator / control | Street-virus versus fixed-virus infection; adult and suckling mouse infectivity titration |
| Endpoint | Excitation, paralysis timing, Negri bodies, cytoplasmic matrices, neuronal degeneration, virus particles around matrices, and brain infectivity titers |
| Result | Street-virus-infected mice showed excitation at 8-10 days and paralysis on the next day; fixed-virus-infected mice showed paralysis without excitation at about five days. Street-virus neurons with Negri bodies appeared comparatively little damaged, while fixed-virus infection produced more extensive neuronal degeneration. Matrices were morphologically related to Negri-body ground substance, but fixed-virus matrices often lacked visible virus particles. Street-virus brain yielded higher virus than HEP Flury fixed-virus brain in the reported tests. |
| Main limitation | The study is intracerebral mouse infection and comparative neuropathology. It does not test bite exposure, salivary transmission, oral exposure, or human disease directly. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that street and fixed rabies viruses differed in clinical timing and neuropathology in this mouse model.
- It can answer that Negri-body and matrix morphology did not map simply onto the same visible-particle pattern for both virus types.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot settle ordinary transmission route.
- It cannot make fixed-virus and street-virus pathogenesis interchangeable.
- It cannot prove all human rabies mechanisms.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if fixed-virus intracerebral mouse findings are generalized to street-virus natural infection without preserving the strain, route, and pathology differences.

## Vault conclusion
Miyamoto and Matsumoto 1967 supports a narrow comparative claim: street and fixed rabies infection produced different neuropathologic patterns in intracerebrally inoculated mice.
