# Studies on Measles I Susceptibility of Monkeys to the Virus of Measles

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary monkey inoculation and passage paper for measles respiratory material
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1921
- Authors: Francis G. Blake and James D. Trask, Jr.
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Animal inoculation, clinical observation, filtration, reinoculation, and serial-passage study |
| Subject / sample | Rhesus monkeys; nasopharyngeal washings from measles patients in preeruptive or early eruptive stages; tissue emulsions and blood from experimentally infected monkeys |
| Exposure or intervention | Intratracheal injection or mucosal application of unfiltered or Berkefeld-filtered nasopharyngeal washings; later monkey-to-monkey passage using tissue emulsions, and some blood passage tests |
| Comparator / control | Negative animals and reinoculation controls; no randomized sham exposure series |
| Endpoint | Incubation period, listlessness, conjunctivitis, enanthem, maculopapular rash, leukopenia, fever, histology of skin lesions, blood culture, and passage to additional monkeys |
| Result | Of ten monkeys inoculated with nasopharyngeal washings from seven measles patients, eight developed a measles-like syndrome after six to ten days. Both unfiltered and filtered washings could produce illness. The authors reported six monkey passages with tissue emulsions from symptomatic animals. Some blood-passage attempts were negative or less direct. |
| Main limitation | The central positive evidence uses artificial inoculation of concentrated patient washings or tissue emulsions in monkeys. It is stronger than blood-inoculation claims, but it is not ordinary human respiratory transmission or purified-virus challenge. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that respiratory material from early measles cases could produce a reproducible measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys under intratracheal or mucosal inoculation conditions.
- It can answer that filtration through Berkefeld filters did not abolish activity in selected experiments.
- It can answer that passage by tissue emulsions preserved a transmissible activity across monkeys.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove natural human-to-human transmission route by ordinary contact.
- It cannot identify the virus morphologically or molecularly.
- It cannot equate blood, nasopharyngeal secretions, and tissue emulsions as equally effective materials.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The paper is vulnerable to route compression: intratracheal and mucosal inoculation of early case material can be retold as if it directly proved ordinary community transmission or purified measles-virus causation.

## Vault conclusion
Blake and Trask 1921 supports a respiratory-material monkey challenge rung for measles. It also preserves boundary information: blood-passage and route questions should remain separate from the positive nasopharyngeal-washing experiments.
