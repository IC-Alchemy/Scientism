# Isolation of a Novel Coronavirus from a Man with Pneumonia in Saudi Arabia

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary case report, virus-isolation, sequencing, and serology paper for the agent first reported as HCoV-EMC and later classified in the MERS-CoV attribution chain
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2012
- Authors: A. M. Zaki, S. van Boheemen, T. M. Bestebroer, A. D. M. E. Osterhaus, and R. A. M. Fouchier
- Source basis in hand: full-text OCR/PDF-room copy of the NEJM article; Ovid page was abstract-only and is not the claim basis

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Single fatal case report with respiratory pathogen immunofluorescence, cell culture in Vero and LLC-MK2 cells, broad viral PCR testing, family-wide coronavirus RT-PCR, sequencing, and infected-cell immunofluorescence serology |
| Subject / sample | A 60-year-old Saudi man admitted in Jeddah in June 2012 with fever, cough, dyspnea, severe pneumonia, renal failure, and death on hospital day 11; samples included admission sputum, blood, tracheal aspirate, and late serum |
| Exposure or intervention | Sputum supernatant was inoculated onto Vero and LLC-MK2 cells and observed for cytopathic effect; cultures and clinical material were tested for multiple respiratory viruses and coronaviruses; virus genome was sequenced from culture material |
| Comparator / control | Clinical testing for influenza A/B, parainfluenza 1-3, adenovirus, respiratory syncytial virus, adenovirus, enterovirus, human metapneumovirus, herpesviruses, and paramyxoviruses; 2400 hospital sera from 2010-2012 served as negative serologic controls |
| Endpoint | Cytopathic effect, repeatable culture passage, coronavirus RT-PCR fragments, nearly full-length viral genome sequence, patient IgG reactivity to infected cells, and control-serum negativity |
| Result | Admission sputum inoculation caused cytopathic effect in LLC-MK2 and Vero cells and reproduced cytopathic effect on passage. Infected cultures were negative for the tested conventional respiratory viruses but positive by family-wide coronavirus PCR. Sequencing yielded nearly the full genome of a novel lineage C betacoronavirus. Late patient sera reacted strongly against infected cells at 1:20, while 2400 tested control sera were negative. No virus was isolated from admission blood or a day-4 tracheal aspirate. |
| Main limitation | This is a single-case isolation and characterization paper. The patient had bacterial isolates, no autopsy was performed, no contacts were serologically tested, and the paper did not experimentally reproduce disease in animals or humans. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a novel lineage C betacoronavirus was isolated and genetically characterized from admission sputum of one fatal pneumonia case.
- It can answer that the isolated agent produced cytopathic effect under the culture conditions used and reacted with the patient's late serum.
- It can answer that the tested historical hospital sera were negative in the reported infected-cell serology assay.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove that HCoV-EMC/MERS-CoV was sufficient to cause the patient's full fatal pneumonia and renal-failure syndrome.
- It cannot separate the role of the isolated coronavirus from bacterial coinfections, host factors, or untested cofactors in this single patient.
- It cannot establish ordinary human transmission route, dose, incubation period, or population-level clinical spectrum.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating a single index-case isolation and sequencing paper as if it already supplied a controlled challenge, re-isolation, and ordinary-transmission proof. Zaki et al. is a crucial isolation rung, but the authors themselves left Koch-style experimental pathogenicity as a future question.

## Vault conclusion
Zaki et al. 2012 supports a primary MERS-CoV isolation and characterization rung from one fatal pneumonia case. It should be used for culture, RT-PCR, sequence, and serology endpoints, not as standalone proof of natural-route human causation or the complete MERS clinical syndrome.
