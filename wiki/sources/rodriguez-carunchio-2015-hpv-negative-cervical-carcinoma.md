# HPV-negative carcinoma of the uterine cervix (Rodríguez-Carunchio et al. 2015)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: HPV-negative/assay-boundary paper for the HPV boundary set
- Journal/year: *BJOG*, 2015
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Observational cohort reanalysis |
| Subject / sample | 136 women with cervical cancer (32 adenocarcinomas, 104 squamous cell carcinomas) who had pre-treatment Hybrid Capture 2 (HC2) testing, Barcelona, Spain |
| Intervention or exposure | Reanalysis of all HC2-negative cases with three PCR assays (SPF10, GP5+/6+, E7-specific) |
| Comparator / control | HPV-positive versus confirmed HPV-negative tumors within the cohort |
| Endpoint | Confirmed HPV-negative percentage; clinicopathological features; disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival |
| Result | 14/136 (10.2%) were HC2-negative. After PCR reanalysis, 8/136 (5.8%) were confirmed HPV-negative; six cases yielded HPVs (types 11, 16 in two tumors, 18, 45, 68). Confirmed negativity was more frequent in adenocarcinomas (5/32, 15.6%) than squamous carcinomas (3/104, 2.9%; P = 0.017). Confirmed HPV-negative tumors had worse DFS (51.9 months, 95% CI 12.2–91.7) than HPV-positive tumors (109.9 months, 95% CI 98.2–121.5; P = 0.010); HPV-negativity and FIGO stage were associated with progression and mortality in multivariate analysis. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; single-center cohort; the three PCR assays test a restricted type spectrum, so "confirmed negative" is assay-bounded, not absolute. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That almost half of HC2-negative cervical cancers in this cohort yielded HPV on more sensitive testing — the same failed-detection pattern as [[sources/kaliff-2020-hpv-negative-tumors-swedish-cohort]].
- That the residual confirmed-negative group is real enough in this dataset to show a different histologic distribution and a worse survival association.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Assay validation details; how DFS confounders (stage, treatment) were distributed; whether the HPV-negative survival difference survives stage-matched comparison.

## Later inferential-leap risk
"HPV-negative tumors exist" is sometimes read as "HPV is not necessary for cervical cancer." This paper's own numbers show the negative pool shrinks sharply under better assays, and the residual group is a small, histologically distinct minority — not a refutation of the association evidence in [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]].

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: in a 136-case cohort, HC2 negativity (10.2%) fell to 5.8% confirmed HPV-negative after three-assay PCR reanalysis; confirmed negatives clustered in adenocarcinomas and carried worse disease-free survival in this dataset.
