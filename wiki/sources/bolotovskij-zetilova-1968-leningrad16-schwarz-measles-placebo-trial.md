# Bolotovskij and Zetilova 1968 - Leningrad-16 vs Schwarz live measles vaccines with placebo control, including 12-month epidemiological effectiveness

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 26, queue item 51) from the complete 6-page scanned PDF of Bull World Health Organ 1968;39(2):293-298 (PMC2554543), retrieved from the WHO IRIS DSpace 7 REST API (handle 10665/266589 -> ORIGINAL bitstream PMC2554543.pdf) because the live PMC PDF endpoint serves a Google reCAPTCHA wall and Europe PMC fullTextXML 500s on scans. Every number below is from that scan, not from any summary. No DOI exists for this article. Authors: V. M. Bolotovskij (Chief, Department of Specific Prophylaxis, Institute of Epidemiology, Moscow) and L. P. Zetilova (junior research worker, same institute); "carried out as part of the WHO collaborative programme on measles vaccines and was financially supported by WHO."

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** randomized, blinded, three-arm comparative vaccine trial with a placebo group, set in "organized communities" (children's establishments; the Russian resume states 1,190 kindergarten pupils in Moscow), with a second observation phase measuring disease incidence over 12 months.
- **Subjects:** healthy children aged 1-6 years with no history of measles, never vaccinated against measles, and no immunoglobulin injections in the previous 6 weeks. Allocation (Table 1): L-16 n=409 (224 male/185 female), Schwarz n=393 (204/189), placebo n=388 (188/200); age bands 1-2 y n=128, 2-3 y n=261, 3-4 y n=182, 4-5 y n=250, 5-6 y n=200, over 6 y n=89.
- **Intervention:** L-16 live measles vaccine (Moscow Institute of Virus Preparations, "from the original strain isolated by Professor Smorodincev, Leningrad"), single subcutaneous injection; Schwarz live vaccine (Glaxo Laboratories, England), single intramuscular injection.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES and inert. Quote: "A control group of children was given injections of a placebo in the form of sterile tissue-culture fluid." Blinding quote: "The vaccines and the placebo were given code numbers, so that neither the medical workers at the children's establishments nor the members of the inoculation teams knew which children were receiving a vaccine and which the placebo." Allocation quote: "The children were allocated to 1 of the 3 groups by the random sampling method."
- **Design balance:** both vaccines and the placebo were used in every establishment, so each site contained three groups "similar to one another in numbers and also in respect of sex, age, physical development and the history of infectious diseases before and after vaccination" (Tables 2-3 document that balance: non-measles infectious-disease cases before vaccination 54.3%/61.1%/52.3% and after vaccination 10.4%/8.0%/9.8% across scarlet fever, whooping cough, infectious hepatitis, mumps, chickenpox, and rubella).
- **Observation intensity:** a daily record sheet for each child for 28 days with morning and evening rectal temperatures and daily physician examination; incomplete sheets excluded from the reaction analysis. Reaction grades: mild 37.1-37.7 C, moderate 37.8-39.4 C, severe >=39.5 C.

## Endpoints (named exactly)

1. **Designed endpoint 1 - reactogenicity (days 6-14 focus, 28-day record):** temperature reactions by grade, catarrhal symptoms, faucial hyperaemia, conjunctivitis, rash, intoxication symptoms, headache, convulsions - analyzed separately in children with and without antibody in the first serum specimen.
2. **Designed endpoint 2 - serology (surrogate):** 2-3 ml venous blood before vaccination and 28-30 days after, haemagglutination-inhibition (HI) test; paired sera available for 127 L-16 children (61 seronegative, 48.0%), 117 Schwarz (58, 49.6%), and 119 placebo (53, 44.5%).
3. **Designed endpoint 3 - epidemiological effectiveness (disease endpoint):** quoted in full - "The epidemiological effectiveness of the measles vaccines was evaluated by comparing the incidence of measles in contacts in each of the groups over a period of 1 year beginning 1 month after vaccination was completed." The endpoint is clinically diagnosed measles among children known to have been exposed ("contacts") in the collectives. Exclusion rule: "Children who had been given immunoglobulin after contact with measles and who did not contract the disease were excluded from the analysis because it was difficult to determine whether they had failed to contract measles as a result of vaccination or as a result of having been given immunoglobulin."

## Results

- **Baseline serology surprise:** of 586 children bled before vaccination, 305 (52.0%) were HI-seropositive despite no measles history, rising from 10.7% at 1-2 years to 74.0% at 5-6 years (GMT 5.9 log2). The authors' explanation - mild atypical attacks in children given immunoglobulin plus "the insufficiently precise recording of the disease" - is an untested hypothesis, not a measurement from this study.
- **Reactions (seronegative children, n=84 L-16 / 89 Schwarz / 87 placebo):** any temperature reaction 37.1 C or over, days 6-14: 45.2% vs 24.7% vs 11.5% - each pairwise difference statistically significant; moderate (37.8-39.4 C) 29.8% vs 12.4% vs 2.3%; one severe temperature (>=39.5 C) in the whole trial, an L-16 child at 39.9 C with delirium for one day; catarrhal symptoms 36.9% vs 21.3% vs 9.2%; rash 16.7% (14/84) vs 7.9% (7/89) vs 1.1% (1/87); 3 of 14 L-16 rashes and 1 of 7 Schwarz rashes resembled a typical measles rash "but without the characteristic succession of stages"; mean pyrexia duration 2.7/2.4/1.8 days; headache 17.9% vs 4.5% vs not stated (significant L-16 vs Schwarz); no convulsions in either vaccine group; children "continued to feel almost completely well." In the 283 seropositive children (100/95/88) no between-arm difference reached significance - background illness levels.
- **Serology (surrogate):** all 61 seronegative L-16 and all 58 seronegative Schwarz children seroconverted (100%); titres >=1:32 in 88.5% (L-16) and 91.3% (Schwarz); GMT 6.4 +-0.4 vs 6.1 +-0.2 log2 - no significant difference between vaccines. "Children in the control group, who were given the placebo, showed no rise in antibody titre." In seropositive children (66/59/66) titre was unchanged in 83.3%/78.0%/77.3% with rises and falls distributed identically across all three arms including placebo - the authors attribute the latter to test error, i.e. the trial itself provides placebo-calibrated evidence that vaccination did not boost existing titres.
- **Epidemiological effectiveness (disease endpoint):** an outbreak began 6 months after the campaign; imported measles cases occurred in 47 of the 56 establishments (28 once, 16 twice, 3 three times). Incidence of clinically diagnosed measles among children exposed to measles, over 12 months starting 1 month after vaccination: placebo 577.7 per 1,000 exposed children; Schwarz 56.6 per 1,000 ("10.2 times less"); L-16 47.1 per 1,000 ("12.3 times less") - the abstract rounds these to 58%, 6%, and 5%. "No statistically significant difference in measles morbidity was found between those inoculated with the L-16 and Schwarz vaccines but a significant difference was found between the vaccinated and control groups."
- **Specificity context:** non-measles infectious-disease incidence was balanced across arms both before (54.3%/61.1%/52.3%) and after vaccination (10.4%/8.0%/9.8%), so the measles-incidence contrast was not mirrored by a general reduction of childhood infections (these are similarity checks, not powered disease-by-disease contrasts).

## Main limitations

- The disease endpoint is clinical diagnosis of measles in Soviet child collectives during a recognized outbreak; no laboratory confirmation of case status is reported for the epidemiological phase.
- The denominators of the exposed subsets are not printed - the 577.7/56.6/47.1 per-1,000 rates are given without the number of "children in contact with measles" per arm.
- No confidence intervals or P-values are printed for the effectiveness comparison; only the two significance statements quoted above.
- The exclusion of immunoglobulin-protected post-contact non-cases is asymmetric in principle: protection attributable to immunoglobulin rather than vaccination is removed only when the child was dosed and stayed well.
- Exposed-contact incidence measures incidence among known contacts, not whole-arm incidence; children never exposed are neither cases nor explicitly counted in the denominator.
- No all-cause endpoint; other childhood diseases were tallied as balance checks, not as powered outcome contrasts.
- Population: 1-6-year-old Moscow children in institutional collectives in 1960s USSR; no long-term follow-up beyond 12 months; no immunogenicity persistence data beyond 28-30 days.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), measles rung - replication row:** the vault's measles vaccination rung previously held one row (Ristori et al. 1964, Chile) whose disease evidence was incidental (2 blinded-window cases) or an unplanned six-month subsample revisit. This trial's 12-month exposed-contact incidence comparison is a designed disease-endpoint phase with an inert placebo arm, upgrading the measles rung from single mixed-grade row to a two-row rung (one mixed-grade, one with a designed clinical-disease endpoint).
- **Vaccine-platform breadth:** it adds a USSR strains comparison (Leningrad-16 vs Schwarz, two manufacturers, Moscow Institute of Virus Preparations and Glaxo) to a rung previously anchored by Chilean Edmonston B, i.e., a second country, second pairing, and second sponsor network.
- **Placebo audit:** the placebo ("sterile tissue-culture fluid") is inert-class; the Ristori-style design family is preserved (blinded inoculation teams and ward staff, code numbers, random allocation).

## Inferential leaps recorded

- The "10.2 times less"/"12.3 times less" statements convert exposed-subset rates into fold-protection claims without denominators, intervals, or per-child exposure measures.
- The abstract's headline percentages (5%/6%/58%) rest on the exposed subsets, not the full randomized denominators (409/393/388).
- The claim that both vaccines "could be considered identical in immunogenic properties" rests on HI GMT concordance; the trial's own reactogenicity data show the strains were not identical (2x fever, ~2x rash, 4x headache for L-16 among seronegatives).
- The authors' explanation of the 52% baseline seropositivity (missed atypical measles after immunoglobulin, imprecise recording) is an untested hypothesis imported to interpret their own unexpected baseline data.
- The trial measures clinical measles only where diagnosis was plausible; asymptomatic or modified infections among "contacts" who stayed well are invisible, so the disease-incidence contrast cannot by itself quantify infection prevention.

> [!warning] Warning
> The disease endpoint here is unconfirmed clinical diagnosis during an epidemic. What the trial shows is that, under blinded allocation with an inert placebo, children exposed in collectives had roughly tenfold lower clinically diagnosed measles incidence over 12 months. It does not establish laboratory-confirmed infection prevention, all-cause health outcomes, or long-term protection. This page is research analysis, not medical advice.
