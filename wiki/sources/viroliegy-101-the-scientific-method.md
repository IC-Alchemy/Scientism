# ViroLIEgy 101: The Scientific Method

## Source metadata

- Raw source: `raw/ViroLIEgy 101 The Scientific Method.md`
- Author named in source: Mike Stone
- Published in source metadata: 2024-02-29
- Source type in this vault: Tier 3 commentary and research lead

> [!warning] Warning
> This page summarizes a Tier 3 commentary source. It can seed questions and comparison work, but it does not by itself justify factual conclusions about virology, germ theory, or the history and philosophy of science.

## What the source claims

The source argues that there is a definite and stable scientific method centered on observation, hypothesis formation, experimental testing, data analysis, and validation or falsification. It presents this method as the necessary boundary between science and pseudoscience.

The source further argues that many scientists, virologists, and critics of virology either misunderstand or deny this method. It claims that accepted virology does not satisfy the required method because the relevant hypotheses are not properly framed, the variables and controls are not correctly defined, and the claimed findings are therefore pseudoscientific.

The source also advances a historical claim that the modern scientific method has deep roots in ancient and medieval inquiry, with special emphasis on Ibn al-Haytham, Roger Bacon, Francis Bacon, Rene Descartes, and later discussion of falsifiability. It treats these historical references as support for a relatively fixed cross-era method.

The source then extends the methodological critique into broader conclusions. It claims that failure to follow the scientific method is a major reason for false published findings, the replication crisis, and the elevation of germ theory and virology to scientific status when they allegedly should not qualify.

## What evidence level this source provides

This source is best treated as commentary that compiles definitions, quotations, screenshots, and references in order to make a polemical methodological argument. It is useful as:

- a record of one critic's framework for evaluating science and pseudoscience
- a lead list for primary and secondary sources on the history of the scientific method
- a lead list for pages on falsifiability, replication, controls, and inferential overreach

This source does not itself establish the truth of its major conclusions. In particular, this page alone does not verify:

- that there is only one authoritative formulation of the scientific method
- that all scientific disciplines must fit a single linear step sequence
- that virology as a field fails the relevant methodological standards
- that germ theory has been experimentally falsified
- that the replication crisis can be reduced to a single cause

## What remains unresolved

- Which parts of the source's account of the scientific method reflect broad historical agreement, and which parts are contested simplifications
- Whether the cited historical figures support the source's strong modern framing of method, controls, and falsifiability
- Whether the source's critique of virology accurately represents specific experimental designs and endpoints used in virology
- How much of the source's argument depends on definitional choices rather than direct engagement with particular experiments
- Which claims in the article can be upgraded through Tier 1 or Tier 2 sourcing and which remain rhetorical assertions

## Notes for future verification

- Check the cited historical and educational references directly before using them in concept pages.
- Separate general method claims from claims about specific virology experiments.
- When evaluating any claimed methodological failure, identify the exact experiment, hypothesis, independent variable, dependent variable, controls, and endpoint.
