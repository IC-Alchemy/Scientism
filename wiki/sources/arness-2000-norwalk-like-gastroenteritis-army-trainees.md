# Norwalk-like Viral Gastroenteritis Outbreak in U.S. Army Trainees

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary outbreak investigation tying a gastroenteritis outbreak to Norwalk-like virus evidence
- Journal/year: *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, 2000
- Authors: M. K. Arness and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text CDC/PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Outbreak investigation |
| Subject / sample | 835 U.S. Army trainees, with hospitalized and questionnaire cohorts |
| Exposure or intervention | Dining-facility and soft-drink exposures investigated epidemiologically |
| Comparator / control | Soldiers not using the implicated facility or exposures; bacterial and parasitic testing |
| Endpoint | Acute gastroenteritis case definition, RT-PCR, electron microscopy, and exclusion of bacterial/parasitic causes in tested samples |
| Result | Ninety-nine soldiers were hospitalized. Genogroup 2 Norwalk-like virus RT-PCR was positive in 17 of 24 stools, EM found 30 nm particles in 7 of 7 tested, and bacterial/parasite tests were negative. |
| Main limitation | No agent was identified from the food handler or suspect foods; outbreak source and exact contamination event remain limited by design and recall. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that outbreak pattern and stool testing implicated Norwalk-like virus in this acute gastroenteritis outbreak.
- It can answer that tested bacterial/parasitic alternatives were not found in the reported samples.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove the exact contamination event with certainty.
- It cannot generalize to all norovirus outbreaks or define infectious dose.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is overclaiming the vehicle/source beyond what epidemiology and specimen testing can support.

## Vault conclusion
Arness et al. 2000 supports a norovirus-like outbreak investigation rung. It is useful for outbreak attribution but not for exact-source certainty.
