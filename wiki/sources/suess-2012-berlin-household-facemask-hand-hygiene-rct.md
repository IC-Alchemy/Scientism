# Facemasks and Hand Hygiene in the Prevention of Influenza Transmission in Households (Berlin cluster-randomized trial)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: second replication row for GAP 2 (controlled natural-route transmission) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] - the Cowling 2009 household design repeated in a European city, across a pandemic season (2009/10, all index infections A(H1N1)pdm09) and the following season (2010/11, A(H1N1)pdm09 plus influenza B).
- Journal/year: *BMC Infectious Diseases*, 2012;12:26
- Authors: Timo Suess, Carsten Remschmidt, Susanne B. Schink, Boris Schweiger, Anja Nitsche, Klaus Schroedter, Joachim Lustig, Markus Beier, Helmut Uphoff, Andrea Duwe, Winfried Matiaske, Ute Brosius, Walter Haas, Gérard Krause, Brunhilde Schweiger, Udo Buchholz, Carl-Peter Czogielek
- Trial registration: NCT00833885; funding: German Federal Ministry of Health (stated to have no role in study conduct or publication)
- Source basis in hand: full open-access article retrieved from Europe PMC fullTextXML (PMC3285078), read end to end including Methods, Tables 1-6, adherence figures, and Discussion/Conclusions.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cluster-randomized controlled trial with households as clusters, 1:1:1 allocation via pre-printed random-number lists given to recruiting physicians; physicians and laboratory blinded to allocation; participants necessarily unblinded (intervention boxes identical, contents differ). Two seasons: November 2009-January 2010 (pandemic) and January-April 2011; 8-day observation per household from index-patient symptom onset, five household visits |
| Subject / sample | 111 households randomized; after exclusions 84 analyzed: 30 control, 26 mask-only (M), 28 mask+hygiene (MH); 302 participants = 84 index patients (median age 7-8 y, 92-100% children) + 218 household contacts (82 C, 69 M, 67 MH; median age ~34-38 y). Index eligibility: >=2 years, presented within 2 days of symptom onset, positive rapid influenza antigen test (later qRT-PCR-confirmed), sole respiratory-ill household member in prior 14 days; excluded: pregnancy, severe health reduction, HIV, one-person households. Index viruses: 2009/10 all A(H1N1)pdm09 (41 index cases); 2010/11 24 A(H1N1)pdm09 + 20 B |
| Intervention or exposure | Natural household exposure to a laboratory-confirmed index patient, then household-level allocation. MH arm: surgical facemasks in child and adult sizes plus alcohol-based hand rub (Sterilium, Bode Chemie - the paper names the product but does not state its composition) with use instructions (hand rub after index contact, before meals/food preparation, on returning home); M arm: masks and instructions only. Masks required when index or any symptomatic member shared a room with healthy contacts; not at night or outside the household |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no**. Quote: "(iii) Control (C) arm: no masks or hand rub was provided. All participating households received general written information on infection prevention". No placebo devices; the control is a no-intervention cluster control, partially softened by the written information given to all arms |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: "The primary outcome measure for secondary cases was qRT-PCR confirmed influenza infection." Serial nasal washes (or swabs) collected from all household members on days 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8 regardless of symptoms; tested by qRT-PCR at the Robert Koch Institute (Centre for Biological Security 2009/10; National Reference Centre for Influenza 2010/11), MagNA Pure extraction, published primer/probe sequences. Symptomatic secondary infection defined as lab-confirmed plus fever >38.0 C, cough, or sore throat during observation; all other lab-confirmed cases termed subclinical. Secondary outcome: WHO-defined ILI (fever plus cough or sore throat). Analysis: intention-to-treat, adjusted chi-square with cluster bootstrap CIs and GEE logistic models; per-protocol pre-specified as secondary. Contacts were tested only for the index virus's type/subtype (stated limitation) |
| Result | Overall secondary attack rate 16% (35/218 contacts). Intention-to-treat, qRT-PCR-confirmed (Table 2): control 23% (19/82), M 9% (6/69), MH 15% (10/67), cluster-adjusted p=0.18 - not significant; ILI endpoint 17%/9%/9%, p=0.37. GEE odds ratios (Table 3): M 0.39 (0.13-1.19, p=0.10), MH 0.61 (0.23-1.66, p=0.34), M+MH pooled 0.50 (0.21-1.20, p=0.12). Subgroup: households with full intervention implementation <36 h after index onset (60 contacts): MH aOR 0.13 (0.01-1.28, p=0.08); pooled M+MH aOR 0.16 (0.03-0.92, p=0.04). Subgroup A(H1N1)pdm09 households: MH aOR 0.27 (0.07-0.99, p=0.049); pooled 0.33 (0.12-0.88, p=0.027). Per-protocol (Table 5): M complete-data aOR 0.30 (0.10-0.94, p=0.04); A(H1N1)pdm09 households per-protocol: M 0.28 (0.08-0.97), MH 0.26 (0.07-0.93), pooled 0.27 (0.10-0.76, p=0.01). Only one significant predictor in main models: contacts >=18 h/day at home had OR 3.1 (1.3-7.33, p=0.01) for confirmed infection. Notable negative cell: MH households in 2010/11 had SAR 25% overall and 35% in influenza-B households, exceeding control, attributed by the authors to that group's low mask/hand-rub adherence (index MH adherence definition 2: 2/11 = 18% in 2010/11 vs 7/17 = 41% in 2009/10) |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors: recruitment-to-implementation delay "could be as long as 3 days"; "we cannot determine whether a possible protective effect of wearing facemasks is more attributable to their use by index patients or by household contacts (or both), nor can we say if intensified hand hygiene provides any additional protection"; contacts tested only for the index's virus subtype ("This could have led to an underestimation of secondary cases"); behavior possibly influenced by the EUR 150 reimbursement and frequent visits (equal across arms); target sample not reached ("The main drawback of the study was that we did not reach the number of households that we had aimed and planned for") - the power calculation assumed a 75% SAR reduction (20% control vs 5%), i.e. the trial was underpowered for any smaller effect. Cross-season confounders documented in Table 1: timely antiviral therapy of index patients far higher in 2010/11 (65-91% vs 0-23%), contact vaccination higher in 2010/11, and randomization significantly later after onset in 2010/11 |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 16 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]]: second GAP 2 replication row. Same structure as Cowling 2009 (household clusters, index case, no-sham control, PCR endpoint), different population (Berlin, mostly adult contacts, child index cases), different strain mix (pandemic H1N1 2009 and influenza B 2011), and serial testing of all members irrespective of symptoms, which Cowling did only on three visit days.
- Cross-cutting: extends the natural-route row of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]; row in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]].

## What the experiment can answer
- That in Berlin households with qRT-PCR-confirmed index patients, no-intervention controls reached a 23% contact-level PCR-confirmed secondary attack rate across both seasons - an independent second measurement of the natural-route transmission quantity, this time with a pandemic strain (2009/10 SAR 13% overall; 2010/11 19%).
- That as randomized, neither masks+hand rub (SAR 15%) nor masks alone (SAR 9%) significantly reduced qRT-PCR-confirmed secondary infection (adjusted p=0.18), and neither reduced the WHO-ILI endpoint (p=0.37).
- That significance appears only in secondary readings: the within-36-hours implementation subgroup (pooled aOR 0.16, p=0.04), the pandemic-strain subgroup (pooled aOR 0.33, p=0.027), and per-protocol analyses (pooled aOR 0.27, p=0.01) - each requiring post-randomization conditioning or adherence-based exclusion.
- That higher time-at-home (>=18 h/day) is a significant exposure marker for confirmed secondary infection (OR 3.1), supporting the endpoint's exposure-response coherence.

## What it cannot answer
- Efficacy as randomized: the primary intention-to-treat result is null, and the trial was powered only for a 75% effect that the observed point estimates (roughly 40-60% reductions) could not reach at n=218 contacts.
- Whether masks act at the index or the contact, or whether hand rub adds anything beyond masks (authors' own stated inability).
- Route attribution; the bundle cannot decompose droplet/contact/fomite/aerosol contributions.
- The 2010/11 MH-cell inversion (SAR above control) shows the result is not robust across seasons; no cause was identified beyond adherence.
- Any claim about influenza causation; the trial presupposes lab-confirmed index infection and measures transmission.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- Conclusion: "results of our study contribute to the body of evidence that NPI may be effective in preventing transmission of influenza in households. Prerequisites include early implementation of the intervention and good adherence." Both cited prerequisites come from secondary (subgroup and per-protocol) analyses; the randomized result is null. This is the same leap pattern as Cowling 2009, recorded there and in [[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]]: a secondary conditioning elevated to the paper's headline claim.
- Abstract: "Concerns about acceptability and tolerability of the interventions should not be a reason against their recommendation" - a policy recommendation from a single underpowered trial whose ITT result was not significant.
- Discussion: "we believe that our data for adherence and tolerability would support a recommendation to use non-pharmaceutical interventions in a pandemic" - adherence/tolerability endpoints do not measure efficacy.

## Vault conclusion
Supported as a replication row at the controlled natural-route rung: a second household cluster-randomized trial with serial qRT-PCR endpoints independently documents a ~23% control-arm secondary attack rate and no significant intervention effect as randomized, with significant effects confined to the 36-hour subgroup, the pandemic-strain subgroup, and per-protocol analyses - the same structure Cowling 2009 reported. Two of three GAP 2 replications now agree on the rung-level result: null as randomized, significant only after conditioning.
