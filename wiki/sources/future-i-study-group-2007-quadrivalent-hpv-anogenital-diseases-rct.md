# Quadrivalent Vaccine against Human Papillomavirus to Prevent Anogenital Diseases (FUTURE I)

> [!warning] Warning
> This page records what one experiment shows for vault analysis. It is not medical advice and makes no vaccination recommendation.

## Bibliographic record

- Authors: The FUTURE I Study Group (Garland SM, Hernandez-Avila M, Wheeler CM, Perez G, Harper DM, Leodolter S, Tang GWK, Ferris DG, Steben M, Bryan J, Taddeo FJ, Railkar R, Esser MT, Sings HL, Nelson M, Boslego J, Sattler C, Barr E, Koutsky LA) for the FUTURE I Investigators.
- Citation: N Engl J Med 2007;356(19):1928-1943 (May 10, 2007).
- PMID 17494926; DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa061760; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00092521.
- Sponsor: Merck Research Laboratories "funded the study in its entirety"; the paper states the study was "designed and managed and the results were analyzed by the sponsor in conjunction with external academic investigators and an external data and safety monitoring board," and that "the manuscript was drafted by employees of the sponsor in collaboration with academic authors." Nine coauthors are named as current or former Merck employees holding stock or options; the academic coauthors list Merck consulting, advisory, lecture, and grant payments.

## Study type

Ongoing, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized phase 3 vaccine-efficacy trial. A fixed-event design required at least 38 cases of each coprimary composite endpoint for 91% power at a one-sided alpha of 0.0125 (multiplicity-adjusted), assuming true efficacy of at least 80%; the triggered interim analysis (38 external and 37 cervical events) was the basis of the FDA licensure application approved June 8, 2006. This report adds one further year: efficacy and safety analyses use visits through June 15, 2006, an average of 3 years after dose 1.

## Subjects

6,463 women screened January 2002-March 2003 at 62 sites in 16 countries (Asia-Pacific, Europe, Americas); 5,455 (84%) enrolled, ages 16-24, drawn "primarily from communities near universities." Eligibility: healthy, not pregnant, no history of genital warts or abnormal cervical cytology, lifetime number of sex partners no more than four, effective contraception required through month 7. Only 0.15% (8/5,388) had serologic or HPV-DNA evidence of infection with all four vaccine types at baseline.

## Exposure / intervention

Quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 L1 virus-like-particle vaccine with amorphous aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate (AAHS) adjuvant (Gardasil, Merck), three intramuscular doses at day 1, month 2, month 6; 30-minute post-dose observation; vaccination report cards for 15 days after each dose.

## Comparator / control

Sham arm: YES - but not inert. The placebo contains the same aluminum adjuvant without antigen.

> "The quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 L1 virus-like-particle vaccine with amorphous aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate (Gardasil, Merck) as an adjuvant and the aluminum-containing placebo were visually indistinguishable and have been described elsewhere."

Recorded: this is an adjuvant-matched comparator, the same comparator class as the sister trial [[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]] (see [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]]). Systemic safety against a true inert placebo is untested inside this trial.

## Endpoint (named exactly)

Two coprimary composite efficacy hypotheses, "as compared with placebo":

1. "the quadrivalent vaccine reduces the combined incidence of anogenital warts, vulvar or vaginal intraepithelial neoplasia grades 1 to 3, or cancer associated with vaccine-type HPV";
2. "the quadrivalent vaccine reduces the combined incidence of cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grades 1 to 3, adenocarcinoma in situ, or cancer associated with vaccine-type HPV."

A case requires a consensus diagnosis by a blinded four-pathology-panel adjudication plus "vaccine-type HPV DNA detected in an adjacent histologic section of the same biopsy specimen"; "Clinical impressions were recorded but were not used to determine an end point in the efficacy analysis." Primary analysis population: HPV-type-specific per-protocol susceptible (PCR- and seronegative for the relevant vaccine type at enrollment and through one month after dose 3, three doses within one year, no protocol violations), with case ascertainment starting one month after dose 3. Two prespecified supportive populations: unrestricted susceptible and intention-to-treat.

## Result

Per-protocol susceptible populations (mean ~3 years follow-up):

- External anogenital/vaginal composite: 0 vs 60 cases (2261 vs 2279 women) - vaccine efficacy 100% (95% CI 94-100).
- Cervical composite: 0 vs 65 cases (2241 vs 2258 women) - 100% (95% CI 94-100).

Unrestricted susceptible population (baseline-negative only, dosing-interval deviations allowed): 95% (4 vs 81 cases) external anogenital/vaginal; 98% (2 vs 89) cervical; 91% (1 vs 11) high-grade vulvar/vaginal lesions; 100% (0 vs 6) adenocarcinoma in situ.

Intention-to-treat (all randomized): against vaccine-type-associated disease 73% (95% CI 58-83; 28 vs 102) external and 55% (40-66; 71 vs 155) cervical - the vaccine-arm cases "occurred in subjects who were infected with vaccine-type HPV before vaccination." Against disease "regardless of the causal HPV type": 34% (15-49; 104 vs 157) external, 20% (8-31; 344 vs 421) cervical. "No cancers associated with any vaccine-type HPV were identified"; one vulvar cancer (non-vaccine-type) occurred in the vaccine group. Seroconversion at one month after dose 3: at least 99.5% per vaccine type. Of six vaccine-arm breakthrough lesions, five were in women with anti-HPV titers similar to the per-protocol population and the sixth had "incorrectly received three doses of placebo."

Safety: injection-site adverse events 87% vaccine vs 77% placebo (pain risk difference 10 percentage points, 95% CI 7.8-12.1); fever 37.8-38.9 degrees C 13.3% vs 10.3% (RD 3.0, 1.3-4.8); "Similar proportions of vaccine and placebo recipients reported a serious adverse event"; one serious vaccine-related adverse event (bronchospasm one day after dose 3) in the seropositive subgroup; "No multiplicity adjustments were made for these comparisons."

## Main limitation

Authors' own statements: lack of long-term follow-up ("The duration of the efficacy of the quadrivalent HPV vaccine and whether boosters are needed are not known"); no minimum protective anti-HPV titer identified; "limited power to definitively address individual components of the composite study end points"; primary analysis restricted to HPV-naive women. Additional vault-recorded boundaries: the placebo is adjuvant-matched, not inert; the disease endpoints are precursor lesions (warts, intraepithelial neoplasia) plus rare cancers - zero cancers of any type were accrued in either arm; and the population is healthy 16-24-year-old women with at most four lifetime partners over a three-year average window.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint) - HPV replication row, second HPV row in the vault. FUTURE II anchored the rung against the cervical CIN2/3+AIS+cancer composite; FUTURE I replicates the design in the same volume/issue with (a) the external anogenital disease endpoint set (warts, VIN/VaIN) as a coprimary endpoint in its own right and (b) an independent trial population (5,455 women 16-24 vs FUTURE II's 12,167 women 15-26). This is rung-9 intervention evidence in [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] and the second row of the intervention rung in [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]].

> [!note] Note
> The designed endpoints are lesion/precursor endpoints. Cancer prevention remains an extrapolation from precursor lesions in both HPV trials; neither arm accrued cancers (one vaccine-arm non-vaccine-type vulvar cancer excepted).

## Inferential leaps recorded

- "Widespread vaccination of young women and adolescent girls should reduce the incidence of cervical and external anogenital disease" - a public-health generalization from a 16-24-year-old, at-most-four-lifetime-partners, three-year average trial window; adolescents were not enrolled in this trial (immunogenicity bridging cited to a separate Pediatrics study).
- "These data also suggest that there is some flexibility in the timing of the vaccination regimen" - inferred from the unrestricted-susceptible analysis (deviations in dosing intervals), not from a randomized schedule comparison.
- Duration-of-protection claims are supported from a separate phase 2 trial (five-year data) cited in the Discussion, not measured in this experiment.
- The authors themselves bound the anti-cancer and therapeutic readings: "There was no clear evidence that vaccination altered the course of disease or infection present before administration of the first dose."

## What this wiki currently concludes

Source-reported: in HPV-6/11/16/18-susceptible young women, three doses of quadrivalent L1 VLP vaccine prevented, with zero vaccine-arm cases, composite wart/VIN/VaIN and CIN/AIS endpoint events carrying vaccine-type HPV DNA in the lesion, against an aluminum-matched placebo over a three-year average window. Vault status: the trial supports a prophylactic-intervention claim for vaccine-type-associated lesion endpoints in the enrolled population. It does not by itself answer cancer prevention, long-term durability, inert-placebo systemic safety, effectiveness in older women or men, or overall-health outcomes.
