# The Efficacy of Live Attenuated, Cold-Adapted, Trivalent, Intranasal Influenzavirus Vaccine in Children

## Source type
- Role in this vault: the vault's first GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint) row for influenza - until this page the GAP 4 rung held PCV9 ([[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]]), rotavirus ([[sources/vesikari-2006-pentavalent-rotavirus-rest-rct]]), HPV ([[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]]), SARS-CoV-2 mRNA and adenovector ([[sources/polack-2020-bnt162b2-covid-rct]], [[sources/sadoff-2021-ad26cov2s-covid-rct]]), and RSV ([[sources/ison-2024-rsvpref3oa-two-season-rct]]) but not the disease at the center of the vault's influenza ladders; fills queue item 26 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]].
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine* 1998;338(20):1405-1412 (May 14, 1998). PMID 9580647, DOI 10.1056/NEJM199805143382002. Not in PMC (pre-deposit era). Sponsor/funder: National Institutes of Health contracts N01-AI-45250/-45248/-45251/-25135/-45252/-45249 plus Aviron (the vaccine manufacturer).
- Authors: Robert B. Belshe, Paul M. Mendelman, John Treanor, James King, William C. Gruber, Pedro Piedra, David I. Bernstein, Frederick G. Hayden, Karen Kotloff, Ken Zangwill, Dominick Iacuzio, Mark Wolff (+ other authors listed in footnote) - 10 study sites, Saint Louis University, Aviron, and Emmes Corporation (statistics).

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter efficacy trial; 10 centers; single influenza season (1996-1997); two cohorts by regimen: 1,314 children assigned to two doses ~60 days apart (doses Aug 21-Oct 23, 1996 and Oct 15, 1996-Jan 11, 1997) and 288 assigned to one dose (enrolled/vaccinated Sep 30-Dec 5, 1996); 2:1 randomization to vaccine or placebo ("randomly assigned in a 2:1 ratio"); block size six, sequential vaccination numbers prepared off-site; each child randomized individually; 97% of the two-dose cohort received both doses; no ClinicalTrials.gov registration (1998 predates it) |
| Subjects | Healthy children 15-71 months at recruitment; excluded: clinically significant egg hypersensitivity, chronic illnesses for which inactivated vaccine would be recommended; Table 1 analysis cohorts: vaccine N=1,070, placebo N=532; 53%/47% female/male (vaccine), 85% white; baseline age mean 43.0 vs 41.5 months, no significant baseline differences; immunogenicity substudy n=203 (~first 21 per site) |
| Intervention | Live attenuated, cold-adapted, trivalent influenzavirus vaccine (Aviron), frozen single-dose intranasal applicators; mean tissue-culture infective dose 10^6.7 per strain; strains matched to the FDA-recommended 1996-1997 inactivated-vaccine antigens: A/Texas/36/91-like (H1N1), A/Wuhan/359/95-like (H3N2), B/Harbin/7/94-like, in egg allantoic fluid with sucrose, phosphate, and glutamate; large-particle aerosol spray, two 0.25 mL aliquots (one per nostril), 0.5 mL total |
| Comparator / control | **Sham arm: YES.** Quote (Methods, Vaccine and Placebo): "The placebo consisted of egg allantoic fluid containing sucrose, phosphate, and glutamate and was indistinguishable in appearance and smell from the vaccine." The placebo is the identical vehicle without virus - antigen-free and adjuvant-free (contrast [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]]'s placebo-composition audit and [[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]]'s aluminum-containing placebo). Dosage form identical (syringe-like calibrated sprayer) for both arms |
| Endpoint (named exactly) | Primary: "The primary efficacy endpoint was the first episode of culture-confirmed influenza for subjects who became ill 28 days or more after the receipt of the first dose of vaccine or placebo or at any time after the receipt of the second dose during the influenza season." Case definition (Methods, Surveillance for Influenza and Case Definitions): "A case of influenza was defined as any illness detected by active surveillance ... that was associated with a positive culture for wild-type influenzavirus" - parents phoned every 2-3 weeks (weekly during local outbreaks), any symptom report (fever, runny nose, congestion, sore throat, cough, headache, myalgia, chills, vomiting, otitis media, decreased activity, irritability, wheezing, shortness of breath, pulmonary congestion) triggered culture within 4 days of illness onset; rhesus-monkey-kidney tissue cultures inoculated within 4 h of collection; isolates phenotyped to separate wild-type from vaccine-strain shedding; cultures within 28 days of a dose excluded from efficacy and typed. Secondary: febrile illness regardless of culture result, febrile otitis media (provider diagnosis + fever, thermometer documentation not required), febrile illness with antibiotic use, HI antibody responses. Statistical note: efficacy = 100 x (1 - PV/PP), i.e., 1 - risk ratio; Koopman's method for CIs; logistic GEE with exchangeable covariance to handle multiple children per family; Bonferroni adjustment for vaccination-reaction comparisons only |
| Result | Culture-confirmed influenza: **14/1,070 vaccine vs 95/532 placebo; efficacy 93% (95% CI 88-96)** (Table 4). One-dose cohort: 3/189 vs 14/99 = 89% (65-96). Two-dose (receiving both doses): 10/849 vs 74/410 = 94% (88-97). By type, all subjects: A(H3N2) 7 vs 64 = 95% (88-97); B 7 vs 37 = 91% (79-96); zero wild-type A(H1N1) circulated anywhere in subjects or communities this season, so no H1N1 efficacy estimate exists. Placebo attack rate 18%; >80% of placebo cases febrile and 20 of 95 with otitis media, vs 8/14 febrile and 1 otitis media among the 14 vaccine failures (illness severity in breakthrough cases lower). Febrile illness (regardless of culture): 21% fewer episodes in vaccine recipients (95% CI 11-30; P<0.001; 0.71 vs 0.90 per child); febrile otitis media 30% fewer (18-45; P<0.001); any febrile illness with antibiotic use 29% fewer (15-39; P<0.001); febrile otitis media with antibiotics 35% fewer (18-45; P<0.001). **Indirect-protection null inside the trial**: among 182 placebo recipients with at least one vaccinated sibling, 32 culture-positive cases (rate 18%) - identical to the overall placebo rate: "Clearly, having a vaccinated sibling was not protective for children given placebo; contact with persons infected with influenzavirus was sufficient to spread the virus to unvaccinated children. Therefore, protection of children will require widespread use of influenzavirus vaccines at a young age." Immunogenicity: post-dose-2 fourfold responses among initially seronegative children: A(H1N1) 61%, A(H3N2) 96%, B 96% (Table 3; placebo 2-11%). Safety: transient rhinorrhea/congestion day-2 RR 1.5 (1.2-1.8, Bonferroni-adjusted P=0.001), fever day-2 RR 4.1 (2.0-8.3, adjusted P<0.001; mean fever 1.4 days, mean 38.2 C, >=38.3 C in 20 vaccine vs 4 placebo on day 2, P=0.08), decreased activity day-2 RR 2.8 (adjusted P=0.008); abdominal pain after first dose 19 vaccine vs 1 placebo (COSTART-coded); no significant group differences on any day after dose 2; 4 serious adverse events within 42 days in vaccine group (S. aureus foot infection, abdominal pain, motor vehicle accident, dehydration) and 1 in placebo (shunt revision) - "in no instance did the investigators attribute the adverse event to the vaccination"; two children withheld dose 2 for adverse reactions after dose 1, "both these children were placebo recipients" (hives; wheezing) - as printed |
| Main limitation | Single season and single geographic antigenic mix: no wild-type A(H1N1) at all, so the H1N1 component is untested against disease; surveillance-triggered culture means asymptomatic infections are invisible (the endpoint is illness-associated culture-confirmed influenza, not all infection - serology was substudy-only, no case ascertainment by seroconversion); culture required within 4 days of symptom onset (missed-culture window); 2:1 allocation; pregnancy/long-term outcomes out of scope; trial healthy-children only (chronically ill children excluded); sponsor = vaccine manufacturer (Aviron) co-funding with NIH contracts; no head-to-head inactivated-vaccine arm (the "at least as efficacious, and probably more efficacious" claim rests on historical comparisons the authors cite, including a Japanese asthmatic-children study) |
| Inferential leap (authors') | (1) "Widespread use of this well-tolerated, safe, convenient, and highly efficacious vaccine would substantially benefit children" - from one season, two circulating subtypes, healthy children 15-71 months, to universal childhood use including future seasons and strains not tested here; (2) the inactivated-vaccine superiority claim - explicitly from "historical data", not from any arm in this trial ("Although we did not evaluate the efficacy of inactivated influenzavirus vaccine, historical data suggest that the live attenuated vaccine is at least as efficacious, and probably more efficacious, than the inactivated vaccine in children"); (3) mechanism inference, flagged by the authors themselves as inference: equal efficacy across ages despite the age-gradient in seroresponse means "additional factors, such as stimulation of secretory antibody or cellular immunity, must be important mechanisms" (serum antibody is "not necessarily the best or the only correlate of protection"); (4) from the vaccinated-sibling null to the population-level claim "protection of children will require widespread use of influenzavirus vaccines at a young age" - a policy extrapolation from an unpowered, unplanned subgroup (182 placebo children with a vaccinated sibling, one season, one household setting); the vault records the sibling-null itself as the hard result |

## What question this experiment answers
Whether, in healthy 15-71-month-old children during a season with circulating A(H3N2) and B, an intranasal cold-adapted trivalent LAIV (10^6.7 TCID50/strain) with an indistinguishable vehicle placebo reduces first episodes of illness-associated culture-confirmed wild-type influenza, febrile illness, and febrile otitis media over one season - and whether intra-household indirect protection was detectable in placebo children whose siblings were vaccinated (it was not). It does **not** answer H1N1-component efficacy (no H1N1 circulated), asymptomatic-infection prevention, efficacy in chronically ill children, multi-season durability, or transmission reduction (no non-sibling transmission measurement).

## What this wiki currently concludes
- GAP 4 now has an influenza row: a randomized double-blind trial with an inert, indistinguishable sham arm, a culture-confirmed disease endpoint, 93% (88-96) efficacy against the primary endpoint, and reductions in downstream febrile-illness and otitis-media outcomes - all from the paper's own Methods and Results.
- The intra-trial sibling analysis is a rare experimental observation of **no** indirect protection to placebo contacts in a vaccine trial setting - recorded here and cross-linked wherever the vault discusses herd-effects claims (contrast [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] GAP 2 natural-route rows, where NPI trials were null as randomized).
- The universal-use and inactivated-vaccine-comparison sentences are attributed leaps, not results of this experiment.
