# Idiopathic CD4+ T-lymphocytopenia: five patients (Spira et al. 1993)

> [!note] Note
> the vault's register previously attributed this paper to "Ho et al." — the first author is Thomas J. Spira. This page corrects that attribution.

## Source type
- Role in this vault: exclusivity-boundary paper — a virology-and-immunology workup of HIV-negative opportunistic-infection cases
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 1993
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Detailed case series with virologic and immunologic testing |
| Subject / sample | Five patients with idiopathic CD4+ T-lymphocytopenia and serious opportunistic infections |
| Intervention or exposure | Serologic testing, culture, and PCR for HIV-1 and HIV-2; serology for HTLV-I/II; lymphocyte phenotyping; immunoglobulin quantitation; lymphocyte-transformation assays; attempts to isolate a retroviral agent |
| Comparator / control | HIV-infected persons matched for CD4 count; normal controls; spouses of patients and one patient's blood donor |
| Endpoint | HIV/HTLV detection; lymphocyte subset counts and percentages; immunoglobulin levels; proliferation responses; stability of CD4 over time |
| Result | No evidence of HIV or HTLV infection in any patient, and no retroviral agent isolated. All had low CD4 percentages and counts with relative (not absolute) CD8 increases; B and NK cells generally normal. Compared with CD4-matched HIV-infected persons, patients had lower CD8 percentages and counts, more lymphopenia, relatively stable CD4 counts over time, and normal or slightly low — rather than high — immunoglobulin levels. Mitogen/antigen responses were depressed. Spouses and the blood donor were normal. The authors concluded ICD differs immunologically from HIV infection and shows apparent lack of progression, with nothing suggesting a transmissible cause. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 5; 1993-era assay sensitivity; absence of isolation does not exclude all possible agents; short observation window for "lack of progression." |

## What the abstract can answer
- That the HIV-negative CD4-deficiency syndrome differs immunologically from matched-count HIV infection (CD8 profile, immunoglobulin levels, CD4 stability) — an immunologic distinction, not merely a testing artifact.
- That aggressive retroviral search methods (serology, culture, PCR, isolation attempts) found no agent.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Per-patient assay tables; follow-up duration; opportunistic-infection spectrum.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Same exclusivity-boundary discipline as [[sources/smith-1993-icd-opportunistic-infections-no-hiv]]: this defines a distinct syndrome; it does not license "opportunistic infections arise without infection" or "HIV is unnecessary to explain AIDS," because the investigated syndrome was rare, non-transmissible in contacts, and immunologically separable from HIV disease.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: five ICD patients showed no HIV/HTLV signal by serology, culture, PCR, or isolation attempts, with an immunologic profile distinct from CD4-matched HIV infection and stable CD4 counts — an exclusivity boundary with an immunologic, not just serologic, separation.
