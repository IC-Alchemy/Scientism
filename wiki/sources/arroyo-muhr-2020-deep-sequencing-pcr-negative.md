# Deep sequencing detects HPV in PCR-negative cervical cancers (Arroyo Mühr et al. 2020, Br J Cancer)

> [!note] Note
> the vault's register previously attributed this paper to "Lagheden et al." — the first author is Arroyo Mühr; Lagheden is second author. This page corrects that attribution.

## Source type
- Role in this vault: failed-detection boundary paper — direct evidence that PCR-negative status in cervical cancer is often a detection limit
- Journal/year: *British Journal of Cancer*, 2020
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC7722749)

## What the source claims
- The abstract's background asserts HPV is "a necessary cause" of cervical cancer — that is author framing carried in from the 1999 necessity literature, not something this sequencing experiment itself tests. The vault keeps the framing attributed and separate from the paper's own endpoint.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Unbiased deep sequencing (total DNA and cDNA) of archived tumor material, plus a cumulative registry of prior PCR testing |
| Subject / sample | 392 HPV PCR-negative invasive cervical cancers from the Swedish 10-year collection; combined database of 2,850 invasive cervical cancers with PCR and/or sequencing data |
| Intervention or exposure | NovaSeq 6000 sequencing of DNA and cDNA; non-human reads queried against all known HPV types |
| Comparator / control | The same tumors' prior PCR-negative status; type-specific PCR detection modeling for HPV31/33/45/73 |
| Endpoint | HPV sequence detection in PCR-negative tumors; HPV type distribution; overall HPV positivity across the database |
| Result | HPV sequences were detected in 169/392 PCR-negative cervical cancers. Thirty HPV types were detected overall, with only five types above 3% of cancers. Across the full database, more than 92% of tumors were HPV-positive by PCR and/or sequencing (95% CI 91.1–93.1). Most prior misses were modeled as failures of insufficiently sensitive type-specific PCR for HPV31/33/45/73 (117/392). |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; sequencing detection is a nucleic-acid endpoint, not proof of viral role in each tumor; PCR-negative sampling of the 392 is conditioned on prior negative results. |

## What the abstract can answer
- How many PCR-negative cervical cancers in a defined national series carry detectable HPV sequences under unbiased sequencing: 169 of 392.
- What overall HPV positivity reaches in a large cancer series when PCR and deep sequencing are combined: above 92%.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Read-depth thresholds and validation of low-level detections; whether the 223 still-negative tumors were tissue-degraded; per-type sequencing confirmation.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap is collapsing "HPV sequence detected" into "HPV caused that tumor." The sequencing rung detects viral nucleic acid; causation for any individual tumor still rests on the separate detection/integration/progression rungs in [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]]. The mirror leap — treating 7.9% residual negativity as proof of a large HPV-independent class — also overreaches given false-negative detection demonstrated within the same dataset.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: unbiased deep sequencing recovered HPV sequences from 169 of 392 PCR-negative cervical cancers and lifted cumulative HPV positivity above 92% in a 2,850-case Swedish series, directly demonstrating that PCR-negative status is substantially a detection boundary.
