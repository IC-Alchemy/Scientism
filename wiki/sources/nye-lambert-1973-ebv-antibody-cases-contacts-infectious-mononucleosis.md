# Epstein-Barr Virus Antibody in Cases and Contacts of Infectious Mononucleosis

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary family-contact serology study for EBV infection around clinically recognized infectious mononucleosis
- Journal/year: *Journal of Hygiene*, 1973
- Authors: F. J. Nye and H. P. Lambert
- Source basis in hand: full-text Cambridge PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Case-control and prospective family-contact serology study |
| Subject / sample | 147 Paul-Bunnell-positive infectious mononucleosis patients from the general population; 306 followed contacts of 132 patients; 322 hospital surgical inpatients as controls; 13 persistently Paul-Bunnell-negative glandular-fever-like patients and their contacts |
| Exposure or intervention | EBV antibody testing by indirect immunofluorescence using EB3 Burkitt lymphoma cells, heterophile antibody testing, atypical-lymphocyte assessment, and testing for cytomegalovirus, adenovirus, toxoplasma, and rubella when relevant |
| Comparator / control | Age- and sex-matched controls, family contacts stratified by baseline EBV antibody status, initially EBV-antibody-negative contacts, and Paul-Bunnell-negative mononucleosis-like cases |
| Endpoint | EBV antibody presence, seroconversion or fourfold antibody change, Paul-Bunnell-positive IM, atypical lymphocytosis, and alternative serologic evidence for CMV or other infections |
| Result | All 147 Paul-Bunnell-positive IM patients possessed or developed EBV antibody, and 93 of 147 showed significant EBV antibody change. Among 306 followed contacts, 21 showed recent EBV infection or reinfection: 12 seroconversions among 63 initially antibody-negative contacts and 9 significant antibody changes among contacts already antibody-positive. Five contacts developed Paul-Bunnell-positive IM. Among 13 Paul-Bunnell-negative glandular-fever-like cases, four showed recent EBV infection and two probable CMV mononucleosis. |
| Main limitation | The study used serology and contact follow-up rather than virus isolation, controlled challenge, or direct transmission tracing. Contact sampling intervals could miss recent infection among those already antibody-positive, and mononucleosis-like illness had multiple etiologies. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that Paul-Bunnell-positive IM cases in this set were consistently EBV-antibody-positive or seroconverted.
- It can answer that EBV infection or reinfection occurred among contacts after index IM cases, but clinical IM was a minority outcome.
- It can answer that some Paul-Bunnell-negative mononucleosis-like cases were associated with EBV, some with CMV, and some remained unexplained in this study.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove EBV transmission route from index case to contact.
- It cannot prove purified-virus causation or sufficiency.
- It cannot make every mononucleosis-like syndrome an EBV disease.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if contact seroconversion and antibody association are treated as if they were direct isolation and challenge evidence. The paper strengthens association, susceptibility, and contact-spread rungs while preserving inapparent infection and alternative-etiology boundaries.

## Vault conclusion
Nye and Lambert 1973 supports a narrow claim: EBV antibody was consistently present or developed in Paul-Bunnell-positive IM cases, and EBV infection markers appeared in a subset of contacts, while clinical IM was not the universal outcome of EBV infection and some mono-like illnesses were not EBV-linked.
