# Analysis of the Physical State of Different Human Papillomavirus DNAs in Cervical Neoplasm

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary biopsy study comparing HPV physical state across cervical disease categories
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1991
- Authors: Allison P. Cullen, Richard Reid, Michael Campion, and Attila T. Lorincz
- Source basis in hand: full-text Europe PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cross-sectional molecular analysis of HPV type and physical state in cervical biopsy specimens |
| Subject / sample | 181 HPV-DNA-positive cervical biopsy specimens selected from a larger set, representing latent infection/normal tissue, CIN1, CIN2-CIN3, and invasive cervical cancer from several North American clinical sites |
| Exposure or intervention | DNA extraction, HPV typing, Southern blotting with HPV type-specific probes, restriction-enzyme panels to distinguish episomal and integrated patterns, and two-dimensional gel electrophoresis when Southern blots were ambiguous |
| Comparator / control | Histologic categories, HPV types, model mixtures of integrated SiHa DNA and episomal HPV16 plasmid DNA, noncutting/cutting restriction enzymes, and repeat biopsy specimens from the same cancer in a subset |
| Endpoint | Episomal HPV DNA, integrated HPV DNA, or mixed episomal-plus-integrated HPV DNA pattern |
| Result | All 12 latent HPV infections and all 39 CIN1 specimens showed only episomal HPV DNA. Three of 61 CIN2-CIN3 specimens showed both episomal and integrated HPV DNA; the rest were episomal only. Among 69 invasive cancers, 56 showed integration: 47 integrated only and 9 mixed. Thirteen cancers, including some HPV16 cancers, showed only episomal HPV DNA. All 23 HPV18-containing cancers had integrated HPV DNA, while 29 of 40 HPV16-containing cancers had integrated or mixed HPV DNA. |
| Main limitation | The study was cross-sectional and selected for HPV-DNA-positive specimens. Histopathology was not reviewed by a single pathologist. Some specimens remained ambiguous or lacked enough DNA for confirmatory analysis. Physical state does not prove causal timing. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that detectable HPV integration was much more frequent in invasive cervical cancers than in latent/CIN1 or CIN2-CIN3 specimens in this selected set.
- It can answer that integration was not universal in cervical cancer, because 13 of 69 cancers showed only episomal HPV DNA.
- It can answer that HPV18-containing cancers in this set all showed integrated HPV DNA, while a substantial minority of HPV16-containing cancers showed only episomal DNA.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that integration causes invasive cancer.
- It cannot prove that integration is necessary for malignant progression.
- It cannot establish prospective progression risk from episomal or integrated HPV because the design is cross-sectional.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This paper is valuable because it blocks a simple integration-equals-cancer story. The leap begins if the high integration frequency in invasive cancers is treated as universal necessity or as proof of temporal causality.

## Vault conclusion
This source supports a narrow claim: Cullen et al. 1991 found HPV DNA integration much more often in invasive cervical cancers than in CIN or latent infection, while also finding invasive cancers with only episomal HPV DNA and therefore showing that detectable integration was not universally required.
