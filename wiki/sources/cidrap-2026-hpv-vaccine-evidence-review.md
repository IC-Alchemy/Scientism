# Safety, effectiveness, and immunogenicity of HPV vaccines (CIDRAP 2026)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: systematic review and meta-analysis; under the vault standard this counts as evidence only because it names the underlying trials and studies (its core is the 2025 Cochrane reviews by Bergman et al. [RCTs] and Henschke et al. [observational], updated with a September 2024–January 2026 search and US-vaccine eligibility limits)
- Producer: Center for Infectious Disease Research and Policy (CIDRAP), University of Minnesota; Rockefeller Foundation–funded with an Alumbra Foundation dissemination grant; protocol registered PROSPERO CRD420261300907 (Feb 2026); 104-page final report held in `raw/`
- Source basis in hand: full PDF text

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Systematic review and random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) meta-analysis; 121 newly identified studies (14 RCTs, 49 cohorts, 19 ecological, 18 cross-sectional, and others) plus a 153-study US-vaccine subset of the two 2025 Cochrane reviews — 274 studies total; 136 risk-of-bias assessments |
| Subject / sample | Humans vaccinated with US-licensed HPV vaccines (4vHPV, 2vHPV, 9vHPV) or comparators; adolescents and adults by dose count and age at initiation |
| Intervention or exposure | Prophylactic HPV vaccination, analyzed by dose count (one vs two vs three doses) and age at initiation |
| Comparator / control | Unvaccinated groups, non-HPV control vaccines (e.g., hepatitis A or other control antigens in RCTs), or other dose counts |
| Endpoint | Safety: serious adverse events, POTS, CFS/ME, CRPS, Guillain-Barré syndrome, premature ovarian failure, infertility, adverse pregnancy outcomes. Effectiveness: HPV-related cancers (cervical, oropharyngeal, and others), CIN3+/CIN2+, incident and persistent HPV16/18 infection. Immunogenicity: seroconversion and antibody titers |
| Result | Cervical cancer: pooled RR 0.34 (95% CI 0.23–0.51) for >5-year follow-up cohorts; RR 0.20 (0.10–0.39) when initiated at or before age 16. CIN3+: RR 0.37 (0.30–0.46); RR 0.27 (0.18–0.42) at initiation ≤16. CIN2+ in RCTs aged 15–25: RR 0.39 (0.29–0.53) for vaccine-matched HPV types; no reduction at age ≥25 (RR 1.03, 0.83–1.28). Safety: no elevated SAE risk (consistent with Cochrane moderate certainty); POTS mixed — one self-controlled case series found autonomic-dysfunction aIRR 1.23 (1.08–1.41), concentrated in ages 18–26 (aIRR 1.40, 1.12–1.75) and absent at 9–17 (1.14, 0.97–1.35), against Cochrane's moderate-certainty finding of no increased POTS risk; a VAERS-based analysis (critical RoB) reported elevated POTS reporting odds. One vs two doses: two doses may prevent an additional 4.7% of residual CIN3+ not prevented by one dose, with overlapping confidence intervals; one-dose antibody levels generally sat below two- and three-dose levels, with ~99–100% seropositivity persisting in the cited long-term cohort |
| Main limitation | Over half of new observational studies had severe-to-critical risk of bias; the VAERS analyses were rated critical RoB; the updated-search portion was not GRADE-rated; findings are limited to US-approved vaccine products; effectiveness outcomes rest mostly on observational designs because the RCTs used premalignant and infection endpoints rather than cancer; the report is a foundation-funded policy-context document as well as a review |

## What the review can answer
- What the randomized trial evidence shows for pre-cancer and infection endpoints (CIN2+ reduction for vaccine-matched types in the vaccinated age bands tested, with no reduction in the ≥25 age band).
- What the pooled observational evidence shows for cancer outcomes (protective associations with wide confounding caveats), including the age-at-initiation gradient.
- Where safety signals stand: SAEs, GBS, CRPS, CFS/ME, pregnancy outcomes show no elevated risk at Cochrane certainty levels; POTS is the one mixed outcome, with the largest study null in adolescents and the Cochrane conclusion of no increase.
- What the one-dose question currently looks like: immunobridging and cohort data support substantial protection from one dose, but antibody levels run below multi-dose levels and the dose-comparison CIs overlap.

## What it cannot answer
- Individual-level causation for rare harms — surveillance and self-controlled designs have confounding limits the report itself flags.
- Lifelong single-dose protection; the follow-up is bounded by the cited cohorts.
- Anything about HPV-negative cervical cancers or non-vaccine HPV types' natural history; the review is an intervention review, not a causation ladder.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Two directions. First, treating the observational cancer-effect estimates (RR 0.34) as trial-grade causation when over half the observational set was severe-to-critical RoB. Second, the mirror-image leap of dismissing the RCT-anchored CIN2+ findings because the review as a whole contains weaker components. Under the vault standard the rungs stay separate: RCT CIN2+/infection endpoints are the strongest rung; pooled observational cancer outcomes are an association rung; the review's synthesis does not merge them.

## Vault conclusion
This source starts the intervention rung of [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]]. It supports: RCT-level reduction of vaccine-matched-type CIN2+ in ages 15–25 (RR 0.39), pooled observational cervical-cancer and CIN3+ reductions with a strong age-at-initiation gradient, Cochrane-certainty safety findings without elevated SAE/GBS/CRPS/CFS-ME risk, one mixed POTS outcome, and an unresolved one-versus-two-dose margin. It does not by itself close necessity, sufficiency, or cofactor questions in the causation ladder above.
