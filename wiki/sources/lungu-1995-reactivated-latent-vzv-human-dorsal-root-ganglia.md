# Reactivated and Latent Varicella-Zoster Virus in Human Dorsal Root Ganglia

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary human autopsy ganglia paper for VZV latent/reactivated states
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 1995
- Authors: O. Lungu and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Human autopsy ganglia study using in situ hybridization and immunohistochemistry |
| Subject / sample | Zoster/reactivation cases and controls |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural latent or reactivated VZV context |
| Comparator / control | VZV-positive ganglia versus seronegative/fetal controls |
| Endpoint | VZV nucleic acid and protein in ganglia |
| Result | VZV signal was detected in neuronal and satellite cells in positive ganglia, while controls were negative under the reported assays. |
| Main limitation | Few cases and autopsy timing; detection does not identify the trigger of reactivation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that VZV material is detectable in human dorsal-root ganglia during latent/reactivated states.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot explain why a particular person develops shingles or define population recurrence risk.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating ganglionic detection as a complete mechanism of reactivation.

## Vault conclusion
Lungu et al. 1995 supports a human ganglionic VZV latency/reactivation rung, not a full trigger mechanism.
