# HPV detection in paraffin-embedded cervical carcinomas and metastases by PCR (Claas et al. 1989)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early improved-assay boundary paper — PCR recovering HPV where DISH (DNA in situ hybridization) had failed, while preserving residual negatives
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Pathology*, 1989
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC1880022)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Laboratory detection study on archived paraffin-embedded tissue |
| Subject / sample | Five HPV16/18/31/33 DISH-negative cervical carcinomas with available metastatic tissue |
| Intervention or exposure | PCR testing for HPV16, 18, 31, 33 on primary tumors and metastases; fixation-method comparison for PCR specificity |
| Comparator / control | Prior DISH-negative status of the same tumors; tumor-metastasis concordance as an internal consistency check |
| Endpoint | HPV type detection in primary tumors and matched metastases |
| Result | HPV16 was detected in the primary squamous carcinomas and their lymph-node metastases of two patients; HPV18 was found in one adenocarcinoma and its lymph node. In the remaining two patients — both squamous carcinomas, one with a bladder metastasis, one with lymph-node and lung metastases — no HPV16, 18, or 33 DNA was detected. HPV positivity in primaries correlated with positivity in metastases. PCR specificity was unaffected by fixation method. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 5; only four HPV types tested, so negatives are type-bounded; no consensus-sequence PCR visible in the abstract. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That PCR detected HPV in three of five DISH-negative cervical carcinomas — an early direct demonstration that method sensitivity, not viral absence, explains many negatives.
- That HPV status concorded between primary tumors and their metastases in this small set.

## What full text would still need to establish
- PCR conditions and validation; broader-type testing of the two negatives; clinical follow-up.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The two PCR-negative tumors are negative only for four tested types with 1989 technology; citing them as HPV-independent cervical cancer would repeat the detection-boundary error this very paper demonstrates.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: in five DISH-negative cervical carcinomas, PCR recovered HPV (16 or 18) in three — with matched-metastase concordance — and two squamous carcinomas remained negative for the tested types.
