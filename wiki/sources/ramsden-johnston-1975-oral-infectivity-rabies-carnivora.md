# Studies on the oral infectivity of rabies virus in Carnivora (Ramsden and Johnston 1975)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: oral-route comparator for the rabies row — not a clean failed-attempt entry; oral infection DID occur, which disciplines overreading the oral-route negatives
- Journal/year: *Journal of Wildlife Diseases*, 1975
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental feeding study |
| Subject / sample | Mature and immature red foxes (*Vulpes vulpes*) and striped skunks (*Mephitis mephitis*) |
| Intervention or exposure | Feeding varying numbers of white mice infected with street isolates and a fixed rabies-virus strain |
| Comparator / control | Between species (fox vs skunk), age classes, inoculum (street vs fixed strain), and dose (mouse numbers); no sham-fed control stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | Rabies deaths; serum neutralizing antibody development |
| Result | Rabies deaths and serum neutralizing antibody occurred in both species after feeding infected mice. The abstract does not give per-group numbers. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; per-group counts, dose-response, and strain differences are not visible in the abstract; no sham control stated. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That under these feeding conditions (whole infected mouse carcasses, both street and fixed strains), oral infection producing death and seroconversion occurred in both foxes and skunks.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Infection rates by species, age, strain, and dose — the boundary conditions that would reconcile this positive result with the negative or mixed results of other oral-route studies.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This comparator exists to prevent two leaps: citing oral-route failures ([[sources/bell-moore-1971-rabies-oral-carnivora]], [[sources/shirakawa-2007-cats-fed-rabid-mouse-brains]]) as "rabies is not orally infectious," or citing this paper as "oral rabies is easy" when dose, strain, and species conditions clearly mattered across the literature.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: feeding rabies-infected mice produced rabies deaths and neutralizing antibody in both red foxes and striped skunks — oral infectivity is established for these conditions, making route, dose, strain, and species the operative variables in the oral-rabies literature.
