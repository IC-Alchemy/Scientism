## Citation reconciliation (queue item 1)
- The queue entry read "Couch et al. 1966, JAMA - experimental influenza, egg-grown inoculum vs placebo". No such paper exists. PubMed lists nine Couch publications dated 1966 and none is in JAMA; Crossref holds no 1966 JAMA article on induced influenza (the only 1966 JAMA influenza-title items are a news piece, "Purified RNA in influenza isolated", and editorials). This is the same name/journal-error pattern recorded for items 8 ("Cutland"→Cutts) and 10 ("Garland"→FUTURE II Study Group).
- Couch's real 1966 volunteer paper (Couch, Cate, Douglas, Gerone, Knight, "Effect of route of inoculation on experimental respiratory viral disease in volunteers and evidence for airborne transmission", *Bacteriological Reviews* 1966;30(3):517-529, PMC378233) was retrieved in full this run and is NOT the queue item: it uses coxsackievirus A21, rhinovirus NIH 1734, and adenovirus type 4 (not influenza), tissue-culture-passaged inocula (not egg-grown), and contains no placebo/sham arm (its implicit background is the authors' observation that about 15% of uninfected inoculated men develop mild rhinitis).
- The nearest Couch-authored controlled human influenza challenge with reachable full text is this 1969 Bulletin paper; it is verified and filed here as the item-1 anchor.
- Reference-list companion verified in run 9: the "Couch, R. B., Douglas, R. G., Jr, Kasel, J. A., Riggs, S., & Knight, V. (1969) Nature (Lond.) (in press)" item - the cited source of the A2/Hong Kong/68 human inoculation procedures - is [[sources/couch-1969-nature-equine-influenza-syndrome-man]], now held and read in full text. It is the clinical report of the same 15-man A/equi-2 trial (13/15 ill, 15/15 shedding, 14/15 seroconversion), and adds the March 1965 10^5.3-TCID50 no-illness pilot.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Controlled experimental infection (human challenge) of adult volunteers, plus reciprocal challenge of Chincoteague ponies; two-part design: primary inoculation with A/Equi-2/Miami/63, then simultaneous heterologous challenge with A2/Hong Kong/68 of the equine-primed volunteers and an unimmunized control group |
| Subject / sample | Human: adult male volunteers aged 21-35 years with no detectable serum neutralizing antibody (<1:2) to A/Equi-2/Miami/63 and low or no titre to A2/Hong Kong/68; "Medical examinations to ensure good health were performed and written informed consent was obtained prior to viral inoculations" - 15 subjects in the equine-inoculation arm, 19 serum-antibody-negative controls in the challenge comparison. Horses: 15 ponies (8 months old, antibody-free) |
| Intervention or exposure | Intranasal inoculation: 10^8.75 TCID50 of A/Equi-2/Miami/63 by spray into the nasopharynx and/or nasal instillation. Inoculum passage history recorded in the paper: egg5, HK2, man1, HK4, man1, HEK3 for the human pool - i.e. egg passage followed by hamster-kidney and human-embryonic-kidney tissue culture; it is not a purely egg-grown inoculum, which is itself a correction to the queue item's description. Challenge: 10^3.2 TCID50 of A2/Hong Kong/68 (pool 2, second HEK passage) intranasally |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no** - the primary equine-virus inoculation had no placebo/sham-inoculation group; the paper contains no sham-inoculation quote because none exists in it. Comparator: **yes** for the second challenge - "all 15 of the infected volunteers and 19 serum-antibody-negative volunteers were simultaneously challenged intranasally with 10^3.2 TCID50 of A2/Hong Kong/68 virus"; Table 3 lists this arm as "Controls". Pony arms likewise compared against 5 antibody-negative control ponies and against A2/Rockville/65 inoculated ponies |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: (1) "illness responses" classified as febrile upper respiratory and systemic illness, febrile upper and lower respiratory and systemic illness, afebrile upper respiratory illness, or none (Table 1); (2) virus shedding measured by isolation of virus from human subjects by day after inoculation and by the number of virus-positive isolates (Tables 2, 3, 5); (3) "4-fold or greater serum neutralizing antibody response" (Tables 3, 4, 6). Infection in the antibody-tier analysis is defined as "illness, virus shedding and/or rise in serum antibody titre" |
| Result | Primary inoculation: 13 of 15 equine-virus volunteers developed illness on day 2-3 (8 febrile upper-respiratory-plus-systemic, 2 febrile with lower-respiratory involvement including paroxysmal cough and substernal discomfort, 3 afebrile upper respiratory); virus shedding occurred in 15 of 15 (from day 1 in 5, day 2-4 in all others, mostly limited to 6 days); 14 of 15 had a >=4-fold antibody rise; 9 of 14 developed heterologous antibody to A2/Hong Kong/68. Challenge phase: illness 1/15 (equine-primed) vs 9/19 (controls), P<0.06; virus-isolate distribution clearly significant between groups, P<0.05 (in the primed group 10 of 15 shed no virus at all; in controls 8 of 19 shed none); >=4-fold rise 4/15 vs 14/19. Protection tracked heterologous antibody tier: infection in 4/6 with no detectable antibody, 2/5 with low titres (4-32), 0/4 with high titres (256-4096), P<0.05 (Yates mean score test). Ponies: A2/Hong Kong/68 caused fever in 6/10 and shedding in 9/10; A2/Rockville/65 caused nothing in 5/5; reciprocal equine challenge showed reduced shedding only in the pool-1 group (P<0.02) |
| Main limitation | No sham-inoculation arm for the primary inoculation, so background non-specific illness rates in inoculated antibody-negative men are not controlled within this paper (the authors' related 1966 work puts it near 15%); the primary dose, 10^8.75 TCID50, is very large relative to the 10^3.2 challenge dose, so illness rates at lower physiological doses are not established; the illness difference at challenge reached only P<0.06 with 19 controls; "indistinguishable from naturally occurring human influenza" is a clinical-judgment comparison against textbook descriptions (Stuart-Harris), not a head-to-head comparison with natural cases; subjects were institutionalized adult male volunteers, limiting generalizability; the equine strain is not a human epidemic strain, so the primary-inoculation illness evidence is cross-species |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 1, GAP 1 (sham-controlled human challenge with purified inoculum), after reconciliation: this is the 1960s Houston-lineage controlled challenge with named clinical, shedding, and serologic endpoints and a concurrent unimmunized control group on the second challenge. It does NOT itself supply a sham-inoculation contrast; GAP 1's sham-controlled requirement is met for influenza by [[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]] and for RSV by [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]].
- Ladder rung 7 (controlled human challenge, inoculum route) in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]] gains its earliest full-text row: purified wild-type-lineage virus (egg and tissue-culture passages) delivered intranasally produced febrile systemic influenza-like illness in 13/15 antibody-negative adults with shedding in 15/15 - with the dose and control caveats above.
- Cross-cutting note added to [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] rung-7 influenza entry.

## What the experiment can answer
- That a characterized A/Equi-2 inoculum, given intranasally at high dose to seronegative adults, reproducibly produced febrile illness classified against natural-influenza clinical descriptions together with day-1-onset shedding and seroconversion - i.e. virus inoculation and illness were coupled in the same subjects.
- That the illness and shedding response to a second, lower-dose heterologous challenge was markedly reduced in equine-primed subjects compared with 19 simultaneous seronegative controls, and that the reduction tracked the tier of heterologous serum antibody.
- That A2/Hong Kong/68 infected ponies (fever 6/10, shedding 9/10) while the antigenically distant A2/Rockville/65 produced no response - a strain-specific infectivity contrast in the animal arm.

## What it cannot answer
- Natural-route transmission: no recipient volunteers were exposed to infected subjects; exposure was investigator inoculation in isolation.
- Illness rates at physiological inoculum doses or via ordinary exposure; the 10^8.75 TCID50 dose has no lower-dose clinical contrast in this paper.
- Whether the challenge illness is identical to ordinary human influenza; the "indistinguishable" judgment is clinical-textbook comparison, not a natural-case control arm.
- Sham/placebo effects of nasal instrumentation: no group received inert inoculum.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- "A/Equi-2 influenza virus is capable of infecting man and producing an illness which is indistinguishable from naturally occurring human influenza" - the experiment supports infection plus a febrile respiratory-systemic syndrome resembling textbook descriptions; "indistinguishable" overstates the design, which had no natural-illness comparison group. The authors themselves list the clinical differences they observed (shorter fever, more nasal discharge, slightly less cough and sore throat).
- "Surveillance data based on serological surveys alone may not be a reliable indicator of interspecies spread" - extrapolates a 15-subject experiment to surveillance policy.
- "An exchange of viruses between these hosts may on occasion occur in nature" - inference from antigenic relatedness plus the pony experiments; no natural interspecies transfer was observed.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the controlled human-challenge rung as a historical anchor: Kasel and Couch report, with named endpoints and a concurrent control group on the heterologous challenge, that characterized influenza A inoculum produced febrile influenza-like illness and universal shedding in seronegative adult volunteers, and that pre-existing heterologous antibody tier predicted resistance to challenge. The paper has no sham-inoculation arm, uses a very large primary dose, and its "indistinguishable from natural influenza" claim is a textbook comparison - all recorded above rather than flattened.
