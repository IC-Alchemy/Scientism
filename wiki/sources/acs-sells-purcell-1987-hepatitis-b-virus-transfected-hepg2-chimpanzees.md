# Hepatitis B Virus Produced by Transfected Hep G2 Cells Causes Hepatitis in Chimpanzees

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular-clone/cell-culture infectivity challenge paper for hepatitis B virus
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, 1987
- Authors: G. Acs, M. A. Sells, R. H. Purcell, P. Price, R. Engle, M. Shapiro, and H. Popper
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF rendered through Europe PMC/PMC, checked against DOI, PubMed, and PMCID metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental chimpanzee inoculation with culture medium from HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2-derived cells, followed by serology, serum enzyme monitoring, liver biopsy histology, viral subtype comparison, and restriction-enzyme analysis of recovered viral DNA |
| Subject / sample | Two captive-born chimpanzees weighing 21-22 kg; both lacked demonstrable HBV markers at inoculation. Chimpanzee 1178 had previously been experimentally infected with hepatitis A virus, and neither had been inoculated with blood-borne non-A/non-B hepatitis virus. |
| Exposure or intervention | Intravenous inoculation with 10 ml of medium from the HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2 clone 2.2.15 after removal of cell debris. The inocula were estimated by Southern blot to contain about 9.0 x 10^8 particles for chimpanzee 1303 and 3.6 x 10^8 particles for chimpanzee 1178. |
| Comparator / control | No sham-inoculated chimpanzee control is reported. Within-animal baseline serum and liver biopsy findings were used before inoculation; viral-subtype and restriction-pattern comparisons were made against input virus/cloned DNA. |
| Endpoint | Appearance and time course of HBsAg, HBeAg, anti-HBs, and anti-HBc; serum alanine aminotransferase and related enzyme activity; serial liver-biopsy histology; recovery and restriction analysis of viral DNA from chimpanzee plasma. |
| Result | Both chimpanzees developed HBV antigenemia and hepatitis markers after inoculation. HBsAg appeared at week 1 in chimpanzee 1303 and week 2 in chimpanzee 1178; HBeAg was detected by week 3 and peaked at week 5 in both. Anti-HBc appeared at week 7. ALT rose at week 5 in chimpanzee 1303 and week 7 in chimpanzee 1178. Liver biopsies progressed from normal baseline to hepatitis, earlier in chimpanzee 1303 and later but more severe in chimpanzee 1178. Viral subtype and restriction patterns from chimpanzee serum matched the cell-produced/input viral DNA pattern. |
| Main limitation | The experiment used two chimpanzees, high-dose intravenous inoculation, and no sham-inoculated animal control. It tests infectivity of cell-produced HBV virions in the chimpanzee model, not ordinary human acquisition, population transmission, clinical chronicity, or all hepatitis syndromes. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that medium from HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2-derived cells contained particles that produced HBV infection markers and hepatitis endpoints in two chimpanzees after intravenous inoculation.
- It can answer that the recovered chimpanzee viral DNA matched the cloned/cell-produced HBV pattern by the restriction-enzyme tests used.
- It can answer that cell-produced HBV virions, rather than merely noninfectious antigen or naked DNA, were experimentally infectious in this chimpanzee setup.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human natural-route acquisition or transmission.
- It cannot prove that every clinical hepatitis B case follows the same time course or severity pattern.
- It cannot answer hepatitis A, hepatitis C, non-A/non-B hepatitis, or other hepatitis syndromes.
- It cannot by itself reconstruct the earlier historical ladder from Australia antigen detection to chimpanzee passage.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This paper is a strong high-rung experiment for HBV infectivity because it links cloned HBV DNA, cell-produced virions, experimental chimpanzee infection, hepatitis markers, histology, and recovered viral DNA. The leap begins if that high-rung molecular challenge is treated as a substitute for all lower and neighboring questions: natural human route, dose, transmission ecology, chronic infection, vaccine effect, or the historical discovery sequence.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in Acs et al. 1987, virions produced by HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2-derived cells produced HBV infection markers and hepatitis endpoints in two intravenously inoculated chimpanzees, with recovered viral DNA matching the input cloned/cell-produced pattern by the restriction analysis used.
