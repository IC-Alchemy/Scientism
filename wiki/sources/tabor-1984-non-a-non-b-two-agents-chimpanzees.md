# More than one agent of human non-A, non-B hepatitis (Tabor et al. 1984)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: pre-HCV chimpanzee transmission/passaging rung for [[synthesis/hepatitis-c-hepatitis-c-virus-evidence-ladder]] — one of the named acquisition gaps, now at abstract level
- Journal/year: *Transfusion*, 1984
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Sequential inoculation, passage, and cross-challenge study in chimpanzees |
| Subject / sample | Chimpanzees; two human-derived inocula (Inoculum I: serum from a chronically infected human; Inoculum IV: fibrinogen from pooled plasma), each of which had transmitted non-A, non-B hepatitis to humans |
| Intervention or exposure | Sequential inoculation with Inoculum I then Inoculum IV; passage of acute-phase serum from either infection into additional chimpanzees; Inoculum IV transmission into animals recovered from Inoculum I |
| Comparator / control | Cross-exposure comparisons between the two inocula (including Inoculum IV into animals with and without prior Inoculum I exposure) |
| Endpoint | Non-A, non-B hepatitis transmission; hepatocyte cytoplasmic tubules by EM (with band periodicity characterization) |
| Result | Both inocula transmitted non-A, non-B hepatitis to chimpanzees, and acute-phase serum from chimpanzee infections with either agent passaged the disease onward. Cytoplasmic tubules in hepatocytes appeared in all Inoculum-I infections but were absent in Inoculum-IV infections except in one animal receiving Inoculum IV without prior Inoculum I exposure. The tubules showed transverse bands with ~17-nm periodicity. The authors concluded two different agents or distinct serotypes were likely present, while noting reactivation or reinfection could not be fully excluded. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; animal numbers not stated; agent identity unknown at the time (pre-HCV); the authors' own caveat about reactivation/reinfection. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That at least two serologically or biologically distinct transmissible non-A, non-B hepatitis agents circulated in human blood products in this record — the multiplicity finding that preceded HCV's isolation.
- That chimpanzee passage retained infectivity for both agents, with a hepatocyte ultrastructural marker tracking one but not the other.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Animal numbers and hepatitis grading; inoculum histories; how cross-challenge timing rules out reactivation.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The ladder's boundary: this pre-HCV record shows transmissible agents existed in blood, not that the later-cloned HCV is one of them — identity is established by the 1989 molecular work downstream in [[synthesis/hepatitis-c-hepatitis-c-virus-evidence-ladder]], not by this paper.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: two human-derived inocula with distinct hepatocyte-ultrastructural signatures each transmitted non-A, non-B hepatitis to chimpanzees and passaged onward — pre-HCV evidence of multiple transmissible agents in blood products.
