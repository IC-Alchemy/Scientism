# Cervical HPV infection and neoplasia: the Manchester cohort (Peto et al. 2004)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: prospective progression/insufficiency boundary paper — the strongest prospective evidence in the boundary set that HPV infection is not sufficient for neoplasia in most infected women
- Journal/year: *British Journal of Cancer*, 2004
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC2409880)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Population-based prospective cohort with linked cytology/histology records and HPV assay of stored samples |
| Subject / sample | 49,655 women attending routine cervical cytology clinics in or near Manchester, 1988-1993; HPV assayed in an age- and period-stratified random sample of 7,278 plus prevalent and incident CIN3 cases |
| Intervention or exposure | None (observational exposure: oncogenic HPV status at entry) |
| Comparator / control | HPV-negative versus HPV-positive women within the cohort; follow-up through laboratory records |
| Endpoint | Subsequent CIN3/CIN2 diagnosis; cumulative CIN3 risk by HPV status; HPV prevalence by age; time since last normal smear |
| Result | Oncogenic HPV at entry strongly predicted subsequent CIN3 (OR 17.2, 95% CI 10.4–28.4) but was only weakly related to CIN2 (OR 2.3, 0.5–10.7) and lesser abnormality (OR 1.4). Oncogenic HPV prevalence fell from 19% (under 25) to under 3% (40+). Among cytologically normal women with oncogenic HPV at entry, cumulative CIN3 risk was 28% (95% CI 18–43) after 14 years — meaning roughly seven in ten did not develop CIN3 in that window. Lifetime CIN3 risk in this population was estimated at 9%. CIN3 prevalence rose with screening interval then plateaued beyond 5 years, suggesting eventual cytologic regression of some CIN3. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; HPV assayed only in a subsample; CIN3 is a premalignant cytologic/histologic endpoint, not invasive cancer; regression estimates rest on screening-interval modeling; no genotyping-stratified progression rates in the abstract. |

## What the abstract can answer
- Whether oncogenic HPV predicts CIN3 prospectively: strongly (OR 17.2) — a prospective-timing rung the molecular papers in [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]] lack.
- Whether infection is sufficient for progression: no — most HPV-positive, cytologically normal women had not developed CIN3 after 14 years (28% cumulative risk).

## What full text would still need to establish
- Type-specific progression rates; how clearance and persistence were timed; invasive-cancer events in the cohort.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Two leaps: (1) "HPV is necessary" quoted from necessity studies does not mean infection is sufficient — this cohort directly shows most infections clear or do not progress on the observed timescale; (2) conversely, the 72% non-progression fraction is bounded by 14-year follow-up, competing risk, and regression of detectable CIN3 — it cannot be read as "HPV rarely matters."

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: the Manchester cohort provides the vault's clearest prospective pair of findings — oncogenic HPV strongly predicts CIN3 (OR 17.2) and most infected women (about 72% in 14 years) do not develop CIN3 — placing necessity and sufficiency as separate, separately-bounded rungs.
