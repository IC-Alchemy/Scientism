# The Susceptibility of Swine to the Virus of Human Influenza

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary cross-species animal-susceptibility and contact-boundary paper
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1936
- Authors: Richard E. Shope, Thomas Francis Jr.
- Source basis in hand: Europe PMC full-text OCR/JATS record for PMCID `PMC2133454`

> Warning: The Europe PMC OCR contains minor character noise. The extraction below uses fields that are clear in the article text and abstract.

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Intranasal animal inoculation, serial passage, bacterial co-exposure, contact-exposure, mouse-inoculation recovery, and neutralization testing |
| Subject / sample | Swine aged 6 to 14 weeks; PR8 and WS human influenza-virus strains; some experiments added *H. influenzae suis* |
| Exposure or intervention | Intranasal instillation of human influenza-virus suspension; later serial swine-lung passages; separate mixtures of human influenza virus with *H. influenzae suis*; pen contact between inoculated and normal swine |
| Comparator / control | Comparison of virus alone versus virus plus *H. influenzae suis*; comparison with prior swine-influenza virus behavior; contact-exposed swine observed without direct inoculation; immunologic identity checked with anti-human and anti-swine sera |
| Endpoint | Clinical illness, fever, lung pathology, mouse infectivity from swine lung lesions, contact-transfer evidence, and virus-neutralizing antibodies |
| Result | Intranasal human influenza virus produced a mild, usually afebrile swine illness with lung lesions and recoverable virus by mouse inoculation. Serial swine passage did not noticeably enhance pathogenicity. Virus plus *H. influenzae suis* produced more severe illness in 11 of 16 swine. Contact transfer occurred in only one of four exposed swine and was clinically inapparent, detected by neutralizing antibody. Recovered fifth-passage virus remained immunologically human-type. |
| Main limitation | Animal model; artificial intranasal inoculation; small contact-exposure set; not a controlled human transmission study and not direct proof of ordinary human causation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that the tested human influenza-virus strains could infect young swine after intranasal inoculation.
- It can answer that lung lesions in experimentally infected swine contained recoverable virus by mouse inoculation.
- It can answer that adding *H. influenzae suis* often increased disease severity in swine, while some animals still showed virus-only disease.
- It can answer that contact transmission among swine was weak in this experiment: one of four exposed animals showed serologic evidence without clinical illness.
- It can answer that the recovered virus after serial swine passage remained immunologically aligned with human influenza virus rather than swine influenza virus.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot establish ordinary human-to-human influenza transmission.
- It cannot show that swine findings generalize directly to humans.
- It cannot prove that human influenza virus alone explains all clinical severity, since the paper itself tests bacterial co-exposure and reports stronger disease under that condition.
- It cannot erase the earlier Rosenau negative human challenge boundary.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The main risk is converting a cross-species susceptibility experiment into a complete human-causation proof. The paper is valuable precisely because it preserves both positive and boundary findings: intranasal swine susceptibility, recoverable virus, stronger disease with bacterial co-exposure, and limited contact transfer.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow animal-model claim: human influenza-virus strains PR8 and WS infected young swine under intranasal experimental conditions, produced mild respiratory disease and recoverable virus, and showed limited contact transfer. It does not by itself prove ordinary human influenza causation or transmission.
