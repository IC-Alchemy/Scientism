# Ristori et al. 1964 - A controlled trial of live-virus vaccine against measles in Chile

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-10 (loop run 18, queue item 32) from the complete 6-page scanned PDF of Bull World Health Organ 1964;30:763-768 (PMC2555087), downloaded from the PMC PDF endpoint after solving the proof-of-work cookie (SHA-256(challenge+nonce) with 4 leading zeros -> cloudpmc-viewer-pow). Every number below is from that scan, not from any summary. No DOI exists for this article. Authors: Conrado Ristori, Horacio Boccardo, Mario Miranda, Manuel Borgoño (National Health Service, Santiago, Chile); vaccine supplied by M. R. Hilleman, Merck Institute for Therapeutic Research; planned in collaboration with WHO following its November 1961 expert group.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized field trial, Santiago, Chile, July-December 1962, among children in institutions and their own homes; part of simultaneous WHO-sponsored Edmonston B trials in several countries.
- **Subjects:** 530 children aged 8 months to 3 years without a clear history of measles; children with malnutrition or from poor homes deliberately included. Group sizes: I = 109, II = 110, III = 110, IV = 108, V (control) = 93. Comparability checked: sex 48.4-55.6% male per group; age bands ~26% 8-12 months, ~49% 1-2 years, ~25% 2-3 years in every group; nutritional state satisfactory in ~92% of all children (43 undernourished, "too small a group to allow of valid conclusions").
- **Intervention:** live lyophilized Enders Edmonston B vaccine, 2500 TCID50 in 0.25 ml subcutaneously into the left arm. Group I: vaccine alone. Group II: vaccine + 0.01 ml gamma-globulin per pound body-weight (right arm, immediately after vaccine). Group III: vaccine + 0.005 ml/lb. Group IV: vaccine + 0.0025 ml/lb.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES. Quote: "Group V (the control group), a placebo injection of sterile tissue-culture medium" - 0.25 ml into the left arm, same volume as vaccine. Blinding quote: "In order to ensure unbiased observation and recording, the observers were not told which group any child had been assigned"; the abstract calls the trial "double-blind". The placebo is inert (tissue-culture medium, no antigen).
- **Follow-up:** children observed daily for at least 21 days, in most cases about 30 days.

## Endpoints (named exactly)

1. **Designed endpoint 1 - reactions:** maximum recorded rectal temperature; occurrence, onset, and duration of rash; coryza, cough, pharyngitis, enanthema, Koplik's spots, conjunctivitis, diarrhoea, convulsions; daily observation for >=21 days.
2. **Designed endpoint 2 - seroconversion (surrogate):** complement-fixation tests on pre-vaccination serum and serum drawn at the end of the observation period (21-32 days later), performed in two laboratories (Chile and South Africa); the reported results are those of the Chilean laboratory.
3. **Disease observation inside the blinded window:** clinically typical measles diagnosed during the 21-30-day observation phase, recorded per group.
4. **Unplanned disease-endpoint follow-up (~6 months):** 100 children who had received vaccine only (Group I) and 85 who had had placebo (Group V) were revisited to determine whether they had had measles in the interval, counting only cases occurring more than two weeks after inoculation. The paper states this "additional investigation was not planned in the original programme but was prompted by the fact that a measles epidemic occurred in Santiago."

## Results

- **Fever:** 25.7% of Group I exceeded 39°C; gamma-globulin did not appreciably reduce the frequency or intensity of reactions (20.0%, 14.5%, 24.1% >39°C in Groups II-IV). One child reached 40°C with short convulsions (Group II, the maximum gamma-globulin dose). Control-group fevers (7.5% >39°C) were attributed to intercurrent diseases. Average fever duration 3.5-5.1 days in vaccine groups, starting on or after day 5.
- **Rash:** Group I - 39.4% no rash, ~23% scattered spots, ~37% diffuse but mild rash; gamma-globulin groups similar. Group V - 91.4% no rash; the 3 children with generalized rash were the two measles cases and one scarlet-fever case.
- **Other symptoms:** enanthema, Koplik's spots, conjunctivitis, and diarrhoea were considerably more frequent in the four vaccine groups than among controls; Koplik's spots in 7 children (3 in Group I). Seven vaccine-group children had enanthema-plus profiles consistent with the vaccine reaction pattern; the paper reports these as reactions, not disease.
- **Clinical measles in the blinded window:** "Two cases of typical measles were seen in this group [Group V, placebo] a few days after the beginning of the follow-up; one case of scarlet fever was also observed among the controls." No measles cases were seen in any of the four vaccinated groups during the same window.
- **Seroconversion:** good serological conversion in most children of Groups I-IV; the abstract states ~90% of the vaccinated converted; "no conversions took place in the control group."
- **Six-month disease follow-up:** "The results clearly demonstrate the efficacy of the vaccine: no cases at all occurred in the vaccine group, whereas 14 cases of measles, with one death, had occurred among the unvaccinated controls" - 0/100 (vaccine-only) vs 14/85 (placebo), one death among controls, during a Santiago measles epidemic, cases counted only if >2 weeks after inoculation.
- **Campaign data (not part of the trial):** after the trial, Chile began mass vaccination in May 1963 (50,000 Merck-provided doses); >15,000 children vaccinated in different cities with "only one accident attributable to the vaccine - a mild case of encephalitis which occurred 10 days after vaccination", still under investigation at the time of writing, patient fully recovered.

## Main limitations

- The designed efficacy endpoint was serologic (complement-fixation conversion) - a surrogate, not disease.
- The clinical-disease evidence is incidental and unplanned: the blinded window caught only 2 placebo measles cases, and the six-month 0-vs-14 comparison used a subsample (100/85 of 530), clinical diagnosis without laboratory confirmation, and was added mid-study when the epidemic struck.
- Reaction burden was substantial (25.7% of vaccine-alone children >39°C; one febrile convulsion), which the authors weigh explicitly against measles mortality in Chile.
- No all-cause or non-measles health outcome was collected; the placebo arm was smaller than each vaccine arm.

## Inferential leaps made by the authors (recorded, not endorsed)

- "The results clearly demonstrate the efficacy of the vaccine" is asserted from the unplanned subsample follow-up; with no laboratory confirmation and clinical diagnosis, the sentence outruns the designed endpoints (reactions + serology).
- Extrapolation from a 530-child trial to a national mass-vaccination campaign, including the recommendation that "vaccination may usefully be carried out in Chile among all children from eight months to three years", and the claim that only one accident in >15,000 campaign vaccinations was "attributable" to vaccine while that case was still under investigation.
- The suggestion to consider killed-virus priming followed by live virus, offered without trial data in this paper.

## What this experiment can and cannot answer

- **Can answer:** whether 2500 TCID50 subcutaneous Edmonston B produces complement-fixing antibody in ~90% of previously uninfected young children within a month and none in a contemporaneous inert-placebo arm; what its reaction profile is at daily observation; and - with the boundaries above - whether clinical measles occurred less often in vaccinated than placebo children during an epidemic season.
- **Cannot answer:** long-term protection beyond six months; efficacy against laboratory-confirmed measles (no case confirmation was performed); indirect protection in unvaccinated contacts (no such arm); all-cause morbidity or mortality; the causal status of the single campaign encephalitis case (unresolved in the paper itself).

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate)** - first measles row in the vault. The trial sits between the rungs: its designed endpoints are surrogate (reactions, serology), but it carries a verbatim inert sham arm (sterile tissue-culture medium), a blinded clinical observation window with measles only in the placebo arm, and a six-month epidemic follow-up with 0/100 vs 14/85 and one control death.
