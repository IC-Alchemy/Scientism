# Sadoff 2021 Ad26.COV2.S ENSEMBLE Covid-19 Vaccine RCT

## Source type
- Role in this vault: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled intervention trial with an adjudicated clinical disease endpoint — replication of the GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint) rung for SARS-CoV-2 in a second platform, filling queue item 22 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]]; extends rung 9 (intervention evidence) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] and the intervention boundary of [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]].
- Trial name: ENSEMBLE (COV3001), Janssen protocol; ongoing 2-year pivotal phase 3 trial, this report is the primary analysis at the FDA-specified median 8-week follow-up (data cut-off January 22, 2021).
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2021;384:2187-2201 (published April 21, 2021).
- Authors: Sadoff J, Gray G, Vandebosch A, Cárdenas V, Shukarev G, Grinsztejn B, et al.
- Funding/oversight (as stated): "Supported by Janssen Research and Development... and in whole or in part by federal funds from... the Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority... and from the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases"; unblinded independent DSMB, including monitoring for vaccine-associated enhanced respiratory disease.
- Source basis in hand: complete article full text from the NEJM author manuscript deposited in PMC (PMC8220996, Europe PMC fullTextXML), read end to end: Abstract, Methods (design, participants, procedures, safety, efficacy assessments, statistical analysis), Results, Discussion, figure/table captions, reference list.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Design quote (Methods): "this ongoing, 2-year, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3, pivotal trial in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, South Africa, and the United States"; 1:1 randomization by randomly permuted blocks, stratified by site, age group, and presence of coexisting conditions; primary analysis triggered at median 8-week follow-up |
| Subject / sample | 44,325 randomized; 43,783 received vaccine or placebo (full analysis set 21,895 vaccine / 21,888 placebo); per-protocol efficacy population 39,321 SARS-CoV-2-seronegative or unknown-serostatus participants (19,630 vaccine / 19,691 placebo); 9.6% baseline-seropositive excluded from per-protocol; adults 18+ including persons with stable coexisting conditions; median follow-up 58 days (range 1-124), 55% with ≥8 weeks; later/slower recruitment of participants ≥60 with coexisting conditions left that subgroup with shorter follow-up (authors' note) |
| Intervention or exposure | Single intramuscular 0.5-ml dose of Ad26.COV2.S at 5×10^10 viral particles: "a recombinant, replication-incompetent human adenovirus type 26 (Ad26) vector encoding a full-length, membrane-bound SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in a prefusion-stabilized conformation", administered by a health care worker unaware of assignment |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes**. Quote (Methods, Procedures): "Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio, with the use of randomly permuted blocks, to receive either Ad26.COV2.S or saline placebo." Saline = inert comparator, same placebo class as [[sources/polack-2020-bnt162b2-covid-rct]]; double-blind per the design quote above |
| Endpoint | Named exactly (Methods, Efficacy Assessments): "The two primary end points were the efficacy of the Ad26.COV2.S vaccine against the first occurrence of centrally confirmed moderate to severe–critical Covid-19 with an onset at least 14 days after administration and at least 28 days after administration in the per-protocol population." Potential severe-critical cases and moderate cases with ≥3 signs or symptoms were classified by an independent, blinded Clinical Severity Adjudication Committee ("a single low oxygen-saturation measurement was not classified as indicating severe Covid-19 unless other clinical findings were consistent"). Case finding: symptom-triggered — participants reported symptoms electronically (Symptoms of Infection with Coronavirus-19 questionnaire); nasal swabs tested by FDA-EUA RT-PCR locally and centrally confirmed (Abbott m-2000). Secondary endpoints: FDA-harmonized symptomatic Covid-19, severe-critical Covid-19, Covid-19 with medical intervention/hospitalization, death, and asymptomatic infection measured by SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid (N) immunoassay (Elecsys, Roche) at entry and days 29 and 71 — a non-spike antigen, so vaccination does not confound the infection readout |
| Result | Primary endpoint, onset ≥14 days (per-protocol): 116 vaccine vs 348 placebo, efficacy 66.9% (adjusted 95% CI 59.0-73.4); onset ≥28 days: 66 vs 193, 66.1% (55.0-74.8). FDA-harmonized symptomatic disease: 468 centrally confirmed cases ≥14 days — estimates within 2 percentage points of the primary endpoint. Severe-critical: 76.7% (54.6-89.1) ≥14 days and 85.4% (54.2-96.9) ≥28 days; 92.4% after day 42 (post hoc). Hospitalization: 2 vs 29 cases ≥14 days, 93.1% (72.7-99.2); 0 vs 16 ≥28 days, 100% (74.3-100.0). All 5 Covid-19 deaths occurred in placebo recipients (South Africa). Asymptomatic infection (preliminary; N-seroconversion day 29→71, only 2,650 participants with a day-71 result): 18 vs 50, 65.5% (39.9-81.1). Variant-era: in South Africa, where 94.5% of sequenced cases were 20H/501Y.V2 (B.1.351), efficacy ≥14 days was 52.0% (moderate to severe-critical) and 73.1% (severe-critical); in Brazil (P.2 lineage with E484K in 69.4% of sequenced cases) maintained (64.0%/81.7% ≥28 days). Safety: solicited events more frequent in vaccine arm; serious adverse events excluding Covid-19 0.4% vs 0.4%; 7 investigator-attributed related SAEs; numeric imbalances — venous thromboembolism 11 vs 3, seizure 4 vs 1, tinnitus 6 vs 0, transverse sinus thrombosis with cerebral hemorrhage 1 vaccine recipient, Guillain-Barré syndrome 1 vaccine recipient ("A causal relationship... cannot be determined"; post-marketing monitoring stated); deaths 3 vs 16, all investigator-classified unrelated |
| Main limitation | The authors' own list: "A limitation of the trial is the relatively short follow-up" (median 58 days); cross-trial comparison precluded by differing background incidence and variants ("this situation, combined with the emergence of viral variants, precludes the comparison of vaccine trials"); the asymptomatic-infection analysis is preliminary (2,650 of ~39,000 per-protocol participants with a day-71 serology result); the ≥60-with-coexisting-conditions subgroup shows a lower ≥28-day point estimate attributed by the authors to imprecision (15 vs 26 cases) and shorter follow-up. Additional vault-recorded boundaries: symptom-triggered case finding means infection without symptoms is measured only in the day-71 serology subset; durability claims rest on ~3,000 participants followed 11 weeks; the VTE/thrombosis signals are numerically raised but causally unresolved at this sample size |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), replication row for SARS-CoV-2 on a second platform (non-replicating adenovector vs mRNA): same endpoint class as [[sources/polack-2020-bnt162b2-covid-rct]] — centrally confirmed, adjudicated symptomatic Covid-19 — with an independent sponsor, independent platform, and an inert saline placebo in both trials.
- Rung 9 (intervention evidence) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] for the COVID-19 chain now holds two independent platform trials: symptom-triggered NAAT-confirmed, committee-adjudicated disease fell ~67% (≥14 days) and ~66% (≥28 days) versus saline placebo in the measured window.
- Intervention boundary for [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]] and cross-pathogen context: the same trial class that anchors GAP 4 for rotavirus ([[sources/vesikari-2006-pentavalent-rotavirus-rest-rct]]) and HPV ([[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]]) exists for SARS-CoV-2.
- GAP 1-3 (sham-controlled challenge, natural-route transmission, prospective cohort) are untouched by this trial.

## What the experiment can answer
- That, under randomized double-blind saline-placebo control, a single 5×10^10-VP dose of Ad26.COV2.S reduced centrally confirmed, adjudicated moderate to severe-critical Covid-19 with onset ≥14 days by 66.9% (116 vs 348) and ≥28 days by 66.1% (66 vs 193) over the ~8-week median window across eight countries on three continents.
- That protection against severe-critical disease and hospitalization ran higher (76.7-85.4% and 93.1-100%) within the same window, including in South Africa during B.1.351 predominance (52.0%/73.1% ≥14 days) — with all 5 Covid-19 deaths in the placebo arm.
- That a preliminary non-spike-antigen serology analysis in 2,650 participants pointed to reduced asymptomatic infection (65.5%, CI 39.9-81.1), wide enough to include much smaller effects.
- That within this window the trial could not establish or exclude causality for the numerically raised thrombotic/seizure/tinnitus signals (11 vs 3 VTE, 4 vs 1 seizure, 6 vs 0 tinnitus, plus one TTS and one GBS case in vaccine recipients).

## What it cannot answer
- Transmission: no endpoint measures onward transmission; the authors' community-transmission sentence (see leaps) is an inference, not a measurement.
- Durability beyond ~2 months for most participants: median follow-up 58 days; waning or persistence claims lie outside this report.
- Mortality as a powered outcome: deaths were a safety capture; the 3-vs-16 all-cause and 0-vs-5 Covid-19 splits are directionally noted but not powered.
- Cross-trial efficacy ranking vs the BNT162b2 trial or any other vaccine — the authors state the preclusion themselves.
- Rare (<~0.01%) adverse events, long-term safety, and efficacy in adolescents/pregnancy (excluded or later).

## Later inferential-leap risk
- "The effect on the incidence of symptomatic and asymptomatic SARS-CoV-2 infection by the vaccine suggests that it might be useful in reducing community-wide transmission" — transmission was never an endpoint; the symptomatic endpoint measures illness in the vaccinee, and the asymptomatic-infection estimate is a 2,650-participant preliminary analysis with a CI spanning 40-81%. Quoting this sentence as evidence of transmission reduction is the paper's largest leap beyond its experiments.
- "This finding shows that a Covid-19 vaccine that was based on the original Wuhan-Hu-1 strain can elicit cross-protective efficacy against new variants" — rests on interim sequencing of 71.7% of cases and country-stratified analyses; the within-trial variant contrasts are observational strata, not randomized comparisons.
- The day-42 severe-critical figure (92.4%) is explicitly post hoc; carrying it as a primary result would be an endpoint substitution.
- "Dramatically decrease the burden on health care systems" extrapolates from 31 hospitalizations (≥14 days) and 5 deaths — real effects in-window, but health-system-scale claims need the burden arithmetic, not the trial alone.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the intervention rung for the SARS-CoV-2 chain, adenovector platform: a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3 trial in eight countries found that one Ad26.COV2.S dose reduced centrally confirmed, adjudicated moderate to severe-critical Covid-19 by ~67% versus saline placebo (≥14 days) within a median 58-day window, with higher estimates against severe-critical disease and hospitalization, preserved efficacy in the B.1.351-predominant setting, and unresolved numeric safety signals. Transmission, durability, powered mortality, and rare-event causality are separate unmeasured rungs; the authors' own transmission sentence is recorded as inference, not experiment.
