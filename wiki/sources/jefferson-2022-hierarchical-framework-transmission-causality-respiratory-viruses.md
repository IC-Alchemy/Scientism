# A Hierarchical Framework for Assessing Transmission Causality of Respiratory Viruses

## TL;DR
This paper is useful as a modern methodological prompt for respiratory-virus attribution. It argues that evidence for transmission causality should be graded and should not be inferred from isolated detection alone.

## Source type and scope
- Methodological framework article in *Viruses*.
- Lens lookup: Lens `025-661-670-628-717`; DOI `10.3390/v14081605`; PMID `35893670`; PMCID `PMC9332164`.
- It is not a primary transmission experiment; it is a framework for ranking the strength of transmission evidence.

## What the source claims
- Respiratory-virus transmission claims need explicit evidence rules, especially when evidence ranges from PCR detection to viral culture, genomic linkage, contact timing, and exclusion of alternative explanations.
- Stronger evidence requires more than detecting viral RNA in a person near a case.
- The paper proposes a hierarchy for evaluating whether a respiratory virus moved from one host to another and whether that movement plausibly explains illness.

## What experiment or observation is involved
- No single experiment is tested.
- The relevant observation class is respiratory-virus transmission evidence: contact timing, source-recipient linkage, replicating virus, lineage similarity, and alternative pathogen exclusion.

## What question the paper is capable of answering
`How can respiratory-virus transmission evidence be ranked so that weak detection evidence is not mistaken for strong causal proof?`

It cannot prove or disprove transmission for a specific outbreak without the underlying outbreak evidence.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This paper strengthens the vault's existing distinction between detection endpoints and transmission-causality endpoints.
- It is especially relevant to [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]] and future influenza, RSV, measles, and norovirus rows where transmission is a central part of the causal claim.
