# Norwalk Virus Shedding after Experimental Human Infection

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary controlled human oral-challenge and shedding paper for Norwalk virus
- Journal/year: *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, 2008
- Authors: R. L. Atmar and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text CDC/PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Controlled human challenge with graded oral inoculum and placebo/dose comparison |
| Subject / sample | Healthy secretor-positive adult volunteers |
| Exposure or intervention | Oral Norwalk virus inoculum at graded RT-PCR-unit doses |
| Comparator / control | Placebo and dose groups; pre/post specimens |
| Endpoint | Infection, viral gastroenteritis case definition, RT-PCR and ELISA shedding, and antibody rise |
| Result | Sixteen volunteers were infected; 11 met the viral gastroenteritis definition. All infected participants shed virus by RT-PCR and had more than a four-fold antibody rise. RT-PCR shedding had a median duration of 28 days. |
| Main limitation | Healthy selected adults; prolonged RNA shedding does not prove infectiousness for every PCR-positive specimen. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that oral Norwalk virus exposure can produce infection, defined gastroenteritis, shedding, and antibody response in susceptible adults.
- It can answer shedding duration by RT-PCR under the study conditions.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that all prolonged RNA-positive shedding is infectious.
- It cannot define all norovirus strains, outbreak vehicles, or pediatric/elderly severity.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is equating PCR positivity with transmissible virus or generalizing the adult challenge model to all outbreak settings.

## Vault conclusion
Atmar et al. 2008 supports a high-value norovirus human challenge and shedding rung, with a clear PCR-versus-infectiousness boundary.
