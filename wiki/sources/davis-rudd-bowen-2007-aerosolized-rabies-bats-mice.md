# Effects of aerosolized rabies virus exposure on bats and mice (Davis, Rudd, Bowen 2007)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: route/species-boundary paper for the rabies row — the aerosol route kills mice but not bats in this setup, and survivor antibody does not guarantee protection
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 2007
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental aerosol-exposure study with modern nebulization, followed by intramuscular challenge |
| Subject / sample | Laboratory mice and two bat species, exposed to three rabies-virus variants |
| Intervention or exposure | Aerosolized rabies virus exposure; later intramuscular street-virus challenge |
| Comparator / control | Between-species comparison (mice vs two bat species) and between-variant comparison; no sham-aerosol control stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | Survival; rabies neutralizing antibody (followed 6 months); outcome of intramuscular challenge |
| Result | All bats survived aerosol exposure and produced rabies neutralizing antibody. Several mice died of rabies as a result of aerosol exposure. On later intramuscular challenge, bats were afforded poor protection despite the presence of neutralizing antibodies. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; no sham control stated; doses and per-group animal numbers not in the abstract; antibody measured as presence, not titer dynamics. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That under this protocol the aerosol route is lethal in mice but not in either bat species — a species/route boundary.
- That neutralizing antibody after sublethal aerosol exposure did not reliably protect bats against intramuscular challenge — an antibody-endpoint versus protection-endpoint boundary.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Exposure doses and chamber validation; per-species and per-variant numbers; histologic confirmation of rabies deaths in mice.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The vault's register frames the relevant endpoint carefully: bat survival after aerosol exposure does not challenge bite-route rabies causation — it bounds the aerosol route and species susceptibility. The antibody-versus-protection finding also warns against treating serology as a protection endpoint, a rung discipline used across the vault's ladders.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: aerosolized rabies killed several mice and no bats; all bats seroconverted yet showed poor protection against intramuscular challenge — an aerosol/species boundary and an antibody-endpoint boundary in one experiment.
