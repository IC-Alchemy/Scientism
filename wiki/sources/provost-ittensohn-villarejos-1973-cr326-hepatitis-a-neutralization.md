# Etiologic Relationship of Marmoset-Propagated CR326 Hepatitis A Virus to Hepatitis in Man

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary CR326 marmoset propagation, neutralization, and human-serology relationship paper
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1973
- Authors: Philip J. Provost, Oswald L. Ittensohn, Victor M. Villarejos, Jorge A. G. Arguedas, and Maurice R. Hilleman
- Source basis in hand: full-text EBM PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Marmoset serum-neutralization, physical-chemical characterization, filtration, and comparative susceptibility study |
| Subject / sample | CR326 hepatitis virus recovered from a Costa Rican hepatitis A case and passed in marmosets; paired human sera from natural hepatitis A and B cases; coded paired sera from MS-1 hepatitis A and MS-2 hepatitis B human inoculation cases; human immune globulin; *Saguinus mystax* and *S. nigricollis* marmosets |
| Exposure or intervention | CR326 virus mixed with human pre-illness or convalescent sera or human immune globulin, then inoculated into marmosets; treated virus tested for heat, ether, acid stability, and filterability |
| Comparator / control | Virus-alone, serum-alone, diluent, pre-illness sera, hepatitis B sera, and human immune globulin controls; species comparison between *S. mystax* and *S. nigricollis* |
| Endpoint | Marmoset serum enzyme elevations, liver histology, neutralization value, susceptibility to later rechallenge, heat/ether/acid stability, and filtration size boundary |
| Result | Convalescent sera from hepatitis A cases, including coded MS-1 hepatitis A subjects, neutralized CR326 more than pre-illness sera, while hepatitis B cases did not show a comparable hepatitis-A-specific rise. Human immune globulin neutralized CR326. Marmosets given neutralized virus remained susceptible to later challenge, while animals infected by non-neutralized virus were immune to reinfection. CR326 was heat-, ether-, and acid-stable and filtered between 25 and 50 nm. |
| Main limitation | The paper did not reinoculate marmoset-grown virus into humans. It establishes a serologic and marmoset-infectivity relationship, not direct ordinary human transmission or purified-virus human challenge. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that CR326 infectivity in marmosets was specifically neutralized by convalescent human hepatitis A sera and human immune globulin.
- It can answer that hepatitis B sera did not show the same hepatitis-A-specific neutralizing response in the tested set.
- It can answer that the CR326 agent had physical properties consistent with the then-defined hepatitis A virus category.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary fecal-oral human transmission by itself.
- It cannot prove clinical hepatitis A in humans after challenge with marmoset-propagated CR326, because that experiment was not performed.
- It cannot replace later particle morphology, tissue localization, and cell-culture propagation evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating serologic neutralization plus marmoset infection as if it fully establishes every human-causation rung. The paper is stronger than association, but it remains a marmoset-neutralization and human-serology bridge.

## Vault conclusion
Provost et al. 1973 supports a hepatitis A marmoset-infectivity and human-serologic-identity rung. It should be used to connect CR326 to human hepatitis A, not as standalone proof of ordinary human transmission.
