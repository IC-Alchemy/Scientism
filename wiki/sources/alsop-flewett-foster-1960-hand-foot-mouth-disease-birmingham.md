# Hand-Foot-and-Mouth Disease in Birmingham in 1959

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early outbreak report linking a hand-foot-and-mouth clinical syndrome to Coxsackie group A type 16 isolation from vesicle fluid
- Journal/year: *British Medical Journal*, 1960
- Authors: J. Alsop, T. H. Flewett, and J. R. Foster
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Clinical-virological outbreak report |
| Subject / sample | Birmingham 1959 outbreak of mild illness with oral lesions and vesicles on hands and feet; vesicle fluid sampled from hand lesions |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural infection; laboratory attempt to isolate virus from lesion material |
| Comparator / control | No formal population control group |
| Endpoint | Clinical syndrome description and Coxsackie group A type 16 isolation from vesicle fluid |
| Result | The outbreak involved a mild illness with mouth, hand, and foot lesions. Coxsackie A16 was isolated from hand-vesicle fluid in one patient. |
| Main limitation | Virological confirmation was sparse. One successful lesion isolate cannot establish that all outbreak cases, all HFMD cases, or every similar syndrome was caused by Coxsackie A16. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that Coxsackie A16 was present in lesion fluid from at least one clinically typical HFMD case in this outbreak.
- It can answer that an HFMD-like clinical syndrome and a Coxsackie A16 isolation endpoint co-occurred in this report.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove transmission route, outbreak source, or causal exclusivity.
- It cannot show that every clinical case in the outbreak had Coxsackie A16 infection.
- It cannot answer EV71-associated HFMD, neurologic complications, or the broader severity spectrum.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating one isolate as proof that every similar HFMD presentation is Coxsackie A16 disease. This paper is a lesion-isolation and outbreak-description rung, not full causal closure.

## Vault conclusion
Alsop et al. 1960 supports an early Coxsackie A16 lesion-isolation rung for HFMD. It should be cited narrowly as evidence of virus recovery from a clinically typical lesion, not as a universal HFMD causation proof.
