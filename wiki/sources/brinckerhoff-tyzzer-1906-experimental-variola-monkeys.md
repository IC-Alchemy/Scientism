# Studies upon Experimental Variola in Monkeys

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary early variola animal-inoculation paper
- Journal/year: *Journal of Medical Research*, 1906
- Authors: W. R. Brinckerhoff and E. E. Tyzzer
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF extraction

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Animal inoculation, clinical observation, temperature tracking, histopathology, and route-comparison study |
| Subject / sample | Mainly *Macacus cynomolgus* and *M. nemestrinus* monkeys, plus an orangutan; variola material from human smallpox cases in Manila and surrounding provinces |
| Exposure or intervention | Variola material rubbed into scarified skin, inoculated onto cornea or mucous membranes, or introduced into tracheal epithelium by tracheotomy or oral access |
| Comparator / control | Comparisons across inoculation route and with vaccine-virus behavior; some later immunity tests by subsequent variola or vaccinia inoculation |
| Endpoint | Primary inoculation lesion, general exanthem, body temperature, lymph-node reaction, mucosal or tracheal lesion, histologic lesions, and parasite-like inclusion descriptions under the authors' terminology |
| Result | Skin inoculation produced a variola-inoculata-like local lesion, fever pattern, lymph-node reaction, and frequent general exanthem in monkeys. Mucous-membrane inoculation produced local lesions but general exanthem much less often. Tracheal inoculation could produce extensive exanthem and tracheal/lung lesions, but tracheotomy experiments were confounded by operation-wound infection. |
| Main limitation | The work uses artificial inoculation and historical cytologic terminology. It does not reproduce ordinary human smallpox exposure or establish community transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that human variola material produced characteristic lesions and sometimes generalized eruption in nonhuman primates under deliberate inoculation.
- It can answer that route mattered: skin inoculation produced generalized exanthem much more often than mucous-membrane inoculation in this paper.
- It can answer that some attempts to model respiratory-tract initiation were technically confounded.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot answer ordinary human smallpox transmission.
- It cannot prove that the monkey disease was identical to variola vera in humans.
- It cannot support purified-virus causation, because the inoculum was clinical variola material rather than a purified or molecularly defined virus preparation.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating artificial variolation of nonhuman primates as if it directly proves natural human smallpox causation and route. The paper is valuable precisely because it separates route, lesion, exanthem, and model-boundary endpoints.

## Vault conclusion
Brinckerhoff and Tyzzer 1906 supports an early animal-challenge and route-boundary rung for variola. It should be used to discuss experimental variola in nonhuman primates, not as a standalone proof of ordinary human smallpox transmission.
