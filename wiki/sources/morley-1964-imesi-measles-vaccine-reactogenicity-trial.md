# Morley et al. 1964 - Measles and measles vaccination in Imesi village, Western Nigeria

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 26, queue item 52) from the complete 7-page scanned PDF of Bull World Health Organ 1964;30:733-739 (PMC2554995), retrieved from the WHO IRIS DSpace 7 REST API (handle 10665/266982 -> ORIGINAL bitstream PMC2554995.pdf), because the live PMC PDF endpoint serves a Google reCAPTCHA wall and Europe PMC fullTextXML 500s on scans. Every number below is from that scan, not from any summary. No DOI exists for this article. Authors: David C. Morley (paediatrician, Wesley Guild Hospital, Ilesha, later London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine), Margaret Woodland SRN (Child Health Unit, West African Council for Medical Research, resident in the village), Saul Krugman and Harriet Friedman (New York University School of Medicine), Bernard Grab (statistician, WHO, Geneva). Supported in part by the Health Research Council of the City of New York (contract U 1056), WHO, and USPHS research grant E. 3718 (R.2) to WHO.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** two components in one paper - (A) a village-based incidence/mortality record (Imesi, Western Nigeria, 1960-1962, with one author resident in the village) and (B) a randomized double-blind three-arm vaccine trial conducted September-October 1962 "in accordance with the suggestions of WHO, use being made of a printed WHO record sheet."
- **Subjects (trial):** 119 eligible children over 8 months without prior measles were available in Imesi itself, so 315 children attending the Under-Fives Clinic at the Wesley Guild Hospital, Ilesha (25 miles away) were added; all were over 8 months and under 3 years. Malnutrition "was not a bar to acceptance"; children with sickle-cell disease or primary tuberculosis were included; inoculation was postponed for febrile or other acute illness.
- **Intervention:** Group 1 - live Enders attenuated Edmonston B vaccine (supplied by Dr Maurice Hilleman, Merck Institute for Therapeutic Research) plus gamma-globulin 0.01 ml/lb body weight, n=131. Group 2 - "further attenuated" (Schwarz) vaccine without gamma-globulin (supplied by Dr Anton Schwarz, Pitman-Moore), n=130.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES and inert, with a gamma-globulin-controlled structure. Quote: "For Group 1 there was a control sub-group of 60 children receiving inert material plus GG, and for Group 2 a sub-group of 113 receiving inert material without GG. As is stated later, there was no evidence of any difference between these two subgroups and they have been combined for the sake of simplicity" - pooled Group 3, n=173. Blinding quotes: the trial "was 'double-blind'"; "The children were assigned to the various groups by a method of random distribution. The workers concerned with recording observations during the post-inoculation period did not know the group to which any child belonged. The key was made available four weeks after inoculation, at which time the control children were given vaccine" - explicitly "to ensure that no children were deprived of the benefits of the vaccine and to satisfy the mothers."
- **Observation intensity:** clinical examinations before inoculation; children "seen daily on the 6th to 12th days inclusive after inoculation" with rectal temperatures and examination "in a good light by a paediatrician"; children febrile on day 12 followed until fever subsided. Attendance in follow-up "over 98% in Imesi and 90% in Ilesha."
- **Serology sampling:** blood from every fifth child just before inoculation and again four weeks later; sera stored at -20 C, flown on ice to New York for haemagglutination-inhibition testing.

## Village measles record, 1960-1962 (component A)

- Method quote: "one of us (M.W.) has remained in the village and as far as possible discovered and recorded every child that developed measles and the outcome of the disease."
- A mild epidemic in 1960 "during which only five deaths were recorded"; a few cases in late 1961 preceded "an explosive epidemic starting in December"; the early-1962 epidemic "was characterized by the prevalence of severe diarrhoea with much blood and mucus," and "in spite of this treatment and the free use of antibiotics, 26 children died in a period of five months."
- Context figures quoted from the authors' earlier work: measles caused about a quarter of affected children to lose 10% or more of body weight; "50% of the children born die before reaching the age of five" in this part of West Africa.

## Endpoints (named exactly)

1. **Designed endpoint 1 - reactogenicity (Table 1, days 6-12):** fever grades by daily rectal temperature (under 100 F; 100.0-100.9 F; 101-101.9 F; 102-102.9 F; 103 F/39.5 C and above), rash, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis, diarrhoea, convulsions, vomiting.
2. **Designed endpoint 2 - serology (surrogate):** HI antibody on paired sera (every fifth child); Table 2 reports 68 paired specimens by group.
3. **Severe-illness narrative:** serious illness and deaths during the trial period, described case by case.

**No clinical-measles protection endpoint was designed or measured in any arm.** The trial window closed at four weeks, controls were then vaccinated, and no follow-up for natural measles is reported here.

## Results

- **Randomization check:** "a preliminary statistical analysis was made of ... age, sex, weight, nutritional status, and location ... indicated that, with respect to these features, there was satisfactory 'randomization' of the children falling into the different groups." Analysis covers the 359 children attending regularly (Group 1 n=102, Group 2 n=108, Group 3 n=149).
- **Fever (Table 1, %):** no fever or under 100 F - 35.3 / 38.9 / 70.5 (Groups 1/2/3); 100.0-100.9 F - 29.4 / 33.3 / 16.1; 101-101.9 F - 16.7 / 15.7 / 8.1; 102-102.9 F - 9.8 / 7.4 / 2.7; 103 F (39.5 C) and above - 8.8 / 4.6 / 2.7. "For each day of the follow-up period, the frequency [of fever >=100 F] was significantly greater in Group 1 than in the control Group 3 (at the 0.05 probability level). A significant difference was also found between Group 2 and Group 3 for the 8th to the 11th day." All 8 children febrile after day 13 were in Group 1.
- **Other signs (%, Groups 1/2/3):** rash 8.8 / 10.2 / 1.3 (mainly days 9-12; 2 controls "reported as showing a measles-like rash"); cough 73.5 / 60.2 / 58.4; coryza 29.4 / 36.1 / 32.9; conjunctivitis 2.0 / 3.7 / 0; diarrhoea 32.4 / 29.6 / 28.9 ("frequent and watery stools" significantly higher in Group 1 than Group 2 on days 8-9); convulsions 2.0 / 0.9 / 0; vomiting 26.5 / 14.8 / 12.8.
- **Severe illness during the trial:** two deaths, both Group 1 - an 8-month-old with sudden collapse at home on day 9 (autopsy: "no macroscopic or microscopic abnormality other than an excess of fluid in the lungs"; four similar non-trial children seen in ten days, two of whom died - "a 'minor epidemic' of an obscure kind"), and a 10-month-old with respiratory infection from day 6, diarrhoea from day 10, convulsions and death on day 11 (no autopsy; the authors note the diarrhoea "appeared at a time when vaccination might be responsible"). A 16-month Group 2 child developed a severe measles rash with darkening and desquamation on day 12, was "severely ill but recovered," with no known measles contact; the authors write that "exposure to infection three days before vaccination could have produced the clinical picture." Three control-group children had unrelated serious illnesses (crying episode; stomatitis with croup; pneumonia) and recovered.
- **Serology (Table 2):** of 68 paired sera - Group 1: 19 tested, 6 immune before, 13 seronegative, 13 showing immunological response; Group 2: 21 tested, 3 immune, 18 seronegative, 18 response; Group 3: 28 tested, 5 immune, 23 seronegative, 0 response. "The 31 vaccinated non-immune children all showed antibody response. ... A raised antibody level was not found in the second specimen from any child in the control group." Overall 14/68 (20%) had prior immunity, "presumably the result of past measles"; the authors assume immune children "were uniformly distributed in the three groups" - an assumption, not a verified group-level finding.
- **Discussion verdicts:** "The Edmonston B vaccine produced more reactions than the 'further attenuated' vaccine. Without gamma globulin, the reactions would no doubt be more severe and use of the Edmonston B vaccine alone in a West African community cannot be recommended." The Schwarz vaccine "provoked significantly less fever ... and less diarrhoea" and "is likely to prove the vaccine of choice."

## Main limitations

- **No disease endpoint:** the trial measures reactions and seroconversion only. Nothing in this paper measures whether vaccination prevented clinical measles - the protection observations associated with Imesi come from other publications and later follow-up, not from this one.
- Controls were vaccinated at four weeks, so the trial structure permitted no longer unvaccinated comparison even by accident.
- Serology covered ~20% of children (68 paired sera), with 20% already immune before vaccination; group-level immune fractions are assumed, not demonstrated.
- Two deaths occurred in one vaccine group during the blinded window; with no autopsy for the second death, vaccination's role "is difficult to assess" - the authors say so themselves.
- Only 119 eligible village children were available; 73% of the trial population came from a clinic 25 miles away, so the trial population is part village, part clinic-attending.
- Fever/rash attribution inside the reaction window is diagnostic-by-timing; the one severe measles-like illness in Group 2 is acknowledged as possibly natural infection from pre-vaccination exposure.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **Measles ladder, controlled-vaccination rung - second controlled-trial row, surrogate endpoints declared:** this is the Imesi trial behind the famous Nigerian measles-vaccine programme, and the vault files it as what it is: a randomized double-blind inert-material-controlled reactogenicity and serology trial. It explicitly does NOT meet GAP 4's disease-endpoint bar, and the page records that boundary so later reading cannot upgrade it.
- **Control-design breadth:** it adds the gamma-globulin-controlled structure (control subgroups receiving the same GG injection with inert vaccine material) - a comparator form no other vault trial uses.
- **Natural-history anchor:** the village record documents the burden (26 deaths in five months in the 1961-62 epidemic) in the same community, giving the trial its context without the two components being conflated - the record is uncontrolled observation; the trial is the controlled part, and neither substitutes for the other.

## Inferential leaps recorded

- The closing recommendation - "the 'further attenuated' vaccine is likely to prove the vaccine of choice for protecting the West African child against measles" - is a protection claim stated from a reactogenicity/serology trial; no protection was measured here (the conditional "if subsequent trials support" is the authors' own hedge, recorded).
- The New York comparison - "trials involving the inoculation of more than 2000 children with the 'further attenuated' vaccine were carried out in the New York City area ... the clinical reaction of these children was similar ... and their immunological response was equally satisfactory (Krugman et al., 1963)" - is cited cross-trial narrative, not an arm of this experiment.
- The uniform-immunity assumption for group composition is an inference drawn from a 20% serology sample.
- The village-record mortality figures are attributed to measles from clinical observation without laboratory confirmation; the paper's own framing (severe desquamating rash, bronchopneumonia, diarrhoea) is a clinical syndrome description.

> [!warning] Warning
> This trial did not measure disease protection in any arm; its window was four weeks and its controls were vaccinated at the end of it. What it supports is: randomized double-blind comparison of Edmonston B+GG vs Schwarz vs inert material on reactogenicity and HI seroconversion, plus an uncontrolled village measles record. Filing it under GAP 4 (disease endpoint) would be an inferential leap and is not made here. This page is research analysis, not medical advice.
