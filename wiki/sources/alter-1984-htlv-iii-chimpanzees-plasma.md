# Transmission of HTLV-III infection from human plasma to chimpanzees (Alter et al. 1984)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: animal-model boundary paper for the AIDS→HIV row — the rung where infection and immune changes transmit to chimpanzees without reproduction of AIDS
- Journal/year: *Science*, 1984
- Authors: Alter, Eichberg, Masur, Saxinger, Gallo, Macher, Lane, Fauci
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental animal transmission (inoculation) study |
| Subject / sample | Three chimpanzees |
| Intervention or exposure | Transfusion of plasma from patients with AIDS or pre-AIDS |
| Comparator / control | None described in the abstract (no sham-plasma control arm stated) |
| Endpoint | Seroconversion to HTLV-III; lymphadenopathy; T4/T8 lymphocyte ratio; blastogenic responses; opportunistic infections |
| Result | Two of three chimpanzees developed serum antibodies to HTLV-III at 10–12 weeks after transfusion. One animal also developed lymphadenopathy, a transient T4/T8 depression, and impaired blastogenic responses. No opportunistic infections occurred. Adenopathy persisted 32 weeks; antibody persisted at least 48 weeks. The authors note lymphocyte-poor plasma transmitted infection, confirming transfusion risk, and propose the chimpanzee as a model for the lymphadenopathy syndrome. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 3 with no sham control stated in the abstract; the endpoint reached was infection plus lymphadenopathy, not AIDS; observation window under one year for the antibody endpoint. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That plasma from AIDS/pre-AIDS patients contained an agent that reproducibly seroconverted chimpanzees and produced a lymphadenopathy/immune-change syndrome in one animal — an infection-transmission rung.
- That this transmission did not, in the observation window, produce the opportunistic-infection syndrome that defines AIDS in humans — the boundary the vault's register draws.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Control details and full endpoint tables; longer follow-up; whether adenopathy histology matched human lymphadenopathy syndrome.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Both directions. Treating seroconversion plus lymphadenopathy as "AIDS reproduced in chimpanzees" overstates the endpoint actually reached. Treating "no opportunistic infections" in three animals as refuting HIV's role ignores that the model did transmit infection and that AIDS took a decade to develop even in the one chimpanzee later reported with AIDS ([[sources/novembre-1997-aids-chimpanzee-hiv-1]]).

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: human plasma from AIDS/pre-AIDS patients transmitted HTLV-III infection to two of three chimpanzees, with lymphadenopathy and transient immune changes in one, and no opportunistic infections — infection transmitted, AIDS syndrome not reproduced in this experiment.
