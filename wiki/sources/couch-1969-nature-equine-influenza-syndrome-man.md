# Production of the Influenza Syndrome in Man with Equine Influenza Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: fulfillment of the run-7 lead tracked on [[sources/kasel-couch-1969-experimental-infection-man-horses-influenza]] and in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] ("Couch, Douglas, Kasel, Riggs, Knight, Nature (Lond.), in press"). This is the cited source of the human A2/Hong Kong/68 inoculation procedures and the clinical report of the 15-volunteer A/equi-2 experiment summarized in the Bulletin paper. It extends rung 7 of [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]] with the trial's clinical endpoints and a within-lab low-dose/no-illness contrast.
- Journal/year: *Nature* (London), 1969;224(5218):512-514, 1 November 1969. Letter, received 4 August 1969.
- Authors: R. B. Couch, R. G. Douglas, Jr., J. A. Kasel, S. Riggs, V. Knight - Baylor College of Medicine, Departments of Microbiology and Medicine, Houston, Texas. Funded by the Clinical Research Centers Branch (NIH) and the Vaccine Development Branch (NIAID).
- Source basis in hand: the complete scanned article PDF (3 pages, 5.07 MB) from the Wayback capture of `nature.com/articles/224512a0.pdf` (2022-10-15 capture, application/pdf, HTTP 200), read end to end in OCR; Table 1 and the strain-name sentence additionally verified visually from page images at 200 dpi. The paper is not in PubMed (no PMID); the DOI resolves to the paywalled nature.com page, whose archived landing pages hold only the abstract.
- Strain-name caveat recorded as printed: the letter says "We used influenza A/equi-2/Miami/1/68" while its own abstract dates the virus's isolation to 1963 and the inoculum passage history (egg5 HK2 man1 HK4 man1 HK3) is identical to the A/Equi-2/Miami/63 pool in the Bulletin companion. The "68" is an apparent isolate-year inconsistency in the original letter, not an OCR error (verified visually); this page records it as printed and treats the virus as the 1963 Miami isolate used by the same laboratory in the same year.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental human virus challenge (volunteer study), single-arm, uncontrolled; preceded by a low-dose pilot in the same laboratory in March 1965. No sham/placebo group. |
| Subject / sample | Human: "fifteen antibody-free prisoner volunteers" inoculated in November 1968 - adult male prisoners; "Before inoculations were carried out the health of the volunteers was established and their written consent was obtained." Pilot: six volunteers, March 1965. |
| Intervention or exposure | November 1968: 2 mL of undiluted A/equi-2/Miami inoculum, stored at -70 degrees C since 1965 ("no possibility of contamination of the inoculum with the Hong Kong variant"), instilled and sprayed into the nasopharynx with a Devilbiss No. 127 atomizer; simultaneous titration in rhesus monkey kidney (RhMK) cultures gave an administered dose of 10^6.7 TCID50. Inoculum passage history: egg5, HK2 (human embryonic kidney tissue culture), man1, HK4, man1, HK3; an aliquot "was shown to be free of extraneous viruses by methods described earlier." Pilot (March 1965): 1:20 dilution of the same inoculum line, administered dose 10^5.3 TCID50. |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no** - the letter contains no placebo or sham-inoculation group and no quote for one exists in it. The only contrasts are (a) the within-lab March 1965 pilot at 10^5.3 TCID50 (6 men: virus recovered 4/6, antibody rise 5/6, "there was no illness") and (b) symptom frequencies "compared with those reported for naturally occurring influenza A illnesses" - a textbook-description comparison, not a concurrent control group. |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: (1) virus recovery - "Throat swab and gargle specimens were obtained on each of days 0 to 6, 8, 10 and 11 and 0.4 ml. of each specimen was tested for virus in RhMK"; (2) "a four-fold or greater neutralizing antibody response to A/equi-2 virus in sera collected 4 weeks after inoculation" (neutralization by the haemadsorption-inhibition method against 32 TCID50 on heat-inactivated sera); (3) illness, with "Physicians ... looked for signs of illness twice daily", classified in Table 1 as febrile URI, LRI, and systemic illness; febrile URI and systemic illness; afebrile URI; no illness (footnotes: URI = upper respiratory illness, LRI = lower respiratory illness, Syst = systemic illness). |
| Result | November 1968 (15 men, 10^6.7 TCID50): virus recovered from **all 15** volunteers (1-9 positive specimens per man, mean 5; 1-6 positive days, mean 3.3); **14/15** had a >=4-fold neutralizing response, so "all fifteen men were infected"; illness in **13/15** on day 2-3. Table 1 (verified visually): 2 febrile URI+LRI+systemic (one volunteer had a second febrile episode on day 10), 8 febrile URI+systemic, 3 afebrile URI, 2 no illness, total 15. Case course (Fig. 1, 26-year-old): day-2 sudden headache, malaise, myalgias, weakness, fever to 38.8 degrees C oral lasting 18 h, rhinitis within hours, non-productive cough, clinically well by day 5. **9/15** also had a >=4-fold response to the A2/Hong Kong variant; the paired magnitudes correlated (Spearman r = 0.4, P < 0.05). Fever was shorter (mean 1.3 days) than textbook natural-influenza descriptions, with slightly more nasal obstruction/discharge and slightly less cough and sore throat. Pilot contrast (6 men, 10^5.3 TCID50, March 1965): 4/6 shedding, 5/6 antibody rise, **no illness**. |
| Main limitation | No sham-inoculation arm, so background non-specific illness after inoculation instrumentation is not controlled (the same laboratory's 1966 volunteer work puts it near 15%). Subjects were institutionalized adult male prisoners in 1968 - a consent and generalizability context that must be stated. The illness comparison against "naturally occurring influenza A illnesses" is clinical-textbook, not a concurrent natural-case arm, and the authors themselves note the symptom differences they found. The authors list undetermined reasons why the 1965 low-dose pilot produced no illness (dose difference, "loss of virus inhibitor with prolonged storage, different environmental conditions at the time of inoculation, and different time of year"). Internal inconsistencies recorded as printed: the narrative calls the three URI-only cases "febrile" while Table 1 labels that row "Afebrile URI" (illness count 13/15 either way); the printed strain name Miami/1/68 conflicts with the abstract's 1963 isolation date; and the administered dose here (10^6.7 TCID50) differs from the 10^8.75 TCID50 the Bulletin companion reports for what appears to be the same 15-man trial - not resolvable from the two papers alone. |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- This is the run-7 lead paper, not an original queue item: the queue's GAP 1 shortlist item 1 was reconciled in run 7, and its reference-list lead ("Couch et al., Nature, in press") is now verified in full text.
- Rung 7 of [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]] (controlled human challenge, inoculum route) gains the clinical companion report to the 1969 Bulletin anchor: same trial (15 seronegative adults, 13/15 ill, 15/15 shedding, 14/15 seroconversion) with the letter adding the passage-history, dose-titration, and day-by-day clinical detail, plus the within-lab 10^5.3-vs-10^6.7 TCID50 no-illness/13-ill contrast - a dose-coupled illness observation inside one laboratory line of work.
- It does **not** fill GAP 1's sham-controlled requirement: the experiment is single-arm. That requirement remains met for influenza by [[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]] and for RSV by [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]].
- Cross-species relevance: this letter is the primary demonstration that an animal-origin (equine) influenza A virus produced a febrile influenza-like syndrome in experimentally inoculated seronegative men - a boundary datum for claims about "species barriers", recorded as experimental inoculation, not natural transfer.

## What the experiment can answer
- That a characterized A/equi-2 inoculum, given intranasally at a titrated dose of 10^6.7 TCID50 to antibody-free men, infected every recipient (universal shedding, 14/15 seroconversion) and produced physician-observed febrile respiratory-systemic illness in 13/15 within 2-3 days - virus inoculation and illness occurred in the same subjects under recorded dose and specimen schedules.
- That the same virus line at 10^5.3 TCID50 infected 5/6-6/6 recipients without illness in March 1965 - an internal, dose-associated illness contrast within one laboratory's inoculum lineage.
- That inoculation with an equine strain also drove heterologous (A2/Hong Kong) antibody rises in 9/15, with paired response magnitudes correlating.

## What it cannot answer
- Natural-route transmission: all exposure was investigator inoculation in isolation; no recipient was exposed to an infected subject.
- Whether the challenge illness equals ordinary community influenza: the comparison is to textbook descriptions, and fever duration and symptom frequencies differed.
- Sham/placebo effects of nasal instrumentation, illness rates at physiological exposure doses, or population attack rates.
- Any natural equine-to-human transfer: no horse exposure, no field observation, no epidemiological measurement is in this letter.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- Closing sentence: "the essential point is that an influenza virus of equine origin produced typical human influenza illness which lends credence to the possibility that in natural conditions equines and equine influenza viruses might contribute to the emergence of new variants of human influenza" - the experiment instilled virus at 10^6.7 TCID50 into seronegative men; it observed no natural transfer, no reassortment, and no emergence event. The pandemic-emergence suggestion is an extrapolation from antigenic relatedness plus this inoculation result.
- Abstract opening: the antigenic relationship "suggests that the Hong Kong variant was derived from the equine virus" - inference from serologic relatedness (Coleman et al., ref. 1), not from any experiment in this letter.
- "Typical human influenza": the authors' own data show shorter fever and shifted symptom frequencies versus textbook descriptions, which they attribute to the inoculation method - "typical" overstates the correspondence their design can measure.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the single-arm experimental-challenge rung: Couch, Douglas, Kasel, Riggs, and Knight report, with named shedding, serologic, and physician-observed illness endpoints, that a titrated 10^6.7 TCID50 A/equi-2 inoculum infected all 15 antibody-free adult male volunteers and produced febrile influenza-like illness in 13. The paper has no sham-inoculation arm, uses institutionalized prisoner volunteers, and its "typical human influenza" framing and closing cross-species emergence suggestion extend beyond what a single-arm inoculation experiment can measure - all recorded above rather than flattened. As the clinical companion to the same trial in [[sources/kasel-couch-1969-experimental-infection-man-horses-influenza]], it corroborates that paper's primary-inoculation arm (13/15 ill, 15/15 shedding, 14/15 seroconverted, heterologous response in ~9) at full-text level.
