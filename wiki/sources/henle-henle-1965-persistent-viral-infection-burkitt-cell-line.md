# Evidence for a Persistent Viral Infection in a Cell Line Derived from Burkitt's Lymphoma

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary cell-culture paper for indirect evidence of persistent viral infection in a Burkitt-lymphoma-derived lymphoblast line
- Journal/year: *Journal of Bacteriology*, 1965
- Authors: Gertrude Henle and Werner Henle
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cell-culture viral-interference and interferon-like protection study |
| Subject / sample | EB2 lymphoblast cell line derived from a Burkitt lymphoma; human embryonic kidney, human amnion, WI-38 or M-9 human diploid cells, green monkey kidney, rabbit kidney RK-13, and mouse L-cell feeder or assay cultures |
| Exposure or intervention | EB2 cells or EB2-conditioned media were exposed to vesicular stomatitis virus and, in some experiments, poliomyelitis type 1, mumps, or Newcastle disease virus; EB2 cells or media were transferred onto feeder layers before challenge with test viruses |
| Comparator / control | Uninfected cultures, cultures challenged without EB2 cells or EB2-conditioned medium, feeder cultures from several species, disintegrated EB2 cells, and non-EB2 culture media |
| Endpoint | Cytopathic effect, VSV antigen by immunofluorescence, virus titers, survival or partial resistance of EB2 cultures, transferred resistance in feeder cells, and interferon-like activity in cell-free media |
| Result | EB2 cultures showed marked resistance to rapidly destructive VSV infection while still producing VSV for at least 30 days. Intact EB2 cells, but not disintegrated cells, transferred resistance to VSV in human and simian feeder cultures. Cell-free EB2 media also conferred rapid VSV protection to human-cell cultures but not to mouse L cells. The authors interpreted the protective principle as meeting then-current criteria for interferon and concluded that the findings were compatible with latent infection by an unidentified virus. |
| Main limitation | The paper did not isolate or identify EBV as the responsible agent. The endpoint is indirect viral-interference and interferon-like activity in a derived cell line, not Burkitt tumor causation, infectious-virus recovery from primary tumor tissue, or clinical disease induction. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that EB2 cultures behaved differently from comparison cultures when challenged with VSV and some other viruses.
- It can answer that intact EB2 cells and EB2-conditioned media could transfer a protective effect to selected human or simian cultures.
- It can answer that the results were compatible with persistent infection by an unidentified agent in the EB2 culture system.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that EBV causes Burkitt lymphoma.
- It cannot identify the agent as EBV from this experiment alone.
- It cannot show viral particles in primary tumor tissue.
- It cannot establish necessity, sufficiency, ordinary transmission, tumor initiation, or malaria/cofactor structure.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if cell-line resistance to heterologous viruses is retold as direct proof of EBV-driven Burkitt lymphoma. This paper supports an indirect persistent-infection endpoint in EB2 cells, not a complete tumor-causation model.

## Vault conclusion
Henle and Henle 1965 supports a narrow claim: the EB2 Burkitt-lymphoma-derived cell line showed viral-interference and interferon-like behavior compatible with persistent infection by an unidentified agent. It does not by itself identify EBV as the cause of Burkitt lymphoma.
