# Newly Discovered Coronavirus as the Primary Cause of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary SARS-CoV multicentre patient-testing and macaque challenge paper
- Journal/year: *The Lancet*, 2003
- Authors: Thijs Kuiken, Ron A. M. Fouchier, Martin Schutten, Guus F. Rimmelzwaan, Geert van Amerongen, Debby van Riel, Jon D. Laman, Ton de Jong, Gerard van Doornum, Wilina Lim, Ai Ee Ling, Paul K. S. Chan, John S. Tam, Maria C. Zambon, Richard Gopal, Christian Drosten, Stephan van der Werf, Nicolas Escriou, Jean-Claude Manuguerra, Klaus Stohr, and Albert D. M. E. Osterhaus
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Multicentre patient testing plus experimental animal challenge, necropsy, histopathology, immunohistochemistry, electron microscopy, virus isolation, RT-PCR, and exclusion testing for other respiratory pathogens |
| Subject / sample | 436 SARS patients in 12 cohorts from six countries; four adult cynomolgus macaques (*Macaca fascicularis*), two male and two female |
| Exposure or intervention | Patient samples were tested for SARS-CoV, human metapneumovirus, and other pathogens. Macaques were inoculated with fourth-passage SARS-CoV from patient 5688 at 1 x 10^6 TCID50 in 5 ml phosphate-buffered saline: 4 ml intratracheally, 0.5 ml intranasally, and 0.25 ml on each conjunctiva, then necropsied on day 6. |
| Comparator / control | Patient-level comparison with human metapneumovirus and other respiratory pathogens; macaque tissue sections from uninfected cynomolgus macaques as immunohistochemistry negative controls; SARS-CoV-infected and uninfected Vero cells as staining controls; PCR/culture checks for influenza A/B, RSV, rhinovirus, OC43/229E coronaviruses, human metapneumovirus, bacterial pathogens, and *Chlamydia pneumoniae*. The macaque challenge itself did not include a sham-inoculated animal group. |
| Endpoint | Patient SARS-CoV diagnosis rate, human metapneumovirus diagnosis rate, macaque clinical signs, gross lung consolidation, diffuse alveolar damage, SARS-CoV excretion, virus isolation, RT-PCR detection in tissues, immunohistochemical localization, transmission electron microscopy localization, and day-6 antibody status |
| Result | SARS-CoV infection was diagnosed in 329 of 436 SARS patients; human metapneumovirus in 41 of 335. In macaques, three of four became lethargic, one had respiratory distress, and macaques 2-4 had pulmonary consolidation. Three of four developed diffuse alveolar damage with epithelial necrosis, exudate, hyaline membranes, type 2 pneumocyte hyperplasia, and syncytia. Macaques shed SARS-CoV from respiratory sites from day 2. Virus was isolated from selected nasal/throat/sputum samples, from lung and kidney of macaque 2, and from lung of macaque 4. SARS-CoV antigen was localized in pneumonic lung areas, especially type 2 pneumocytes and syncytia; coronavirus-like particles were seen in pneumocytes. No macaque had detectable SARS-CoV antibody by day 6. |
| Main limitation | The animal challenge used only four macaques, combined high-dose intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival inoculation, ended at day 6, and lacked a sham-inoculated macaque group. It supports model-specific pathogenicity and re-isolation, not ordinary human transmission dose or route by itself. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that SARS-CoV was the most frequent laboratory signal in the multicentre SARS case set compared with human metapneumovirus and other tested pathogens.
- It can answer that experimentally inoculated cynomolgus macaques developed SARS-like lung pathology under the specified artificial exposure protocol.
- It can answer that SARS-CoV could be detected, localized, and in some cases re-isolated from experimentally infected macaques.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove the ordinary human natural exposure route or dose for SARS.
- It cannot isolate the contribution of intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival exposure because they were combined.
- It cannot provide sham-controlled macaque symptom attribution or longer-term clinical course beyond day 6.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating a strong macaque challenge/re-isolation result as if it were identical to ordinary human transmission. The paper supplies a major experimental rung, but the conclusion should remain inside the stated host, route, dose, and endpoint boundaries.

## Vault conclusion
Kuiken et al. 2003 is the strongest SARS source in this pass for experimental pathogenicity: it combines multicentre patient association with cynomolgus macaque challenge, lung pathology, viral localization, RT-PCR, and partial re-isolation. Its main boundary is artificial combined-route exposure without a sham-inoculated macaque comparator.
