# Transmission and Serotype Features of Hand Foot Mouth Disease in Household Contacts in Dong Thap, Vietnam

## Source type
- Role in this vault: longitudinal household transmission study tying named exposure (household contact with a clinically diagnosed HFMD index case over a defined window), infection markers (enterovirus RT-PCR plus paired neutralizing-antibody titers to EV-A71/CV-A6/CV-A16), and timing (a two-week per-index follow-up) in the same population; fills GAP 3 (exposure + infection markers + disease timing) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] for the HFMD/enterovirus cluster, which had no household-transmission row before this page.
- Journal/year: *BMC Infectious Diseases*, 2019;19:448 (received 10 January 2019, accepted 21 October 2019, published 5 November 2019); CC BY open access
- Authors: Hoang Cuong Quoc, Nguyen Thao Thanh Thi, Ho Nguyen Xuan, Nguyen Hai Duc, Nguyen An Binh, Nguyen Tham Hong Thi, Phan Hung Cong, Phan Lan Trong (Pasteur Institute in Ho Chi Minh City and Dong Thap Hospital)
- Funding: grant from the Viet Nam Ministry of Health, with the disclosed funder role "in the design of the study and protocol improvement, and do not have a role in the collection, analysis, interpretation of data, or writing the manuscript"
- Ethics: Pasteur Institute Ho Chi Minh City IRB (reference 02/PAS-HĐĐĐ); written informed consent for adults and parental consent for minors
- Source basis in hand: complete open-access full text from Europe PMC (PMC6833133), including Methods, Results, Discussion, Tables 1-7 (annex tables included), and figure captions

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Longitudinal household transmission study with two-week follow-up, hospital-ascertained index cases; April-August 2014, Cao Lanh City and District, Dong Thap Province (a highest-incidence southern province); first 3-4 eligible cases per weekday, ~20 per week over 8 weeks |
| Subject / sample | 150 index cases under 15 years clinically diagnosed with HFMD at Dong Thap provincial hospital (median age 1.5 y, IQR 0.2-5.2; 80 male; 45/150 under 1 y) plus 600 household members, of whom 581/600 completed the study (median age 33 y, IQR 23-48). Household-contact relations: parents 46.0%, grandparents 27.2%, siblings 15.6%, uncles/aunts 11.0%, other 0.2% |
| Exposure | Household contact with the index case, defined as family members "who lived with the index case in the same apartments or houses for the 2 weeks before hospital admission and for the 2 weeks after hospital discharge", having close contact during the 2 weeks before enrollment. Clinical case definition for the index: "febrile illness (>37.5 °C), accompanied by a papulovesicular rash of the oral mucosa, limb extremities and/or buttocks" |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no** sham-infection procedure exists in a natural household transmission study; there is no unexposed comparison arm, and the "attack rate" denominator is all enrolled contacts. Authors' stated reasoning: "If we consider all households as susceptible because they were all located in an area where HFMD is endemic, attack rate among household contacts was calculated based on the number of laboratory confirmed HFMD cases divided by the total enrolment of household contacts" |
| Sampling and laboratory methods | At enrollment, throat swab (RT-PCR) and 2 mL blood (neutralizing antibody) for both index cases and contacts; telephone symptom interviews during the 2-week follow-up; symptomatic participants had swab and blood repeated, asymptomatic contacts had a second blood sample only (second samples obtained for 142/150 index and 545/581 contacts). RT-PCR: published generic enterovirus plus EV-A71 primers (one-step multiplex), VP1 PCR sequencing for serotype/subgenogroup. Neutralization: plasma diluted 1:8-1:1024 against 100 TCID50 virus, rhabdomyosarcoma cells, quadruplicate wells, 6-7 days CPE reading; seropositivity >=1:8; seroconversion = seronegative-to-positive or >=4-fold titer rise between samples |
| Endpoint | Named exactly - "Laboratory confirmed HFMD was defined as a positive RT-PCR, or a 4-fold change in a single EV-A71, CV-A6, CV-A16 (or more) serotype neutralizing antibody titers between sample 1 and sample 2." Contact cases were additionally defined by the same clinical definition applied to the window "2 weeks before admission and 2 weeks after discharge of the index case" |
| Result | Index cases: 113/150 (75%) laboratory confirmed - 90/150 (60%) RT-PCR-positive and 101/142 (71%) with a >=4-fold NT rise (against CV-A6 61%, CV-A16 18%, EV-A71 13%); 23% of clinically diagnosed index cases were test-negative. Household contacts: 148/581 (25.5%) laboratory confirmed - 12/581 (2%) RT-PCR-positive and 142/545 (26%) NT-seroconversion (CV-A6 19%, CV-A16 7%, EV-A71 7%). Symptom dissociation: of 28 contacts reporting illness in the period, zero were RT-PCR-positive and 6 were NT-positive; contacts without symptoms contributed 12 RT-PCR positives and 136 NT seroconversions. Serotype distribution (typed specimens): CV-A6 predominant 57/87 (65.5%) among index; EV71 subgenogroup B5 11/12 (92%) with one C4; index-contact same-serotype concordance 7/12 sequenced instances (CV-A6 x3, EV71-B5 x2, E11, E20). Risk factors (multivariate Poisson): parent/sibling relationship to the index aRR 5.20 (cRR 4.96, 3.04-8.11) vs aunt/uncle/grandparent (parents aRR 5.70, siblings aRR 5.58); male contacts aRR 0.61 (P=0.02), i.e., female contacts at higher risk. No deaths |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors: only hospital-referred symptomatic children were enrolled ("only symptomatic children, of course, is not representative of what happens in the general population, as many cases are asymptomatic"); two-week follow-up is short ("We only followed up household contacts for 2 weeks"); the rate is an underestimate ("we did not count clinical cases of HFMD among index cases and household contacts, and we thus underestimated the actual rate of the transmission among household contacts"); "household transmission and serotypes of cases infected by contact with the asymptomatic case is unclear"; EV-A71 "was not dominant" during the study period |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 46 (claimed run 24), GAP 3 (exposure + infection markers + disease timing in the same people): first HFMD/enterovirus row. The HFMD ladder previously had an early serology-with-contacts row (Brown & O'Leary 1974, CV-A16 fluorescent-antibody responses in family/school contacts) but no modern household-transmission row with per-contact infection markers and a timing window; this page supplies it.
- [[synthesis/hand-foot-mouth-disease-enteroviruses-evidence-ladder]]: new rung "household transmission (natural exposure)" placed between the serology row and the animal-model row, so the ladder now reads isolation/serology -> controlled household transmission -> animal model -> replicated intervention.

## What the experiment can answer
- That, among 581 household contacts of hospital-diagnosed pediatric HFMD cases in an endemic province, 25.5% acquired laboratory-confirmed enterovirus infection within the defined per-index window, with parent and sibling contacts at ~5-fold higher adjusted risk than other relatives.
- That most of that infection burden is subclinical by the study's own markers: only 12/581 contacts were RT-PCR-positive at enrollment and the seroconversion signal (142/545) dwarfs the symptomatic fraction (6 of 28 self-reported illnesses were NT-positive; 12 of 140 RT-PCR positives had no reported illness).
- That the same serotypes (CV-A6, EV71-B5, E11, E20) were found in 7 of 12 sequenced index-contact pairs, consistent with within-household transmission but not proving direction or source.

## What it cannot answer
- Direction of transmission: contacts' enrollment samples cannot distinguish index-to-contact transmission from coprimary infection or an outside source inside the window; the authors concede the asymptomatic-transmission pathway is "unclear".
- Baseline susceptibility: no pre-enrollment serostatus was measured, so the 25.5% "attack rate" mixes new infections with ongoing or past exposures; the susceptible-denominator assumption is stated, not demonstrated.
- Etiological completeness: the contact endpoint is dominated by paired serology to only three serotypes, and PCR-confirmed contact infection is rare (12/581); a 4-fold NT rise over two weeks cannot date the exposure precisely, and heterotypic rises (2-3%) were excluded from confirmation.
- Whether any intervention would reduce household transmission: no intervention arm exists, despite the paper's closing recommendation.

## Inferential leaps to watch
- From household infection clustering to intervention targeting: "it seems justified to also consider the household setting as an additional target for intervention programs" - no intervention was tested in this study.
- From serotype concordance and literature to route: "It could be explained that infections can be spread by the large-droplets from the oral cavity" - a cited explanation (from the Taiwan household study), not a measurement made here.
- From asymptomatic infection fractions to screening policy: "A high percentage of asymptomatic infections found in the current study suggest a need for screening household contacts" - no screening intervention was evaluated.
- External benchmarking: the 25.5% figure is compared against a Taiwanese 52% and a Singaporean lower rate without design harmonization.
