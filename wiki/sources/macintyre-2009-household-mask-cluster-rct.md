# Face Mask Use and Control of Respiratory Virus Transmission in Households (MacIntyre et al. 2009)

> [!warning] Warning
> This page records what one experiment shows for vault analysis. It is not medical advice and makes no mask-use recommendation.

## Bibliographic record

- Authors: MacIntyre CR, Cauchemez S, Dwyer DE, Seale H, Cheung P, Browne G, Fasher M, Wood J, Gao Z, Booy R, Ferguson N (University of New South Wales; Children's Hospital at Westmead; Imperial College London; Westmead Hospital; Wentworth Division of General Practice, Sydney).

## Study type

Prospective cluster-randomized household trial with three parallel arms, run over two winter seasons (August-end October 2006; June-end October 2007) in Sydney, Australia. Participants and trial staff unblinded ("it is not technically possible to blind the mask type to which participants were randomized"); laboratory staff blinded to arm. Intention-to-treat primary analysis; multivariate Cox proportional-hazards survival analysis with Gaussian household random effects for the secondary timing/adherence models. Trial registered nowhere in the paper; CONSORT flow diagram included.

## Subjects

Households recruited through the emergency department of a pediatric hospital and a pediatric primary care practice in Sydney. Eligibility: household with >2 healthy adults >16 years; an index child 0-15 with fever >37.8 degrees C and cough or sore throat, the first and only ill person in the family in the previous 2 weeks, not hospitalized; adults consented; any enrolled adult or sibling with respiratory symptoms excluded at enrollment. Recruited: 290 adults from 145 families; randomized 47 households (94 enrolled adults) to surgical masks, 46 (92 adults) to P2 masks, 52 (104 adults) to no-mask control; 2 control families lost to follow-up. Index-child specimens collected from 141 children; respiratory viruses detected by multiplex RT-PCR in 90/141 (63.8%) - influenza A in 19/141 (13.5%), influenza B 7/141 (4.9%), picornaviruses 22/141 (15.6%), adenovirus 7/141, PIV 8/141, hMPV 8/141, RSV 5/141, coronavirus OC43 3/141; 11 children (7.8%) dual/co-infected.

## Exposure / intervention

Two adults per household wore masks "at all times when in the same room as the index child, regardless of the distance from the child" for one week of daily telephone follow-up: arm 1, 3M surgical masks (cat. no. 1820); arm 2, 3M P2 flat-fold respirators (cat. no. 9320; "an almost identical specification as N95 masks used in the United States"), not fit-tested (fit testing judged infeasible for community use). All arms received the same New South Wales Health infection-control pamphlet. Adherence recorded by daily self-report and exit interviews.

## Comparator / control

Sham arm: NO - the third arm used no intervention device.

> "3) a control group (no masks used)."

No placebo-mask arm and no placebo-advice arm; all arms received identical infection-control pamphlets, so the randomized contrast is mask-versus-no-mask, not intervention-versus-inert.

## Endpoint (named exactly)

Primary outcomes in enrolled adults: "the presence of ILI or a laboratory diagnosis of respiratory virus infection within 1 week of enrollment."

1. ILI: fever >37.8 degrees C, feeling feverish or history of fever, plus >2 listed symptoms (sore throat, cough, sneezing, runny nose, nasal congestion, headache), or 1 listed symptom plus laboratory confirmation.
2. Laboratory-confirmed infection: ">=1 of the symptoms listed above plus laboratory detection of a respiratory virus" - combined nose/throat swabs tested by multiplex RT-PCR for influenza A and B, RSV, PIV types 1-3, picornaviruses, adenovirus, coronaviruses 229E/OC43, and hMPV (Victoria, Australia laboratory panel).

Recording method: adults were swabbed only if symptoms developed during daily telephone follow-up (symptom-triggered swabbing; specimens from 43/51 (84%) sick adults), as were index children at enrollment. Time from recruitment to infection was analyzed with incubation assumed 1-2 days. The paper does not name an influenza-specific endpoint; the lab-confirmed endpoint pools all panel viruses.

## Result

Intention-to-treat - null on both outcomes:

| Outcome | Control (n=100) | Surgical (n=94) | P2 (n=92) | All masks (n=186) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ILI, by individual | 16 (16%) | 19 (20%), RR 1.29 (0.69-2.31), p=0.46 | 14 (15%), RR 0.95 (0.49-1.84), p=1 | 33 (18%), RR 1.11 (0.64-1.91), p=0.75 |
| ILI, by household | 12/50 (24%) | 15/47 (32%), RR 1.33 (0.70-2.54) | 10/46 (22%), RR 0.91 (0.43-1.89) | 25/93 (27%), RR 1.12 (0.62-2.03) |
| Laboratory-confirmed infection | 3 (3%) | 6 (6%), RR 2.13 (0.55-8.26), p=0.32 | 8 (9%), RR 2.90 (0.79-10.6), p=0.12 | 14 (8%), RR 2.51 (0.74-8.5), p=0.19 |

The lab-confirmed comparison is numerically inverted (more confirmed infections in the mask arms) and non-significant. Of the 17 laboratory-positive adults, 10 carried the same virus as the index child (surgical 3, P2 5, control 2), 2 were the only infected person, and 5 carried a virus different from the child's; no dual infections in adults.

Adherence (self-reported): "most or all of the time" on day 1 - 36/94 (38%) surgical, 42/92 (46%) P2; falling to 31% and 25% by day 5; only 21% of mask-arm adults reported wearing masks "often or always" across follow-up.

Adherent-use analysis (non-randomized; adherence as time-dependent covariate): HR 0.26 (0.09-0.77), p=0.015 under a 1-day incubation assumption; HR 0.32 (0.11-0.98), p=0.046 under 2 days. The authors' own boundary: "it is only possible to talk about a statistical association between adherent mask use and reduction in the risk of ILI-infection. The causal link cannot be demonstrated because adherence was not randomized in the trial."

Power statement: 94 adults/arm planned to detect >75% efficacy at 80% power assuming a 20% secondary attack rate and 30% intraclass correlation; observed adult ILI was 17.1% with virus identified in only 34.7% of adult ILI cases (63.8% in children).

## Main limitation

Adults swabbed only when symptomatic, so asymptomatic and subclinical infections are invisible to the lab-confirmed endpoint (the same design property as [[sources/belshe-1998-laiv-children-culture-confirmed-influenza-rct]]); lab-confirmed counts are small (3-8 per arm); influenza was only 18.4% of confirmed infections, so influenza-specific power is very low; adherence <50% with self-report measurement; no blinding of participants or staff; the significant adherent-use result is a non-randomized subgroup contrast the authors themselves decline to call causal.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

GAP 2 (controlled natural-route transmission, human half) - fifth randomized human row, and the first in an Australian population with a pooled multi-virus RT-PCR endpoint. The four prior rows are influenza-endpoint trials ([[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]], [[sources/aiello-2012-facemasks-hand-hygiene-young-adults-rct]], [[sources/suess-2012-berlin-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]], [[sources/stebbins-2011-school-hand-sanitizer-cough-hygiene-rct]]); this one measures all panel viruses, which broadens the route claim beyond influenza but lowers influenza-specific power (recorded both ways). MacIntyre's own Discussion notes the earlier randomized non-pharmaceutical-intervention trial was null (its reference 12, Cowling et al. 2008 PLoS ONE preliminary report - cited here as narrative, not treated as evidence). The rung-level randomized result is now 5/5 null on the intention-to-treat analysis, with significance confined to non-randomized adherence/subgroup/ancillary contrasts.

## Inferential leaps recorded

- "results indicate the potential efficacy of masks in contexts where a larger adherence may be expected, such as during a severe influenza pandemic" - projected adherence and effect sizes from a trial whose randomized result was null; the pandemic-scenario efficacy was not and cannot be measured in this experiment.
- "Masks may therefore play an important role in reducing transmission" - from an ITT-null trial plus a non-randomized adherence association the same Discussion says cannot be read causally.
- "assessing multiple respiratory viruses allows our results to be generalized more broadly to other infections, including new respiratory viruses that may emerge in the future" - route-similarity assumption across viruses the trial itself notes "may differ in their relative dependence ... on different transmission mechanisms."
- The 60-80% adherent-use risk-reduction estimate is model output conditioned on an assumed 1-2 day incubation period and self-reported adherence timing.

## What this wiki currently concludes

Source-reported: in Sydney households where two exposed adults cared for an index child with fever and respiratory symptoms, randomizing adults to surgical masks, P2 masks, or no masks produced no significant difference in ILI or RT-PCR-confirmed respiratory-virus infection within one week (lab-confirmed counts numerically higher in the mask arms, non-significantly), under <50% mask adherence and symptom-triggered swabbing; the significant protective signal appears only when adherence is analyzed as a non-randomized time-varying exposure, which the authors explicitly decline to interpret causally. Vault status: this is a fifth independent randomized null on the human natural-route intervention rung, consistent with the 4/4 prior pattern; it answers whether seasonal household mask distribution measurably reduced infections as randomized (it did not), and it does not measure pandemic-condition adherence, influenza-specific efficacy, asymptomatic infection, or any disease endpoint beyond illness with fever and respiratory symptoms.
