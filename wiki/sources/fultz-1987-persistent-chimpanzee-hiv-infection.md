# Persistent infection of chimpanzees with HIV (Fultz et al. 1987)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: animal-model boundary paper for the AIDS→HIV row — the persistent-infection rung
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1987
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC255895)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental animal inoculation with two-year serologic and virologic follow-up |
| Subject / sample | Three chimpanzees |
| Intervention or exposure | Intravenous inoculation: two animals with HTLV-IIIB (as infected H9 cells or cell-free virus), one with chimpanzee-passaged LAV-1 |
| Comparator / control | Comparison between inoculum forms (cell-associated vs cell-free) and between strains; no sham-inoculated control stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | Virus-specific antibody titers (core, envelope), type-specific and group-specific neutralization, complement-dependent cytolysis, reisolation and peptide mapping of gp120 |
| Result | All three animals maintained persistently high antibody titers to viral core and envelope antigens over two years, with progressively broadening type-specific neutralizing responses extending to heterologous strains by 6–12 months. Sera neutralized homologously and heterologously reisolated chimpanzee viruses and mediated complement-dependent cytolysis of HIV-infected cells. Viruses reisolated at 20 months showed highly similar gp120 peptide maps with one deleted peptide and a new or modified peptide common to reisolated LAV-1 and HTLV-IIIB. The abstract reports no AIDS-like clinical endpoint. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 3; serologic/virologic endpoints only; no disease endpoint reported; no sham control stated. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That inoculated chimpanzees support persistent productive HIV-1 infection with durable, broadening humoral responses and stable, recoverable virus — the persistent-infection rung.
- That this persistence, in the reported window, is not accompanied by reported AIDS-like disease — the boundary.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Clinical monitoring details; longer follow-up; inoculum doses and controls.

## Later inferential-leap risk
"Chimpanzees infected but not diseased" is sometimes quoted to argue HIV cannot cause AIDS. Within the vault's ladder this is a model-boundary: it shows the animal model reproduces infection without (in this window) the syndrome, while [[sources/novembre-1997-aids-chimpanzee-hiv-1]] shows AIDS did eventually develop in a long-infected chimpanzee — so the boundary is temporal and strain-dependent, not absolute.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: three chimpanzees inoculated with HIV-1 strains showed persistent infection with broadening neutralizing responses and recoverable virus over two years, without reported AIDS-like disease — a persistent-infection endpoint, not a disease-reproduction endpoint.
