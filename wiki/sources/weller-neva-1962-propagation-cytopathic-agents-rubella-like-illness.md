# Propagation in Tissue Culture of Cytopathic Agents from Patients with Rubella-Like Illness

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary tissue-culture propagation and serology paper for rubella-like illness
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1962
- Authors: Thomas H. Weller and Franklin A. Neva
- Source basis in hand: full-text official journal PDF from *Experimental Biology and Medicine*, checked against DOI metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Patient urine or blood inoculation into human tissue cultures, serial passage, cytopathic-effect observation, host-range tests, filtration/ether/stability tests, and paired-serum neutralization |
| Subject / sample | Four patients with rubella-like illnesses yielded strains from urine or clotted blood; paired sera from 15 rubella cases across institutional epidemics were tested |
| Exposure or intervention | Urine sediment/supernatant or blood-clot suspensions were inoculated into primary human amnion, human embryonic skin-muscle, and other cultures; isolates were serially propagated in primary human amnion cultures |
| Comparator / control | Parallel control cultures of the same tissue and same media batches; cultures from other long-term experiments; paired sera from other viral infections including varicella, measles, infectious mononucleosis, ECHO-virus infection, mumps, and primary herpes simplex |
| Endpoint | Unique cytopathic changes in primary human amnion cultures, serial passage, filtration, failure to grow in embryonated eggs or produce disease in newborn mice, absence of hemagglutination/hemadsorption, Sindbis interference, ether sensitivity, and neutralizing-antibody rise |
| Result | Agents were isolated from urine or blood of four rubella-like cases and propagated serially in primary human amnion cultures. Parallel controls did not show the specific changes. Paired sera from 15 rubella cases showed antibody rises to one or both tested strains, while the other viral-infection comparison sera did not show comparable neutralization-index changes. |
| Main limitation | The paper's isolates came from a small number of rubella-like cases, some cytopathic effects required long observation or passage, and the study did not demonstrate human transmission or congenital disease. The authors treated the etiologic role as suggested, not fully complete. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that cytopathic agents were recovered and serially propagated from urine or blood of several rubella-like cases in primary human amnion cultures.
- It can answer that paired sera from rubella cases showed neutralizing-antibody rises against the agents under the reported assay, while several other viral illness sera did not.
- It can answer that the agents had defined laboratory properties, including filterability, ether sensitivity, and failure to grow in the tested eggs or newborn mice.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human-to-human rubella transmission.
- It cannot prove congenital rubella syndrome.
- It cannot establish that all clinically diagnosed rubella-like illness is caused by these agents.
- It cannot by itself resolve whether the agents were identical to all other rubella isolates reported by other laboratories.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating propagation in human amnion culture plus neutralization as full causal closure. This paper strengthens the tissue-culture and serology rungs, but ordinary transmission, clinical sufficiency, and congenital endpoints remain separate questions.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in Weller and Neva's 1962 experiments, agents from urine or blood of rubella-like cases could be propagated in primary human amnion cultures and were associated with neutralizing-antibody rises in paired sera from rubella cases.
