# Werzberger 1992 - Formalin-inactivated hepatitis A vaccine in healthy children (randomized placebo-controlled efficacy trial)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 21, queue item 39) from the complete full-text HTML captured by the Internet Archive on 2023-06-29 (www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199208133270702, Methods/Results/Discussion complete; Tables 1 and 2 and Figure 1 are images in that capture and were read visually from page images of the complete 5-page scanned publisher PDF, 815 KB, captured 2012-12-02 at nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJM199208133270702). Citation correction: the queue draft said NEJM 327:661-667 with PMID 1495674; PubMed/Crossref give **NEJM 1992;327(7):453-457, August 13, 1992, PMID 1320740**. Authors: Werzberger A, Mensch B, Kuter B, Brown L, Lewis J, Sitrin R, Miller W, Shouval D, Wiens B, Calandra G, Ryan J, Safary A, Nalin D, Koff B. Supported by a grant from Merck Research Laboratories; the vaccine was manufactured and the serology assayed at Merck. Every number below is from the article itself, not from any summary.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled single-dose efficacy trial in an outbreak setting - "at the beginning of a summer outbreak" in Kiryas Joel, a Hasidic Jewish community in Monroe, New York, "that has had recurrent outbreaks of hepatitis A"; protocol approved by Western Institutional Review Board; enrollment June 24 - November 5, 1991.
- **Subjects:** 1,037 healthy children 2 to 16 years, residents of Kiryas Joel, screened for hepatitis A antibody by finger-stick dried-blood-spot radioimmunoassay (modified HAVAB, Abbott); 35% of screened children had detectable antibody and were excluded; 519 children received vaccine, 518 placebo. Table 1: mean age 7.99 vs 7.84 years (median 7.44 vs 7.31, range 2-16), M/F 267/252 vs 268/250, mean follow-up 104 vs 102 days; "None of the differences between the groups were significant." Exclusions: seropositive, immunodeficient, seizure within past year, allergy to vaccine components, immune globulin within 6 months, prior hepatitis A vaccine, any other vaccine within 15 days.
- **Intervention:** one 0.6-ml intramuscular dose of a highly purified formalin-inactivated hepatitis A vaccine prepared at Merck Research Laboratories from attenuated strain CR326F' grown in MRC-5 monolayers (the cell-culture line the Merck/Hilleman program developed - the Provost & Hilleman 1979 propagation rung of [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]] is the direct ancestor), detergent-lysed, purified, formalin-inactivated, adsorbed to aluminum hydroxide; single lot stored at 2-8 degrees C; each dose contained 25 radioimmunoassay units of antigen (formerly expressed as 400 ng of virus antigen).
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES but NOT inert. Quote: "Like the vaccine, each dose of the placebo - aluminum hydroxide diluent - contained 300 ug of aluminum and thimerosal at a 1:20,000 dilution." Adjuvant-matched, exactly the FUTURE II/Coutinho comparator class already tracked in [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]].
- **Randomization and blinding:** children assigned "according to a schedule for randomly assigning numbers that was based on the sequential order of injection"; "The randomization code was not revealed to any study personnel, the manufacturer's research staff for hepatitis A, or the participants and their parents or guardians until the termination of the study. When labeled with the numbers, the vials of vaccine and placebo were indistinguishable." Suspected-case IgM and ALT/bilirubin testing during the blinded phase was done by an independent commercial laboratory; post-code serology was done at Merck.
- **Endpoint (named exactly):** a clinical case of hepatitis A disease required ALL of: a diagnostic level of IgM antibody to hepatitis A virus; a serum alanine aminotransferase level at least twice the upper limit of normal during the illness "with no other obvious cause"; and at least one defined clinical sign or symptom - icterus with total bilirubin >=2.0 mg/dL, fatigue, malaise, abdominal pain, emesis, oral temperature >=38.3 C without other cause, clay-colored stools, or dark urine. An independent monitoring committee "determined whether each clinical case reported met the case definition." Case detection: parent reporting plus active semimonthly telephone surveillance of every participant; investigator examination and blood draw on any symptomatic child.
- **Efficacy analysis window:** only cases occurring >=50 days after injection counted toward the primary analysis, "to eliminate from consideration children who were already infected before the injection"; pre-specified stopping rule - the committee could stop if the lower bound of a one-sided 95% confidence interval of efficacy reached 47.6%. Design assumption: annual incidence >=3% in this community; 600 children per group at 80% assumed vaccine efficacy gives 95% power to reject equal attack rates.

## Result

- **Primary endpoint (days 50-137), Table 2:** vaccine 0 cases / 498 evaluated vs placebo 25 cases / 496 evaluated; efficacy estimate 100%, P<0.001, one-sided 95% CI lower bound 87.3.
- **All 25 placebo cases were clinically and serologically typical:** IgM anti-HAV positive, ALT more than twice the upper limit of normal (mean 1,383 U/L, range 240-2,850), symptoms typical of hepatitis A; 13 of 25 had icterus confirmed by elevated bilirubin.
- **Early cases outside the efficacy window:** days 5-18 - 7 vaccine recipients vs 3 placebo recipients, each meeting the same case definition (vaccine-arm early cases: ALT mean 1,579 U/L, range 196-4,100; 3 of 7 icteric); zero cases after day 21 in vaccine recipients through day 137. No cases occurred on days 0-4, day 19-20, or days 47-49. The authors attribute the vaccine-arm early cases to children "already exposed and in the incubation phase of the disease" before injection; the placebo arm had 3 cases days 5-18 and 34 cases days 21-137.
- **Trial conduct:** first interim analysis November 6, 1991 showed high efficacy; the committee "recommended that the study be terminated as soon as vaccine could be provided for the placebo recipients"; case accrual continued blinded until November 19, when placebo recipients began vaccination; 10 additional cases (7 >=50 days, 3 <50 days) were reported Nov 6-18. The final analysis is the 25-vs-0 set above.
- **Serologic responses:** of 305 initially seronegative vaccine recipients studied one month after vaccination without clinical hepatitis A, 304 had detectable antibody (geometric mean titer 42 mIU/mL); regression on age showed a significant inverse titer-age relation (P<0.001).
- **Safety:** "There were no serious adverse reactions during the study"; local injection-site reactions 42/515 (8%) vaccine vs 44/510 (9%) placebo, P=0.82 within 24 hours; oral temperature >=38.3 C in 5 vaccine vs 7 placebo; no severe or fulminant hepatitis A and no deaths in either arm during the trial.

## Main limitation (as recorded by the trial itself)

- **Early stop at the first interim analysis** with immediate vaccination of the placebo arm - the design therefore never produced a longer follow-up of unvaccinated controls; mean follow-up was 104 vs 102 days (Table 1). The "10-month follow-up period" sentence in the Discussion refers to case surveillance in the community, not a controlled follow-up.
- **Single insular community mid-outbreak:** the trial estimates efficacy in one age range, one serologically naive population, one HAV strain environment, during one epidemic; the authors record that the ongoing outbreak "does not permit precise determination of the minimal interval between vaccination and exposure to wild virus required to ensure protection."
- **Subclinical infection unmeasured:** "it does not show whether the vaccine will also prevent subclinical infection. The only means by which subclinical infection could have been detected was repeated serologic monitoring... This obviously was not feasible"; at the one-month bleed only 3 asymptomatic IgM-positive cases were detected in the placebo group - "inadequate for analysis."
- One dose only; boosters were planned, not tested here. The 100% point estimate is also a zero-numerator result with a one-sided 95% CI lower bound of 87.3 (printed in Table 2), and its 87.3 bound is what the number itself supports.

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

- **"The vaccine that we used should replace immune globulin as the agent for preexposure prophylaxis against hepatitis A and may merit trials for use in postexposure prophylaxis"** - a practice-replacement recommendation from one outbreak trial of one dose in one community; the postexposure half is at least explicitly framed as needing trials. The comparison arm here is aluminum diluent, not immune globulin - no head-to-head globulin arm exists in this experiment.
- **"Thus, the vaccine should prove protective against strains of all geographic origins"** - extrapolated from literature citations on antigenic conservation, not from any cross-strain arm in this trial.
- **"This suggests that after seroconversion, a single dose of vaccine might confer long-lasting protection"** - from a decay-curve argument and a separate natural-reexposure study; nothing in this trial measured protection beyond ~4 months.
- **"Routine use... could substantially decrease the morbidity and mortality associated with this disease"** - a population-level disease-impact claim from an outbreak-containment efficacy trial; transmission, subclinical infection, and duration are unmeasured (and stated so in the Discussion).

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), first row for Hepatitis A virus.** Exposure = one dose of purified formalin-inactivated cell-culture HAV under ongoing natural outbreak exposure; comparator = adjuvant-matched aluminum-hydroxide placebo (quoted, non-inert - recorded); endpoint = adjudicated clinical hepatitis A with serologic and enzymatic confirmation - clinical disease, not antibody titers; result = 0 vs 25 cases from day 50, efficacy 100% (CI lower bound 87.3).
- The hepatitis A ladder ([[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]]) gains its first intervention rung: the chain from marmoset propagation (Provost 1973) through particle characterization, cell-culture propagation (Provost & Hilleman 1979), and chimpanzee challenge now ends in a controlled human disease-endpoint intervention using that same CR326 lineage - the intervention rung is consistent with the infectious-agent hypothesis for hepatitis A without filling its natural-transmission rung, which remains open in this vault.
- Chain boundary: this experiment measures protection of inoculated children against clinically apparent disease; it does not measure infection (subclinical), transmission, or indirect effects.
