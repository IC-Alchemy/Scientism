# Direct Isolation in Cell Culture of Human Rotaviruses and Their Characterization into Four Serotypes

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary cell-culture isolation and serotyping paper for human rotaviruses
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Microbiology*, 1983
- Authors: R. G. Wyatt and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cell-culture isolation and serotyping study |
| Subject / sample | 73 rotavirus-positive fecal specimens |
| Exposure or intervention | Cultivation in MA104 or primary African green monkey kidney cells |
| Comparator / control | Serotype neutralization assays and prototype strains |
| Endpoint | Serial cultivation and serotype classification |
| Result | 39 of 73 specimens yielded cultivable human rotavirus, and four serotypes were identified. |
| Main limitation | Culture success was partial, and culture conditions may select for strains that grow under the tested laboratory conditions. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that human rotaviruses from some fecal specimens can be directly propagated in cell culture.
- It can answer that cultured isolates could be grouped into serotypes under neutralization assays.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove clinical causality by itself.
- It cannot equate culture success rate with natural infectiousness or disease burden.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating culture success or serotype classification as disease causation rather than a laboratory propagation and typing rung.

## Vault conclusion
Wyatt et al. 1983 supports rotavirus culture/isolation and serotyping. It should be paired with tissue, clinical, and challenge evidence rather than treated as standalone proof.
