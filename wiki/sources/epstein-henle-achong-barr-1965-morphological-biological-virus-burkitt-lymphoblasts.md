# Morphological and Biological Studies on a Virus in Cultured Lymphoblasts from Burkitt's Lymphoma

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary paper for virus-particle morphology in Burkitt-derived lymphoblast cultures and for negative biological recovery tests
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1965
- Authors: M. A. Epstein, G. Henle, B. G. Achong, and Y. M. Barr
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Electron-microscopy, animal inoculation, and tissue-culture virus-isolation study |
| Subject / sample | EB1 and EB2 cultured human lymphoblast strains derived from two Burkitt lymphoma tumors, one maxillary and one ovarian |
| Exposure or intervention | Thin-section electron microscopy of cultured cells; inoculation of whole or disrupted lymphoblast material into chick embryos, newborn hamsters, newborn Swiss mice, newborn C57 black mice, and nine test tissue-culture systems |
| Comparator / control | Multiple test host systems and cell types; tests with whole cells, sonicated material, and freeze-thaw supernatants; hemagglutination and hemadsorption assays; mycoplasma testing |
| Endpoint | Presence and morphology of intracellular particles, particle frequency, animal illness or pathology after inoculation, hemagglutination, hemadsorption, cytopathic effect, serial culture passage, and mycoplasma recovery |
| Result | EB1 and EB2 cultures repeatedly contained morphologically similar virus-like particles over months. About 1-2% of lymphoblasts usually carried particles. Immature particles were about 75 nm; larger presumed mature particles were about 110-115 nm and cytoplasmic. Attempts to isolate or identify the virus biologically failed: chick embryo inoculation produced no pathology or hemagglutinating activity, newborn hamsters and mice showed no ill effects over five weeks, and nine tissue-culture systems showed no detectable changes or hemadsorption. No mycoplasma was isolated. |
| Main limitation | The study is based on derived cell lines, not primary tumor tissue. The biological tests did not recover an infectious agent in the tested systems, and the paper did not prove that the particles induced cell proliferation or caused Burkitt lymphoma. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that EB1 and EB2 Burkitt-derived lymphoblast cultures contained recurring virus-like particles under electron microscopy.
- It can answer that the particles were seen in a small minority of cells and were often associated with altered or degenerating cells.
- It can answer that the attempted animal and tissue-culture recovery systems used in the paper were negative.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot show that the particles were necessary or sufficient for Burkitt lymphoma.
- It cannot show ordinary EBV transmission.
- It cannot show tumor initiation in humans or animals.
- It cannot equate cultured-cell particle observation with direct causation in primary tumors.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The important boundary is positive morphology plus negative biological recovery. The leap begins if visible particles in derived lymphoblast cultures are treated as if they already prove a complete Burkitt lymphoma causal chain.

## Vault conclusion
Epstein, Henle, Achong, and Barr 1965 supports a narrow cell-culture claim: EB1 and EB2 Burkitt-derived lymphoblast cultures carried recurring virus-like particles, but the tested animal and tissue-culture systems did not recover or identify the agent biologically.
