# Inhaled Zanamivir for the Prevention of Influenza in Families

## Source type
- Role in this vault: GAP 4 influenza row #2 and the first row on the influenza ladder whose intervention endpoint is itself household transmission measured under randomized conditions - the family with a symptomatic index case is the experimental unit, and the primary endpoint counts whether influenza crossed from index to contacts. Fills queue item 48 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]].
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine* 2000;343(18):1282-1289 (November 2, 2000). PMID 11058672, DOI 10.1056/NEJM200011023431801. Not in PMC. Sponsor/funder: Glaxo Wellcome, which also provided the study drug; "Drs. Hayden and Monto are paid consultants for Glaxo Wellcome" (Funding and Disclosures). Glaxo Wellcome employees are coauthors (Elliott, Hammond, Ossi, Heartland's Klein as investigator-coauthor).
- Authors: Frederick G. Hayden, Larisa V. Gubareva, Arnold S. Monto, Thomas C. Klein, Michael J. Elliott, Janet M. Hammond, Michael J. Ossi, et al., for the Zanamivir Family Study Group (University of Virginia, University of Michigan, Heartland Research, Glaxo Wellcome; 15 centers in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Finland).
- Source basis in hand: complete full-text HTML (text 42 KB; Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Funding and Disclosures, Appendix, all 28 references) recovered from the Internet Archive 2022-05-04 capture of nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM200011023431801 (live nejm.org is Cloudflare-protected from this sandbox). Read end to end. The numbered tables and Figure 1 are images in the capture and were not readable; all quantitative results below are taken from the narrative text, which reports every primary and subgroup figure.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial; family (cluster) randomization 1:1; 15 centers in four countries; enrollment fall 1998, intervention phase December 1998-April 1999, single influenza season with A/Sydney/5/97(H3N2) predominant and influenza B also circulating; 799 families underwent prospective surveillance, 337 families with a new influenza-like illness (index case) were randomized; primary analysis intention-to-treat; exact stratified test by center, Mantel-Haenszel relative risks, protective efficacy = 1 - RR; sample-size target 270 families for 90% power at 70% assumed efficacy (337 randomized, target met). No trial registration (2000 predates mandatory registration) |
| Subjects | Families of 2-5 members including at least one adult and one child 5-17 years; members excluded from medication: age <5 years, hypersensitivity to zanamivir, immunocompromised state, current anti-influenza agent use, pregnancy, breastfeeding. Dosed: index cases 163 zanamivir / 158 placebo; healthy contacts 414 zanamivir / 423 placebo. Discontinued study medication early: 18 (2%); withdrew prematurely: 11. Compliance: at least 98% of participants (including children 5-11) took 8-10 doses. In 16 families the index case was a child under 5 years (unmedicated): 10 placebo, 6 zanamivir |
| Intervention | Index case: inhaled zanamivir 10 mg twice daily for 5 days. All healthy household contacts: inhaled zanamivir 10 mg once daily for 10 days, started within 36 hours of index-symptom onset. Eligibility trigger: influenza-like illness in one member = at least 2 of fever (>=37.8 C), feverishness, cough, headache, sore throat, myalgia, once community influenza activity documented |
| Comparator / control | **Sham arm: YES.** Both arms inhaled through the identical device: "Families were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive zanamivir or placebo administered through an inhaler (Diskhaler, Glaxo Wellcome, Ware, United Kingdom)." The article does **not** name the placebo composition (recorded, not assumed - contrast the named egg-allantoic-fluid placebo of [[sources/belshe-1998-laiv-children-culture-confirmed-influenza-rct]]). All members of one family received the same arm (including the index case), so the design combines index-case treatment with contact prophylaxis and cannot separate the two effects by randomization |
| Endpoint (named exactly) | "The primary efficacy end point was the proportion of families with at least one initially healthy member in whom symptomatic, laboratory-confirmed influenza A or B developed. Symptomatic influenza was defined by the presence of at least three consecutive diary-card entries listing at least two of the following signs or symptoms: fever (temperature, >=37.8 C), feverishness, cough, headache, sore throat, and myalgia." Laboratory confirmation: viral isolation (Covance central laboratory for North America, local laboratories for Europe), RT-PCR (Public Health Laboratory Service, London), or seroconversion (>=4-fold rise in HI titer between day 1 and day 28 against B/Harbin/7/94, A/Sydney/5/97(H3N2), and A/Beijing/262/95(H1N1); all household members bled on day 1). Contacts swabbed within 2-3 days of their own illness onset; twice-daily symptom diaries for 14 days. Secondary endpoints: time to alleviation of symptoms without relief medication; infection with or without symptoms; resistance monitoring (neuraminidase-inhibition assay with an R292K-resistant control; sequencing of NA and HA1 genes) |
| Result | **Primary endpoint: 19% of placebo families vs 4% of zanamivir families had at least one contact with symptomatic laboratory-confirmed influenza (P<0.001) = 79% protection** (ITT). Subgroups: families whose index illness was laboratory-confirmed influenza - 29% vs 8% (P<0.001), 72% protection; families whose index illness was not influenza - 8% vs 1% (P=0.04), 87% protection (the authors' own attribution: "most of the protection was due primarily to chemoprophylaxis, not to the treatment of subjects with index cases"). Excluding contacts whose symptoms began <1 day after prophylaxis started: 15% vs 2%, P<0.001 = 84% protection. Infection with or without symptoms: reduced by approximately 50%. Protection covered both influenza A and B (underpowered for type-by-type comparison, stated). Timing: 3 of 7 zanamivir-arm contact illnesses (43%) began within half a day of prophylaxis start (read as pre-prophylaxis infections); placebo 7 of 40 within half a day and 22 within 2.5 days. Outside-acquisition marker: 13 families with a discordant contact/index type or contact illness with a non-influenza index (4 placebo vs 1 zanamivir; 7 placebo vs 1 zanamivir; P=0.02). Index-case treatment effect: among laboratory-confirmed index cases, median time to symptom alleviation without relief medication 5.0 (zanamivir, n=76) vs 7.5 days (placebo, n=81), P=0.01; antibiotic-treated complications 5% vs 8%. Breakthrough illness on zanamivir shorter than on placebo (5.5 vs 8.0 days median; n=7 vs 40). Resistance: all 64 isolates from contacts and their index cases sensitive (IC50 <11 nM); no amino-acid differences between within-family isolates in NA or HA1; day-1/day-5 isolates from 5 treated index cases within 3-fold IC50. Tolerability: possible drug-related adverse events 30 zanamivir vs 27 placebo; asthma exacerbation 6% vs 11% |
| Main limitation | Single season and single dominant strain mix (A/Sydney H3N2 + B); healthy families only (immunocompromised and <5-year contacts excluded, so the unmedicated <5-year index setting is a small confounded subgroup, not an arm); prophylaxis started up to 36 h after index onset - pre-existing contact infections are part of both arms (43% of the zanamivir arm's affected-contact illnesses began within half a day, and the <1-day exclusion analysis moves protection from 79% to 84%); family-level randomization makes index-treatment and contact-prophylaxis effects inseparable by design (the influenza-negative-index subgroup is an observational contrast inside the randomization); contact sampling triggered by illness, so subclinical infection is captured only by the day-1/day-28 serology, not by serial swabs; placebo composition not named; sponsor = drug manufacturer with paid-consultant authors; no severe-outcome or all-cause endpoints; no direction-of-transmission measurement (coprimary cases indistinguishable; 13 families document outside acquisition) |
| Inferential leap (authors') | (1) "Our findings indicate that prophylaxis with zanamivir is an effective option for preventing the transmission of influenza within households. Further studies of prophylactic administration of inhaled zanamivir in other high-risk groups ... are warranted" - the household result is measured; the extension to high-risk groups, nursing homes, and institutions is recommendation, not experiment. (2) "chemoprophylaxis may be clinically valuable because it affords immediate protection" with a list of use-cases (no vaccine, peri-vaccination, vaccine non-response) - a practice claim built on one season in healthy families. (3) "Hence, zanamivir is better at preventing clinical illness than infection" - a cross-trial comparison with a separate 4-week seasonal-prophylaxis study, not a within-trial contrast. (4) The amantadine/rimantadine resistance narrative in the Discussion imports external studies (references 4-7, 22-24) to frame the no-resistance result; the vault records the no-resistance finding itself as the hard result of this trial's monitoring. (5) The 87%-protection influenza-negative-index contrast is narrated as proving the prophylaxis mechanism; it is an unplanned, post-randomization subgroup (indices not influenza because many ILI triggers are not influenza), not a randomized comparison |

## What question this experiment answers
Whether, in households with a new influenza-like index illness during a season with circulating A(H3N2) and B, a 1:1 family-randomized regimen of inhaled zanamivir (index treated 5 days, contacts prophylaxed 10 days, all within 36 h of onset) versus an identical inhaler placebo reduces the proportion of families in which at least one initially healthy contact develops symptomatic laboratory-confirmed influenza within the observation window - and whether zanamivir-resistant variants emerge under concurrent index treatment and contact prophylaxis (none detected). The design also yields the vault's first randomized measurement of the household-transmission step itself: under placebo, influenza crossed from index to at least one contact in 19% of families (29% when the index was laboratory-confirmed); under zanamivir, 4% (8%). It does **not** answer per-pair direction of transmission, asymptomatic-infection control (serology-only), efficacy in under-5s or immunocompromised people, seasonal or institutional prophylaxis, severe outcomes, or any intervention other than a neuraminidase inhibitor.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- GAP 4's influenza rung now holds a second platform: an antiviral chemoprophylaxis RCT with an inhaled-placebo sham arm, a named symptomatic-plus-laboratory-confirmation disease endpoint, and 79% protection against the primary endpoint - all from the paper's own Methods and Results.
- This is also the first influenza row in the vault in which **household transmission itself was reduced by a randomized intervention** (19% -> 4% of families with an affected contact): the experiment demonstrates that blocking infection at the household step lowers secondary disease, which is transmission-relevant evidence the 5/5-null nonpharmaceutical trials on the GAP 2 rung never produced (different interventions - chemoprophylaxis vs masks/hygiene - different endpoints, no contradiction claimed).
- The placebo arm's 19%/29% family-level secondary-attack figures are a randomized-baseline measurement of household influenza spread, cross-linked on [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] and [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]] wherever ordinary human transmission is at issue.
- The negative-index and "better at preventing illness than infection" mechanism sentences are attributed inferences (subgroup and cross-trial), not results of this experiment.
