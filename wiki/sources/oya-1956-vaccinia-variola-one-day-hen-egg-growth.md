# Growth of Vaccinia and Variola Viruses in the One-Day-Old Fertile Hen's Egg

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary comparative growth paper for variola and vaccinia in a defined egg system
- Journal/year: *Japanese Journal of Medical Science and Biology*, 1956
- Author: Akira Oya
- Source basis in hand: full-text J-STAGE PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | In ovo growth, dose-response, pock-forming-unit titration, hemagglutinin testing, and host-system comparison |
| Subject / sample | Vaccinia Dairen I strain and variola Yamamoto strain maintained in embryonated eggs; one-day fertile hen eggs; selected mouse and rabbit tests for vaccinia progeny |
| Exposure or intervention | Virus inoculation into one-day fertile eggs; egg-white replacement in selected experiments; daily harvests and titration |
| Comparator / control | Normal chorioallantoic membrane suspension controls; vaccinia versus variola comparison; 1-day egg progeny versus 11-day egg vaccinia in selected pathogenicity and rabbit-skin tests |
| Endpoint | Embryo death or survival, infective dose, PFU per egg, hemagglutinin unit detection, viral release into saline after egg-white replacement, mouse LD50 for vaccinia, and rabbit-skin infectivity for vaccinia |
| Result | One-day eggs supported multiplication of both variola and vaccinia. Variola required more than about 100 PFU to infect this host and yielded lower virus levels than vaccinia; hemagglutinin was not detected in variola-infected whole-egg samples under the method used. Infected embryos were usually killed within a few days. Vaccinia progeny from one-day eggs did not differ detectably from parent virus in the tested mouse and rabbit endpoints. |
| Main limitation | The paper is an egg-growth and comparative propagation study. It does not measure clinical smallpox, human infection, or transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that variola and vaccinia multiplied in one-day fertile hen eggs under the reported conditions.
- It can answer that variola growth in this system differed quantitatively from vaccinia growth.
- It can answer that the tested vaccinia progeny did not show altered mouse or rabbit virulence endpoints.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot answer whether variola causes human smallpox.
- It cannot answer human transmission, route, dose, or clinical disease endpoints.
- It cannot prove that egg growth is equivalent to isolation from human cases.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating growth in a defined egg system as causal closure. Oya 1956 belongs in the propagation rung, not the disease-reproduction rung.

## Vault conclusion
Oya 1956 supports a variola/vaccinia comparative propagation rung in one-day fertile hen eggs and should be used only for laboratory growth and host-system questions.
