# Efficacy and Safety of RSVPreF3 OA in Older Adults Over 2 RSV Seasons (AReSVi-006)

## Source type

| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes - placebo-controlled**, quoted: "Pre-RSV season 1, participants were randomized 1:1 to receive 1 intramuscular injection of AS01E-adjuvanted RSVPreF3 OA or placebo." | Endpoint (named exactly) | **RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease (RSV-LRTD)**: ARI = "2 or more respiratory symptoms/signs or 1 or more respiratory and 1 systemic symptom/sign, lasting 24 hours or longer"; LRTD = "2 or more lower respiratory symptoms/signs (including >=1 lower respiratory sign) or 3 or more lower respiratory symptoms, lasting 24 hours or longer"; RSV A/B confirmed by quantitative RT-PCR on participant-collected nasal self-swabs or visit nasal/throat swabs; **an external adjudication committee reviewed all qRT-PCR-confirmed RSV-LRTD cases and only committee-adjudicated cases entered the analyses**. Secondary endpoints: severe RSV-LRTD (2 case definitions: clinical signs/investigator assessment; supportive therapy) and RSV-ARI (RT-PCR-confirmed). This report's confirmatory objectives: efficacy of 1 dose and of dose-1-then-revaccination against RSV-LRTD over 2 seasons post-dose 1, success criterion = lower CI limit >20%, Bonferroni-adjusted 97.5% CIs (1-sided alpha 1.25%) |
| Result | **One dose over 2 seasons: RSV-LRTD 67.2% (97.5% CI 48.2-80.0) - 30 vs 139 cases (2.0 vs 8.0 per 1000 person-years); severe RSV-LRTD 78.8% (52.6-92.0) - 7 vs 48; RSV-ARI 52.7% (40.0-63.0) - 94 vs 292**; post hoc model without season covariate: 74.5% (60.0-84.5). Season 1 only: 82.6% (57.9-94.1) LRTD; 94.1% (62.4-99.9) severe; 71.7% (56.2-82.3) ARI. Season 2 only: 56.1% (28.2-74.4) LRTD; 64.2% (6.2-89.2) severe; 40.6% (19.0-57.0) ARI. Efficacy declined with follow-up: 82.6% (1 season) -> 78.9% (1 year) -> 77.3% (13.9 months) -> 67.2% (17.8 months). Revaccination: 67.1% (48.1-80.0) over 2 seasons, severe 78.8%, ARI 60.3%; "did not seem to provide additional efficacy benefit"; season 2 only 55.9% (27.9-74.3). By subtype (1 dose, 2 seasons): RSV-A 80.5%, RSV-B 59.7% (RSV-B dominant both seasons: 70.2% and 62.9% of typed cases). By age: 60-69y 65.4%, 70-79y 74.9%, **>=80y 38.4% (-118.2 to 86.1) - uninformative, too few cases**; frail subgroup estimate negative and uninformative (-147.9). **Hospitalization: "efficacy... could not be evaluated against RSV-related hospitalizations, because only 1 participant in the RSV_1dose group and 5 in the placebo group were hospitalized"**; zero hospitalized in the revaccination arm. Safety (dose 2): solicited AEs 63.6% revax vs 26.4%/22.0% placebo groups within 4 days (mostly mild-moderate, resolving 2-3 days); any SAE 4.2/4.4/4.6% and pIMD 0.3% balanced; fatal SAEs 20/26/41 (0.4/0.5/0.4%) balanced |
| Main limitation | Authors' own list: relatively few frail and >=80-year participants with low case numbers in these highest-risk subgroups ("Conclusions on efficacy in these subgroups might be drawn as more cases accumulate"); RSV-related hospitalization numbers too low in all arms to inform that endpoint; two-season comparison complicated by season-2 groups half the size of season-1 groups; interseason epidemiologic differences (more RSV circulation in season 2 after COVID-measure lifting) presumably contributed to the lower 2-season estimate. Additional observation recorded by this vault: the prespecified season-as-covariate model lowers the headline (67.2%) vs the post hoc no-season model (74.5%), because "efficacy is driven by the season with the largest person-years of follow-up" - both are reported in the paper |

## What the experiment can answer
- That in >=60-year-olds, one AS01E-adjuvanted prefusion-F RSV vaccine dose given before an RSV season reduced the incidence of RT-PCR-confirmed, externally adjudicated RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease by ~two-thirds over two seasons (30 vs 139 cases), with the season-1 effect (7 vs 40 cases, 82.6%) re-reported from the same blinded randomized comparison - a disease-endpoint intervention result, not an antibody-titer result.
- That severe RSV-LRTD (clinical-signs definition) was reduced (78.8% over 2 seasons; all severe cases met the clinical-signs definition), and RSV-ARI incidence roughly halved (52.7%).
- That protection waned measurably within 18 months and that a second dose one year later did not measurably raise two-season protection - an experimentally observed durability/booster boundary.
- That the blinded placebo arm balanced safety signals (SAE, pIMD, death rates similar), with reactogenicity elevated in vaccine arms as expected from the adjuvant.

## What it cannot answer
- All-cause health outcomes: no mortality, all-cause hospitalization, or all-cause disease endpoint was collected as an efficacy endpoint (fatal SAEs 20/26/41 are safety captures, not powered outcome comparisons).
- Hospitalization prevention specifically: one vs five events - "could not be evaluated."
- Efficacy in >=80-year-olds and the frail: estimates are uninformative (38.4% with CI -118.2 to 86.1; frail -147.9).
- Transmission or asymptomatic infection: the endpoint case definitions are symptomatic illness; no shedding/asymptomatic-infection endpoint was analyzed here.
- Which placebo composition was used (not named in this report) and any conclusion about the NEJM primary's full text beyond its re-reported season-1 numbers.

## Inferential leaps to watch
- The closing policy inference: "The high efficacy... over 1 year (78.9%...)" plus co-administration immunogenicity studies "supports flexible administration of RSVPreF3 OA, either concomitantly with other vaccines... or separately ahead of the season" - an administration-scheduling extrapolation from a point estimate the same paper shows declining by season 2 (56.1% season 2 only).
- "Although no immunological correlate of protection against RSV disease has been established" (Discussion) is used to rationalize the null revaccination benefit via immunogenicity studies - an immunogenicity-based argument inside a paper whose own trial did not test correlates.
- "Did not seem to provide additional efficacy benefit" - the trial was not designed/powered as a formal non-inferiority test of 1-dose vs 2-dose; this is an observation from similar point estimates and overlapping CIs.
- Subgroup efficacy claims (comorbidities, pre-frail) come from post-stratified descriptive analyses without multiplicity control (paper states no adjustment for secondary objectives).
