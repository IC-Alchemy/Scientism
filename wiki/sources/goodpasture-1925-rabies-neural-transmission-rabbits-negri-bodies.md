# A Study of Rabies with Reference to Neural Transmission in Rabbits and Negri Bodies

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary rabbit inoculation and histopathology paper for rabies neural-spread and Negri-body endpoints
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Pathology*, 1925
- Author: Ernest W. Goodpasture
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Animal inoculation, serial passage, clinical observation, and histopathology study |
| Subject / sample | Adult rabbits; dog street-rabies brain material and later rabbit brain-passaged material |
| Exposure or intervention | Injection of rabid brain suspension into the right masseter muscle; later passage and timed killing of selected rabbits |
| Comparator / control | No clean sham-inoculated control series; the paper includes one inoculated rabbit that did not show observed rabies and was killed without brain examination |
| Endpoint | Fever, clinical rabies signs, localized paralysis, brain and nerve lesions, Negri bodies, "lyssa bodies," and distribution of lesions in fifth motor nuclei and central nervous system |
| Result | Goodpasture reported that rabbits could be infected fairly regularly by right-masseter inoculation with street virus. Most animals allowed to live until symptoms showed paralysis first in the forelegs, more often on the inoculated side. Negri or lyssa bodies were always demonstrable in the right fifth motor nucleus after right masseter inoculation but also in the left nucleus and widely throughout the central nervous system once detectable. |
| Main limitation | This is an artificial intramuscular inoculation and pathology model. It does not reconstruct ordinary bite exposure, dose, saliva transmission, or human rabies causation by itself. The neural-spread inference is based on lesion distribution and timing, not direct visualization of virions moving along axons. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that street-rabies brain material could produce rabies-like clinical disease and rabies-associated histopathology in rabbits after right masseter inoculation.
- It can answer that lesion distribution was compatible with neural spread from the inoculated muscle, while also showing rapid bilateral and wider CNS spread once inclusion bodies were detectable.
- It can answer that Goodpasture distinguished Negri bodies from lyssa bodies and did not treat those bodies as the virus itself.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary bite-route transmission in natural conditions.
- It cannot identify the physical virion.
- It cannot quantify natural exposure dose, incubation distribution, or host range.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if a masseter-muscle rabbit model is retold as complete natural rabies transmission proof. The paper supports artificial-route neural-spread and pathology endpoints.

## Vault conclusion
Goodpasture 1925 supports a narrow animal-model claim: street-rabies material injected into the masseter muscle infected rabbits and produced clinical and histological endpoints compatible with neural spread, but the experiment does not by itself settle ordinary bite-route transmission or human causation.
