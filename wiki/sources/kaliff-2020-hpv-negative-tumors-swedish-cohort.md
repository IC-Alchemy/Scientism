# HPV-negative Tumors in a Swedish Cohort of Cervical Cancer (Kaliff et al. 2020)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: HPV-negative/assay-boundary paper for [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]] and the HPV boundary set in [[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]]
- Journal/year: *International Journal of Gynecological Pathology*, 2020
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free full text exists at PMC7147426 for a later pass)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Retrospective re-investigation of cervical tumors with primary negative or invalid HPV test results |
| Subject / sample | Cervical cancer tumors from a Swedish cohort with primary negative/invalid HPV results |
| Intervention or exposure | Repeated genotyping targeting L1, then an alternative E6/E7-targeted assay on existing or additional tumor material; histologic re-evaluation with exclusions for low tumor-cell content and non-cervical origin; immunohistochemistry (CK5, pan-CK, p63, p16, p53) on confirmed negatives |
| Comparator / control | Within-cohort comparison of confirmed HPV-negative versus HPV-positive cases; no external control group stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | HPV-negative proportion before and after retesting; detection conditions (cycle number, target, amplicon length); p16 immunoreactivity |
| Result | Retesting reduced the HPV-negative proportion by half, from 14% to 7%. Additional positives were often detected only in late PCR cycles, with an alternative (E6/E7) or the same (L1) target, or with shorter amplicon methods. Confirmed HPV negativity was associated with worse prognosis, higher patient age, longer storage time, and adenocarcinoma histology. Some confirmed-negative cases showed strong diffuse p16 immunoreactivity, which the authors read as indicating remaining false negatives. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record: the denominator (how many tumors entered re-investigation) is not stated in the abstract; p16 positivity in PCR-negative tumors is interpreted, not resolved; stored-material degradation is a confounder the authors themselves invoke. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That a substantial share of "HPV-negative" cervical cancers in this cohort were detection failures rather than HPV-independent tumors: half the negative fraction converted on retesting.
- That false HPV negativity in this cohort was, per the authors, mainly methodological (assay target, amplicon length, late-cycle detection, material storage).

## What full text would still need to establish
- The exact cohort denominators and retesting flow; how many cases remained negative after all exclusions; the full IHC interpretation rules.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Both directions: citing "7% HPV-negative" as a clean true-negative fraction (the p16 findings undercut that), or citing the false negatives as if HPV testing were unreliable in general (the same data show retesting recovers most cases).

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: retesting roughly halved the HPV-negative fraction in this Swedish cohort (14% to 7%), with residual negatives skewed toward adenocarcinomas and some p16-positive cases still suspected of being false negatives. The true HPV-negative fraction remains uncertain from the abstract alone.
