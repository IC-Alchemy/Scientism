# Cutts 2005 PCV9 Gambia RCT

## TL;DR
This is the clearest direct vaccine evidence currently filed in this vault for a specific vaccine improving broader health outcomes. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in eastern Gambia, three doses of nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine reduced all-cause admissions and all-cause mortality compared with placebo in children followed to age 30 months.

> Warning: This page is an evidence audit, not medical advice.

## Source identity
- Full title: "Efficacy of nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine against pneumonia and invasive pneumococcal disease in The Gambia: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial."
- Authors: Cutts FT, Zaman SMA, Enwere G, Jaffar S, Levine OS, Okoko JB, Oluwalana C, Vaughan A, Obaro SK, Leach A, McAdam KP, Biney E, Saaka M, Onwuchekwa U, Yallop F, Pierce NF, Greenwood BM, Adegbola RA, for the Gambian Pneumococcal Vaccine Trial Group.
- Journal: The Lancet, 2005, volume 365, issue 9465, pages 1139-1146.
- PMID: [15794968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15794968/)
- DOI: [10.1016/S0140-6736(05)71876-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(05)71876-6)

## Study type
Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind clinical trial with individual allocation.

## Subject type
Children in eastern Gambia, age 6 to 51 weeks at allocation. The trial randomized 17,437 children: 8,718 to pneumococcal conjugate vaccine and 8,719 to placebo.

## Intervention or exposure
Three doses of a nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine. The vaccine contained pneumococcal polysaccharides from serotypes 1, 4, 5, 6B, 9V, 14, 18C, 19F, and 23F linked to CRM197.

## Comparator or control
Placebo lyophilized powder, identical in appearance to the vaccine powder. Vaccine or placebo powder was reconstituted with liquid DPT/Hib and given by intramuscular injection. Children also received concurrent hepatitis B and oral polio vaccines according to local policy, so this trial tests the added effect of PCV9 in a routine-vaccine context, not the effect of "all vaccination" versus "no vaccination."

## Endpoint
- Primary endpoint: first episode of radiological pneumonia.
- Secondary endpoints: clinical pneumonia, severe clinical pneumonia, invasive pneumococcal disease, and all-cause admissions.
- A priori broad-outcome endpoints: all-cause mortality and admissions with potentially invasive pneumococcal disease-related diagnoses.
- Safety endpoints: serious adverse events within 7 days of any study dose, outpatient consultations after vaccination, and a nested short-term reactogenicity assessment.

## Result
Per-protocol results reported:

| Endpoint | Vaccine group | Placebo group | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Radiological pneumonia | 333 / 8,189 children; 26.0 per 1000 child-years | 513 / 8,151 children; 40.9 per 1000 child-years | 37% efficacy, 95% CI 27 to 45 |
| All-cause admissions | 1,065; 89.4 per 1000 child-years | 1,216; 104.7 per 1000 child-years | 15% efficacy, 95% CI 7 to 21 |
| All-cause mortality | 330; 25.2 per 1000 child-years | 389; 30.1 per 1000 child-years | 16% efficacy, 95% CI 3 to 28 |
| Serious adverse events within 7 days | 110 | 131 | Fewer events in the vaccine group; one vaccine-group cellulitis admission judged definitely related |

The intention-to-treat analysis was directionally similar: all-cause admissions were reduced by 13% and all-cause mortality by 14%.

## What question this experiment can answer
It can answer whether adding this PCV9 product to the routine vaccine context for young children in eastern Gambia reduced the trial's named endpoints: radiological pneumonia, invasive pneumococcal disease, all-cause admissions, and all-cause mortality during follow-up.

It is direct support for the narrow claim: in this setting and population, children randomized to receive PCV9 had fewer all-cause admissions and fewer deaths than children randomized to placebo.

## What it cannot answer
- It does not answer whether all vaccines, the whole childhood schedule, or vaccination as a class improves overall health.
- It does not compare a completely unvaccinated group with a completely vaccinated group.
- It does not establish the same mortality effect in lower-mortality settings, older children, adults, or with later PCV formulations without additional evidence.
- It does not settle rare or long-term adverse outcomes beyond what the trial captured.

## Main limitation
The broad health signal is strong for a vaccine trial because it includes all-cause admissions and all-cause mortality with randomized allocation and blinding. The main limitation is external validity: the trial tested one PCV9 product in young Gambian children in a high-burden setting, added to a routine-vaccine context. It supports use-claims only to the extent that the proposed use matches that population, pathogen burden, vaccine formulation, schedule, and outcome profile.

## Relation to the vault question
This source is now the best current counterexample to a blanket claim that no vaccine has direct evidence for improved broader health outcomes.
