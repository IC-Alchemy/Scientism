# Coronavirus as a Possible Cause of Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary patient-series, virus-isolation, molecular, and serology paper for SARS-associated coronavirus
- Journal/year: *The Lancet*, 2003
- Authors: J. S. M. Peiris, S. T. Lai, L. L. M. Poon, Y. Guan, L. Y. C. Yam, W. Lim, J. Nicholls, W. K. S. Yee, W. W. Yan, M. T. Cheung, V. C. C. Cheng, K. H. Chan, D. N. C. Tsang, R. W. H. Yung, T. K. Ng, K. Y. Yuen, and members of the SARS study group
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Retrospective clinical and laboratory investigation, virus culture, electron microscopy, random RT-PCR sequencing, specific RT-PCR, and indirect immunofluorescence serology |
| Subject / sample | 50 Hong Kong patients fitting a modified WHO SARS definition; nasopharyngeal aspirates and sera from all patients; paired sera, feces, and one lung biopsy from subsets; masked control specimens from patients with unrelated diseases and blood donors |
| Exposure or intervention | Clinical samples were tested for conventional respiratory pathogens, cultured on several cell lines including FRhK-4 cells, examined by electron microscopy, sequenced by random RT-PCR, and tested with coronavirus-specific RT-PCR and serology |
| Comparator / control | Controls included 40 nasopharyngeal/fecal samples from patients with unrelated diseases for RT-PCR, 80 sera from patients with respiratory or other diseases, 200 blood-donor sera, and tests for conventional viral and bacterial pathogens |
| Endpoint | Clinical SARS case status, cell-culture cytopathic effect, coronavirus-like particle morphology, 646 bp coronavirus polymerase sequence, RT-PCR positivity in nasopharyngeal aspirate or feces, paired-serum antibody rise, and control negativity |
| Result | Coronavirus isolates were obtained from two patients, one from lung biopsy and one from nasopharyngeal aspirate. Culture showed rounded refractile cells after inoculation and faster cytopathic effect on passage. Electron microscopy showed enveloped coronavirus-compatible particles. A 646 bp sequence was most closely related to group II coronaviruses but distinct. RT-PCR found viral RNA in 22 of 44 available nasopharyngeal samples and 10 of 18 fecal samples. Thirty-two of 32 paired sera seroconverted or had more than a four-fold antibody rise; five additional paired sera from other Hong Kong clusters also seroconverted. Combining serology or viral RNA, 45 of 50 SARS patients had evidence of infection, while the tested controls were negative. |
| Main limitation | The paper is a patient-series association, isolation, and diagnostic paper. It does not experimentally expose animals or humans to purified SARS-CoV to reproduce SARS, and five SARS cases lacked virologic evidence in the tested window. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a novel coronavirus-compatible agent was isolated and characterized from two SARS patients in this Hong Kong series.
- It can answer that SARS patients in this set frequently had RT-PCR or serologic evidence of infection with the agent, while the tested control samples were negative.
- It can answer that the isolate was distinct from known human 229E and OC43 coronaviruses under the methods used.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove that SARS-CoV alone was sufficient to cause the full SARS syndrome.
- It cannot answer experimental disease reproduction, re-isolation after challenge, or ordinary transmission dose.
- It cannot rule out all cofactors or all alternative explanations for the five clinically defined SARS cases without virologic evidence in this paper.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating a strong association/isolation/serology paper as if it already included an animal challenge and re-isolation rung. This paper is stronger than a case report or PCR-only association, but it remains a patient-series diagnostic and characterization rung.

## Vault conclusion
Peiris et al. 2003 supports a high-value SARS-CoV patient-association and isolation rung: culture, electron microscopy, sequence, RT-PCR, and paired-serology endpoints align in most tested SARS cases and not in tested controls. It does not, by itself, settle experimental causation or natural-route transmission.
