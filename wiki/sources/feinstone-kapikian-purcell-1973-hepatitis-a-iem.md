# Hepatitis A: detection by IEM of a viruslike antigen (Feinstone, Kapikian, Purcell 1973)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: human acute-case detection rung for [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]] — the canonical 1973 Science IEM paper, previously a metadata-only gap
- Journal/year: *Science*, 1973
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract (short-form); full text still not obtained — the ladder's "do not strengthen claims beyond the gap" rule remains in force

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Immune electron microscopy detection study on patient stools |
| Subject / sample | Stools from hepatitis A patients in the acute phase of disease |
| Intervention or exposure | None — detection assay on collected specimens |
| Comparator / control | Serologic specificity testing against this disease entity (the abstract states serologic specificity but does not name the control disease panels) |
| Endpoint | Visualization of 27-nm spherical particles; serologic response to the particle antigen |
| Result | Spherical 27-nm particles were visualized in acute-phase stools of hepatitis A patients. The particle was serologically specific for this disease, and every hepatitis A patient tested demonstrated a serologic response to the antigen. The authors suggest it is the etiologic agent of hepatitis A. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; the abstract does not state sample sizes, control-disease panels, or assay details; the etiologic suggestion is author interpretation layered on a detection endpoint. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That a serologically specific 27-nm particle was visualized in acute-phase hepatitis A stools with serologic responses in all tested patients — the human acute-detection observation the ladder's marmoset chain (CR326) was built around.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Numbers, controls, and how the serologic specificity was demonstrated — before any manuscript-strength use.

## Later inferential-leap risk
"IEM particles in stools" is a detection endpoint; the ladder keeps detection separate from chimpanzee challenge, cell culture, and ordinary human transmission. The abstract's closing etiologic suggestion must stay attributed, not adopted.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: 27-nm serologically specific particles were visualized in acute-phase hepatitis A stools with serologic responses in every tested patient. Full-text extraction remains required before this rung is upgraded.
