# DNA of Epstein-Barr Virus Detected in Tissue of Burkitt's Lymphoma and Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular-detection paper for EBV DNA in tumor biopsy tissue
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America*, 1973
- Authors: M. Nonoyama, C. H. Huang, J. S. Pagano, G. Klein, and S. Singh
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Tumor-biopsy DNA extraction and EBV-specific cRNA-DNA membrane hybridization study |
| Subject / sample | 23 African Burkitt lymphoma biopsies, 23 nasopharyngeal carcinoma biopsies, 24 other African tumor specimens, Raji Burkitt lymphoma cell-line DNA, and Hep2 control cell DNA |
| Exposure or intervention | DNA was extracted from tumor tissue, denatured, fixed to nitrocellulose filters, and hybridized with EBV-specific complementary RNA |
| Comparator / control | Other African tumor types; Raji non-virus-producing Burkitt lymphoma cell-line DNA as a genome-equivalent calibration point; Hep2 human epithelial cell-line DNA as nonspecific background control |
| Endpoint | EBV DNA detection by hybridized counts, estimated EBV genome equivalents per cell, and available VCA and early-antigen antibody titers |
| Result | EBV DNA was detected in 22 of 23 Burkitt lymphoma biopsies, with estimated genome equivalents from 4 to 113 per cell. EBV DNA was detected in 18 of 23 nasopharyngeal carcinoma biopsies. Five of 24 other African tumors were also positive. The single Burkitt lymphoma without detectable EBV DNA had less than one genome equivalent per cell by the reported test. The authors stated that the quantitative demonstration tightened but did not prove the argument that EBV causes Burkitt lymphoma. |
| Main limitation | Detection of EBV DNA in biopsy tissue is molecular association, not proof of tumor initiation. The method does not localize EBV DNA to malignant cells versus infiltrating lymphoid cells in every specimen, and the authors explicitly retained that concern for some tumor types. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that EBV DNA was detected in most tested African Burkitt lymphoma biopsies under the reported cRNA-DNA hybridization method.
- It can answer that EBV DNA was not specific to Burkitt lymphoma in this sample set, because some nasopharyngeal carcinomas and other tumors were also positive.
- It can answer that one histologically typical Burkitt lymphoma biopsy was negative or below the method's detection limit.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that EBV is necessary for every Burkitt lymphoma.
- It cannot prove that EBV is sufficient to initiate Burkitt lymphoma.
- It cannot establish temporal order of infection and tumor formation.
- It cannot resolve cofactors such as geography, host susceptibility, or malaria.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This is stronger than cell-line particle observation because it uses biopsy tissue, but it is still a molecular-association endpoint. The leap begins if "EBV DNA detected in most tumors" is retold as "EBV alone causes Burkitt lymphoma."

## Vault conclusion
Nonoyama et al. 1973 supports a narrow tumor-tissue detection claim: EBV DNA was present in most tested African Burkitt lymphoma biopsies by cRNA-DNA hybridization, while one typical Burkitt lymphoma was below detection and the authors explicitly stated that the result did not prove causation.
