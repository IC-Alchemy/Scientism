# Structure and Development of Rabies Virus in Tissue Culture

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary tissue-culture paper correlating rabies-virus morphology, antigen staining, and infectivity
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1967
- Authors: Klaus Hummeler, Hilary Koprowski, and Tadeusz J. Wiktor
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Tissue-culture infection study with electron microscopy, fluorescent-antibody staining, and infectivity titration |
| Subject / sample | BHK/21 baby hamster kidney cells infected with fixed rabies-virus strains, including Flury HEP and Pitman-Moore |
| Exposure or intervention | Virus adsorption to BHK/21 monolayers, with and without DEAE dextran to enhance uptake; sequential harvest for morphology, FA staining, and titers |
| Comparator / control | Control cell cultures and time-course comparisons with and without DEAE dextran |
| Endpoint | Cytoplasmic matrix formation, fluorescent-antibody staining, intracellular infectious-virus rise, particle appearance, budding, particle dimensions, and surface structure |
| Result | Structural changes appeared in the cytoplasm 8-9 hours after infection when FA staining first appeared. Particles appeared at matrix edges and inside matrices at 24-48 hours, corresponding to rising intracellular infectious virus. Budding from cell membranes was seen at 72 hours. Complete particles were bullet-shaped, averaging about 180 by 75 nm, with filamentous core, membranes, and surface protrusions. |
| Main limitation | This is fixed-virus growth in BHK/21 tissue culture. It is not ordinary animal or human rabies transmission and does not reconstruct street-virus bite-route disease. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that fixed rabies virus could replicate in BHK/21 cells with correlated antigen, infectivity, and electron-microscopy endpoints.
- It can answer that the tissue-culture particles had a bullet-shaped morphology and a time-course of matrix formation and budding.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary natural-route rabies causation.
- It cannot show full street-virus pathogenesis in vivo.
- It cannot answer host range or bite transmission.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if tissue-culture replication is treated as equivalent to natural infection. The paper supports laboratory growth and morphology, not route ecology.

## Vault conclusion
Hummeler, Koprowski, and Wiktor 1967 supports a tissue-culture growth claim: fixed rabies virus replicated in BHK/21 cells with correlated morphology, antigen staining, and infectivity endpoints.
