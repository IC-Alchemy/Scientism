# Secondary Household Transmission of 2009 Pandemic Influenza A (H1N1) Virus among an Urban and Rural Population in Kenya, 2009-2010

## Source type
- Role in this vault: prospective population-based household cohort tying named exposure (co-residence with an RT-PCR-confirmed pandemic H1N1 index patient), infection markers (index RT-PCR; clinic-based surveillance available to participants), and illness timing (≤8 days after index symptom onset) in the same population; fills GAP 3 (prospective cohort with exposure + infection markers + disease timing) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]], a gap the vault had no full-text row for before this page.
- Journal/year: *PLoS ONE*, 2012;7(6):e38166 (received 8 September 2011, accepted 1 May 2012)
- Authors: Clara Y. Kim, Robert F. Breiman, Leonard Cosmas, Allan Audi, Barrack Aura, Godfrey Bigogo, Henry Njuguna, Emmaculate Lebo, Lilian Waiboci, M. Kariuki Njenga, Daniel R. Feikin, Mark A. Katz, Benjamin J. Cowling
- Ethics: approved by KEMRI (SSC#932) and the US CDC (IRB#4566)
- Source basis in hand: complete open-access full text from Europe PMC (PMC3372521), including Methods, Results, Discussion, and the published Correction (PMC3966769); the PMC copy carries the corrected Table 4 (the Correction swapped two Kibera/Lwak rows, verified below).

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective population-based infectious disease surveillance (PBIDS) cohort analysis of households, with case households and matched comparison households; not randomized; surveillance platform running since late 2005 |
| Subject / sample | ~28,000 (Kibera, urban informal settlement, Nairobi) and ~25,000 (Lwak, rural western Kenya) PBIDS participants. Index patients: 115 (Kibera) and 66 (Lwak) laboratory-confirmed pandemic H1N1 (pH1N1) cases from 170 and 83 total RT-PCR-confirmed cases after exclusions (no household visit ≤20 days after onset, missing data, not-first household case). Case-household persons: 584 (Kibera), 352 (Lwak). Comparison-household persons: 2,272 (Kibera), 1,289 (Lwak) |
| Exposure | Co-residence in a household with an index patient whose pH1N1 infection was laboratory-confirmed by real-time RT-PCR (CDC pH1N1 protocol) on nasopharyngeal/oropharyngeal swabs collected at field clinics from patients meeting ILI or acute-lower-respiratory-illness criteria; index patient = household member with earliest symptom onset. Household morbidity visits (biweekly, upgraded to weekly at the relevant times) recorded symptoms, onset dates, temperature, and respiratory rate for every household member |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no** sham-infection procedure exists for a natural cohort; the control is matched comparison households. Quote: "To estimate the underlying illness rate in the community, we randomly selected from the PBIDS database 4 comparison households per case household, matched by the number of children aged <5 years. Comparison households did not have laboratory-confirmed pH1N1 cases, and had to have had a household interview ≤10 days before or after the matched patient symptom onset date" |
| Endpoint | Named exactly - secondary case definition: "For case-household persons, we considered laboratory-confirmed pH1N1 infections or episodes of ILI that occurred ≤8 days after the index patient's symptom onset date as a secondary cases. The cutoff of 8 days was determined on the basis of average duration of pH1N1 shedding in Kibera". ILI definition: "self-reported cough or sore throat, and a self-reported fever" within the ≤8-day window (case households) or ≤8 days before the surveillance interview (comparison households). Infection markers for the index: real-time RT-PCR subtype confirmation; for contacts: clinic-based RT-PCR was available to surveillance participants, but the reported secondary endpoint is dominated by self-reported ILI in the timing window. Also reported: mean serial interval (4.0 d Kibera, 3.2 d Lwak) |
| Result | Kibera: 27/584 (4.6%) case-household persons had secondary ILI vs 62/2,272 (2.7%) comparison persons - RR 1.8 (95% CI 1.1-2.8). Lwak: 29/352 (8.2%) vs 43/1,289 (3.3%) - RR 2.6 (1.6-4.3). Rural Lwak higher than urban Kibera (aRR 0.5, 0.3-0.9). Corrected Table 4: children <5 in Lwak had 22.5% (11/49) secondary ILI vs 5.9% (18/303) in persons ≥5 (RR 3.8, 1.9-7.5); Kibera 6.1% (6/98) vs 4.3% (21/486) (RR 1.4, 0.6-3.4, ns). Secondary ILI did not differ significantly by index-patient age group (P=.33 Kibera, P=.06 Lwak) |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors: "We used self-reported ILI rather than laboratory-confirmed influenza among secondary cases to estimate transmission, and consequently might have classified illnesses caused by something other than pH1N1 as secondary pH1N1 cases, especially in malaria-endemic Lwak"; "Our study could not distinguish secondary ILIs attributable to direct transmission from a confirmed pH1N1 patient and illnesses acquired outside the household or from a household member other than the confirmed patient"; recall limitations from biweekly visit intervals; 28%/26% of lab-confirmed index cases would not themselves have met the secondary-ILI definition, indicating the ILI definition misses a large fraction of confirmed infections. Published Correction (PMC3966769) swapped two Kibera/Lwak rows in Table 4; the PMC full text already carries the corrected values used above |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue GAP 3 (prospective cohort with exposure + infection markers + disease timing in the same people): this is the vault's first full-text GAP 3 row for influenza, substituting for the two unreachable historical candidates (Fox/Hall Seattle Virus Watch IV, item 6; Monto/Koopman/Longini Tecumseh XIII, item 7 - both remain `unverifiable` in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]]). It delivers the exposure-to-marker-to-timing structure those studies were shortlisted for: named exposure (co-residence with a confirmed case), infection marker (index RT-PCR; clinic RT-PCR available to contacts), and disease timing (≤8-day window anchored on index onset), under continuous prospective surveillance.
- Influenza ladder: new rung "prospective household cohort, natural exposure" in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]]; cross-cutting note added in [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]].

## What the experiment can answer
- That, in these two Kenyan surveillance populations during the 2009-2010 pandemic period, households containing an RT-PCR-confirmed pH1N1 index patient had significantly more ILI in the ≤8-day post-onset window than matched households without a confirmed case (RR 1.8 and 2.6), consistent with household transmission during that window.
- That the timing structure of the excess (serial interval mean 3.2-4.0 days) matches an incubation-plus-shedding chain anchored on a virologically confirmed index case.
- That secondary ILI concentrated in young children in the rural site (RR 3.8 for <5 years) and did not track index-patient age significantly.

## What it cannot answer
- Whether the excess ILI was actually pH1N1: secondary cases were overwhelmingly self-reported ILI, not virologically confirmed - the authors state this directly and quantify it (28-26% of confirmed index infections would not have met the ILI definition).
- Route attribution: cannot separate index-to-contact transmission from outside-household acquisition or from another household member; urban/rural contrast explanations are hypothesis-level (no mixing data collected).
- Clinical severity: endpoints are self-reported symptoms; no hospitalization or death outcomes are attributed to secondary transmission.
- Any claim that influenza itself was proven to cause the ILI; the study presupposes the pathogen for the index case and measures timed symptom clustering around it.

## Inferential leaps to watch
- The abstract-level conclusion "Substantial pH1N1 household transmission occurred" names the virus for every secondary case, but the measured endpoint for those cases is self-reported cough/sore-throat/fever in an 8-day window. The leap from timed ILI excess to "pH1N1 transmission" is the authors' inference, and this page records it as such.
- The urban/rural explanation (density, community vs household mixing) is explicitly unmeasured in this paper.
- Comparisons with other countries' secondary attack rates import those studies' differing definitions (some use lab-confirmed contacts, some ILI) into one discussion.
