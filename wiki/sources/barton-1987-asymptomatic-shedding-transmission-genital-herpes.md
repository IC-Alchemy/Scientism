# Asymptomatic Shedding and Subsequent Transmission of Genital Herpes Simplex Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary genital HSV asymptomatic-shedding and transmission-chain case investigation
- Journal/year: *Genitourinary Medicine*, 1987
- Authors: S. E. Barton and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Transmission case investigation |
| Subject / sample | Female case with asymptomatic cervical shedding and male partners |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural sexual exposure context |
| Comparator / control | No formal rate-estimating comparator; restriction enzyme DNA analysis used for strain comparison |
| Endpoint | Viral isolation and restriction enzyme DNA typing |
| Result | Authors reported transmission from asymptomatic cervical shedding and subsequent prodromal-phase transmission, supported by viral isolation and DNA analysis. |
| Main limitation | Case/cluster investigation; not designed to estimate transmission rates. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that asymptomatic genital shedding can be involved in a biologically plausible documented transmission chain.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot estimate probability of transmission or generalize across populations.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using a documented transmission case as a population rate estimate.

## Vault conclusion
Barton et al. 1987 supports an HSV asymptomatic-shedding transmission case rung, but not transmission-rate quantification.
