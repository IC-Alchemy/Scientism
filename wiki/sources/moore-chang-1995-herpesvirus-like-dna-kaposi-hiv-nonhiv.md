# Detection of Herpesvirus-Like DNA Sequences in Kaposi's Sarcoma in Patients with and Those without HIV Infection

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary blinded PCR study testing whether the KSHV-like marker appeared in Kaposi sarcoma across HIV status and clinical categories
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 1995
- Authors: Patrick S. Moore and Yuan Chang
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF from the University of Pittsburgh Tumor Virology archive

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Blinded PCR tissue-association study with Southern confirmation of PCR products and sequencing of representative amplicons |
| Subject / sample | 21 Kaposi sarcoma lesion samples: 11 AIDS-associated KS, 6 classic KS, and 4 KS cases in HIV-seronegative homosexual men; uninvolved skin from KS patients; control PBMC and skin samples from people without KS |
| Exposure or intervention | PCR amplification of KS330233, Southern hybridization confirmation with an internal oligonucleotide, control human interferon PCR for amplifiable DNA, and sequencing of representative PCR products |
| Comparator / control | Uninvolved tissue from KS patients, PBMC from 10 healthy HIV-seronegative donors, skin from 11 control subjects without KS, and negative/positive PCR controls in each batch |
| Endpoint | Presence of the KS330233 KSHV-like DNA marker in KS lesions versus control tissues and sequence similarity across KS forms |
| Result | KS330233 was detected in 20 of 21 KS lesions: 10 of 11 AIDS-associated KS lesions, 6 of 6 classic KS lesions, and 4 of 4 KS lesions in HIV-seronegative homosexual men. Three of 14 uninvolved-skin samples from KS patients were positive. One of 21 control specimens was initially positive, but repeat testing after unblinding was negative. Representative sequences from the three KS forms were highly similar. |
| Main limitation | The study was cross-sectional and small. The endpoint was PCR detection of a marker sequence, not infectious-virus isolation, prospective timing, transmission, or tumor causation. The paper stated that virus isolation and formal identification were still pending. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that the same KSHV-like PCR marker was detected in AIDS-associated KS, classic KS, and KS in HIV-seronegative homosexual men in this tested set.
- It can answer that the marker was not limited to HIV-positive AIDS-associated KS lesions.
- It can answer that representative amplicons from different KS forms were closely similar at the tested locus.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that KSHV/HHV-8 caused the tumors.
- It cannot prove that the marker preceded the lesions.
- It cannot prove sufficiency, transmission route, or cofactor structure.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This paper is strong against the narrow idea that the KSHV-like signal was only an AIDS-KS artifact. The leap begins if cross-status PCR detection is treated as a completed tumor-causation experiment.

## Vault conclusion
Moore and Chang 1995 supports a narrow claim: KSHV-like DNA was detected in most Kaposi sarcoma lesion samples across AIDS-associated, classic, and HIV-seronegative homosexual categories in a blinded PCR study. It does not settle causal necessity, sufficiency, or timing.
