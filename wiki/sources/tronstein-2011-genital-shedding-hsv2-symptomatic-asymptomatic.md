# Genital Shedding of Herpes Simplex Virus among Symptomatic and Asymptomatic Persons with HSV-2 Infection

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary prospective daily genital shedding paper for HSV-2
- Journal/year: *JAMA*, 2011
- Authors: E. Tronstein and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective shedding cohort |
| Subject / sample | 498 immunocompetent HSV-2-seropositive persons |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural shedding; daily self-collected genital swabs for at least 30 days |
| Comparator / control | Symptomatic versus asymptomatic HSV-2 infection |
| Endpoint | HSV DNA by quantitative PCR and symptom status |
| Result | HSV-2 was detected on 20.1% of days in symptomatic persons and 10.2% of days in asymptomatic persons; much shedding was subclinical. |
| Main limitation | PCR DNA endpoint and self-swabbing; not direct infectious dose or per-act transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that HSV-2 genital DNA shedding is common even without recognized symptoms.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove infectiousness of every PCR-positive specimen or per-contact transmission probability.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is equating DNA shedding with infectious transmission without infectious-virus or transmission endpoints.

## Vault conclusion
Tronstein et al. 2011 supports a strong HSV-2 genital shedding rung with a PCR-boundary limitation.
