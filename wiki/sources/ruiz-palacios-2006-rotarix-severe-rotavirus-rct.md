# Ruiz-Palacios 2006 - Rotarix monovalent rotavirus vaccine, Latin America phase 3 (randomized placebo-controlled trial)

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** multinational randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3 trial; investigators in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, Honduras, Mexico, Nicaragua, Panama, Peru, Venezuela, and Finland recruited 6-to-13-week-old healthy infants at public pediatric clinics or hospitals; enrollment August 5, 2003 - March 12, 2004; ethics committees at every center; Declaration of Helsinki stated.
- **Subjects:** 63,225 infants enrolled (the safety cohort), randomized 1:1 to 31,673 vaccine / 31,552 placebo, followed for safety until July 23, 2004 (median 100 days after dose 1); the first 20,169 enrolled infants (10,159 vaccine / 10,010 placebo) form the efficacy cohort, followed to one year of age. Groups "were similar in terms of sex distribution and distribution of race or ethnic background and in terms of mean age"; withdrawal proportions similar (Figure 1 of the Supplementary Appendix - image, not archived).
- **Intervention:** two oral doses (visit 1 and visit 2, one to two months later; ~2 and ~4 months of age) of the HRV vaccine Rotarix (GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals), 10^6.5 median cell-culture infective doses of the live attenuated RIX4414 G1P[8] strain, reconstituted with 1.3 ml liquid calcium carbonate buffer; oral poliovirus vaccination kept at least two weeks away from any dose.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES and constituent-matched. Quote: **"The placebo had the same constituents as the active vaccine but without the vaccine virus."** Both reconstituted identically in the same buffer and administered in a blinded manner. This is an inert-class comparator (buffer/excipients matched, no virus), in contrast to the aluminum-adjuvant placebos of FUTURE I/II, Coutinho 1983, and Werzberger 1992 recorded in [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]].
- **Surveillance:** an active-surveillance system was implemented **six months before study initiation** in all medical facilities able to receive infants with the captured outcomes; missed outcomes recaptured at scheduled visits; a capture-recapture method used for the intussusception count.
- **Endpoint (named exactly):** primary safety endpoint - **definite intussusception within 31 days after each dose**, classified by an independent clinical-events committee blinded to study-group assignment using the Brighton Collaboration case definition (definite = confirmation at surgery, autopsy, or imaging: gas/liquid-contrast enema or abdominal ultrasonography); primary efficacy endpoint - **severe rotavirus gastroenteritis by the clinical case definition from two weeks after the second dose until one year of age**, where severe gastroenteritis = diarrhea (>=3 loose or watery stools in 24 h) with or without vomiting requiring overnight hospitalization OR rehydration equivalent to WHO plan B (oral) or plan C (intravenous) in a medical facility; rotavirus confirmed by enzyme immunoassay (Rotaclone) at GlaxoSmithKline Biologicals; G/P typing by RT-PCR plus reverse hybridization at Delft Diagnostic Laboratory. Secondary endpoints: severe gastroenteritis with Vesikari score >=11 (0-20 scale, the scale used for the rhesus-human reassortant trial), type-specific efficacy, post-dose-1 efficacy, rotavirus-gastroenteritis hospitalization, hospitalization for any reason, severe gastroenteritis from any cause.
- **Power:** 20,000 infants assumed to give >=80% power for a 95% CI lower bound >50% vaccine efficacy, assuming 1.5% placebo attack rate, 70% true efficacy, 10% withdrawal. Primary efficacy analysis in the according-to-protocol cohort; an intention-to-treat-style overall cohort used for the dose-1 analyses. The prespecified safety criterion: the two-sided 95% CI of the intussusception risk difference must include zero and lie below 6 per 10,000.

## Result

- **Primary safety endpoint (definite intussusception within 31 days of either dose):** 6 vaccine vs 7 placebo (incidence 1.89 vs 2.21 per 10,000; risk difference -0.32 per 10,000, 95% CI -2.91 to 2.18; relative risk 0.85; P=0.78) - the prespecified safety objective met. Outside the 31-day window: 3 vs 9 (P=0.08). Entire surveillance period: 9 vs 16 (2.84 vs 5.07 per 10,000; difference -2.23, CI -5.70 to 0.94; P=0.16). Of 25 definite cases: 10 after dose 1 (3 vac/7 plac), 15 after dose 2 (6 vac/9 plac), no temporal cluster, 15 of 25 at age 4-5 months; treatment by enema 6, surgery 19; all infants recovered completely (mean hospitalization 5 days).
- **Primary efficacy endpoint (per-protocol, 2 weeks post-dose-2 to age 1):** 12 vs 77 children with severe rotavirus gastroenteritis (2.0 vs 13.3 per 1000 infant-years, P<0.001), **vaccine efficacy 84.7%** (P<0.001). Overall cohort from dose 1: 81.1% (95% CI 68.4-95.3, P<0.001). The per-protocol CI is printed in Table 3 (image not archived; narrative point estimate only - recorded).
- **Severity-graded efficacy:** Vesikari >=11: 11 of 12 vaccine episodes vs 71 of 77 placebo episodes, VE 84.8% (P<0.001); efficacy increased with severity, reaching **100% against Vesikari scores 19-20**. Cumulative hazard ~sevenfold lower in vaccinees at one year (log-rank P<0.001).
- **Type-specific efficacy:** homologous G1P[8] 90.8% (P<0.001); G3P[8]/G4P[8]/G9P[8] (sharing P[8] only) 87.3% (P<0.001); G2P[4], sharing neither the G nor the P antigen: 5 vs 9 episodes, **41.0%, P=0.30** - not statistically distinguishable from zero in this trial. The Discussion quotes this row as "45 percent" while Results print 41.0 (internal inconsistency recorded).
- **Burden endpoints (all-cause):** severe gastroenteritis of any cause requiring WHO plan B/C rehydration: 30.9 vs 51.7 per 1000 infant-years, **40.0% reduction (P<0.001)**; hospitalization for diarrhea of any cause **reduced 42.0% (P<0.001)**; post hoc: hospitalization for gastroenteritis/diarrhea of any cause in the 100-day window 100 vs 179, reduction 42% (CI 28.6-53.1, P<0.001). Rotavirus was detected in only 26% of severe gastroenteritis control cases.
- **Serious adverse events and deaths:** SAEs fewer in the vaccine arm (293.0 vs 331.8 per 10,000, P=0.005), driven by gastroenteritis-related events; hospitalizations 279.7 vs 317.9 per 10,000 (P=0.005). **Deaths: 56 vaccine vs 43 placebo (P=0.20)**; diarrhea-related 4 vs 2 (P=0.41), causes undetermined (no stool samples). A stratified look found a **pneumonia-death imbalance: 16 vaccine vs 6 placebo, P=0.05**, which the investigators then probed: pneumonia SAEs 280 vs 276, pneumonia hospitalizations 277 vs 273, no temporal clustering within 31 days of either dose - "not supported by observation of other pneumonia-related serious adverse events" (the imbalance itself is recorded; the resolving analyses are observational within-trial contrasts, not powered tests).
- **Blinding conduct:** double-blind with identical reconstitution; the intussusception adjudication committee and the death-cause committee blinded; the data-monitoring committee and statistician unblinded by design and empowered to stop. No quantitative blinding check reported (recorded).

## Main limitation (as recorded by the trial itself)

- **Efficacy window short and age-bound:** primary efficacy runs only from 2 weeks after dose 2 to one year of age; no second-year or long-term data in this report.
- **Stool testing without multiplex controls:** "Testing for other enteropathogens was not part of the study protocol" - rotavirus attribution inside the severe-gastroenteritis funnel rests on EIA alone; the all-cause 40-42% reductions exceed the 26% rotavirus-detected fraction and are explained by the authors as probable undetected rotavirus (an inference, recorded below).
- **Power structure:** efficacy powered for the ATP cohort in a 20,169-infant subset; G2P[4] row has 14 episodes total and is formally non-significant (P=0.30).
- **Sponsor control of data and analysis:** GSK Biologicals "held the data and performed the analyses"; manuscript written jointly; the safety-review and adjudication committees are described as independent but the raw data never left the sponsor (recorded).
- 99 deaths across arms with a P=0.05 pneumonia-death imbalance left statistically unresolved by multiple unpowered contrasts (no mortality-efficacy analysis of any kind was performed - the same pattern as the REST trial's 24 vs 20 deaths).

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

- **Burden-inflation leap:** from the 42% all-cause hospitalization reduction with rotavirus EIA-detected in only 26% of control cases, the authors conclude "the burden of disease caused by rotavirus is much greater than that reflected by the incidence of rotavirus-associated hospitalizations calculated by antigen detection" and recommend RT-PCR probe studies. The experiment measured the clinical endpoint; the unmeasured-rotavirus explanation is offered explicitly as "may be explained in part," i.e., an interpretive bridge, not a measured attribution.
- **Cross-protection leap:** the G2P[4] result here is 41.0%, P=0.30; the claim that the vaccine "can also protect, though to a lesser extent, against strains that do not share G or P epitopes" (67% in a pooled meta-analysis with the phase 2 trials) imports evidence from other trials - recorded as cross-trial inference, not a within-experiment result.
- **Intussusception-mechanism leap:** "These data suggest that the intussusception problem encountered with the tetravalent rhesus-human reassortant vaccine may have resulted from the use of a rhesus strain, rather than from the oral administration of live rotavirus in general" - a strain-level attribution from a null safety result in a different vaccine.
- **Policy leap:** "Efforts should now be focused on bringing this vaccine to infants as part of routine immunization programs, especially in areas where rotavirus is associated with an important proportion of the burden of illness and childhood death" - a program-recommendation sentence extending beyond this trial's age window, mortality (unanalyzed), and duration measurements.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), second row for rotavirus - platform replication of the REST trial (Vesikari 2006).** Exposure = live attenuated human G1P[8] (Rotarix, monovalent) vs the REST trial's bovine-human pentavalent reassortant (RotaTeq); comparator = same-constituents-minus-virus placebo (quoted, inert-class) vs REST's visually indistinguishable placebo; endpoint = hospitalization/rehydration-anchored severe rotavirus gastroenteritis with Vesikari grading and EIA confirmation in both trials; result 84.7% here vs REST's 98% severe / 74% any severity in the US/Finland setting; both report no intussusception excess. The rotavirus intervention rung is now replicated across independent platforms, sponsors, and populations - and both remain target-disease trials: neither shows an all-cause mortality benefit (REST 24 vs 20 unanalyzed deaths; this trial 56 vs 43, P=0.20, with a P=0.05 pneumonia-death imbalance investigated but unpowered).
- The rotavirus ladder ([[synthesis/rotavirus-gastroenteritis-evidence-ladder]]) gains a second intervention-rung source; the chain pediatric mucosal antigen -> hospitalized-child association -> adult oral challenge -> culture/isolation -> intervention now ends in two concordant placebo-controlled disease-endpoint trials.
- Chain boundary: this experiment measures protection against clinically severe, EIA-confirmed rotavirus gastroenteritis in infants under active surveillance for one year. It does not measure transmission, asymptomatic infection, duration past age 1, or all-cause mortality benefit.
