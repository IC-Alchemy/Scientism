# Stebbins et al. 2011 - School hand sanitizer and cough hygiene RCT (PIPP)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-10 (loop run 16, queue item 28) from the complete 13-page NIH author-manuscript PDF hosted on CDC stacks (https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/41868/cdc_41868_DS1.pdf); this is the accepted manuscript ("Published in final edited form as" Pediatr Infect Dis J 2011;30(11):921-926). Every number below is from that full text, not from the abstract or any summary page.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** cluster-randomized controlled trial ("The Pittsburgh Influenza Prevention Project (PIPP), a cluster-randomized trial"), Pittsburgh, PA, 2007-2008 influenza season; surveillance and intervention ran 11/1/07 through 4/24/08; RT-PCR testing of absent ILI children ran 1/7/08 through 4/17/08 (dates fixed by county and state public health input; the testing period "corresponded precisely with the weeks when greater than 10%" of NREVSS specimens were influenza-positive).
- **Subjects:** 3,360 students in the largest 10 of 13 interested Pittsburgh K-5 elementary schools; all roster-enrolled students eligible, parents could decline.
- **Intervention:** the "WHACK the Flu" NPI set in 5 intervention schools - (W)ash or sanitize hands often, (H)ome when sick, (A)void touching eyes/nose/mouth, (C)over coughs and sneezes, (K)eep distance from sick people - delivered as 45-minute grade-specific trainings plus a January refresher, with Purell 62%-alcohol dispensers installed in every classroom and common area and a taught goal of four uses per day (observed mean 2.4 doses/day; teacher surveys reported maintained behavior).
- **Comparator/control:** no sham arm. The paper's exact wording: "Children in five schools acted as controls." Control-school NPI usage "had rates of NPI usage similar to the pre-season rates in the intervention schools." Blinding "was not possible because of the nature of the intervention." Allocation used constrained randomization on 2006-2007 covariates, created by one author and concealed until assignment.
- **Endpoint (named exactly):** primary outcome = "an absence episode associated with an influenza-like illness (ILI) that was subsequently laboratory confirmed as influenza A or B," using the CDC ILI definition fever >=38 C with sore throat or cough; one nasal swab tested with the QuickVue A+B rapid test, the second sent to the UPMC Clinical Virology Laboratory for RT-PCR (influenza A, B, and H1/H3 subtyping, equine arteritis virus internal positive control). Secondary outcomes: absence episodes and cumulative absence days for ILI, any illness, and all causes.
- **Sample size:** powered to detect a 40% reduction in the cumulative incidence of laboratory-confirmed influenza (alpha 0.05, power 0.80, Hayes-Bennett cluster adjustment, coefficient of variation 0.1, 10 schools).

## Result

- **Primary outcome - null as randomized:** all laboratory-confirmed influenza (A and B) adjusted IRR 0.81 (95% CI 0.54-1.23), p=0.33.
- **Influenza A:** adjusted IRR 0.48 (95% CI 0.26-0.87), p<0.02 - a "protocol-specified ancillary outcome" (the discussion states 52%); 20 intervention vs 34 control cases.
- **Influenza B:** adjusted IRR 1.45 (95% CI 0.79-2.67), p=0.23 - a non-significant *excess* in intervention schools (31 vs 19 cases); B peaked ~4 weeks after A.
- **Total absence episodes:** 4,710 intervention vs 5,462 control, IRR 0.74 (95% CI 0.56-0.97); the effect was largest in the first two quarters (Q1 0.68, Q2 0.69) and non-significant in the last two (Q3 0.91, Q4 0.86).
- **Case funnel:** 6,569 absence episodes during the testing-season period; reason ascertained for 2,246 (34%); 1,107 (49%) illness-related; 361 (33%) met ILI; 279 (77%) home-visited and swabbed; 104 RT-PCR positives (54 A, 50 B; one child positive for both at different times).
- Intra-cluster correlation for the primary outcome was 0.01, lower than the design assumption.

## Main limitations (including the authors' own)

- "A limitation of this study was that the reasons for absence could be ascertained for only 34% of absences. This resulted in the study being under-powered for most outcomes." (authors' own words)
- Unblinded by design; arm imbalance in age, race, and subsidized-lunch proportion despite constrained randomization (adjusted in models).
- Symptom-triggered swabbing only (absent ILI children): asymptomatic and non-absent infections are invisible to the endpoint.
- One season; the A/B temporal split means each subtype's comparison rests on part of a season; the authors themselves offer multiple unresolvable explanations for the B excess (late-season compliance waning, youngest-grade compliance, viral interference) and ask for larger studies with different A/B distributions.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 2 (controlled natural-route transmission), human half, fourth replication row.** Natural household/school exposure, cluster-randomized allocation, laboratory-confirmed infection endpoint. Joins [[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]], [[sources/aiello-2012-facemasks-hand-hygiene-young-adults-rct]], and [[sources/suess-2012-berlin-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]] on the rung; row recorded in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]] and [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]].
- The rung-level pattern after four trials is unchanged and now better replicated: 4/4 null as randomized on the primary endpoint; every significant finding sits in a subgroup, ancillary outcome, or per-protocol analysis. Stebbins 2011 is the first of the four whose abstract headline names the *subtype*-split ancillary finding rather than the null primary; the null primary is recorded here prominently for that reason.

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

1. "Our results suggest that NPIs can be an important adjunct to influenza vaccination programs to reduce the number of influenza A infections among children" - a programmatic recommendation drawn from a null primary endpoint plus one ancillary outcome in one season.
2. "the results provide support for currently recommended respiratory hygiene behaviors during seasonal and pandemic influenza outbreaks" - pandemic-level policy support from a single-season, single-city school trial whose primary endpoint was not significant.
3. The influenza-B discussion (interferon-mediated interference, compliance decay) is mechanism speculation offered as possible explanation; the authors do not mark it as hypothesis in every instance. Recorded here as inference, not result.
