# The Pathogenesis of Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection in Cotton Rats

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary RSV animal-model pathogenesis paper
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Pathology*, 1978
- Authors: G. A. Prince, A. B. Jenson, R. L. Horswood, E. Camargo, and R. M. Chanock
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental animal infection model |
| Subject / sample | Cotton rats inoculated with RSV Long strain |
| Exposure or intervention | Intranasal RSV inoculation |
| Comparator / control | Time-course tissue recovery, antigen localization, histology, and neutralizing antibody endpoints; control structure should be checked in the full article before stronger claims |
| Endpoint | Recoverable virus from nose, lung, and trachea; immunofluorescent antigen localization; histopathology; neutralizing antibody |
| Result | RSV reached high lung and nasal titers, antigen localized in nasal and bronchial/bronchiolar epithelium, and mild proliferative bronchiolitis was observed. |
| Main limitation | Cotton-rat model with high-dose experimental inoculation; no direct infant-human causation endpoint. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that RSV can infect cotton-rat respiratory tissue and produce model-specific lower-airway pathology.
- It can localize viral antigen and recover virus from specified respiratory sites in the model.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary infant bronchiolitis causation or human transmission route.
- It cannot replace human clinical and diagnostic evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating cotton-rat bronchiolitis-like pathology as direct proof of infant bronchiolitis pathogenesis.

## Vault conclusion
Prince et al. 1978 supports an RSV cotton-rat animal pathogenesis rung. It is useful model evidence, not direct human infant disease proof.
