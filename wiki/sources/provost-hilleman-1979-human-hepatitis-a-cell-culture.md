# Propagation of Human Hepatitis A Virus in Cell Culture in Vitro

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary HAV in vitro propagation paper
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1979
- Authors: Philip J. Provost and Maurice R. Hilleman
- Source basis in hand: full-text EBM PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | In vitro serial propagation, immunofluorescence, serum-blocking, neutralization, immune adherence, radioimmunoassay, immune electron microscopy, and marmoset infectivity study |
| Subject / sample | CR326 human hepatitis A virus passed in *S. mystax* and *S. labiatus* marmosets; primary *S. labiatus* liver explant cultures; FRhK6 fetal rhesus kidney cell line |
| Exposure or intervention | Inoculation and serial passage in liver explant cultures and FRhK6 cells; harvests tested for HAV antigen and infectivity; fifth FRhK6 passage inoculated into *S. labiatus* marmosets |
| Comparator / control | Uninoculated serial cell-culture controls; pre-illness versus convalescent human hepatitis A sera for blocking and neutralization; cell-culture passage controls in marmosets |
| Endpoint | Immunofluorescent cytoplasmic granules, blocked fluorescence, serum neutralization, IA and RIA antigen detection, immune electron microscopy, infectivity titer, marmoset enzyme elevation, marmoset antibody, and marmoset liver antigen |
| Result | HAV was serially propagated through five marmoset liver-culture passages and eight FRhK6 passages. FRhK6 harvests showed stronger antigen and infectivity, reaching about 10^6 to 10^7 infectious units/ml in passage 5 or 6 harvests. Immunofluorescence and infectivity were blocked or neutralized by convalescent human hepatitis A sera but not pre-illness sera. Fifth-passage FRhK6 virus induced hepatitis A endpoints in two inoculated *S. labiatus* marmosets, while control cultures did not. No cytopathic effect was observed. |
| Main limitation | The system used marmoset-adapted CR326 and nonhuman primate cells. It is a laboratory propagation and marmoset infectivity endpoint, not direct human disease reproduction or ordinary human transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that human HAV strain CR326 could be reliably propagated in vitro under the reported culture conditions.
- It can answer that identity was supported by multiple assays: immunofluorescence blocking, neutralization, IA, RIA, IEM, and marmoset inoculation.
- It can answer that HAV propagation was noncytopathic in these cultures.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary fecal-oral transmission in humans.
- It cannot prove that cell-culture-adapted virus retains all properties of wild human HAV.
- It cannot answer clinical disease severity or population transmission.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating cell-culture propagation as if it replaces the human clinical and transmission rungs. This paper is a high-value propagation rung, but not a complete causation ladder by itself.

## Vault conclusion
Provost and Hilleman 1979 supports a reliable HAV cell-culture propagation rung with serologic identity and marmoset infectivity confirmation, while preserving the absence of cytopathic effect and the nonhuman model boundary.
