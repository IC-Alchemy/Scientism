# A Study of New and Old World Monkeys to Determine the Likelihood of a Simian Reservoir of Smallpox

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary host-range and reservoir-boundary paper
- Journal/year: *Bulletin of the World Health Organization*, 1970
- Author: J. Noble, Jr.
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental monkey inoculation, hemagglutination-inhibition serology, and reservoir plausibility study |
| Subject / sample | New World monkeys including *Cebus apella*, *Ateles paniscus*, and *Lagothrix lagothricha*; Old World monkeys including *Macaca irus* and *Cercopithecus aethiops*; 535 wild or captive monkey sera |
| Exposure or intervention | Intranasal, intramuscular, or intraperitoneal exposure to variola major or variola minor strains in selected animals; serologic survey for poxvirus antibody |
| Comparator / control | Species comparisons; pre- and post-exposure sera; wild/captive serologic survey groups |
| Endpoint | Clinical lesions, illness, hemagglutination-inhibition antibody response, survival, and serologic prevalence in monkey populations |
| Result | *Macaca irus* was susceptible to variola under some experimental conditions, but many exposed monkeys developed serology without lesions or no detectable infection. New World monkeys tested against Brazilian variola minor showed no lesions and no HI antibody in the small intranasal set. Of 535 monkey sera, 509 were negative and the remaining low-titer positives were mostly unconfirmed or suspected nonspecific. The paper found no clear support for a wild simian reservoir of smallpox. |
| Main limitation | Numbers were small for several species and exposure conditions. The study was about reservoir plausibility and host range, not human smallpox causation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that tested monkey species differed in susceptibility to variola strains and routes.
- It can answer that the sampled monkey sera did not show strong evidence of widespread poxvirus antibody compatible with a smallpox reservoir.
- It can answer that experimental *Macaca irus* susceptibility did not imply naturally maintained simian smallpox.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove or refute human smallpox causation.
- It cannot rule out every possible nonhuman reservoir from all species and regions.
- It cannot substitute for human transmission-chain evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is overreading a negative reservoir study as a negative human causation study, or overreading experimental monkey infection as proof of a wild simian reservoir. The paper addresses host range and reservoir plausibility.

## Vault conclusion
Noble 1970 supports a smallpox simian-reservoir boundary. It strengthens the smallpox ladder by separating experimental monkey susceptibility from evidence for natural maintenance in monkeys.
