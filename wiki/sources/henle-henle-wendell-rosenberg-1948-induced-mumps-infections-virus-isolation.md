# Isolation of Mumps Virus from Human Beings with Induced Apparent or Inapparent Infections

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary induced human mumps exposure, serology, and saliva-isolation paper
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1948
- Authors: Gertrude Henle, Werner Henle, K. K. Wendell, and P. Rosenberg
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF rendered through Europe PMC/PMC, checked against DOI and PubMed/PMC metadata

> Warning: This page describes a historical human exposure experiment. It is not medical advice and does not assess the experiment under modern research-ethics rules.

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Induced human exposure study with clinical observation, complement-fixation serology, serial saliva sampling, chick-embryo virus isolation, hemagglutination, and isolate identification |
| Subject / sample | Fifteen institutionalized children selected as susceptible because their pre-exposure sera did not react with mumps complement-fixation antigens |
| Exposure or intervention | Fifth-amniotic-passage mumps-virus strains F and B, originally isolated from parotitis saliva, applied either as coarse spray to the oral mucosa near Stensen's duct or as atomized droplets/droplet nuclei inhaled through the mouth |
| Comparator / control | No sham-exposed control group is reported. The internal baseline was pre-exposure seronegativity, ward isolation before exposure, serial clinical observation, and subsequent serologic/virus-isolation follow-up. |
| Endpoint | Clinical parotitis, submaxillary swelling, orchitis, absence of clinical illness, complement-fixing antibody response to soluble and virus antigens, and recovery of mumps virus from saliva by chick-embryo amniotic inoculation |
| Result | Of 15 exposed children, 6 had salivary-gland involvement, 1 had orchitis without salivary-gland involvement, and 8 had no signs of mumps but were classified as inapparent infections by serologic response. Virus was isolated from saliva in all salivary-gland cases beginning 11-15 days after exposure and 2-6 days before clinical signs; from the orchitis case on days 15-16 after exposure; and from 6 of 8 inapparent infections for 1-9 or more days. Overall, virus was isolated from 13 of 15 exposed children. |
| Main limitation | No sham control, small subject count, institutionalized child population, intense experimental exposure, and chick-embryo-passaged virus rather than ordinary community exposure. The authors themselves noted that the data were not quantitatively or statistically definitive. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that exposure to fifth-amniotic-passage mumps virus under the tested spray/atomizer protocols was followed by clinical parotitis or submaxillary swelling in some susceptible children, orchitis in one child, and serologic infection without clinical mumps signs in others.
- It can answer that mumps virus was recoverable from saliva before clinical salivary-gland swelling in the reported cases and also from some inapparent infections.
- It can answer that clinical illness, serologic response, and saliva virus isolation can be separated rather than treated as identical endpoints.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot provide a sham-controlled estimate of clinical causation.
- It cannot prove the dose and route of ordinary community mumps transmission.
- It cannot determine whether saliva or contaminated clothing caused subsequent cottage cases mentioned by the authors.
- It cannot establish population-level transmission dynamics or all possible mumps manifestations.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This paper is stronger than an animal model because it includes induced human exposure with clinical, serologic, and saliva-isolation endpoints. The leap risk is treating it as if it were a modern sham-controlled natural-route transmission study. It is better read as a human induced-exposure rung with no sham control and with unusually intense, laboratory-prepared exposure.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: in Henle and colleagues' 1948 historical experiment, susceptible children exposed to chick-embryo-passaged mumps virus developed a mixture of clinical, orchitic, and inapparent serologic outcomes, and virus was recovered from saliva in most exposed children, often before clinical signs. It strengthens the human exposure and shedding rungs while leaving ordinary community-route causation and sham-controlled clinical causation unresolved.
