# Propagation in Tissue Cultures of Cytopathogenic Agents from Patients with Measles

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary tissue-culture isolation paper for measles-associated cytopathogenic agents
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1954
- Authors: John F. Enders and Thomas C. Peebles
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Tissue-culture isolation, serial passage, cytopathology, neutralization, complement-fixation, filtration, and host-range study |
| Subject / sample | Blood, throat washings, and feces from seven clinically typical measles patients; human and monkey renal cell cultures and other tissue cultures |
| Exposure or intervention | Fresh patient specimens inoculated into roller-tube or stationary tissue cultures; serial passage of culture fluid; neutralization with acute and convalescent sera |
| Comparator / control | Uninoculated culture fluids; cultures from other tissues; unrelated cytopathic agents including a herpes-simplex-like isolate and a monkey-kidney culture agent; acute versus convalescent sera |
| Endpoint | Syncytial giant-cell cytopathic effect, nuclear changes, serial passage, infectivity titer, serum neutralization, complement fixation, filtration, heat stability, mouse/chick-embryo tests |
| Result | Eight virus-like agents were isolated from blood or throat washings of five of seven measles cases. The agents produced syncytial giant-cell changes in human or simian renal cells, could be serially passaged, were inhibited by convalescent measles sera, and generated complement-fixing antigen reacting with convalescent sera. Fecal samples tested in three cases were negative, and mice or chick embryos did not show useful demonstrable infection under the tested conditions. |
| Main limitation | The authors explicitly described the evidence as indirect and stated that two essential experiments remained: producing measles in monkey and man with sufficiently passaged tissue-culture material, followed by recovery of the virus. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that early measles patient blood or throat washings contained agents that could be propagated in human or simian renal-cell cultures with a recognizable cytopathic effect.
- It can answer that convalescent measles sera neutralized cytopathogenicity and reacted with infected-culture antigen in complement-fixation tests.
- It can answer that culture and serology substantially strengthened the association beyond earlier monkey-blood experiments.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove that culture-passaged material produced ordinary measles in humans.
- It cannot settle natural respiratory transmission.
- It cannot treat cytopathic effect alone as specific without controls, because the authors themselves encountered non-measles cytopathic agents.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The paper can be overread as completed causal proof. Its actual conclusion is a strong presumption that the agents represented measles virus, while reserving decisive experimental-host reproduction and recovery as future work.

## Vault conclusion
Enders and Peebles 1954 is the central measles tissue-culture rung in this pass. It supports isolation/propagation and serologic specificity endpoints, while preserving the authors' own caution about uncompleted monkey and human reproduction experiments using culture-passaged material.
