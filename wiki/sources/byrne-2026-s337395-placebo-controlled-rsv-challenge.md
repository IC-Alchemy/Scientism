# Phase 2a Randomized Placebo-Controlled Human Challenge Trial of the Respiratory Syncytial Virus L-Protein Inhibitor S-337395

## Source type
- Role in this vault: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human RSV challenge trial; the infected placebo arm is the sham-controlled exposure-to-disease row for RSV, filling the RSV half of GAP 1 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] alongside the dose-finding companion [[sources/devincenzo-2010-rsv-human-challenge-model-validation]]. It is the RSV analogue of [[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]].
- Journal/year: *Clinical Infectious Diseases*, 2026;83(3):e652-e660 (trial conducted April-October 2024, UK)
- Authors: Stuart Byrne, Takamichi Baba, Nao Kawaguchi, Atsuko Yamamoto, Yoshiyuki Nakano, Caroline Dorrepaal, Brandon Londt, Alex Mann, Nikolay Veselinski, Takeki Uehara
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article (PMC13644856), rendered page text extracted end to end, including Methods, Results, Discussion, and references

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Phase 2a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, single-center proof-of-concept trial inside the validated adult RSV human challenge model (hVIVO quarantine, UK); 114 participants randomized 5:13:13:13:13 to S-337395 1 mg / 10 mg / 30 mg / 300 mg or placebo, treatment started on detection of infection and given once daily for 5 days |
| Subject / sample | 114 healthy adults 18-55 years meeting predefined eligibility criteria; all inoculated on Day 0 with RSV-A Memphis 37b (M37b) at ~4 log10 PFU, a titer predetermined by infectivity assay; 60 (53%) developed laboratory-confirmed infection and formed the intent-to-treat infected (ITT-I) efficacy set; one 30-mg participant discontinued for a family emergency |
| Intervention or exposure | Intranasal inoculation with GMP-purified wild-type-derived RSV-A M37b, then quote: "Participants were randomized 5:13:13:13:13 to receive S-337395 (1, 10, 30, or 300 mg) or placebo, once daily for 5 days upon evidence of infection" (infection detected by twice-daily nasal-wash PCR from Day 2; randomization ~12 h after first positive result); S-337395 and placebo were identical powder-in-bottle preparations reconstituted on site |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes**, as a placebo-drug arm (not a non-inoculated group). Quote: placebo was administered to 26 randomized participants under the same double-blind conditions after the identical challenge exposure. Quote on the exposure: "Healthy adults aged 18-55 years meeting predefined eligibility criteria were inoculated with RSV-A Memphis 37b on Day 0." There is no non-inoculated arm; the placebo group is a randomized infected-disease-incidence group |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: "The primary endpoint was efficacy, determined as the RSV viral load area under the curve (VL-AUC), measured by qRT-PCR of nasal wash samples from initial administration of S-337395 or placebo up to planned discharge (Day 13)." Secondary endpoints: viral load by cell-based viral culture infectivity assay, time to cessation of viral detection by both assays, and total symptom score AUC (TSS-AUC, 13-item diary card, graded 0-3, thrice daily). Safety endpoints: incidence of adverse events (MedDRA-coded) |
| Result | ITT-I n=60. Covariate-adjusted mean VL-AUC by qRT-PCR: 300 mg reduced vs placebo by 88.94% (P<.05) and 30 mg by 64.87% (P<.05); 1 mg and 10 mg not significant; 1-300 mg showed a dose-ordered response. Viral culture VL-AUC: 300 mg -86.17%, 30 mg -72.32% (both P<.05). Median time to cessation of virus detection: 3.0 days (300 mg) vs 9.0 days (placebo) by qRT-PCR, and 1.0 vs 5.0 days by culture. TSS-AUC reduced 78.15% at 300 mg (P<.05); other doses not significant for symptoms. All adverse events mild or moderate; no serious adverse events, deaths, or discontinuations; treatment-emergent AEs 2/26 placebo vs 26/88 active-treated |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors: "the translation of human challenge study results to the clinical setting may have additional limitations, as RSV challenge uses a single RSV-A strain and includes an immunocompetent population"; rapid-treatment commencement "may not be feasible in clinical settings"; and "a small number of planned participants." From this vault's standard: the placebo arm controls the drug, not inoculation; there is no non-inoculated group, so placebo-arm disease rates rest on the within-trial contrast plus the model validation paper; young healthy adults; median time-to-event differences not statistically tested as significant |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue GAP 1 (sham-controlled human challenge with purified inoculum), RSV half: this trial supplies the randomized placebo-controlled element - defined quantified inoculum, blinded placebo arm among infected volunteers, and named virological plus clinical endpoints - replicating for RSV the two-paper structure used for influenza (Memoli 2015 dose-finding + Han 2021 placebo-controlled).
- Ladder rung 7 (controlled human challenge) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]; new rows in [[synthesis/bronchiolitis-rsv-evidence-ladder]].
- Boundary note: exposure was inoculation in quarantine, so GAP 2 (natural-route transmission) is not touched; for influenza that rung is now anchored by [[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]], and no equivalent controlled natural-route RSV trial is in hand.

## What the experiment can answer
- That the defined RSV-A M37b inoculum under quarantine produced laboratory-confirmed infection in 53% of inoculated adults (60/114), with the infected placebo arm showing measurable viral-load courses and symptom scores under blinded randomized conditions.
- That reducing viral replication pharmacologically (30-300 mg S-337395) significantly reduced qRT-PCR and culture viral load and, at 300 mg, total symptom score AUC - the direct causal test of the viral-load/disease association that [[sources/devincenzo-2010-rsv-human-challenge-model-validation]] could only correlate.
- That qRT-PCR-based viral load and culture-based infectivity both responded, with culture-based detection ceasing faster than qRT-PCR-based detection in all arms.

## What it cannot answer
- Natural-route transmission, household attack rates, or community effectiveness; exposure was inoculation in isolation.
- RSV-B disease, at-risk populations (infants, elderly, immunocompromised), or severe lower-respiratory outcomes; by design this was healthy adults with self-limited upper-respiratory infection.
- Clinical efficacy in naturally acquired infection; the authors state translation limits explicitly.
- Long-term or rare safety signals; 114 volunteers, 5-day dosing, mild/moderate events only.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- The leap from "reduced viral load and symptom scores in inoculated young adults" to "supports further development of S-337395 as a potential oral treatment for RSV infection" is a development-stage claim, not evidence of clinical benefit in infants, the elderly, or hospitalized patients - the populations where RSV causes severe disease.
- Challenge-model symptom reduction should not be quoted as natural-infection efficacy; the placebo arm here defines disease incidence under inoculation, not community attack rates.
- Percent reductions (88.94%, etc.) are covariate-adjusted means in n~12-per-arm infected subgroups; small-denominator precision should not be flattened into point claims.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the sham-controlled-human-challenge rung for RSV-A: under randomized double-blind placebo control, the defined Memphis 37b inoculum produced laboratory-confirmed infection in ~53% of inoculated adults, and the infected placebo arm documented viral-load and symptom courses under blinded conditions; active drug reduced both viral load and symptom burden at the top doses. Natural-route transmission for RSV remains untested in this vault; infant bronchiolitis causation remains a separate rung ([[synthesis/bronchiolitis-rsv-evidence-ladder]]).
