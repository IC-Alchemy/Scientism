# Electron Microscope Studies of Rabies Virus in Mouse Brain

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary infected-brain electron-microscopy paper for street-rabies particle morphology
- Journal/year: *Journal of Cell Biology*, 1963
- Author: Seiichi Matsumoto
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Electron-microscopy study of experimentally infected mouse brain |
| Subject / sample | Two- or three-day-old C3H mice infected with two street rabies virus strains |
| Exposure or intervention | Intracerebral inoculation with about 10^5 LD50 of virus; brains examined after typical paralysis at 10-12 days |
| Comparator / control | The paper refers to absence of characteristic particles or matrix in uninfected controls; the main experimental comparison is infected tissue morphology |
| Endpoint | Rod-like particles, matrix structures, nerve-cell and astrocyte localization, particle dimensions, and relation to Negri bodies |
| Result | Characteristic rod-like or elongated particles were found within cytoplasmic matrix in nerve cells and astrocytes. Two particle forms were described: one about 110-120 nm wide with double membranes and another about 120-130 nm wide with a single limiting membrane. Negri bodies appeared as dense round bodies with coarse structures but no virus particles in the observed sections. |
| Main limitation | The study visualizes particles in intracerebrally infected suckling mouse brain. It does not show natural exposure, transmission route, infectious dose, or clinical causation in humans. The biological relation between the two particle forms was reported as unknown. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that intracerebrally infected mouse brain contained characteristic rod-like particles associated with cytoplasmic matrix.
- It can answer that the observed Negri bodies were not identical to accumulations of visible virions in this study.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary rabies transmission.
- It cannot prove the complete virus life cycle alone.
- It cannot substitute for infectivity or route-specific challenge evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if particle morphology in intracerebrally infected mouse brain is treated as direct proof of natural-route disease causation.

## Vault conclusion
Matsumoto 1963 supports a narrow morphology claim: street-rabies-infected mouse brain contained characteristic cytoplasmic particles and matrices, while Negri bodies were distinct structures in the examined sections.
