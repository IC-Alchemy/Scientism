# Vesikari 2006 Pentavalent Rotavirus Vaccine REST RCT

## Source type
- Role in this vault: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled vaccine trial whose endpoints are the disease itself (lab-confirmed rotavirus gastroenteritis, severity-scored, with health-care-use outcomes); fills queue item 9 / GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] and adds an intervention row to [[synthesis/rotavirus-gastroenteritis-evidence-ladder]].
- Trial name: Rotavirus Efficacy and Safety Trial (REST), Merck protocol V260-006; vaccine later licensed as RotaTeq.
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2006;354(1):23-33.
- Authors: Vesikari T, Matson DO, Dennehy P, Van Damme P, Santosham M, Rodriguez Z, Dallas MJ, Heyse JF, Goveia MG, Black SB, Shinefield HR, Christie CDC, Ylitalo S, Itzler RF, Coia ML, Onorato MT, Adeyi BA, Marshall GS, Gothefors L, Campens D, Karvonen A, Watt JP, O'Brien KL, DiNubile MJ, Clark HF, Boslego JW, Offit PA, Heaton PM.
- Source basis in hand: full-text article (HighWire HTML archived by the Wayback Machine, 2009 snapshot of content.nejm.org/cgi/content/full/354/1/23; same snapshot carries the article PDF). Read end to end: Methods, Results, Discussion, References.

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Randomized, double-blind (with sponsor blinding), placebo-controlled trial, 2001-2004, 11 countries; group-sequential enrollment (60,000 minimum) because the prespecified primary hypothesis was a safety one. 70,301 enrolled; 69,274 randomized subjects in the clinical database; 68,038 received at least one dose: 34,035 vaccine, 34,003 placebo |
| Subject / sample | Healthy infants 6-12 weeks old (median age at entry 10 weeks); mostly developed-country populations (Finland, US incl. Navajo Nation and White Mountain Apache Tribe, and other sites) |
| Intervention or exposure | Three 2-ml oral doses, 4-10 weeks apart, of live pentavalent human-bovine (WC3) reassortant rotavirus vaccine (G1, G2, G3, G4, P[8] surface proteins on the WC3 bovine strain), ~6.7x10^7 to 12.4x10^7 infectious units per dose |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes**. Quote: "Infants were randomly assigned, in a 1:1 ratio, to receive three 2-ml oral doses of vaccine or visibly indistinguishable placebo, 4 to 10 weeks apart." Design sentence: "double-blind (with sponsor blinding), placebo-controlled." Dosing year-round; oral polio vaccine excluded in a 42-day window; other routine vaccines and breast-feeding unrestricted and balanced by randomization |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: (a) primary safety — confirmed intussusception within 42 days after any dose (blinded adjudication committee; confirmation "by radiography or at surgery or autopsy"); (b) primary efficacy — wild-type G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis occurring 14 or more days after completion of the three-dose series through the first full rotavirus season (per-protocol efficacy substudy); (c) severe disease — rotavirus gastroenteritis scoring >16 on a 24-point severity scale (fever, vomiting, diarrhea, behavior); (d) health-care use — hospitalization and emergency-department visits for G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis; (e) immunogenicity — serum antirotavirus IgA seroconversion (>=3-fold rise). Case definition: "three or more watery or looser-than-normal stools within a 24-hour period or forceful vomiting, along with the detection of rotavirus by enzyme immunoassay in a stool specimen obtained within 14 days after the onset of symptoms"; G serotyping by RT-PCR sequencing |
| Result | Safety: intussusception within 1 year of dose 1 in 12 vaccine vs 15 placebo recipients (RR 0.8, 95% CI 0.3-1.8); within 42 days of any dose 6 vs 5 (multiplicity-adjusted RR 1.6, 95% CI 0.4-6.4) — prespecified primary safety hypothesis satisfied. Serious adverse events 2.4% vs 2.5%; deaths 24 vs 20 (mostly SIDS; none attributed to vaccination). Vaccine-strain shedding 12.7% (17/134) after dose 1, 0 after doses 2-3. Efficacy substudy per-protocol (2,207 vaccine / 2,305 placebo, 397 G1-G4 cases: 82 vs 315): 74.0% efficacy against G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis of any severity (95% CI 66.8-79.9); 98.0% against severe disease (95% CI 88.3-100; one severe case among vaccine recipients). Second-season follow-up (813 vs 756 evaluable): 62.6% any severity (95% CI 44.3-75.4), 88.0% severe (95% CI 49.4-98.7). Health-care use, per-protocol 28,646 vs 28,488: combined hospitalization/ED visits reduced 94.5% (95% CI 91.2-96.6; 19 vs 329 events), hospitalization alone 95.8% (6 vs 138), ED alone 93.7% (13 vs 191), clinic/office visits 86.0% (95% CI 73.9-92.5). Modified ITT (any time after dose 1, first season): 60.0% (95% CI 51.5-67.1). IgA seroconversion 95.2% vs 14.3%. All-cause gastroenteritis-related hospitalization efficacy 58.9% (95% CI 51.7-65.0) |
| Main limitation | Sponsor (Merck) designed, managed, and analyzed the trial and drafted much of the report; primary efficacy analysis is per-protocol in a nested substudy (4,512 of 5,673 enrolled evaluable); 81-86% completed follow-up windows; mostly healthy infants from developed countries, so the authors themselves state efficacy "in children who are malnourished or infected with multiple enteric pathogens" is unconfirmed; serotype efficacy limited to circulating strains; intussusception is uncommon, so the CI (0.4-6.4) does not exclude a modest relative risk increase and the authors call for continued monitoring |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 9 / GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate): the endpoints are clinically defined, lab-confirmed gastroenteritis and its severity, plus medically attended disease — not antibody titers. Immunogenicity is reported as a secondary/exploratory field.
- Ladder rung 9 (intervention evidence) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] for the rotavirus chain: blocking a G1-G4,P[8] set of reassortant rotaviruses reduced G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis by ~74% and severe disease by ~98% versus placebo.
- Contrast boundary preserved: this is target-disease evidence, not a broader-health-outcomes proof — all-cause mortality was 24 vs 20 (no efficacy analysis reported) and all-cause gastroenteritis-related hospitalization was 58.9%, i.e. most gastroenteritis hospitalizations were not rotavirus-attributable. See [[synthesis/specific-vaccine-overall-health-outcome-evidence]].
- GAP 1-3 (challenge, transmission, cohort) are untouched by this trial.

## What the experiment can answer
- That, under randomized double-blind placebo control, three oral doses of the pentavalent WC3 reassortant vaccine reduced lab-confirmed G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis meeting the prespecified case definition by 74% (per-protocol, first season) and severe scored disease by 98% in the efficacy substudy population.
- That medically attended rotavirus disease (hospitalization, ED visits, clinic visits) fell by 86-95% in the same direction, on large denominators.
- That within this trial's power (groups of ~34,000, adjusted RR 1.6, 95% CI 0.4-6.4), no intussusception signal like RotaShield's was detected, and febrile/vomiting/diarrheal adverse-event rates were similar between arms in the detailed safety substudy.

## What it cannot answer
- Efficacy in malnourished children, low-resource settings at high multiple-enteric-pathogen burden (the authors flag this explicitly; the Gambia-style setting is tested by a different vaccine, [[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]]).
- Long-term protection beyond two seasons; efficacy against serotypes not circulating 2001-2004 (only a few G9 cases).
- A population-level mortality claim: rotavirus deaths were not an endpoint in this developed-country cohort.
- Natural transmission dynamics: vaccination reduces disease in vaccinated infants; indirect (herd) effects are not measured here.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- The authors close with a global-morbidity/mortality framing ("Widespread administration... could substantially reduce the morbidity and mortality associated with this global childhood disease"), but this trial measured no rotavirus deaths; the mortality inference rests on external burden studies, not on this experiment.
- "Not all rotavirus vaccines are associated with intussusception" is a safety-criterion-satisfied statement, not a zero-risk proof: the 42-day adjusted RR interval (0.4-6.4) is compatible with a small increase, and the authors themselves order continued monitoring.
- The 94.5% health-care-use reduction applies to G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis specifically; quoting it as a reduction in "hospitalizations" tout court would erase the disease-specific denominator (all-cause gastroenteritis hospitalization reduction was 58.9%).

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the intervention rung for rotavirus: a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in ~68,000 infants found that the pentavalent human-bovine reassortant vaccine reduced lab-confirmed G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis (74% any severity, 98% severe, per-protocol first season) and its medical-care use, with no detected intussusception excess within the trial's prespecified criteria. Broader all-cause health outcomes and developing-setting efficacy are separate, unproven rungs for this vaccine.
