# Replication and Shedding of MERS-CoV in Upper Respiratory Tract of Inoculated Dromedary Camels

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary reservoir/transmission-ecology paper for MERS-CoV replication and shedding in experimentally inoculated dromedary camels
- Journal/year: *Emerging Infectious Diseases*, 2014
- Authors: D. R. Adney, J. van Doremalen, V. R. Brown, T. Bushmaker, M. G. Scott, E. de Wit, V. J. Munster, and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental dromedary-camel inoculation with clinical monitoring, serial swabs, infectious-virus titration, qPCR, necropsy, histopathology, immunohistochemistry, and neutralizing-antibody measurement |
| Subject / sample | Three native-born adult male dromedary camels aged 2, 3, and 5 years, seronegative for MERS-CoV neutralization and bovine coronavirus ELISA before inoculation |
| Exposure or intervention | 10^7 TCID50 HCoV-EMC/2012 in 15 ml total, administered intratracheally, intranasally to both nostrils, and conjunctivally |
| Comparator / control | Pre-inoculation seronegativity and serial within-animal sampling; no sham-inoculated camel group |
| Endpoint | Clinical signs, nasal/oral/fecal/urine/blood/exhaled-breath viral RNA and infectious virus, tissue infectious virus, histopathology, viral antigen localization, and neutralizing-antibody response |
| Result | All three camels developed mild rhinorrhea and mild temperature elevations without severe disease. Shedding began on days 1-2; infectious virus was found in nasal swabs for less than 7 days and viral RNA persisted longer. No viral RNA was detected in feces or urine, and no infectious virus or RNA was detected in serum or whole blood. In the day-5 necropsied camel, infectious virus was found in upper-respiratory tissues, trachea, one lung lobe, and lymph nodes. Lesions and viral antigen were concentrated in respiratory epithelium, not alveoli. The later-necropsied camels seroconverted by day 14. |
| Main limitation | The study used only three male camels, a human isolate, and high-dose simultaneous intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival exposure; it did not test route-specific efficiency, age effects, milk/meat transmission, or direct human disease causation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that dromedary camels can support upper-respiratory MERS-CoV replication and nasal shedding after high-dose combined-route inoculation.
- It can answer that the tested camels developed only mild clinical disease while shedding infectious virus from the upper respiratory tract.
- It can answer that the tested feces, urine, serum, and whole-blood samples did not show the same viral signal under the reported methods.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that camels transmitted MERS-CoV to humans in ordinary settings.
- It cannot answer which single exposure route is sufficient or most efficient.
- It cannot prove human clinical MERS causation, severe disease, or human-to-human transmission.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using a camel reservoir/shedding model as if it were a human clinical-causation experiment. This paper belongs on the transmission-ecology rung, not the human disease-sufficiency rung.

## Vault conclusion
Adney et al. 2014 supports a primary dromedary-camel replication and shedding rung for MERS-CoV. It strengthens the plausibility of camel reservoir ecology but does not by itself prove human clinical MERS causation or ordinary camel-to-human transmission.
