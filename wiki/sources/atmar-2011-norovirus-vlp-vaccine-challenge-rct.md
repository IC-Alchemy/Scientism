# Atmar 2011 - Norwalk VLP vaccine against experimental human Norwalk illness (randomized placebo-controlled vaccine-under-challenge trial)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 22, queue item 42) from the complete NIH author manuscript HTML at PMC3761795 (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3761795/, 200 OK, "Published in final edited form as: N Engl J Med. 2011 Dec 8;365(23):2178-2187"; the live nejm.org site is Cloudflare-walled per prior runs; the PMC PDF/OA routes 404'd). Citation reconciliation: the queue draft listed a 12-author set ending "Borate J"; PubMed and the PMC record give **PMID 22150036, N Engl J Med 2011;365(23):2178-2187, December 8, 2011**, authors Atmar RL, Bernstein DI, Harro CD, Al-Ibrahim MS, Chen WH, Ferreira J, Estes MK, Graham DY, Opekun AR, **Richardson C (LigoCyte Pharmaceuticals), Mendelman PM (LigoCyte Pharmaceuticals)**. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00973284. Sponsored by LigoCyte Pharmaceuticals (vaccine sponsor; designed by the academic authors in collaboration with sponsor employees; two sponsor employees are authors and a LigoCyte scientist reviewed a draft manuscript) and by NIH grant M01 RR-000188 (Baylor GCRC); data collected through EMMES; the study protocol was said to be available with the full text at NEJM.org. Every number below is from the article itself, not from any summary.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter vaccine-challenge trial at four clinical sites (Baylor, Cincinnati Children's, Johns Hopkins, SNBL Clinical Pharmacology Center Baltimore, University of Maryland); enrollment September 2009 - January 2010; the trial ran in two stages - a vaccination stage and a separate Norwalk virus challenge stage.
- **Subjects:** healthy men and women 18-50 years who were **positive for a functional FUT2 gene**, determined phenotypically by identification of histo-blood group antigens in saliva - i.e., the trial enrolled only people genetically capable of Norwalk infection. Of 454 persons screened, 98 were enrolled and randomized 1:1 stratified by site (50 vaccine / 48 placebo); mean age 32.1 years, 41% women, 65% blood type O. 90 received both doses; 84 went on to the challenge (4 lost to follow-up, 1 withdrew, 1 became pregnant) and constitute the intention-to-treat efficacy population. 7 further participants were removed from the per-protocol set: 5 for malfunction of at least one of the four dose-delivery devices, 1 for infection with a **different norovirus (GII.4)** during the challenge period, and 1 for "excessive eating followed by vomiting."
- **Intervention:** two intranasal doses 3 weeks apart of a lyophilized Norwalk virus (GI.1) viruslike-particle vaccine - 100 ug VLP per dose (baculovirus expression system), adjuvanted with monophosphoryl lipid A (GlaxoSmithKline) plus chitosan (ChiSys, Archimedes Development) as mucoadhesive, with sucrose/mannitol excipients; each dose delivered by two Bespak UniDose devices, one per nostril, 50 ug each.
- **Comparator/control:** sham arm YES, but **the placebo did not contain the adjuvants**. Quote: "The placebo contained only the sucrose and mannitol excipients." The authors flag this themselves as limitation #1: "the placebo did not include the monophosphoryl lipid A and chitosan adjuvants, raising the possibility that the observed protection was due to nonspecific effects" - recorded as the comparator-class entry in [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]].
- **Challenge:** participants completing the 3-week post-dose-2 visit were eligible; admitted to an inpatient challenge facility day 0/-1; oral inoculation with **48 RT-PCR units of Norwalk virus ("approximately 10 times the amount of inoculum required to infect 50% of persons")**; assessment for gastroenteritis signs/symptoms at least twice daily until discharge, minimum stay 96 hours; stool samples collected for infection endpoints. 82 of 84 began the challenge 3 weeks after vaccination (1 vaccine and 1 placebo participant began 19 and 9 weeks after - timing heterogeneity recorded).
- **Endpoint (named exactly):** primary end point = "viral gastroenteritis caused by the Norwalk virus"; "Definitions of infection and illness are provided in the Supplementary Appendix" - i.e., the operational case definition itself is not in the article body (recorded). A committee "that was unaware of the study-group assignments determined the dates of onset and resolution of acute gastroenteritis by reviewing the participants' clinical signs and symptoms." Severity was graded on a **modified Vesikari scale 0-17** (the scale built for childhood rotavirus). Infection endpoints: fecal Norwalk virus by real-time RT-PCR, viral antigen by sandwich ELISA, and seroconversion, individually and combined ("positive on any assay").
- **Power:** ~40 participants per group for ~85% power, assuming a 40% placebo gastroenteritis rate and 70% vaccine efficacy; Fisher's exact test for event rates; two-sided P values.

## Result

- **Primary endpoint - Norwalk gastroenteritis, per-protocol (38 vaccine / 39 placebo):** 37% vs 69%, absolute reduction 32 percentage points (P=0.006), relative reduction 47% (per-protocol CI 15-67). Intention-to-treat (43/41): 40% vs 71%, relative reduction 44% (95% CI 15.0-63.2). "Vaccine efficacy was similar in the per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses."
- **Infection endpoints:** any-assay infection 61% vs 82% (relative reduction 26%, P=0.05); RT-PCR 61% vs 82%; seroconversion 61% vs 82%; fecal antigen ELISA 53% vs 59% (relative reduction 11%, CI -33 to 40 - i.e., the antigen-detection endpoint shows no significant reduction).
- **Severity and timing:** mean Vesikari score all participants 3.6 vs 5.5 (P=0.009, 35% relative reduction); among infected only, 5.0 vs 6.1 (P=0.14); among those with gastroenteritis, 6.4 vs 6.7 (P=0.72 - "no significant reduction in disease severity... most likely owing to the small numbers"). Illness onset delayed 2.1 h (P=0.12 per-protocol) and 4.3 h (P=0.02 ITT); no significant reduction in duration of illness among the infected.
- **Immunogenicity:** serum IgA seroresponse (>=4x rise) in 70% of vaccinees after two doses (total-antibody ELISA also 70%); IgA GMT increase factor 7.5; histo-blood group antigen-blocking antibody seroresponse only 32%. Among infected participants, antibody rises after challenge exceeded rises after vaccination (e.g., IgA GMT increase 61.9 in infected placebo vs 11.6 in infected vaccine).
- **Post hoc correlate:** reading reverse cumulative-distribution curves, the authors selected a prechallenge HBGA-blocking titer >=200 and report >50% relative reduction in both illness (18% vs 63%, OR 8.1, CI 2.1-31.2) and infection (35% vs 82%, OR 8.2, CI 2.5-26.9) in per-protocol participants - explicitly "post hoc," recorded as such.
- **Safety:** local symptoms similar after dose 1, more frequent in vaccinees after dose 2; no severe nasal symptoms; no vaccine-related severe adverse events; two SAEs (both placebo arm: appendicitis at 76 days, psychosis at 42 days post inoculation), both judged unrelated. One placebo participant vomited 19 minutes after inoculation - that participant was infected with GII.4, not Norwalk, and had no Norwalk shedding.
- **Blinding conduct:** the adjudication committee was blinded; participants and staff received the same double-blind schedule; no quantitative blinding check is reported (recorded).

## Main limitation (as recorded by the trial itself)

- **Unmatched placebo:** the placebo lacked monophosphoryl lipid A and chitosan - the authors' own first limitation, with their counterargument (nonspecific TLR4-agonist protection lasts <1 week in animals; intestinal TLR4 downregulation; protection correlates with virus-specific antibodies). The experiment itself cannot separate virus-specific from adjuvant/nonspecific effects (recorded as an unresolved boundary in this vault).
- **Device failures:** the Bespak delivery device "malfunctioned several times, adversely affecting vaccine delivery"; 5 of 7 per-protocol exclusions are device-related.
- **Enriched, maximally-susceptible population:** the design section states the trial "was designed to maximize the likelihood of identifying a vaccine effect" - FUT2-functional adults, blood groups O/A, homologous GI.1 challenge, moderate dose "so as not to overwhelm" vaccine immunity, and challenge shortly after vaccination. Young children, the elderly, natural exposure, and heterologous strains are all untested (authors' own limitations).
- **Duration and breadth:** natural-infection immunity is short (<2 years); vaccine-protection duration undetermined; GI/GII diversity means "a multivalent vaccine will most likely be required."
- **Endpoint definition not in the article body** (supplementary appendix), and the antibody-titer correlate analysis is post hoc.

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

- **"This study shows that it may be possible to use a vaccination strategy to prevent norovirus disease"** and the closing summary of "homologous protection" - the experiment measured protection against one homologous GI.1 strain at one dose in one enriched adult population under a 96-hour observed window; the generalization to norovirus disease broadly is a proof-of-concept extension.
- **"Vaccine efficacy must also be determined in a natural setting"** - the authors say this themselves, which is the boundary line: nothing here measures field efficacy, transmission, herd effects, or non-Norwalk genotypes.
- The HBGA-blocking >=200 correlate, presented with odds ratios and confidence intervals, is derived post hoc from the same subjects it is applied to - it is hypothesis-generating within this trial, not a validated correlate.
- The Discussion's epidemiologic framing ("21 million cases... in the United States", "218,000 deaths... among children in developing countries") cites reviews and modeling, not experiments in this trial - kept out of the experimental claim set per the vault standard.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate), first row for norovirus - and structurally different from every other GAP 4 row in the corpus:** the exposure itself is a controlled human challenge (inpatient, inoculum quantified in RT-PCR units, blinded placebo arm also challenged), so both arms face an identical measured exposure. This is the norovirus counterpart of the Han 2021 placebo-controlled influenza challenge ([[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]]) and the Byrne 2026 placebo-controlled RSV challenge ([[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]]) - GAP 1's sham-controlled design combined with GAP 4's disease endpoint.
- The norovirus ladder ([[synthesis/norovirus-gastroenteritis-outbreaks-evidence-ladder]]) gains its first intervention rung: the chain from Norwalk particle visualization (Kapikian 1972) through controlled adult challenge/shedding (Atmar 2008), outbreak investigation (Arness 2000), and enteroid replication (Ettayebi 2016) now ends in a controlled human trial where blocking the agent reduced a clinical gastroenteritis endpoint and virologically confirmed infection - consistent with the infectious-agent hypothesis for Norwalk gastroenteritis without filling a natural-transmission rung (which stays open for norovirus in this vault).
- Chain boundary: this is efficacy against homologous challenge illness in a susceptible enriched population. It does not measure natural exposure, infection transmission, disease in FUT2-negative or non-O/A hosts, heterologous (GII.4) protection, or duration.
