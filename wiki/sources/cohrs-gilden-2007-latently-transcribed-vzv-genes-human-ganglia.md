# Prevalence and Abundance of Latently Transcribed Varicella-Zoster Virus Genes in Human Ganglia

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular latency paper for VZV gene transcription in human ganglia
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 2007
- Authors: R. J. Cohrs and D. H. Gilden
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Molecular latency study using real-time PCR |
| Subject / sample | 28 trigeminal ganglia from 14 humans |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural latent VZV context in postmortem tissue |
| Comparator / control | Transcript abundance across VZV genes |
| Endpoint | VZV RNA prevalence and copy number |
| Result | Gene 63 transcripts were most prevalent and abundant; gene 21 was not detected. |
| Main limitation | Postmortem tissue; transcript detection is not direct proof of infectious reactivation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that selected VZV genes are transcribed in latently infected human ganglia.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define the complete latency program or reactivation trigger.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating latent transcript detection as complete reactivation mechanism.

## Vault conclusion
Cohrs and Gilden 2007 supports a VZV latent-transcription rung.
