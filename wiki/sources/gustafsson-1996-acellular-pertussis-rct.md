# Gustafsson 1996 - Two- and five-component acellular vs whole-cell vs DT pertussis trial (Sweden)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 21, queue item 40) from the complete 7-page publisher PDF of NEJM 1996;334(6):349-355, downloaded from the Internet Archive capture of 2022-10-19 (nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJM199602083340602, 136 KB, PDF v1.3) and read end to end via pdftotext. Queue-draft DOI corrected from ...3340601 (a different article in the same issue) to **...3340602**; PMID 8538705. Authors: Gustafsson L (Sachs' Children's Hospital), Hallander HO, Olin P, Reizenstein E, Storsaeter J (Swedish Institute for Infectious Disease Control); funded by NIAID contract N01-AI-15125; approved by the Karolinska Institute ethics committee. Every number below is from that PDF.

## What experiment was run

- **Study type:** randomized, double-blind, four-arm infant efficacy trial in Sweden, where no pertussis vaccine had been in the vaccination program since 1979 - which is why a control vaccine with no pertussis component was "ethical" (the paper's own reasoning, quoted: "Since a diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis (DTP) vaccine was not routinely administered in Sweden, the inclusion of a control vaccine consisting of diphtheria and tetanus toxoids (DT) alone was ethical").
- **Subjects:** parents of all infants born 1992 in defined catchment areas were invited (24,336 eligible); 14,507 did not participate (declined 34.4%, not reached 16.9%, non-Swedish-speaking 3.6%, other 2.9%, medical contraindications 1.1%, moving 0.7%); 9,829 infants (40.4% of eligible) were randomized at the first injection. Arms balanced on study site (eight areas, Umea to Stockholm), sex (~48.5% female), age at first dose, household size, unprotected elder siblings, and care arrangements (Table 1).
- **Intervention (four arms, all aluminum-salt adsorbed, 0.5-ml doses, same batch per arm):**
  - Two-component acellular DTP, SmithKline Beecham, Rixensart (2,566): 25 ug glutaraldehyde/formalin-inactivated pertussis toxoid + 25 ug formalin-treated filamentous hemagglutinin + 25 Lf diphtheria + 10 Lf tetanus toxoid.
  - Five-component acellular DTP, Connaught, Toronto (2,587): 10 ug glutaraldehyde-inactivated pertussis toxin + 5 ug FHA + 5 ug fimbriae 2 and 3 + 3 ug pertactin (69-kd outer-membrane protein) + 15 Lf diphtheria + 5 Lf tetanus.
  - Whole-cell DTP licensed in the United States, Connaught, Swiftwater (2,102): 5.7 protective units pertussis + 6.65 Lf diphtheria + 5 Lf tetanus.
  - DT control, Swedish National Bacteriological Laboratory (2,574): 15 Lf diphtheria + 3.75 Lf tetanus, no pertussis component.
- **Comparator/control:** a pertussis-target sham arm YES - the DT control contains no pertussis antigen, so it is a placebo for pertussis efficacy while remaining an active (non-inert) vaccine; quote: "or (as a control) a vaccine containing diphtheria and tetanus toxoids (DT) alone." Because of whole-cell supply logistics, only the two acellular vaccines and DT were given during the first two months of the trial, so whole-cell vs DT efficacy uses the four-arm portion only.
- **Randomization/blinding:** identical vials labeled with unique computer-generated randomization numbers; 12-unit blocks for the three groups randomized during the first two months, 16-unit blocks thereafter; block sizes withheld from investigators; blinding held until data analysis began June 1995. Blinding check: 14 days after dose 3 nurses "could identify 53.5 percent of the recipients of whole-cell vaccine but could not distinguish between recipients of the acellular vaccines and the DT vaccine"; partial unblinding of the whole-cell arm had been "raised early in the trial" because whole-cell required vigorous shaking to resuspend.
- **Dosing and follow-up:** three intramuscular doses at 2 months (56-92 days), then 8-week intervals; deferral rules for fever >=38.0 C or recent other vaccines; nurses enrolled ~250 infants each and contacted every household every 6-8 weeks; parents called the nurse for cough >7 days or suspected household whooping cough; on suspicion the nurse visited, took standardized clinical data, and took nasopharyngeal aspirates from ALL household members suspected of whooping cough, plus paired sera (second at 6-8 weeks, second aspirate at 1 week if culture negative with persisting symptoms).
- **Laboratory:** aspirates cultured on charcoal medium with 40 mg/L cephalexin (Regan-Lowe), slide agglutination for B. pertussis and B. parapertussis, biochemical verification and serotyping at the Swedish Institute for Infectious Disease Control, PCR for colonies that could not be subcultured or agglutinated; serology: standardized ELISA with US reference sera, IgA and IgG against pertussis toxin and FHA, "a substantial serologic response" = >=100% rise in IgG or IgA; immunogenicity substudy with paired sera from 689 children (prevaccination, 1 month post-dose-3).
- **Endpoint (named exactly) - the primary case definition (WHO 1991 criteria):** "at least 21 consecutive days of paroxysmal cough plus one of the following: culture-confirmed B. pertussis, an increase of 100 percent or more in IgG or IgA antibodies against pertussis toxin, an increase of 100 percent or more in IgG or IgA antibodies against filamentous hemagglutinin (in the absence of positive results for B. parapertussis on culture or PCR), or documented contact with an infected household member with culture-confirmed B. pertussis who began to cough within 28 days before or after the onset of cough in the study child." Secondary definitions used laboratory confirmation (culture, serology, or PCR) with cough >=1, >=7, >=21, or >=30 days and paroxysmal cough >=14 or >=21 days. Efficacy from Cox proportional-hazards hazard ratios vs DT; assumed true efficacy 80%, null hypothesis 70% or less.

## Result

- **Primary definition, during a mean 21-23.5 months after dose 3:** 737 cases met the primary case definition. Three-dose cohorts (2-comp / 5-comp / whole-cell / DT): 159 / 59 / 148 / 371 cases; incidence 0.032 / 0.012 / 0.040 / 0.078 per person-year; vaccine efficacy vs DT 58.9% (95% CI 50.9-65.9), 85.2% (80.6-88.8), 48.3% (37.0-57.6). Intention-to-treat (>=1 dose): 165 / 65 / 159 / 385; 58.8% (50.5-65.7), 84.3% (79.6-88.0), 48.3% (37.3-57.3).
- **Relative to whole-cell (four-arm portion only):** five-component relative risk 0.29 (0.21-0.40); two-component 0.83 (0.65-1.05) - i.e., the two-component acellular vaccine did not significantly outperform the US whole-cell vaccine on the primary definition.
- **Secondary definition (laboratory-confirmed pertussis with >=1 day of cough):** five-component 77.9% (72.6-82.2), two-component 42.3% (32.6-50.6), whole-cell 41.2% (29.7-50.9). Figure 2: whole-cell efficacy declined across the two years after dose 3 while five-component stayed sustained.
- **Confirmation mix and ascertainment bias control:** culture-confirmed 56% / 42% / 59% / 73% (2-comp / 5-comp / whole-cell / DT); PT-serology-confirmed 29% / 37% / 36% / 24%; FHA-serology-confirmed 13% / 12% / 5% / 2%; contact-only 2% / 9% / 0% / 1%. The authors explain the pattern - vaccinees seroconvert on exposure so culture is relatively less often positive - and note they used pre-episode sera (drawn at 1-2 years) precisely so "failure to demonstrate a serologic response was expected mainly in vaccine recipients"; FHA-only-confirmed potential false positives were evenly distributed between groups, and excluding them only marginally changed the estimates.
- **Adverse events - whole-cell arm sharply more reactogenic, acellular arms matched DT:** protracted crying >=3 h in 23 whole-cell recipients vs 0-4 in each other arm (1.1% vs 0-0.2%, P<0.001); rectal temp >=40.0 C within one day in 28 whole-cell (1.3%) vs 2-7 in the others (0.1-0.4%); generalized cyanosis after dose 1 in 4 whole-cell (P=0.040 vs acellular); pronounced local reactions plus general symptoms contraindicating further doses 13 whole-cell vs 1 five-component (P<0.001). Five hypotonic, hyporesponsive episodes: 4 whole-cell (4 after dose 1) and 1 five-component (dose 3). Twenty-four convulsions (5 two-comp / 7 five-comp / 3 whole-cell / 9 DT), 5 within 48 h of a dose (2 two-comp, 1 whole-cell, 2 DT). Forty-eight serious adverse events within 60 days, "similar between groups (P=0.66)"; 16 other serious events 4-61 days post dose; 163 events contraindicating further doses. Four deaths judged unrelated to vaccination: 2 SIDS (1 day after dose 1 in whole-cell; 27 days after dose 1 in two-component), 1 progressive convulsions with renal and liver failure, 1 bronchiolitis (the last two at 7 and 14 months). One death followed the third dose of DT by one day (not included in the four: an SIDS death in a DT recipient is listed separately in the sentence "One child died of sudden infant death syndrome 1 day after the first dose of the whole-cell vaccine, and another child died of this syndrome 27 days after the first dose of the two-component vaccine... Two deaths... occurred 7 and 14 months, respectively, after the third dose of the DT vaccine" - recorded as printed).
- **Immunogenicity (n=689):** both acellular vaccines induced high anti-PT IgG; the two-component vaccine induced HIGHER anti-PT than the five-component (P<0.001) and higher anti-FHA as well; whole-cell anti-PT was "very low"; five-component induced the higher fimbriae and pertactin responses. The efficacy ordering is the reverse of the anti-PT/FHA ordering - the paper's own numbers show antibody level did not predict protection (five-component lower titers, higher efficacy; Discussion concedes the relative antigen contribution "remains to be shown").
- **Completion:** 199 children did not finish the primary series (culture-confirmed pertussis before dose 3 in 47, withdrawal 40, contraindicating events 11/18/67/16 by arm, overall P<0.001 - the whole-cell arm's 67 is itself reactogenicity); later losses: 205 moved (2.1%), 116 other (1.2%).

## Main limitation (as recorded by the trial itself)

- 59.6% of eligible infants did not participate (mostly parental declination) - a self-selected 40.4%; no external generalization check is possible from this trial.
- The whole-cell arm existed only in the four-arm portion of the trial, and the authors record that whole-cell efficacy "may even have been overestimated because of partial unblinding" (their citation to the three-year follow-up report) - with the whole-cell arm's resuspension behavior breaking blinding, and nurse guessing at 53.5% for whole-cell.
- Two years of follow-up after dose 3, no booster dose administered ("the efficacy of the whole-cell vaccine declined... might have been avoided if a fourth dose had been given at 18 months").
- Serologic confirmation is differential by design between vaccinees and controls; the pre-episode-serum and FHA-distribution controls address it, but the confirmatory mix itself differs by arm (culture 42-59% vaccine vs 73% DT).

## Inferential leaps beyond the experiment

- **Transmission/control leap:** "Improved protection against infection may diminish the spread of the disease, which in turn should improve the control of pertussis" - the trial never measures infection, shedding, or any indirect (household or community) effect; a household contact clause in the endpoint definition uses contacts diagnostically and says nothing about vaccinated children protecting others.
- **Policy leap:** "economic and public health advantages that would lead many countries to switch from whole-cell to acellular vaccines" - a national-program recommendation from one country's epidemic-era trial of two products.
- **Cross-trial generalization:** "The data from this and three other randomized, placebo-controlled trials suggest that multicomponent vaccines are more protective against both typical pertussis and mild disease than one-component and two-component vaccines" - a claim built by pooling separate trials with different vaccines, countries, and epidemic conditions, inside a paper whose own two-component arm did not significantly beat whole-cell on the primary definition.
- **Scheduling leap:** the "optimal vaccination schedule... two primary doses at 2 to 6 months of age, followed by a booster at 12 to 18 months" proposal is reasoned from historical comparisons across trials (including the earlier Swedish one-component trial), not tested in this experiment.
- The authors' own boundary kept: "The relative contribution of each antigen remains to be shown" - printed immediately after the multicomponent superiority sentence.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint), second pertussis row - platform replication.** The GAP 4 pertussis rung now holds the whole-cell MRC 1951 field trials ([[sources/mrc-1951-whooping-cough-vaccine-field-trials]], three killed-organism doses, 78% reduction vs a killed-bacterial sham) and this acellular-era four-arm trial (85.2% five-component / 58.9% two-component vs a DT no-pertussis control under the WHO case definition) - two platforms, two manufacturers' generations, ~45 years apart, both disease endpoints.
- Negative/boundary data carried: whole-cell efficacy of only 48.3% in a modern trial of a US-licensed product, with declining efficacy over two years and the trial's own reactogenicity contrasts; the antibody-titer inversion (higher titer, lower efficacy) recorded as an in-trial observation.
- Chain boundary: this trial measures protection of inoculated infants against defined cough illness; it does not establish the pertussis causation chain (isolation, natural-route transmission rungs remain absent in this vault) and cannot measure indirect protection.
