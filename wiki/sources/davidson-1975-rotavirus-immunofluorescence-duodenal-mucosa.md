# Immunofluorescence in Duodenal Mucosa of Children with Acute Enteritis Due to a New Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early primary tissue-localization and immune-response paper for rotavirus-associated pediatric acute enteritis
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Pathology*, 1975
- Authors: G. P. Davidson and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Clinical tissue-antigen and serology association study |
| Subject / sample | Children with acute non-bacterial enteritis; duodenal mucosa and sera |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural acute enteritis |
| Comparator / control | Recovery tissue or other patient specimens as reported in the paper |
| Endpoint | Viral antigen localization by immunofluorescence, electron microscopy, and early IgM response |
| Result | Viral antigen localized in villus epithelial cytoplasm; early IgM was detected; particles from different patients shared antigenic reactivity. |
| Main limitation | Small early series; tissue association and immune response do not quantify population burden or prove all gastroenteritis cases are rotavirus disease. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a shared viral antigen was present in diseased intestinal mucosa in the tested children.
- It can answer that the antigen localized to villus epithelial cytoplasm under the reported immunofluorescence method.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot establish general incidence, full causal sufficiency, or all severity patterns.
- It cannot replace human challenge, culture, or broader epidemiologic evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is turning early tissue association into broad disease-burden or policy claims without preserving the limited sample and endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
Davidson et al. 1975 supports a rotavirus tissue-antigen localization rung for pediatric acute enteritis. It should be used as mucosal association evidence, not standalone causal closure.
