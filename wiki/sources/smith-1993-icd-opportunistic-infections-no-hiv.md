# Unexplained opportunistic infections and CD4+ T-lymphocytopenia without HIV (Smith et al. 1993)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: exclusivity-boundary paper — a syndrome with AIDS-defining-type illnesses occurring without detectable HIV, investigated for transmissibility
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 1993
- Authors: Smith, Neal, Holmberg (CDC Idiopathic CD4+ T-lymphocytopenia Task Force)
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | National case-finding investigation: review of the CDC AIDS Reporting System (230,179 cases), then interviews, medical-record review, and laboratory analyses |
| Subject / sample | 47 patients meeting the ICD case definition (CD4 < 300 cells/mm³ or < 20% of T cells on two occasions with no HIV-test evidence); 31 patients and 23 contacts interviewed; blood tested from 28 patients |
| Intervention or exposure | None (investigation); HIV testing per case definition |
| Comparator / control | Sexual contacts, household contacts, children, and blood donors of patients as transmission checks |
| Endpoint | Clinical status (AIDS-defining vs non-AIDS-defining conditions), CD4/CD8 counts, transmission markers in contacts and donors |
| Result | Patients: 29 male, 18 female; mean age 43. Thirty-eight percent had HIV risk factors (seven hemophilia, six homosexual sex, six transfusions, two at-risk partners); 62% had none. Forty percent had AIDS-defining illnesses (18 with opportunistic infections); 53% had non-AIDS-defining conditions; 6% asymptomatic. Of 28 patients with blood tested, 29% had CD4 < 300. All tested contacts (10 sex partners, 3 household contacts, 4 children) and 6 blood donors were immunologically and clinically normal. The investigation concluded the disorder is rare, represents various states, and found no evidence of a new transmissible agent causing lymphocytopenia. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; case definition depends on HIV-test performance of 1993 assays; small tested subset of identified cases; "no transmissible agent" is absence-of-evidence from contact investigation, not a challenge experiment. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That opportunistic-infection/CD4-depletion syndromes occurred in people without HIV-test evidence, including without risk factors — an exclusivity boundary showing the syndrome constellation is not definitionally equivalent to HIV infection.
- That contact investigation found no transmission pattern for this HIV-negative syndrome.

## What full text would still need to establish
- The exact testing algorithms; case-level clinical detail; follow-up of the cohort.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap is converting "AIDS-defining illness without HIV exists" into "HIV is irrelevant to AIDS." Within the vault's framing this is an exclusivity boundary: a distinct, rare, apparently non-transmissible syndrome. It does not weaken the transmission/progression evidence in the HIV-positive cohorts ([[sources/darby-1995-mortality-before-and-after-hiv-infection-in-uk-haemophiliacs]]), and its non-transmissibility contrasts with HIV's documented transmission chains.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: 47 investigated ICD cases had opportunistic-type illnesses without HIV-test evidence, no identified transmissible agent, and normal contacts — the syndrome exists as an exclusivity boundary, rare and distinct.
