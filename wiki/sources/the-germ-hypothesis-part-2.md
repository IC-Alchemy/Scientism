# The Germ Hypothesis Part 2

## TL;DR
This raw source is a revisionist essay by Mike Stone arguing that Koch is often credited with proving more than his experiments justified. It is useful because it names concrete experimental disputes, but it remains a Tier 3 adversarial interpretation rather than a settled historical account.

## Source type and scope
- Modern commentary and critique.
- Focused on Koch, anthrax, tuberculosis, cholera, and the standards later associated with Koch's postulates.
- Builds an argument by juxtaposing historical quotations and later criticism.

## What the source claims
- Stone argues that Koch did not conclusively prove the broad germ-theory claims later attached to his work.
- The essay claims that key anthrax, tuberculosis, and cholera episodes involved inferential overreach or later relaxation of standards.
- It also argues that defenders of germ theory often blur the difference between an observed association, an artificial challenge model, and a general causal doctrine.

## What experiment or observation is involved
- The page discusses historical experimental work on anthrax, tuberculosis, and cholera.
- It also treats Koch's postulates as a claimed standard of proof and asks when later practice did or did not satisfy that standard.
- The essay uses ingestion, inoculation, culture, and animal-model disputes as pressure points.

## What question the source is capable of answering
- It can help identify where critics think the historical burden of proof is weakest.
- It can support the narrower claim that retrospective celebrations of Koch often compress distinct methodological issues into a single success story.
- It cannot, on its own, establish that germ theory as a whole was disproved.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This is one of the more useful Tier 3 critique pages because it points to specific experiments and standards rather than only rhetoric.
- Its factual claims still need source-by-source checking against primary papers and careful history.
- Within the vault, it supports an unresolved question: which parts of the later Koch narrative are direct experimental results, and which parts are broader historical interpretation.
