# Study of 1-Adamantanamine Hydrochloride Used Prophylactically During the Hong Kong Influenza Epidemic in the Family Environment

## Source type
- Role in this vault: the null half of the influenza GAP 4 rung's antiviral-platform pair - a controlled household prophylaxis trial with clinical-disease and serologic endpoints that found no protective effect as randomized, 31 years before the positive zanamivir trial ([[sources/hayden-2000-zanamivir-family-prophylaxis-rct]]). Fills queue item 50 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]].

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Double-blind, placebo-controlled household prophylaxis trial; randomization by family ("The families were divided randomly into drug- or placebo-treated groups. All the members of one family, including the index case, received the same treatment"); 72 general practitioners volunteered through the RCGP Epidemic Observation Unit, 29 contributed results (22 England, 4 Wales, 3 Scotland), each asked to enroll families of up to 4 index cases; single epidemic period January-March 1969, A2/Hong Kong/68; no trial registration (1969) |
| Subjects | "a total of 58 families, comprising 176 individuals" (text). Index case = "the first person over the age of 2 years to contract clinical influenza in a household"; contacts = members living in the household with daily contact with the index. Table 2 contact denominators: 94 aminoadamantane / 82 placebo (mean ages 28.8 / 25.9; 43%/41% male; ages 2 to 80+). **Numeric inconsistency recorded:** Table 2's contact total (94+82 = 176) equals the text's total *individuals*, although index cases are a separate category and Table 4's sub-denominators also sum to 176 (58+49+36+33); the paper does not reconcile this, so per-arm contact denominators carry that ambiguity. Exclusions: infants under 2 years untreated; nobody influenza-vaccinated in the preceding year |
| Intervention | Aminoadamantane hydrochloride (amantadine HCl): adults one 100-mg capsule every 12 hours; children 10-15 years 100 mg every 24 hours; children 2-10 years proportional syrup dose; 10 days from the index case first being seen, started "from the time the index case in the family was first seen." Capsule = soft gelatine, drug suspended in oil; children's syrup 50 mg/5 ml |
| Comparator / control | **Sham arm: YES.** Quote (Dosage): "Placebo capsules (oil base without drug) or syrup were used on a similar dosage schedule." Same oil-base vehicle without drug, same schedule, double-blind. Key design difference vs the authors' own earlier 1967-68 positive study (Discussion): "In the 1967-68 study, all index cases received placebo but in this investigation, the index case in each family received capsules of the same composition as those taken by the members of the household, aminoadamantane or placebo" - so this trial cannot separate index-treatment from contact-prophylaxis effects, and the 1969 result is the pooled-regimen result |
| Endpoint (named exactly) | Clinical: "A cough, accompanied by a fever of 100 F (37.8 C) or higher was taken as the criterion for a diagnosis of influenza", counted in contacts "within 10 days of entering study"; temperatures recorded by the GP for the index and by contacts themselves on a separate form. Laboratory: paired sera (day-1 and 2-3 weeks later) - "In both CF and HI tests a 4-fold or greater rise in antibody titre was regarded as diagnostic of influenza A infection"; HI against 8 HA doses of A2/Hong Kong/1/68 virus, CF against influenza A 'S' antigen (PR/8 origin). Index cases classified as serologically confirmed (infection "Present", 36 families) or not ("Absent", 22 families); contacts subdivided the same way. Virus sensitivity control: quantitative haemadsorption (QH) assay of epidemic strain vs earlier A2 strains in grivet-monkey-kidney cells |
| Result | **NULL as randomized.** Families with a serologically confirmed index case (36): contact clinical influenza within 10 days 9/58 treated (15.5%) vs 8/49 placebo (16.3%), P=0.9; serologically proven contact illness 5/44 (11.4%) vs 6/42 (14.3%), P=0.7-0.5; serologic evidence of infection irrespective of illness 21/44 treated (47.7%) vs 14/42 placebo (33.3%), P=0.3-0.2 - **numerically higher in the treated arm, not significant**. Families with an unconfirmed index (22): clinical 4/36 (11.1%) vs 4/33 (12.1%), P=0.8-0.9; proven illness 0/21 vs 0/23; infection 2/21 (9.5%) vs 3/23 (13.0%). Baseline balance: 88% (treated) vs 92% (placebo) of contacts had initial HI titres <=1:12 (90% combined); differences not significant. Breakthrough-infection antibody responses not blunted by the drug: mean HI rise x6.0 treated vs x5.4 placebo; CF x10.3 vs x8.7. Table 5: among contacts with initial HI <=1:12, infection 20/37 treated (54%) vs 13/39 placebo (33%), P=0.2-0.1 - again against the drug. Strain-sensitivity control: A2/Hong Kong/1/68 QH50 0.2 ug/ml, identical to A2/England/10/67, both as sensitive as A2/Scotland/49/57 (0.1) - the epidemic strain was drug-sensitive in vitro. Adverse events: 2 headaches in treated contacts, no other reactions, none in placebo. The abstract states the headline: "In a double-blind, placebo-controlled investigation of aminoadamantane given prophylactically ... **no protective effect of the drug was demonstrated**" |
| Main limitation | Null trial in a highly susceptible population - 90% of contacts had initial HI <1:12, which the authors themselves make the crux of their explanation ("in the previous study the corresponding figure was 40%"), so the trial tests the drug in the subgroup where its offered mechanism predicts least effect; small per-arm denominators (49-58 contacts) with no confidence intervals; P values given only as coarse ranges; family-level randomization with index and contacts on the same arm (no separation of index-treatment vs prophylaxis effects; the authors' own in-trial note: "There was no apparent difference in the degree of spread of influenza infection to contacts in families whose index cases received aminoadamantane and those whose index cases received placebo"); infection endpoint rests on two sera (serology-dominated; no virus isolation from contacts); 10-day observation window; temperatures partly self-recorded by contacts; contact-denominator arithmetic inconsistent (94+82=176 vs "58 families, comprising 176 individuals"); first author employed by the drug's marketer (Geigy U.K.); the claimed positive 1967-68 predecessor was reported only as a conference presentation, with this paper as its published companion - the vault has not verified the predecessor trial |
| Inferential leap (authors') | (1) The explanation offered for the null - "there is an enhancement of the inhibitory effect of aminoadamantane on influenza A2 infections in individuals who had pre-existing anti-haemagglutinin antibody" - is imported from the authors' previous unverified report and is not tested by any measurement in this trial (no arm or stratum with high pre-existing antibody was prophylactically challenged here; Table 5's >=1:18 cells hold 10 contacts). (2) "This study therefore further suggests possible advantages to be gained by combining aminoadamantane treatment with immunization" - a recommendation resting on the untested hypothesis. (3) The abstract's framing ("In view of the positive effect of the drug in the earlier study ... an explanation ... is offered") contrasts a verified null against an unverified positive; the vault records the null as the only experimentally supported half of that comparison. |

## What question this experiment answers
Whether, in British households with a clinical influenza index case during the January-March 1969 A2/Hong Kong/68 epidemic, whole-family amantadine prophylaxis (100 mg/12 h adults, 10 days, oil-base placebo) reduced clinical influenza (cough + fever >=37.8 C within 10 days) or serologically evidenced A2 infection (>=4-fold HI/CF rise) in household contacts. It answered no - on clinical influenza (15.5% vs 16.3%, P=0.9), on proven illness (11.4% vs 14.3%), and, numerically in the wrong direction, on infection (47.7% vs 33.3%). It also records an in-trial observation relevant to transmission questions: spread from index to contacts did not differ between families whose index received drug vs placebo, and an in-vitro control shows the epidemic strain was drug-sensitive. It does **not** answer why the effect failed beyond the authors' antibody-enhancement hypothesis (untested here), nor what the drug does in populations with high pre-existing HI antibody, nor seasonal prophylaxis, nor any effect on severe outcomes.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- GAP 4's influenza rung now holds an antiviral-platform pair with a discordant replication structure: amantadine household prophylaxis 1969 = null as randomized; zanamivir household prophylaxis 2000 ([[sources/hayden-2000-zanamivir-family-prophylaxis-rct]]) = 79% protection with the same family-randomized design, a comparable endpoint (cough/fever symptom criteria plus laboratory confirmation), and a comparable placebo-arm household attack rate (19% of families). Different drug classes (M2 ion-channel inhibitor vs neuraminidase inhibitor), different populations, 31 years apart; the vault carries both rows without flattening the discordance, and notes that the 1969 trial's own design (everyone on the same arm) cannot test the treat-the-index strategy that its predecessor claimed worked.
- The 1969 placebo-arm figures (33-47% of confirmed-index-family contacts serologically infected within 10 days) are an early controlled measurement of household influenza spread, consistent with the modern placebo-arm household attack rates on the ladder pages.
- The null is experimentally supported; the antibody-enhancement explanation and the vaccine-combination recommendation are attributed hypotheses, not results.
- The unverified 1967-68 "positive effect" predecessor (conference presentation; Lancet 1969;2:1026-1028 report not yet fetched) is recorded as a bibliographic lead only - it must not be used as evidence until its full text is verified; flagged as a future queue lead.
