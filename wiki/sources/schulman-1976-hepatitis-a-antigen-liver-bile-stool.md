# Hepatitis A antigen particles in liver, bile, and stool of chimpanzees (Schulman et al. 1976)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: chimpanzee tissue/shedding rung for [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]] — one of the named Oxford/JID acquisition gaps, now at abstract level
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 1976
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Serial-specimen experimental infection with tissue and fluid sampling |
| Subject / sample | Three chimpanzees infected with stool filtrates containing HA Ag particles |
| Intervention or exposure | Inoculation with HA-Ag-positive stool filtrates |
| Comparator / control | Within-animal serial intervals (serum, stool, liver biopsy, bile); no separate control arm stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | Mild hepatitis timing; de novo convalescent anti-HA antibody; virus-like particles in liver cell cytoplasm by EM; HA Ag detection by IEM and radioimmunoassay in isopycnically banded liver, bile, and stool samples; particle density |
| Result | Animals developed mild hepatitis 19–21 days after inoculation, and de novo anti-HA antibody appeared in convalescent serum. During acute illness, virus-like particles similar to HA Ag were seen in liver cell cytoplasm by electron microscopy. HA Ag particles were detected by IEM and a new radioimmunoassay in banded samples of liver, bile, and stool, at densities of 1.29–1.39 g/cm³ with a major peak around 1.34 g/cm³. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 3; no separate control arm stated; density characterization is antigen-detection, not infectious-virus titration. |

## What the abstract can answer
- Whether HA-Ag material localizes to liver and shed into bile and stool in infected chimpanzees with matching density characteristics: yes in this set — the tissue-distribution/shedding rung.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Per-organ titers, histology, and whether the banded material was infectious.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Antigen particles in liver, bile, and stool are localization and shedding endpoints; they do not titrate infectiousness or establish human natural-route transmission. The ladder keeps them distinct.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: three chimpanzees infected with HA-Ag-containing filtrates developed mild hepatitis with de novo antibody, hepatic cytoplasmic virus-like particles, and HA Ag recoverable from liver, bile, and stool with a ~1.34 g/cm³ major peak.
