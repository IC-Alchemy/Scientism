# Visualization by Immune Electron Microscopy of a 27-nm Particle Associated with Acute Infectious Nonbacterial Gastroenteritis

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early primary detection and serologic-association paper for the Norwalk agent
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1972
- Authors: A. Z. Kapikian and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Immune electron microscopy and serologic association study |
| Subject / sample | Infectious stool filtrate from the Norwalk, Ohio, nonbacterial gastroenteritis outbreak plus experimentally or naturally infected persons |
| Exposure or intervention | Outbreak-derived filtrate or natural infection context |
| Comparator / control | Acute and convalescent serology |
| Endpoint | Visualization of a 27 nm particle by immune EM and serologic response |
| Result | A 27 nm particle was observed in infectious filtrate, and infected individuals developed serologic evidence associated with the particle. |
| Main limitation | Pre-genomic-era particle association and immune response; no routine culture system and no full taxonomic norovirus diversity claim. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a small particle was associated with Norwalk nonbacterial gastroenteritis material and host immune response.
- It can answer that immune EM could visualize the candidate particle in infectious filtrate.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define all norovirus genotypes or all outbreak ecology.
- It cannot by itself prove culture replication, dose-response, or modern molecular taxonomy.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating one prototype outbreak particle as the whole norovirus disease ecology. This is a discovery/association rung, not a comprehensive outbreak-causation proof.

## Vault conclusion
Kapikian et al. 1972 supports a Norwalk-agent particle and serologic-association rung. It should be paired with challenge, outbreak, and culture-system evidence.
