# Physical, Chemical and Morphologic Dimensions of Human Hepatitis A Virus Strain CR326

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary CR326 particle characterization and infected-liver localization paper
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the Society for Experimental Biology and Medicine*, 1975
- Authors: Philip J. Provost, Bohdan S. Wolanski, William J. Miller, Oswald L. Ittensohn, William J. McAleer, and Maurice R. Hilleman
- Source basis in hand: full-text EBM PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Virus purification, isopycnic density banding, marmoset infectivity testing, neutralization, electron microscopy, liver thin-section microscopy, and physical/chemical inactivation study |
| Subject / sample | CR326 human hepatitis A virus propagated in marmosets; purified infectious marmoset serum; infected marmoset liver extracts and liver sections |
| Exposure or intervention | CsCl density purification; heat, ultraviolet irradiation, and formalin treatment; marmoset inoculation of purified or treated preparations; immune electron microscopy with human hepatitis A antibody |
| Comparator / control | Pre-illness versus convalescent human hepatitis A serum; untreated virus and diluent controls; comparison with properties of enteroviruses, rhinoviruses, hepatitis B particles, parvoviruses, and GB virus |
| Endpoint | Infectivity in marmosets, neutralization by convalescent serum, particle morphology, particle diameter, buoyant density, intracellular location, immune aggregation, and inactivation profile |
| Result | Purified CR326 consisted of 27 nm spherical particles with main CsCl density about 1.34. Infectivity of purified virus was neutralized by convalescent human hepatitis A serum. Similar particles were observed in infected marmoset liver cytoplasm, usually in vesicles, and reacted with hepatitis A antisera by immune electron microscopy. The virus was acid, ether, and partially 60 C heat stable, but was destroyed by 100 C heat, formalin, and ultraviolet irradiation under the tested conditions. |
| Main limitation | The paper characterizes a marmoset-propagated strain and infected marmoset tissue. It does not show ordinary human infection, fecal-oral transmission, or clinical hepatitis in humans. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that CR326 particles were physically and morphologically consistent with a small RNA-type enterovirus-like agent.
- It can answer that purified CR326 infectivity remained linked to hepatitis A serology by neutralization.
- It can answer that similar particles appeared in infected marmoset liver cytoplasm.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove human natural-route causation by itself.
- It cannot substitute for stool-particle detection in human acute cases when that full text is not in hand.
- It cannot establish cell-culture propagation or direct human challenge.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is converting particle characterization into complete causal closure. The paper strengthens the morphology, tissue-localization, and physical-characterization rungs, but it does not answer every transmission or clinical-causation question.

## Vault conclusion
Provost et al. 1975 supports a strong CR326 characterization rung: particle morphology, density, tissue localization, stability, and serologic identity in the marmoset system.
