# Infectivity of Respiratory Syncytial Virus by Various Routes of Inoculation

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary adult human inoculation-route paper for RSV infection endpoints
- Journal/year: *Infection and Immunity*, 1981
- Authors: C. B. Hall, R. G. Douglas Jr., K. C. Schnabel, and J. M. Geiman
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Controlled adult human inoculation-route study |
| Subject / sample | 32 healthy adult volunteers |
| Exposure or intervention | Safety-tested live RSV instilled into nose, eye, or mouth at serial dilutions |
| Comparator / control | Route and dose comparison; no sham-placebo comparator |
| Endpoint | Infection by viral shedding and serologic response |
| Result | At 5.2 log10 TCID50, infection occurred in 3 of 4 nasal and 3 of 4 eye inoculations; mouth inoculation produced 1 of 8 infections, likely secondary spread. Lower dose reduced or eliminated infection. |
| Main limitation | Adult volunteers, artificial instillation, mild upper-respiratory illness, and no sham comparator; does not establish infant bronchiolitis causation. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that RSV can infect adults by nasal and ocular exposure routes under the study conditions.
- It can compare route and dose efficiency in the tested adult challenge model.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove infant bronchiolitis pathogenesis.
- It cannot define ordinary household transmission or lower-airway disease in infants.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is importing adult route-inoculation infection into infant bronchiolitis causation. This is a transmission-route/infection rung, not a bronchiolitis endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
Hall et al. 1981 supports RSV adult route-specific infectivity by nasal and ocular inoculation. It should stay separate from infant bronchiolitis disease causation.
