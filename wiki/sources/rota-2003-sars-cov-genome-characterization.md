# Characterization of a novel coronavirus associated with SARS (Rota et al. 2003)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: genome-characterization rung for [[synthesis/sars-sars-cov-evidence-ladder]] — one of the three named acquisition leads, now at abstract level
- Journal/year: *Science*, 2003
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Complete-genome sequencing and comparative analysis |
| Subject / sample | The SARS-CoV isolate discovered in March 2003 in association with SARS cases |
| Intervention or exposure | None — sequencing and bioinformatic characterization |
| Comparator / control | Phylogenetic comparison against previously characterized coronaviruses |
| Endpoint | Genome length, open reading frame count and organization, phylogenetic position |
| Result | The SARS-CoV genome is 29,727 nucleotides with 11 open reading frames and genome organization similar to other coronaviruses. Phylogenetic analyses and sequence comparisons showed SARS-CoV is not closely related to any previously characterized coronavirus. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; characterization-only record with no disease endpoint; single-isolate genome in this abstract (diversity analyses are in the full text). |

## What the abstract can answer
- What the SARS-CoV genome is (size, ORF structure, coronavirus-type organization) and how it relates phylogenetically to known coronaviruses (distinct).

## What full text would still need to establish
- Within-outbreak sequence diversity, mutation tracking, and the annotations behind the ORF assignments.

## Later inferential-leap risk
A genome sequence identifies an agent; it does not itself establish disease causation. In the ladder this rung feeds the identification chain, not the causation chain — the macaque and transmission rungs stay separate.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: SARS-CoV's complete genome is 29,727 nucleotides with 11 ORFs and coronavirus-type organization, phylogenetically distant from all previously characterized coronaviruses.
