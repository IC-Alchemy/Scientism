# Isolation of a cDNA Clone Derived from a Blood-Borne Non-A Non-B Viral Hepatitis Genome

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular-identification paper for the agent later named hepatitis C virus
- Journal/year: *Science*, 1989
- Authors: Qui-Lim Choo, George Kuo, Amy J. Weiner, Lacy R. Overby, Daniel W. Bradley, and Michael Houghton
- Source basis in hand: full-text PDF scan of the *Science* article, checked against DOI and PubMed metadata

## Vault extraction
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Study type | Random-primed cDNA library construction from infectious chimpanzee plasma, immunoscreening with chronic non-A non-B hepatitis serum, host-DNA and RNA hybridization tests, strand and size characterization, recombinant polypeptide immunoblot/RIA testing |
| Subject / sample | Infectious plasma and liver material from chimpanzee 910 with chronic non-A non-B hepatitis after passage of a human factor VIII-derived agent; serum from a chronic NANBH patient; additional NANBH patient sera; normal donor sera; serial sera from experimentally infected chimpanzees with NANBH, HAV, or HBV |
| Exposure or intervention | Nucleic acid from ultracentrifuged infectious plasma was converted into a cDNA library and screened for clones expressing antigen recognized by NANBH patient serum; clone 5-1-1 and overlapping clone 81 were characterized |
| Comparator / control | Control human DNA, uninfected chimpanzee liver RNA, control SOD bacterial lysates, normal donor sera, and chimpanzees infected experimentally with HAV or HBV |
| Endpoint | Whether candidate clones were host-derived or exogenous; whether homologous RNA was present in infectious material; RNase versus DNase sensitivity; RNA size/strand orientation; antibody reactivity to PS5/clone 5-1-1 antigen in NANBH versus control samples |
| Result | The clones did not hybridize to host DNA but did hybridize to RNA from infected chimpanzee liver and infectious plasma-derived material. The plasma signal was RNase-sensitive and DNase-resistant. A recombinant 5-1-1 polypeptide reacted with sera from NANBH patients and experimentally infected NANBH chimpanzees, while normal donor sera and HAV/HBV chimpanzee controls were negative in the reported tests. The authors termed the associated agent hepatitis C virus. |
| Main limitation | The paper is a molecular identification and antigen-association study. It does not contain an infectious full-length clone, direct virion culture, controlled human transmission experiment, or clinical causation trial. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that the investigated infectious NANBH material contained an exogenous positive-strand RNA signal associated with a recombinant antigen recognized in NANBH-linked sera.
- It can answer that the candidate clone was not detectably host DNA under the Southern-blot tests used.
- It can answer that PS5/clone 5-1-1 antibody reactivity separated NANBH-linked sera from the normal donor and HAV/HBV chimpanzee controls tested.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove that a complete cloned HCV genome is infectious.
- It cannot prove ordinary route, dose, or all clinical outcomes of hepatitis C.
- It cannot rule out all other non-A non-B hepatitis agents.
- It cannot substitute for prospective transfusion-recipient or molecular-clone infectivity evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The paper is foundational for HCV molecular identification, but the leap begins if a partial cDNA clone plus antibody association is treated as the same endpoint as infectious molecular clone, transmission, chronic disease mechanism, or intervention evidence.

## What this wiki currently concludes
This source supports a narrow claim: Choo et al. 1989 identified cDNA clones and a recombinant antigen associated with blood-borne non-A non-B hepatitis material and serologic responses, providing a molecular-identification rung for HCV but not by itself a complete causation ladder.
