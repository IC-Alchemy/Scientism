# A New Type of Papillomavirus DNA in Genital Cancer Biopsies and Cervical Cancer Cell Lines

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular-cloning, biopsy-survey, cell-line, and physical-state paper for HPV18
- Journal/year: *The EMBO Journal*, 1984
- Authors: Michael Boshart, Lutz Gissmann, Hans Ikenberg, Andreas Kleinheinz, Wolfram Scheurlen, and Harald zur Hausen
- Source basis in hand: full-text Europe PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Molecular cloning and characterization of a new HPV type, plus biopsy and cell-line hybridization survey and viral DNA physical-state analysis |
| Subject / sample | Cervical carcinoma biopsy WV-341 for HPV18 cloning; cervical carcinomas from Africa/Brazil and Germany; penile carcinoma; cervical dysplasias, genital warts, carcinomas in situ, normal cervical biopsies; HeLa, KB, and C4-1 cervical-cancer-derived cell lines |
| Exposure or intervention | Restriction digestion, Southern blot hybridization under low- and high-stringency conditions, cloning of viral DNA, comparison with HPV6 and HPV16 prototypes, and analysis of episomal/integrated viral DNA patterns |
| Comparator / control | Other HPV probes and types, benign lesions, carcinoma in situ, normal cervical tissue, cell lines, and stringency conditions |
| Endpoint | Detectable HPV18-hybridizing DNA, viral genome size/identity, presence in cancers/cell lines, and physical state of viral DNA |
| Result | HPV18 was cloned from a cervical carcinoma. HPV18 DNA was detected in 9 of 36 Africa/Brazil cervical carcinomas, 2 of 13 German cervical tumors, 1 of 10 penile carcinomas, and in HeLa, KB, and C4-1 cells. It was not detected in 17 cervical dysplasias, 29 genital warts, 8 carcinomas in situ, or 15 normal cervical biopsies. Four cervical cancer biopsies were examined for physical state; the results suggested integration, with one tumor showing head-to-tail tandem repeats and some episomal molecules. |
| Main limitation | The paper is an association and molecular-structure study. It does not experimentally infect tissue, follow progression, or demonstrate transformation in human tissue. Some integration evidence remained indirect in parts of the analysis. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that HPV18-like DNA was identifiable as a distinct HPV type in the tested cervical carcinoma material.
- It can answer that HPV18 DNA was present in some genital cancers and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines in the tested materials.
- It can answer that the physical state of HPV18 in some tested tumors was compatible with host-genome integration or tandem-repeat arrangements.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that HPV18 caused the cancers.
- It cannot establish why HPV18 was absent from sampled benign and normal tissues.
- It cannot determine temporal order: whether integration preceded, accompanied, or followed malignant change.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if HPV18 detection or inferred integration is treated as causal closure. The source supports molecular identification, tumor association, and physical-state rungs, not necessity, sufficiency, or human transformation proof.

## Vault conclusion
This source supports a narrow claim: Boshart et al. 1984 cloned HPV18 from cervical carcinoma material and found HPV18 DNA in some genital cancers and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines, while leaving the causal role of HPV18 unresolved.
