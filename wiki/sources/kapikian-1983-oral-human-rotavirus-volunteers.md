# Oral Administration of Human Rotavirus to Volunteers

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary controlled human oral-challenge paper for rotavirus infection and illness endpoints
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 1983
- Authors: A. Z. Kapikian and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text scan lead surfaced through CiteSeerX/Oxford record; claims should be rechecked against retained full text if used for later high-stakes expansion

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Human volunteer oral challenge with serology and shedding follow-up |
| Subject / sample | 18 adult volunteers; two volunteers rechallenged |
| Exposure or intervention | Oral administration of human rotavirus strain D |
| Comparator / control | Prechallenge immune status and rechallenge comparison |
| Endpoint | Diarrheal illness, virus shedding, and serologic response |
| Result | Four of 18 volunteers developed diarrheal illness, five of 18 shed virus, and 12 of 18 had serologic evidence of infection. Rechallenged volunteers did not redevelop diarrhea. |
| Main limitation | Adult volunteers, small sample, and challenge conditions do not directly represent infant disease burden or natural transmission probability. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that oral human rotavirus exposure can induce infection, shedding, serologic response, and diarrhea in some susceptible adult volunteers under the protocol used.
- It can answer that prior exposure or rechallenge status affected illness in the tested volunteers.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define pediatric disease burden or infant severity.
- It cannot prove population transmission probability or all natural-exposure outcomes.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is projecting adult volunteer challenge response onto infant population disease severity or treating partial illness response as universal outcome.

## Vault conclusion
Kapikian et al. 1983 supports a high-value rotavirus human oral-challenge rung. It should remain bounded by adult sample, protocol, and small size.
