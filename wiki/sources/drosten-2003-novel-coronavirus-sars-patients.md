# Identification of a Novel Coronavirus in Patients with Severe Acute Respiratory Syndrome

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary SARS-CoV identification paper using patient samples, cell culture, random amplification, sequence comparison, specific PCR, and serology
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2003
- Authors: Christian Drosten, Stephan Gunther, Wolfgang Preiser, Sylvie van der Werf, Hans-Reinhard Brodt, Stephan Becker, Holger Rabenau, Marcus Panning, Larissa Kolesnikova, Ron A. M. Fouchier, Annemarie Berger, Ana-Maria Burguiere, Jindrich Cinatl, Markus Eickmann, Nicolas Escriou, Klaus Grywna, Stefanie Kramme, Jean-Claude Manuguerra, Stefanie Muller, Volker Rickerts, Martin Sturmer, Simon Vieth, Hans-Dieter Klenk, Albert D. M. E. Osterhaus, Herbert Schmitz, and Hans Wilhelm Doerr
- Source basis in hand: full-text NEJM PDF from Erasmus repository

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Virus identification, cell culture, broad pathogen testing, electron microscopy, random RT-PCR sequencing, phylogenetic comparison, specific conventional and real-time RT-PCR assay development, and serology |
| Subject / sample | Frankfurt index SARS patient and two contacts; 49 specimens from 18 suspected or probable SARS patients in Hanoi; 21 healthy contact persons; 54 German stool samples used as controls |
| Exposure or intervention | Respiratory, blood, stool, and culture specimens were tested for known respiratory pathogens, inoculated onto Vero, Madin-Darby canine-kidney, and A549 cells, examined by electron microscopy, sequenced after random amplification, and tested with SARS-CoV-specific RT-PCR assays |
| Comparator / control | Broad assays for known viral and atypical respiratory pathogens; healthy contacts in the Hanoi set; German stool controls; comparison with known coronavirus sequences |
| Endpoint | Cell-culture isolation, coronavirus-like sequence fragments, specific conventional and real-time RT-PCR positivity, viral RNA copy number, specimen distribution, and seroconversion against the Vero-cell isolate |
| Result | A novel coronavirus isolate, FFM-ic, was identified from SARS patient material. Random amplification yielded coronavirus-related fragments including a 300 nucleotide sequence and a 90 nucleotide sequence; the sequence was distinct from known coronaviruses and linked to the CDC sequence by a contiguous 3 kb fragment. Viral RNA was detected in multiple clinical specimen types from SARS patients, with sputum reaching up to about 100 million RNA molecules/ml. In the Hanoi set, RT-PCR was positive in 5 of 5 probable SARS patients, 3 of 13 suspected SARS patients, and 0 of 21 contacts. The Frankfurt index patient and Contact 1 developed antibody reactivity to the infected Vero cells. |
| Main limitation | This is a virus identification and association paper. It does not reproduce SARS by experimental challenge, and the authors explicitly noted that the serologic response used primary culture antigen, leaving a possible co-isolated-agent boundary until plaque-purified virus or recombinant antigens were used. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that a novel coronavirus-compatible agent could be isolated and genetically characterized from SARS patient material.
- It can answer that specific RT-PCR assays detected this viral RNA in probable SARS patients in the tested set and not in healthy contacts.
- It can answer that the Frankfurt index patient and one contact developed serologic reactivity to the infected Vero-cell culture.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot by itself prove experimental SARS reproduction in an animal or human host.
- It cannot prove ordinary human transmission route, infectious dose, or causal sufficiency.
- It cannot fully separate antibody reactivity to the novel coronavirus from a possible co-isolated unknown agent under the antigen method used in the paper.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating virus identification plus PCR and serology as if they were the whole causation ladder. This paper is a strong identification rung, but the experimental challenge and pathology rungs have to be supplied by other full-text sources.

## Vault conclusion
Drosten et al. 2003 supports SARS-CoV identification, sequence characterization, diagnostic PCR, specimen detection, and early serologic association. It should be cited for those endpoints, not as a standalone proof of ordinary natural-route SARS causation.
