# Safety, tolerability and viral kinetics during SARS-CoV-2 human challenge in young adults (COVHIC001)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: the vault's GAP 1 (controlled human inoculation) row for a third pathogen, SARS-CoV-2 - the model-validation paper of the pattern used for influenza ([[sources/memoli-2015-h1n1pdm09-human-challenge-model-validation]]) and RSV ([[sources/devincenzo-2010-rsv-human-challenge-model-validation]]); fills queue item 25 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]].
- Journal/year: *Nature Medicine* 2022;28(5):1031-1041 (online 2022-03-31). Citation correction recorded: the queue's draft entry said "Nat Med 27:961-965 (2021)"; PubMed/Crossref and the article itself give volume 28, pages 1031-1041, 2022.
- Authors: Ben Killingley, Alex J. Mann, Mariya Kalinova, Alison Boyers, Niluka Goonawardane, Jie Zhou, ... Christopher Chiu (senior) - UCLH, hVIVO Services, Imperial College London, Royal Free London NHS Trust, and others. Funded by the UK Vaccine Taskforce (BEIS); the funder had no role per the acknowledgements.
- Registration: NCT04865237 (registered after enrollment began because the MHRA discussion about clinical-trial status delayed registration - recorded in Methods).

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Single-center, phase 1, open-label, first-in-human challenge study; explicitly non-randomized with no control arm; adaptive stepwise cohort progression (sentinel n=3, then n=7, then sentinel n=3 without remdesivir, then groups of 7, 7, 9); enrollment 6 March 2021 - 8 July 2021, high-containment quarantine unit at the Royal Free London NHS Foundation Trust, individual negative-pressure rooms, >=14 days quarantine with virological discharge criteria, planned 1-year follow-up |
| Subjects | 36 healthy volunteers aged 18-29 years without evidence of previous infection or vaccination (age range printed 18-30 in Methods inclusion criteria; Table 1 max 29); seronegative at screening (Quotient MosaiQ antibody microarray); severe-COVID risk factors excluded via QCOVID threshold (1:250,000 death / 1:4,902 hospitalization risk equivalent); egg-hypersensitivity analog excluded via screening incl. serology, spirometry, echocardiography, chest radiograph; 2 participants excluded post hoc from the per-protocol analysis for seroconversion between screening and inoculation -> per-protocol n=34; CONSORT: 26,937 registered interest, 187 assessed, 36 inoculated; demographics: 72% male, 92% white |
| Exposure / intervention | Intranasal drops of 10 TCID50 (10^1; chosen as "the lowest infectious dose that could be reliably quantified by viral culture"; stated equivalent to 55 FFU) of wild-type SARS-CoV-2/human/GBR/484861/2020 (20A clade, B.1 lineage, D614G; GenBank OM294022), 100 microliter per naris via pipette, participant supine 10 min then 20 min sitting wearing a nose clip; virus isolated from a UK patient sample via ISARIC4C, propagated on qualified cGMP Vero cells, challenge master virus bank GMP-manufactured at the Zayed Centre for Research (Great Ormond Street), dose vials GMP-released with identity/infectivity/adventitious-agent testing and whole-genome confirmation |
| Comparator / control | **Sham arm: NO.** Open-label, non-randomized, single-arm inoculation - no placebo-inoculation group exists. Design quote (Abstract): "36 volunteers aged 18-29 years without evidence of previous infection or vaccination were inoculated with 10 TCID50 of a wild-type virus ... intranasally in an open-label, non-randomized study." Partial-drug confound recorded: the first 10 challenged participants were prospectively assigned pre-emptive remdesivir (100 mg IV twice daily x 5 days) once two consecutive 12-hourly swabs showed quantifiable PCR, as pandemic-era risk mitigation; 6/10 became infected; the DSMB and steering committee then deemed it unnecessary and it was stopped; "no statistically significant effect on VL or symptoms was detectable in this small cohort" and "infected participants were, therefore, analyzed together" (no significant differences in nasal VL during treatment; the trend was driven by one participant). The authors state the boundary themselves: "This study was not designed nor powered to assess the efficacy of early treatment with remdesivir." |
| Endpoint (named exactly) | Primary objective: "to identify a dose of wild-type SARS-CoV-2 in healthy volunteers with an acceptable safety profile that induced laboratory-confirmed infection in >=50% of participants." **Laboratory-confirmed infection** defined in Methods as: "quantifiable RT-PCR detection greater than the lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) from mid-turbinate and/or throat swabs on two or more consecutive 12-hourly time points, starting from 24 hours after inoculation and up to discharge from quarantine." Secondary/exploratory endpoints: viral load kinetics by qRT-PCR (CDC N-gene protocol) and viable virus by focus-forming assay (FFA on Vero cells); self-reported symptom diaries (19 symptoms, 0-3 scale, three times daily); smell by University of Pennsylvania Smell Identification Test (UPSIT, significant drop >4); serum microneutralization NT50 and anti-spike IgG ELISA; safety labs (blood counts), spirometry, ECG, chest CT day 5 (all) and day 10. Methods state: "only analyses of VL were pre-specified in the protocol; all others were post hoc"; P values two-sided and "unadjusted for multiplicity, as these investigations were exploratory" |
| Result | 18/34 = 53% (95% CI 35-70, per-protocol) met laboratory-confirmed infection - within the protocol target 50-70%, so no dose escalation. Shedding onset: throat quantifiable at median 40 h (95% CI 40-52) vs nose 58 h (40-76), P=0.0225; peak VL nose 8.87 (8.41-9.53) log10 copies/mL vs throat 7.65 (7.39-8.24), significant; viable virus (FFA) first detected earlier in throat (P=0.0058); FFA positivity median 6.5 days nose; mean time to clearance of viable virus 10.2 days (nose) / 8.7 days (throat); latest FFA-positive sample day 12 (nose, one participant); qPCR positivity persisted: 15/18 (83%) still positive at discharge, 6/18 (33%) at day 28, all negative by day 90. Symptoms: 16/18 (89%) infected participants reported mild-to-moderate symptoms from 2-4 days post inoculation (upper-respiratory dominant: rhinitis, nasal stuffiness, sneezing, sore throat); 2/18 (11%) asymptomatic; peak symptoms aligned with nasal VL peak (112 h vs 88 h throat FFA) but **no correlation between viral-load AUC and symptom-score AUC** (nose qPCR r=0.067, P=0.79; nose FFA r=-0.17, P=0.49); fever >37.8 C in 7/18 (39%); anosmia/dysosmia in 15/18 (83%), complete anosmia 9 (50%), UPSIT nadir 6-7 days later than other symptoms, resolving by day 28 in most (11/18 still partial at day 28; 1 with measurable impairment at day 180). Antibodies: all infected seroconverted (NT50 median 425, IQR 269 at day 14; 863.5, IQR 403 at day 28); uninfected showed no rise except the one post-discharge natural infection. Uninfected participants had only low-level non-consecutive qPCR detections (3 nose, 6 throat). LFA (Innova): none of the uninfected ever positive; all infected positive >=2 days; log10 FFA nose predicted LFA positivity OR 5.01 (2.93-8.57); modeled twice-weekly LFA would diagnose before 70-80% of viral AUC had been generated. Safety: no serious adverse events; no rescue-therapy (REGEN-COV) criteria met; 18 adverse events probably/possibly related to infection - transient leukopenia/neutropenia and mild mucocutaneous findings; no spirometry/CT deterioration; compensation up to GBP 4,565 per participant (NIHR formula) |
| Main limitation | Authors' own (Discussion): "An important limitation of this study, in keeping with first-in-human studies generally, was the small sample size that limits our ability to detect rare events or more subtle differences." Also: healthy, selected, young adults only (risk-stratified out via QCOVID) - severity in other populations unaddressed; post hoc endpoint analyses with unadjusted multiplicity; pre-emptive remdesivir in the first 6 infected participants (analyzed together, no detectable effect); single pre-alpha D614G strain (variant-era extrapolation is the authors' argument, not a result); quarantine conditions (not natural exposure); this report presents data to day 28 with smell follow-up to day 180, not the planned 1-year follow-up. Vault-level note: no sham arm means the attack-rate denominator includes any non-specific inoculation effect only via symptom diaries of uninfected challenged participants - the design cannot separate vaccine-style reactogenicity from infection in the infected arm, though PCR confirmation makes the primary endpoint robust |
| Inferential leap (authors') | Four policy leaps recorded, none measured in this experiment: (1) face coverings - "these data provide clear evidence that emphasizes the critical importance of wearing face coverings over the nose as well as the mouth" (from nose-vs-throat VL contrast; no masks studied); (2) isolation policy - "These data, therefore, support the isolation periods of 10 days after symptom onset advocated in many guidelines to minimize onward transmission" (from viable-virus clearance timing under quarantine; transmission itself never measured); (3) transmission risk - "our data clearly show that SARS-CoV-2 viral shedding occurs at high levels irrespective of symptom severity, thus explaining the high transmissibility of this infection and emphasizing that symptom severity cannot be considered a surrogate for transmission risk in this disease" (shedding treated as a proxy for transmission; no transmission endpoint); (4) testing programs - twice-weekly LFA modeling framed as informing "routine asymptomatic testing programs" (modeled VL-AUC proxy). Also the dose-external-validity claim: "this suggests that the model can recapitulate higher exposure than naturally acquired infection events" - explicitly built on the natural infectious dose being unknown and on a household-SAR comparison (~38%), i.e., inference. Delta/Omicron relevance is argued from antigenic divergence, not from this experiment. The remdesivir and "further challenge studies are now underway" passages are properly bounded by the authors |

## What question this experiment answers
Whether a single fixed, GMP-characterized, low inoculum dose (10 TCID50) of wild-type SARS-CoV-2 safely produces laboratory-confirmed infection in >50% of screened seronegative young adults under quarantine, and what the within-person virus, symptom, smell, and antibody kinetics are from a known exposure time. It does **not** answer natural-route transmission, severity in older or comorbid populations, variant behavior, or intervention efficacy - all of which the authors assign to future studies.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- GAP 1 now has a SARS-CoV-2 row: challenge-model validation exists in full text (this page), mirroring the Memoli 2015 (influenza) and DeVincenzo 2010 (RSV) rung. The **sham-controlled** rung of GAP 1 remains covered for influenza ([[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]]) and RSV ([[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]]) only; the vault holds no placebo-controlled SARS-CoV-2 challenge trial.
- The experiment itself is clean and well documented: named endpoint, quantifiable-PCR case definition, GMP inoculum, per-protocol denominators (34), and negative findings stated plainly (VL-symptom decoupling; 11% asymptomatic with high viral loads).
- The policy sentences quoted above are inferential leaps beyond this experiment and are flagged as such wherever this page is cited.
