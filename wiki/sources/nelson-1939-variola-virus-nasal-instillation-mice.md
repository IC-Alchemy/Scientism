# The Response of Mice to the Nasal Instillation of Variola Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary variola respiratory-route and mouse-host boundary paper
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1939
- Author: J. B. Nelson
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Animal inoculation, egg propagation, tissue recovery, histology, and host-range comparison |
| Subject / sample | Variola virus from human smallpox crusts, cultivated through embryonated eggs; rhesus monkeys, rabbits, and mice |
| Exposure or intervention | Mouse nasal instillation of egg-passaged variola virus; monkey skin scarification; rabbit cutaneous or testicular exposure |
| Comparator / control | Vaccinia challenge or comparison in selected host tests; time-point lung harvests after nasal instillation |
| Endpoint | Clinical response, weight and appearance, nasal-passage lesions, lung virus recovery by egg inoculation, lung histopathology, rabbit skin/testis reaction, and monkey skin lesions |
| Result | Nasally instilled mice showed no ordinary clinical illness or upper-airway lesion, and no elementary bodies were found in nasal passages. Variola could later be recovered from mouse lungs through day 5 and sometimes day 7, with progressive lung pathology, but titration did not prove robust multiplication. Monkey skin scarification produced local variola-like nodules and later resistance to variola rechallenge. Rabbits and mice did not show ordinary variola disease. |
| Main limitation | Mouse nasal instillation was a host and route model, not human smallpox. Lung recovery and pathology do not prove ordinary symptomatic smallpox or efficient replication. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that variola virus could survive or remain recoverable in mouse lungs after nasal instillation for several days.
- It can answer that the tested mice did not develop ordinary clinical smallpox-like disease or upper-respiratory mucosal infection under this protocol.
- It can answer that rhesus monkey skin scarification produced local variola-like lesions with some later resistance to variola rechallenge.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot answer human smallpox causation or transmission.
- It cannot prove productive variola multiplication in mouse lungs from the titration evidence as extracted.
- It cannot make mice a faithful model of human smallpox.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk cuts both ways. A positive lung-recovery endpoint should not be retold as symptomatic smallpox in mice. The negative clinical endpoint should not be retold as a failure of variola causation in humans.

## Vault conclusion
Nelson 1939 supports a respiratory-route and host-range boundary: nasal variola exposure in mice produced lung recovery/pathology without ordinary clinical smallpox. It is a boundary paper, not a global causation refutation.
