# Fluorescent Antibody Responses of Cases and Contacts of Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary serologic response paper for Coxsackie A16-associated HFMD cases and contacts
- Journal/year: *Infection and Immunity*, 1974
- Authors: G. C. Brown and T. P. O'Leary
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Serologic assay study using clinical cases and contacts |
| Subject / sample | 22 clinical HFMD cases, 21 familial contacts, and 16 school contacts |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural HFMD exposure or infection |
| Comparator / control | Comparison with neutralization-test titers and contact groups |
| Endpoint | IgG, IgM, and IgA fluorescent-antibody responses to tissue-culture-adapted Coxsackie A16 |
| Result | All 22 cases developed IgG; 11 developed IgM and 10 developed IgA. Many contacts showed IgG evidence of subclinical infection. |
| Main limitation | Antibody response supports exposure or infection timing but does not prove lesion-site replication or transmission direction in each individual. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that Coxsackie A16-specific fluorescent-antibody responses tracked clinical HFMD cases and some exposed contacts.
- It can answer that subclinical infection markers appeared in contacts under the reported assay conditions.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove acute causation in every symptomatic case without direct isolation or detection from each case.
- It cannot identify the direction of household or school transmission.
- It cannot answer EV71-associated HFMD.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using antibody positivity alone as definitive proof of acute individual causation. Serology is an immune-response rung, not a lesion-isolation or controlled-exposure rung.

## Vault conclusion
Brown and O'Leary 1974 supports a Coxsackie A16 serology and contact-response rung for HFMD. It strengthens the attribution chain but should not be treated as standalone proof for every clinical case.
