# Clinical Features of Rotavirus Gastroenteritis

## Source type
- Role in this vault: prospective hospital clinical-association paper for rotavirus in pediatric gastroenteritis
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infection*, 1982
- Author: J. G. McCormack
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective hospital clinical association study |
| Subject / sample | 518 children under 5 admitted with gastroenteritis over 12 months; 108 controls |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural rotavirus infection detected by stool electron microscopy |
| Comparator / control | Non-specific gastroenteritis cases and matched controls |
| Endpoint | Stool electron-microscopy positivity, clinical features, dehydration, and intravenous fluid use |
| Result | Rotavirus was detected in 132 of 518 gastroenteritis cases and none of the controls. Vomiting before diarrhea, fever, dehydration, and IV fluids were more prominent in rotavirus-positive cases. |
| Main limitation | Hospitalized young-child population; electron-microscopy-era diagnosis; clinical features are not pathogen-specific. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that stool rotavirus detection was associated with a specific hospitalized pediatric gastroenteritis pattern in this study.
- It can answer that tested controls were negative under the reported electron microscopy endpoint.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove causation for every gastroenteritis case.
- It cannot define community incidence or all transmission routes.
- It cannot make clinical features alone diagnostic.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using clinical pointers or stool detection as definitive diagnosis or broad disease burden without preserving the study population and method limits.

## Vault conclusion
McCormack 1982 supports a rotavirus pediatric hospital-association rung with control negativity. It does not by itself prove all-cause pediatric gastroenteritis attribution.
