# Pathogenesis of Herpes Simplex Labialis

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary serial human oral-shedding paper for recurrent herpes labialis
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Microbiology*, 1984
- Author: S. L. Spruance
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Serial observational human study |
| Subject / sample | Eight subjects with a history of herpes labialis |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural recurrence and oral-cavity sampling |
| Comparator / control | Time periods with and without common cold, oral trauma, or labialis; assessment against lesion-contamination explanation |
| Endpoint | HSV isolation from oral gargle specimens by centrifugal inoculation of primary human amnion cells |
| Result | HSV was isolated from 47 of 637 oral-cavity specimens. Isolation increased during common cold and oral trauma and often paralleled labialis, but was not fully explained by contamination from lesions. |
| Main limitation | Very small cohort and culture-based method; oral excretion does not measure transmission probability. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that infectious HSV can be recovered from the oral cavity of some subjects with recurrent labialis.
- It can answer that recovery can occur outside obvious lesion contamination under the reported culture method.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define causal triggers for recurrence.
- It cannot estimate person-to-person transmission probability.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating oral excretion as direct measured transmission or treating culture-negative periods as absence of viral DNA.

## Vault conclusion
Spruance 1984 supports an HSV-1 oral excretion rung for recurrent labialis. It does not prove the full recurrence trigger or transmission chain.
