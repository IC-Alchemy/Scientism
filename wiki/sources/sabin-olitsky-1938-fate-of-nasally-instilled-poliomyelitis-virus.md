## What the paper can answer
- It can answer how recoverable poliomyelitis virus was distributed over time in the specifically sampled tissues after intranasal instillation in rhesus monkeys.
- It can answer that the abstract-level record did not show recoverable virus in nasal mucosa or olfactory bulbs during the first 48 hours after instillation in normal animals.
- It can answer that recoverable virus later appeared in olfactory bulbs and persisted there after it was no longer detected in nasal mucosa.
- It can answer that the paper explicitly treated host-to-host transmission as an unresolved problem worth discussing, not as a settled conclusion already demonstrated by the reported tissue-recovery sequence.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot, from the abstract alone, establish actual host-to-host transmission success or failure between animals.
- It cannot establish the whole early poliomyelitis literature as a record of repeated natural-route failure.
- It cannot justify a vault claim that only invasive inoculation methods ever worked in poliomyelitis experiments.
- It cannot answer ordinary human epidemic transmission in full, because the experiment is a rhesus-monkey intranasal instillation model with sampled tissue recovery endpoints.

## Interpretive boundaries
- The core endpoint named in the abstract is recovery of infective virus from selected tissues over time, not direct monkey-to-monkey transmission success.
- "The bearing of these observations on the problem of host to host transmission" means the paper is relevant to transmission arguments, but the abstract does not itself report a completed host-to-host challenge series.
- The abstract supports a narrow timing claim: early intranasal placement did not yield recoverable virus in nasal mucosa or olfactory bulbs for the first 48 hours, with later recovery in olfactory bulbs as clinical signs approached.
- The abstract does not support large aggregate claims such as "hundreds of failed experiments" or "every natural route failed."

Abstract is adequate for a cautious source-page ingest focused on the named tissue-recovery endpoints and transmission-boundary claims. Full text is still needed for exact procedures, denominator details, and any discussion the paper gives about host-to-host transmission.

## API abstract
With a method of intranasal instillation of poliomyelitis virus that brings about infection of all M. rhesus monkeys subjected to it, a study was undertaken of the fate of nasally instilled virus in normal and convalescent, immune animals. Control experiments revealed that nasal mucosa of normal monkeys contained no observable antiviral factors and that when five or ten minimal cerebral infective doses were added to the mucosa, virus could be detected by the employed procedure. In the olfactory bulbs even a single infective dose could be recovered, since suspensions of both bulbs could be transferred to the brain of a monkey without any loss of material. After nasal instillation of virus in normal monkeys, it disappeared quickly (4 hours or less) and could be recovered neither from the excised nasal mucosa nor from the olfactory bulbs during the first 48 hours. At 72 hours, just before or coincident with the first rise of temperature, virus was found in very small amounts in the nasal mucosa and for the first time also in the olfactory bulbs. At 96 hours, at least 3 days before the appearance of nervous signs, and later, while virus continued to be present in considerable amounts in the olfactory bulbs (and presumably elsewhere in the central nervous system), none was detected in the nasal mucosa. In convalescent, immune animals receiving the same strain of virus intranasally which caused the original infection, none could be recovered from the nasal mucosa or central nervous system at 4 hours, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 7 days. The bearing of these observations on the problem of host to host transmission of poliomyelitis virus is discussed.

## Why it matters for failed natural-route or host-to-host transmission claims
- This paper is stronger than a title-only lead because the abstract preserves an actual negative or non-confirmatory component: after intranasal instillation that reportedly infected the monkeys, recoverable virus was absent from the sampled tissues during the first 48 hours.
- That makes the paper relevant to a narrower methodological point in [[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]]: the experimental path from intranasal exposure to a broader host-to-host transmission claim was not straightforward even within this primary paper.
- It also matters for [[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]] because a tissue-recovery sequence in an animal model can be overextended into a broader natural-causation story if the endpoint boundary is not kept explicit.
