# Viral Load Drives Disease in Humans Experimentally Infected with Respiratory Syncytial Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: dose-escalation validation of the adult wild-type RSV human challenge model with a GMP-manufactured clinical-strain inoculum; supplies the dose-finding/inoculum-purification element of GAP 1 (RSV) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]]. It is the RSV analogue of [[sources/memoli-2015-h1n1pdm09-human-challenge-model-validation]] and, like Memoli 2015, has **no sham or placebo arm** - the placebo-controlled element is supplied by [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]].
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine*, 2010;182(10):1305-1314 (epub 9 July 2010)
- Authors: John P. DeVincenzo, Tiffany Wilkinson, Arun Vaishnaw, Steven Cehelsky, Rachel Meyers, Shanta Nochur, Laura Harrison, Paul Meeking, Arnold Mann, Elena Moane, John Oxford, Rajat Pareek, Robert Moore, Edward Walsh, Robert Studholme, Philip Dorsett, Roberto Alvarez, Robert Lambkin-Williams
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article (PMC3001267), rendered page text extracted end to end, including Methods, Results, Discussion, and references

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Open-label dose-escalation experimental-infection (challenge) study in healthy adult volunteers; five sequential cohorts (A-E) inoculated with escalating inoculum (about 3.0-5.4 log10 PFU/person, a ~500-fold range) of the same GMP wild-type inoculum; 13-day quarantine with twice-daily sampling; single-center design (Memphis, TN and London, UK ethics approvals) |
| Subject / sample | 36 healthy volunteers 18-45 years (mean age 26.7, 44% women) selected from 523 screened; inclusion required serum RSV microneutralization titers in the lowest ~third of the screening distribution; one withdrew (35 virologically evaluated); all PCR-negative for a panel of respiratory viruses on Day -1; no asthma, no recent respiratory illness |
| Intervention or exposure | Intranasal drops (0.5 mL/naris) of wild-type RSV-A Memphis 37, isolated from an infant hospitalized with bronchiolitis, plaque-picked, passaged five times, and GMP-manufactured in Vero cells. Quote on inoculum purification: identity confirmed "by an immunofluorescent antibody assay, electron microscopy, and N-gene sequencing"; freedom from adventitious agents tested by (1) 28-day culture in five indicator cell lines under RSV-neutralizing conditions, (2) product-enhanced reverse transcriptase assay, (3) PCR panels for human pathogens, (4) electron microscopy. Each cohort received a plaque-assay-quantified aliquot of the same inoculum |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no**. Quote: "All volunteers were inoculated with RSV" - there is no placebo-inoculated or non-inoculated group, and no randomization; cohort assignment was sequential. Within-trial contrast is only infected vs uninfected outcome classification after inoculation |
| Endpoint | Named exactly in the methods: infection defined as "RSV was detected in respiratory secretions at two or more sequential time points, the first occurring between Days 2 and 8 (inclusive) after inoculation"; viral load endpoints: peak viral load, duration of shedding, and area under the curve (AUC) viral load, measured by quantitative HEp-2 plaque assay (log10 PFU/mL, LOD 1.7 log PFU/mL) and qRT-PCR N-gene assay (log10 PFU equivalents/mL); disease endpoints: twice-daily subject-reported symptom score card, once-daily physician-directed physical examination score, and 24-hour nasal mucus weights; safety: adverse events through Day 28 |
| Result | 27/35 (77%) met the infection definition by qPCR; 21/35 (60%) by quantitative culture; incubation period 3.1 (+/-1.5) days; mean shedding duration 7.4 (+/-2.5) days by qPCR with 40.7% still qPCR-positive on Day 12. Infection rate, viral loads, and disease were similar across the ~500-fold inoculum range (no dose response). Infected volunteers had higher symptom scores (P=0.021) and mucus weights (P=0.0056) than uninfected; within infected volunteers, increasing viral load correlated with all three disease measures (P<0.0001 each), and peak viral load timing coincided with peak symptom severity. Disease stayed upper-respiratory; no serious adverse events; one volunteer developed bilateral otitis media |
| Main limitation | Stated by the authors in discussion: the volunteers were not immunologically naive to RSV ("there is no such thing as an immunologically RSV-naive adult"), only the low-titer third of adults was studied, and observed disease was upper-airway only, so "when extrapolating these results to natural disease in the pediatric population, certain potential differences should be considered." From this vault's standard: no comparator arm of any kind, so all rate statements describe inoculated persons only; small n per cohort |

## What the experiment can answer
- That a GMP-purified, plaque-quantified wild-type RSV-A clinical strain delivered by intranasal drops reproducibly infects ~77% of low-antibody adults (qPCR definition) and produces self-limited upper-respiratory illness, independent of inoculum size across ~3.0-5.4 log10 PFU.
- That within this model, quantitative viral load (peak, duration, AUC) is positively associated with symptom scores, physical examination findings, and mucus output, with coincident viral and disease kinetics.
- That qPCR detects shedding for substantially longer than quantitative culture in the same samples - a PCR-vs-infectiousness boundary the authors themselves flag as unresolved.

## What it cannot answer
- Whether inoculation-route infection equals natural-route transmission; no contact exposure occurred.
- Whether the model reproduces infant bronchiolitis; disease was restricted to the adult upper respiratory tract, and the authors explicitly discuss why naive-infant extrapolation is not direct.
- Any causal rate comparison; without a non-inoculated group, nothing here separates challenge exposure from background respiratory illness except the quarantine protocol and Day -1 screening.
- Causality of the viral-load/disease association; the correlation is consistent and repeated but observational within inoculated volunteers, not an intervention on viral load (later antiviral trials, e.g. [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]], test that directly).

## Later inferential-leap risk
- The title claim "viral load drives disease" is causal language for what is demonstrated here as a correlation under inoculation; the direct causal test (drug-reduced viral load with reduced symptoms under placebo control) is the later placebo-controlled trials.
- The authors' leap: "Human experimental wild-type RSV infection described here can inform us well regarding the pathogenesis of natural human infections, even those occurring in infants and children" - an adult low-titer, upper-airway model is offered as informative for naive-infant lower-airway disease; the authors hedge but the leap is stated.
- The extended qPCR-shedding result ("40.7% still RSV qPCR positive on Study Day 12") feeds PCR-based infectiousness claims; the paper itself notes culture-negative/qPCR-positive dissociation and that "these and other explanations deserve to be evaluated."

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the controlled-human-challenge (dose-finding) rung for RSV: a purified GMP wild-type clinical-strain inoculum reproducibly infects low-antibody adults and produces upper-respiratory disease correlated with viral load, across a wide dose range with no dose response. No comparator arm exists; the placebo-controlled rung for RSV rests on [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]]. Infant bronchiolitis causation and natural-route transmission remain separate rungs ([[synthesis/bronchiolitis-rsv-evidence-ladder]]).
