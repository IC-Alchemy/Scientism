# The Survival of Variola and Vaccinia Viruses in the Lungs of Mice Previously Infected with Variola

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary follow-on variola/vaccinia lung-recovery paper
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1940
- Author: J. B. Nelson
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Reinfection, tissue-recovery, histology, and cross-protection experiment in mice |
| Subject / sample | Mice previously exposed intranasally to variola virus and normal control mice; vaccinia challenge in a subset |
| Exposure or intervention | Reintranasal exposure to variola after recovery; later intranasal vaccinia challenge in mice previously infected with variola |
| Comparator / control | Previously variola-exposed mice compared with normal mice challenged under similar conditions |
| Endpoint | Variola or vaccinia recovery from lung suspensions, tissue titer, lung histology, symptoms, and mortality after vaccinia challenge |
| Result | After variola reinfection, virus was recovered from previously exposed mice early, but by day 5 most recovered-mouse lung suspensions were virus-free while normal controls still yielded virus. Prior variola exposure partly reduced vaccinia severity and lung virus compared with normal vaccinia controls, but did not prevent vaccinia disease. |
| Main limitation | The experiment measures lung survival/recovery and partial cross-protection in mice, not human smallpox disease or transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that prior variola exposure changed later variola recovery kinetics in mouse lungs.
- It can answer that prior variola exposure gave incomplete protection against intranasal vaccinia challenge in mice.
- It can answer a local tissue-recovery and cross-immunity question.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human variola transmission or disease causation.
- It cannot establish full immunity between variola and vaccinia across species and exposure routes.
- It cannot turn mouse lung recovery into a clinical smallpox endpoint.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using lung-recovery kinetics as if they were a complete causal model for smallpox. The paper is useful for separating survival, recovery, pathology, and partial immunity endpoints.

## Vault conclusion
Nelson 1940 supports a mouse lung-recovery and partial cross-protection rung. It should be linked to the smallpox ladder as route/host-boundary evidence.
