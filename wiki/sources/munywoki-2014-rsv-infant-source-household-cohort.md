# The Source of Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection in Infants: A Household Cohort Study in Rural Kenya

## Source type
- Role in this vault: prospective household cohort tying named exposure (co-residence in a household with serially detected RSV infections), infection markers (real-time PCR on nasopharyngeal swabs from every member every 3-4 days, plus RSV G-gene sequencing), and infection timing (episodes defined by sample dates) in the same people; fills GAP 3 for RSV in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] (the second full-text GAP 3 row in the vault after the influenza row).
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 2014;209(11):1685-1692 (online 2013-12-23; CC-BY 3.0)
- Authors: Patrick K. Munywoki, Dorothy C. Koech, Charles N. Agoti, Clement Lewa, Patricia A. Cane, Graham F. Medley, D. James Nokes - KEMRI-Wellcome Trust Research Programme, Kilifi, Kenya, and University of Warwick
- Funding/ethics: Wellcome Trust grants 090853 and 084633 (funder had no role); Kenyan National Ethical Review Committee and University of Warwick Biomedical Research Ethical Committee
- Source basis in hand: complete open-access full text from Europe PMC (PMC4017365), including Methods, Results, Discussion, Tables 1-4, and figure captions.

> [!note] Note

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Household-based prospective cohort; rural coastal Kenya inside the Kilifi Health and Demographic Surveillance System (KHDSS); households recruited November-December 2009, intensive sampling 11 January - 4 June 2010 (24 weeks), timed to cover one full RSV epidemic season; no randomization, no intervention |
| Subjects | 60 households (596 participants) recruited, 13 households (103 participants) lost to follow-up, 3 excluded (infrequently sampled infant) - analysis on 44 households, 451 participants (44 study infants + 407 other members). Households required an infant born after 1 April 2009 (post-2008-09 season) and >=1 older sibling <13 y; median 8 members; infants median age 4.2 months (range 13 days-9.9 months); 47.7% male infants |
| Exposure / surveillance | Co-residence with serial infection detection: deep nasopharyngeal swabs (Copan flocked) from **all household occupants every 3-4 days regardless of symptoms** (4-week weekly run-in), plus illness data at each visit; 15,396 of 17,985 expected NPS collected (85.6%); infants ~100% collected, siblings 94.3%, mothers 98.8%, other adults 64.3%; weekly swabs of the field team (none infected - a built-in "outside-introducer" control) |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **no** - a natural cohort cannot have sham infection, and the paper has no comparison-household arm; the internal contrast is source-of-infection classification per Table 1 definitions: primary case (first individual episode in a household episode), household outbreak (>1 individual episode), within- vs outside-household acquisition, coprimary (concurrently first). Quote (methods): "Households were not enrolled if >=1 individual refused to participate... sampling visits were timed to begin and end coincident with the start and finish of the expected RSV season" |
| Endpoint (named exactly) | **RSV infection episode**: individual episode = PCR-positive specimens (in-house real-time multiplex PCR, cycle threshold <=35) for the same RSV group (A or B) with <=14 days separating positives; household episode = >=1 individual episode with no >=14-day negative gap; household outbreak = >1 individual episode; infant infection source = temporal ordering of episodes in the household, checked by sequencing the RSV attachment (G) gene ectodomain (648 nt for A, 732 nt for B) of primary-case/infant pairs |
| Result | 84.1% of households (37/44) and 38.4% of individuals (173/451) had >=1 RSV episode; 73 separate household introductions, of which 32 (43.8%) produced a household outbreak; the study infant was infected in 28 of the 32 outbreaks (87.5%). Of 44 infants, 28 (63.6%) were infected during one season (21 once, 5 twice, 2 three times). Of the 28 first infant episodes: **15 (53.6%) acquired within the household, 9 (32.1%) from outside, 4 (14.3%) inconclusive** (infant and sibling coprimary). G-gene sequencing: 10/15 within-household primary-infant pairs showed complete nucleotide homology (the other 5 failed sequencing at high Ct, a low-viral-load proxy); 3/4 coprimary pairs identical. The 15 within-household primary cases: 11 older children (10 of 11 school-attending; mean age 6.9 y), 1 mother, 1 father, 2 other adults. No household or individual characteristic distinguished households with vs without infant infection (Table 4, all P>.05) |
| Main limitation | Authors state the sequence of observations constrains attribution: source classification rests on temporal ordering under 3-4-day sampling (the "primary case" is the first *detected*, not necessarily the first *infected*); sequencing failed in 5/15 pairs and 1/4 coprimary sets (high Ct); 3 excluded households had infrequently sampled infants; other adults were sampled on only 64.3% of expected visits; single season, single rural site; the endpoint is infection (PCR), so the study measures RSV *transmission*, not bronchiolitis or other disease outcomes |

## What the experiment can answer
- That in 44 rural Kenyan households under twice-to-thrice-weekly PCR surveillance of every member across one RSV season, most households were infected (84.1%), 63.6% of infants acquired at least one PCR-defined RSV infection, and roughly half of infant infections were acquired from a household member while about a third arrived from outside the household.
- That within-household introductions to infants came predominantly from older children (11 of 15 primary cases), mostly school-attending, with sequence homology between the introducing and infant viruses in 10/10 successfully sequenced pairs.
- That the timing structure of episodes (episodes defined on sample dates, <=14-day clustering within RSV groups) is consistent with a household transmission chain anchored on PCR-confirmed infections, not on self-reported illness (contrast [[sources/kim-2012-kenya-household-pdm09-transmission-cohort]], whose secondary endpoint was reported ILI).

## What it cannot answer
- Disease causation: the endpoint is PCR-confirmed infection, not bronchiolitis or clinical RSV disease; illness data were collected (20.9% of all specimens collected during symptoms; 52.1% of infant samples) but transmission attribution is infection-based.
- Route of transmission (droplet, fomite, aerosol): co-residence bundles all routes; no separation was attempted.
- Attack rates outside this demographic (median household size 8, 55% of members <15 y) or this season's strain mix (groups A and B co-circulated).
- Whether targeting school children (the "source of infection" implication) would actually protect infants - no intervention was tested.

## Inferential leaps to watch
- The stated purpose - to inform RSV vaccination strategy ("The Source of Respiratory Syncytial Virus Infection In Infants") - imports a policy question the study cannot test: 44 households, one season, no intervention, and a source-classification method that is an inference from temporal ordering plus (in 10/15 cases) sequence identity. The paired editorial commentary ("Protecting the Family to Protect the Child: Vaccination Strategy Guided by RSV Transmission Dynamics", JID 2014;209:1679-1681) is commentary, not evidence, and is not used here.
- "Primary case" language reads as causation ("who infected the infant"); what was measured is who was PCR-positive first, with identical G-gene sequence in 10 pairs as support. First-detection ordering under 3-4-day sampling can misrank true onsets.
- The 84.1% household-infection figure is sometimes generalized as RSV's household attack potential; it is one season in a crowded rural population with an infant-anchored sampling design.
