# Profitable Sacred Cows That Are Clearly Wrong but Profitable Continue Getting Fu

## TL;DR
This raw file is mostly a user-and-AI exchange about hypothetical statistical distortion. It is low-signal as evidence, but it does point to a real methodological category: denominator quality and under-reporting can affect risk-benefit analysis if the underlying claims are true.

> Warning: This page is for data-quality research only. It is not medical advice.

## Source triage
- Type: AI chat and commentary
- Signal: low
- Limitation: the note does not document the underlying dataset carefully enough to verify the opening claim, and most of the later text is hypothetical analysis layered on top of it

## What the source claims
- Some state-level vaccination tallies for older adults may exceed the apparent population denominator.
- If numerator inflation and harm undercounting both occur, statistical analysis of vaccine safety or effectiveness could be distorted.

## What question the underlying evidence would need to answer
- Whether the reported numerator and denominator values are accurate and comparable.
- Whether the discrepancy is due to reporting lag, population-estimate mismatch, cross-jurisdiction counting, duplicate records, or genuine error.
- Whether adverse-event under-reporting exists at the scale claimed.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- The methodological point is valid in principle: distorted numerators and denominators can mislead analysis.
- This raw file is not strong enough to establish that the specific vaccination-count discrepancy or under-reporting magnitude is real.
- It should be treated as a prompt to gather auditable datasets, not as evidence that the distortion has already been demonstrated.
