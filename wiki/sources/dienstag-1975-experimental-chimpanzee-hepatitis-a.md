# Experimental infection of chimpanzees with hepatitis A virus (Dienstag et al. 1975)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: chimpanzee challenge rung for [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]] — one of the named acquisition gaps, now at abstract level
- Journal/year: *Journal of Infectious Diseases*, 1975
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text still not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Controlled experimental infection in seronegative animals, with route pairing and a housing-exposure arm |
| Subject / sample | Four seronegative infant chimpanzees, plus two additional seronegative chimpanzees for inoculum titration |
| Intervention or exposure | Two animals inoculated with HA-Ag-positive acute-phase stool filtrate (rich in 27-nm particles) — one intravenously, one orally; two control animals inoculated with HA-Ag-negative preinfection stool filtrate |
| Comparator / control | HA-Ag-negative filtrate controls, pair-matched by route; later exposure of controls to the infected pair |
| Endpoint | Biochemical, histologic, and clinical evidence of acute viral hepatitis; 27-nm particle shedding in stools by IEM; convalescent anti-HA antibody |
| Result | One month after inoculation, both animals receiving HA-Ag-positive filtrate developed biochemical, histologic, and clinical hepatitis. 27-nm particles were detected in their stools with a shedding pattern similar to human volunteers. Convalescent sera developed anti-HA antibody by IEM. Control animals remained free of illness at that time but developed hepatitis three to five weeks after exposure to the two infected chimpanzees. The infectious inoculum was titrated in two further seronegative chimpanzees. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; n = 2 per arm; infant animals; artificial inoculum preparation; the hepatitis endpoint in chimpanzees is mild and not human clinical disease; full histology and titration detail need the text. |

## What the abstract can answer
- Whether HA-Ag-positive (but not HA-Ag-negative) stool filtrate transmits hepatitis to seronegative chimpanzees by both intravenous and oral routes: yes, in this small controlled set — with control animals ill only after housing exposure.
- Whether shedding resembles the human pattern: the abstract states the pattern was similar.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Histology plates, titration endpoints, exact dose, and the timecourse of the housing-exposure transmission — the onward-transmission rung is only sketched in the abstract.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The abstract supports chimpanzee transmission of an HA-Ag-positive filtrate agent. The ladder's boundary stands: chimpanzee challenge is not ordinary human natural-route proof, and the housing-exposure result is a single small observation, not a transmission-chain reconstruction.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: HA-Ag-positive stool filtrate, but not HA-Ag-negative filtrate, produced hepatitis in seronegative chimpanzees by both tested routes, with stool shedding and seroconversion, and control animals became ill only after housing with infected animals — a controlled chimpanzee rung, still pending full text.
