# Correlation between Detection of Herpes Simplex Virus in Oral Secretions by PCR and Susceptibility to Experimental UV Radiation-Induced Herpes Labialis

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary experimental UV-reactivation model paper with HSV oral secretion PCR/culture endpoints
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Microbiology*, 1994
- Authors: J. D. Kriesel and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental UV radiation reactivation model with oral secretion testing |
| Subject / sample | 25 patients |
| Exposure or intervention | Ultraviolet radiation exposure to induce herpes labialis |
| Comparator / control | Lesion developers compared with non-developers; PCR compared with culture |
| Endpoint | HSV detection in oral secretions by culture and PCR |
| Result | HSV was detected in 5 of 12 lesion developers by culture and 8 of 12 by PCR. Oral shedding did not increase before UV-induced lesions. |
| Main limitation | Small short paper; oral secretions were tested rather than lesion swabs, and the model does not represent all natural sunlight recurrences. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that PCR detected HSV in oral secretions more often than culture in this UV-reactivation model.
- It can answer that increased pre-lesion oral shedding was not observed in this tested setup.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot define natural recurrence risk across populations.
- It cannot prove lesion pathogenesis from oral secretion data alone.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating PCR positivity as infectious virus or treating a UV model as all natural recurrence.

## Vault conclusion
Kriesel et al. 1994 supports a UV-reactivation and PCR-versus-culture detection rung for herpes labialis.
