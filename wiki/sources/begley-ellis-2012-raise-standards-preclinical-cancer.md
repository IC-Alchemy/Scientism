# Drug development: Raise standards for preclinical cancer research

## Source type
- Role in this vault: replication-boundary lead for cancer biology; the best-known source for the "about 10%" preclinical-cancer replication figure
- Genre: Nature Commentary (perspective/comment genre), 2012
- Authors: C. Glenn Begley (Amgen) and Lee M. Ellis (MD Anderson Cancer Center)
- Source basis in hand: PubMed record; the commentary itself. The per-study replication data were not published in inspectable detail.

## What the source claims
- Amgen scientists attempted to reproduce the findings of 53 landmark preclinical cancer studies — studies that had been influential and often replicated in name — and could confirm only 6 (about 11%) even after contacting original authors, sharing data, and repeating experiments under the original authors' exact conditions.
- The commentary attributes the failures to non-blinded experiments, failure to repeat studies, selective reporting of positive results, and focusing on findings that fit the expected story rather than controls.

## Direct evidence present
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Industry internal replication program, described retrospectively in a commentary |
| Subject / sample | 53 published preclinical cancer studies selected by Amgen for validation before drug programs were built on them |
| Intervention or exposure | Independent re-running of the original experiments, including repeating conditions specified in consultation with original authors |
| Comparator / control | Each attempted replication against the original published result |
| Endpoint | Whether the original finding could be confirmed under the reproduced conditions |
| Result | 6 of 53 findings confirmed (about 11%), as stated in the commentary |
| Main limitation | This is a commentary, not a data paper. The list of studies, the replication protocols, and the per-study outcomes were not published in inspectable detail, so the project does not fully meet this vault's inspectability standard even though it is widely cited. |

## What the source can answer
- As a historical document: that an industrial drug-discovery group reported, at a specific time, that landmark preclinical cancer findings rarely survived internal validation, and that this report shaped subsequent reproducibility initiatives.
- It cannot by itself establish a field-wide replication rate, because the underlying data are not inspectable.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- We can conclude that the ~11% cancer-biology figure exists as a reported internal replication experience, not as a published systematic replication study. The figure should be attributed as such whenever used.
- The vault treats the claim as unresolved pending any fuller published account of the underlying project.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap is citing "only 11% of cancer studies replicate" as if it came from a controlled replication project like [[sources/osc-2015-estimating-reproducibility-psychological-science]]. It did not; it comes from a first-person commentary describing an internal program whose data were not released.
