# Efficacy of a Heat-Inactivated Hepatitis B Vaccine in Male Homosexuals (Coutinho et al. 1983)

> [!warning] Warning
> This page records what one experiment shows for vault analysis. It is not medical advice and makes no vaccination recommendation.

## Bibliographic record

- Authors: Coutinho RA, Lelie N, Albrecht-van Lent P, Reerink-Brongers EE, Stoutjesdijk L, Dees P, Nivard J, Huisman J, Reesink HW (Municipal Health Service Amsterdam; Central Laboratory of the Netherlands Red Cross Blood Transfusion Service; Municipal Health Service Rotterdam).
- Citation: BMJ (Clin Res Ed) 1983;286(6374):1305-1308 (April 23, 1983).
- PMID 6404440; PMCID PMC1547658; DOI 10.1136/bmj.286.6374.1305.
- Support: Netherlands Foundation for Preventive Medicine grant 28-440. An ethics committee decided the code-break date; "an independent doctor to whom hepatitis B virus infections were reported was the only one who had access to the code."

## Study type

Randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind vaccine-efficacy trial with life-table (actuarial) attack-rate analysis, Desu survival comparison, chi-square, t-test, ANOVA, and Greenwood-formula z-tests. Follow-up 21.5 months; trial end point 645 days after the first injection.

## Subjects

Homosexual men at high risk, screened from June 1980 by the Municipal Health Service, Amsterdam (serology program background: among 2,946 Dutch homosexual men, 60.3% had past/present HBV infection markers and annual attack rate 27.6% among 316 susceptible men). Inclusion: age 16-50, negative for HBsAg, anti-HBs, and anti-HBc, ALT < 50 IU/l, no serious illness, at least two different male sexual partners in the preceding six months, informed consent. Between November 10, 1980 and December 31, 1981, 835 men entered; 35 were excluded because HBV markers appeared by the first injection; 800 analyzed: 397 vaccine / 403 placebo. Lost to follow-up: 19 vaccine / 16 placebo. Dosing-interval checks: first-to-second injection 30.5-30.7 days (target 30.5), first-to-third 60.4-61.6 days (target 61).

## Exposure / intervention

Heat-inactivated plasma-derived HBsAg vaccine (CLB, Netherlands Red Cross Blood Transfusion Service): pooled HBsAg-positive plasma of subtypes ad and ay, heated 90 seconds at 101.0-104.5 degrees C, adsorbed to aluminium phosphate and pasteurised 10 hours at 65 degrees C; one dose = 3 micrograms HBsAg adsorbed to 0.6 mg aluminium phosphate; three intramuscular injections at monthly intervals. Quality control per WHO proposed requirements.

## Comparator / control

Sham arm: YES - adjuvant-matched placebo (identical aluminium phosphate content, antigen replaced by human albumin).

> "One dose of the placebo consisted of 600 ug human albumin adsorbed to 0.6 mg aluminium phosphate. Vaccine and placebo were indistinguishable and were stored at 4°C."

Recorded: this is an aluminium-phosphate-matched comparator, not an inert saline placebo (same comparator class as the AAHS-matched placebos in [[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]] and [[sources/future-i-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-anogenital-diseases-rct]]; see [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]]).

## Blinding and endpoint adjudication conduct

"The trial was conducted double blind." Anti-HBs measurements from month 2 onward "were not made available until the code of the trial was broken." Infections "were classified by an independent committee before the code of the trial was broken and without access to the measurements of anti-HBs." Monthly blood samples for five months, then every three months; every sample tested for HBsAg, anti-HBc, and ALT.

## Endpoint (named exactly)

Definite infections - "HBsAg or anti-HBc or both were present in at least three sequential blood samples," subclassified:

1. Hepatitis B: "HBsAg present in at least one blood sample and serum alanine transferase activity > 21 IU/l in at least two sequential blood samples taken one to three weeks apart with at least one sample with activity > 50 IU/l" - the clinical-disease endpoint, enzyme-confirmed (upper normal limit 20 IU/l).
2. HBsAg-positive infection: HBsAg present in at least one sample.
3. Anti-HBc-positive infection: only anti-HBc present, HBsAg always absent.

Possible infections: anti-HBc in two sequential samples followed by one or more samples negative for both HBsAg and anti-HBc. This is a serology-plus-liver-enzyme endpoint set; no biopsy, jaundice verification, or symptom scoring is part of the case definition.

## Result

By the code break (September 1, 1982): 69 definite and 4 possible infections total. Placebo arm 56 infections (31 HBsAg-positive); vaccine arm 17 (9 HBsAg-positive). Life-table attack rates at the 645-day end point (Table II):

| Category | Vaccine attack rate | Placebo attack rate | p | Protective efficacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hepatitis B (clinical, ALT-confirmed) | 1.5% (n=5) | 7.7% (n=23) | <0.001 | 80.7% |
| All HBsAg-positive infections | 2.7% (n=9) | 16.9% (n=31) | <0.003 | 84.3% |
| Anti-HBc-positive infections | 1.6% (n=6) | 7.4% (n=23) | <0.005 | 78.5% |
| All definite infections | 4.2% (n=15) | 23.1% (n=54) | <0.0001 | 81.8% |
| All infections (possible included) | 4.8% (n=17) | 23.8% (n=56) | <0.0001 | 79.7% |

Breakthrough pattern: "All nine HBsAg-positive infections were found in subjects who had not (yet) formed anti-HBs"; the seven anti-HBc-positive infections and the possible infection "occurred in vaccinated subjects who had low titres of anti-HBs (>1 and <10 mIU)" and "none of these men had any clinical symptoms."

Anti-HBs response: 33.1% at month 1 (GMT 3.5 mIU), 72.3% at month 2, 87.4% at month 3, maximum 89% at month 4 (46.3% >100 mIU); 85.7% still anti-HBs-positive at month 17; GMT peaked at 107.6 mIU (month 5), declining to 47.1 mIU at month 17.

Safety: soreness at the injection site 65.4% vaccine vs 44.5% placebo after dose 1 (p<0.001), 39.8% vs 24.7% and 32.1% vs 15.1% after doses 2 and 3, subsiding within one to two days. "The vaccine had no serious side effects." (The paper gives no numbered serious-adverse-event table; n=800 over 21.5 months bounds what this sentence can support.)

Baseline balance: mean age 30.7 vs 30.1; dosing intervals on target; "For most characteristics both groups were similar (table I); only the percentage of men giving a history of jaundice was higher in the vaccine group (p < 0.025)." (OCR of Table I is partially garbled; other cells are not quoted here.)

## Main limitation

The dominant endpoints are serologically defined infections (HBsAg/anti-HBc seroconversion); the clinical-disease endpoint (hepatitis B with ALT confirmation) covers the smaller subset (5 vs 23 cases). Trial window 21.5 months; 19/16 participants lost to follow-up; history-of-jaundice imbalance at baseline; population restricted to high-risk homosexual men in a low-endemic country; no all-cause endpoint; rare-serious-event power not stated for the "no serious side effects" sentence.

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills

GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint) - first hepatitis B row in the vault, and a new pathogen on the rung (existing GAP 4 pathogens: pneumococcus, rotavirus, HPV, SARS-CoV-2, RSV, influenza, pertussis, measles). It gives the intervention rung to [[synthesis/hepatitis-b-hepatitis-b-virus-evidence-ladder]], which previously had no vaccination rung. The named ideal anchor for this rung, Szmuness et al. 1980 (NEJM 303:833-841), remains full-text-unreachable; the Coutinho trial is the independent placebo-controlled same-era trial of a different plasma-derived vaccine, and it names the Szmuness result in its own Discussion (40 ug Merck formaldehyde-inactivated vaccine, 81.4% protective efficacy at 735 days) - that secondary citation is not treated as evidence for the Szmuness trial here.

## Inferential leaps recorded

- "The vaccine had no serious side effects" from 800 men followed 21.5 months - the trial cannot bound rare serious events and reports no numbered SAE table; the sentence is a category claim the experiment does not power.
- "We conclude that the heat inactivated hepatitis B virus vaccine ... is immunogenic, has no serious side effects, and offers good protection" - generalized beyond the enrolled high-risk population and the 21.5-month window; duration beyond month 17 and booster effect are explicitly under study, not established ("The effect of a booster dose in male homosexuals is now being studied").
- The Discussion compares protective efficacy across the Szmuness (81.4%, 40 ug dose) and CDC Francis (55.9%, 20 ug dose) trials - cross-trial narrative comparison of different vaccines, doses, populations, and endpoint lengths, not a head-to-head experiment.
- The low-responder protection claim ("even vaccinated subjects with a low antibody response ... were found to be protected from HBsAg-positive infections") rests on seven anti-HBc-positive breakthroughs among low responders with no symptoms - a small subgroup.

## What this wiki currently concludes

Source-reported: in HBV-marker-negative high-risk homosexual men, three 3-microgram doses of a heat-inactivated plasma-derived HBsAg vaccine given against an aluminium-phosphate-matched placebo reduced life-table attack rates at 645 days for clinical hepatitis B (1.5% vs 7.7%) and for all serologically defined HBV infections (4.8% vs 23.8%, 79.7% protective efficacy), with every HBsAg-positive vaccine-arm infection occurring in a vaccinee who had not yet seroconverted. Vault status: this is direct experimental intervention evidence consistent with an infectious-agent hypothesis for hepatitis B, with disease and infection endpoints named and adjudicated blinded to the code. It does not answer long-term protection, rare serious adverse events, all-cause outcomes, or protection in other populations, and the placebo's adjuvant-matching bounds inert-placebo systemic-safety inference.
