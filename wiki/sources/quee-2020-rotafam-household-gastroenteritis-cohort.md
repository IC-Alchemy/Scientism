# Quee 2020 - RotaFam: community burden and within-household transmission of norovirus and rotavirus gastroenteritis (prospective household cohort)

> [!note] Note
> Full text read and verified 2026-11-11 (loop run 23, queue item 45) from the complete 10-page publisher PDF (Elsevier typeset, "Downloaded for Anonymous User (n/a) at Utrecht University from ClinicalKey.com by Elsevier on July 07, 2026") retrieved from the Utrecht University Library repository (DSpace 7 REST API, item uuid ce5ecfe7-4945-4f6b-ada6-604a80bcec51, bitstream 1-s2.0-S147330992030058X.pdf, 511 KB) after Unpaywall flagged the repository copy; live Lancet/ScienceDirect is publisher-walled and the article has no PMCID. Citation confirmed against PubMed: PMID 32087775, Lancet Infect Dis 2020;20(5):598-606, published online February 20, 2020. Authors: Franke A Quee, Marieke L A de Hoog, Rob Schuurman, Patricia Bruijning-Verhagen (University Medical Centre Utrecht + RIVM). Funded by The Netherlands Organisation for Health Research and Development (ZonMw-VENI grant 2015 91616158); the funder had no role in study design, analysis, or the report. Every number below is from the article itself.

## What study was run

- **Study type:** prospective, household-based cohort study (observational; no intervention, no sham arm - recorded explicitly). Two winter seasons, January-March 2016 and January-March 2017; follow-up windows Jan 5-Jun 12, 2016 and Jan 3-Jun 11, 2017.
- **Subjects:** 30,660 households approached after computer-generated random selection from the nationally representative Dutch Population Register (eligible: >=3 members, >=1 child younger than 2 years, smartphone/tablet access); 1,011 responded (3%); **604 households / 2,298 individuals enrolled (2% of those approached)**. Mean household size 3.8; 642 (28%) participants aged 0-1 y, 356 (15%) 2-5 y, 96 (4%) 6-14 y, 1,204 (52%) adults (mean age 34). 66% of adults had higher vocational/university education (authors' recorded selection: 27% in the general population).
- **Exposure measurement:** the RotaFamApp smartphone application recorded, for EVERY household member, a daily symptom diary (diarrhoea, vomiting, nausea, loss of appetite, stomach ache/cramps, fever >38 C, or explicit none) for 10 consecutive weeks; 158,452 of 163,158 diaries (97%) completed. Onset of acute gastroenteritis (AGE) triggered automated requests for a stool sample from the case and from one randomly selected household member (control); enrolment-point stool screening of every member was added in the 2016 cohort only (1,091/1,112 = 98%).
- **Comparator/control:** none (natural cohort). There is no sham-exposure or placebo arm; the "control" is the sampled asymptomatic household member, used for asymptomatic-transmission estimation.
- **Laboratory endpoint definition:** stools tested by laboratory-developed rtPCR for norovirus GI and GII, rotavirus, adenovirus 40/41, and astrovirus in an ISO15189-accredited laboratory, with an internal non-human inhibition control spiked into every sample and positive/negative controls in every run; results reported qualitatively (presence/absence). No genotyping/sequencing was performed.
- **AGE endpoint named exactly (diary algorithms):** onset = diarrhoea and vomiting the same day; or diarrhoea or vomiting on 2 consecutive days with diarrhoea on >=1 day; or diarrhoea/vomiting on days 1 and 3 plus additional symptoms on 2 of 3 days with >=1 diarrhoea day (diarrhoea = >=3 watery or looser-than-normal stools/24 h). Episode end (re-entry at risk) = >=3 symptom-free days, >=7 days without diarrhoea, >=5 days without diarrhoea of which 2 symptom-free, or >=7 consecutive missing diary days.
- **Transmission endpoints named exactly:** (a) *secondary AGE episode* = any episode in a household member other than the index within **14 days** of index onset (epidemiological link, irrespective of microbiology; coprimaries = same-day cases treated as one index event; minimum primary-to-secondary interval 24 h based on minimum incubation estimates); (b) *symptomatic transmission* = the same pathogen detected by PCR in both index and secondary case; (c) *asymptomatic transmission* = the same virus detected in the index's stool and in the stool of an **asymptomatic** household member sampled within **7 days** of the index event. Severity = modified 20-point Vesikari score (health-care visit substituted for percentage dehydration), mild <8, moderate 9-10, severe >=11.
- **Power:** 70 index cases per pathogen planned for >=21 observed transmission events with CI width <=0.14, assuming ~0.03 weekly household rotavirus incidence and 400 households.

## Result

- 697 AGE episodes in 358 of 604 households (59%); stool obtained for 609/697 (87%) episodes, plus a household-control sample for 570 (82%). Overall AGE incidence 3.1/100 person-weeks (95% CI 2.9-3.3); household AGE incidence 11.7/100 household-weeks; highest in children <2 y.
- Enrolment screening (2016): any virus 166/1,091 (15%) - norovirus 8%, rotavirus 1%, astrovirus 4%, adenovirus 3%. Screen-positive was more common after AGE in the preceding 2-4 weeks (23.2% vs 12.9%, p=0.0003), from which the authors estimate prolonged shedding accounts for ~25% of all virus detections.
- AGE episodes: any virus 287/609 (47%); **norovirus 150/609 (25%)**, rotavirus 91/609 (15%), astrovirus 50 (8%), adenovirus 24 (4%); norovirus-rotavirus coinfection 7. Control household samples: 156/570 (27%) positive (norovirus 15%) - virus detected in symptomatic vs sampled asymptomatic members at 47% vs 27% (p<0.0001).
- Severity: rotavirus more severe than norovirus (mean modified Vesikari 7.7 vs 6.2 overall; severe >=11: 15/91 (17%) rotavirus vs 6/150 (4%) norovirus; all 27 severe cases were infants <2 y except none in older ages - severe episodes occurred exclusively in infants <2 y); health-care seeking rotavirus 22% vs norovirus 10%; no hospital admissions in the cohort.
- **Within-household transmission (Table 4, the study's primary output):**
  - Index events: 96 norovirus, 56 rotavirus (from 107 and 62 index cases; same-day cases merged).
  - Per-household secondary AGE rate: norovirus **35% (34/96)** vs rotavirus **46% (26/56)** (p=0.018).
  - Per-person secondary attack rate: norovirus **15% (37/244)** vs rotavirus **28% (33/120)** (p=0.0063).
  - Microbiologically confirmed *symptomatic* transmission: norovirus **10% (25/241)**, rotavirus **18% (21/119)** (households with unsampled secondary episodes excluded).
  - *Asymptomatic* transmission: norovirus **51% (52/102)** vs rotavirus **22% (12/55)** (p=0.0004) - half of sampled asymptomatic household members were PCR-positive for norovirus within a week of an index case.
  - Symptomatic transmission rates sat below the epidemiological secondary attack rates for every virus, which the authors attribute either to detection sensitivity or to some secondary episodes being infections with pathogens outside the four-virus panel.
- Norovirus AGE incidence was roughly stable across ages (adjusted 0.60-0.77/100 person-weeks in children, 0.76 in adults), i.e. norovirus disease affected all ages; rotavirus concentrated in 0-1 y.

## Main limitations (authors' own)

- Seasonal design (Jan-Mar): results "cannot be extrapolated to the rest of the year."
- 2% response rate with heavy high-education over-representation and high daycare attendance; incidence "might, therefore, not be representative for populations with lower day-care attendance."
- Some infections undetected and some detections possibly cross-contamination (they argue validated PCR performance makes substantial misclassification unlikely).
- **Attribution boundary, quoted: "because actual transmission processes cannot be directly observed, our method of estimation cannot correct for infections among household contacts that resulted from an unknown external source, rather than from household transmission. Similarly, some secondary cases might have been coprimary cases in which the index and household contact were both infected by a common source. Our method might therefore have overestimated within-household transmission to some extent."**
- Design boundary recorded by this vault in addition: "same virus" is GI/GII-level rtPCR without sequencing, so an "asymptomatic transmission" event can be a same-genogroup different-strain detection; only one asymptomatic household member per episode was sampled; and the asymptomatic-transmission denominator is sampled members, not all members at risk.

## Which gap and which ladder rung this fills

- **GAP 3 (prospective cohort with exposure + infection markers + disease timing in same people), first row for norovirus** - and in the same paper the **first GAP 3 row for rotavirus**. The norovirus ladder previously ended at intervention (Atmar 2011) and outbreak investigation (Arness 2000) with no natural-transmission cohort rung; the rotavirus ladder had "natural-transmission limits" only. This row adds household-exposure measurement with daily symptom timing and PCR confirmation in the same people, in an endemic (non-outbreak) high-income setting - the design distinction the authors themselves draw against prior outbreak-recruited household studies.
- Boundary against the respiratory GAP 3 rows: sampling was AGE-triggered (case + one random member), not serial-regardless-of-symptoms as in PHIRST/CASCADIA; asymptomatic infections are measured through the one sampled control per episode and the 2016 enrolment screen only.

## Inferential leaps recorded

- "Interventions aimed at reducing household transmission - for instance, through vaccination - could affect the population spread of these viruses": no intervention or population-spread measurement exists in this study.
- "The observation of substantial within-household transmission ... suggests a central role of the household in driving the epidemiology of rotavirus and norovirus acute gastroenteritis": direction of transmission is assumed from the 14-day window and same-pathogen PCR, not observed (the authors' own overestimation caveat quoted above).
- The asymmetric asymptomatic-transmission comparison (norovirus 51% vs rotavirus 22%) is read as a pathogen-level biological difference ("suggests that in an endemic setting, the ratio of asymptomatic to symptomatic norovirus infections is higher than the previously estimated 30%") - a single-season, single-sample-per-contact estimate without sequencing.
- Vaccine-policy use sentences ("can inform vaccine policy decisions and act as a baseline for impact evaluations") go beyond what a descriptive cohort without intervention arms can test.
