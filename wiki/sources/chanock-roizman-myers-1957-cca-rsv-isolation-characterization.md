# Recovery from Infants with Respiratory Illness of a Virus Related to Chimpanzee Coryza Agent I

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early primary isolation and tissue-culture characterization paper for viruses later classed with respiratory syncytial virus
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Hygiene*, 1957
- Authors: R. M. Chanock, B. Roizman, and R. Myers
- Source basis in hand: full-text scan used in this pass

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Virus-isolation and culture-characterization study |
| Subject / sample | Infants with severe lower respiratory illness; throat swab specimens |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural respiratory illness; inoculation of specimens into tissue culture |
| Comparator / control | Infants with nonrespiratory illness and antigenic comparisons against CCA, adenovirus, measles, mumps, croup-associated virus, PAP virus, and psittacosis/LGV group |
| Endpoint | Recovery of cytopathogenic agent and characterization in tissue culture |
| Result | Long virus was recovered from 1 of 41 bronchopneumonia patients and Snyder virus from 1 of 18 laryngotracheobronchitis patients. Both produced syncytial cytopathic effect and were antigenically indistinguishable from chimpanzee coryza agent. |
| Main limitation | The authors explicitly did not treat recovery as proof of causation for the associated illnesses; recovery was sparse. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that CCA-related syncytium-forming agents were recoverable from some infants with respiratory illness.
- It can answer culture behavior and antigenic relatedness to CCA under the tested methods.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that the recovered agents caused bronchiolitis or bronchopneumonia.
- It cannot quantify RSV burden or reproduce disease experimentally in infants.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating early recovery from a small fraction of infants as if it settled infant bronchiolitis causation. This paper is an isolation/characterization rung.

## Vault conclusion
Chanock, Roizman, and Myers 1957 supports early RSV/CCA-related virus isolation and characterization. It should not be overread as direct proof of bronchiolitis causation.
