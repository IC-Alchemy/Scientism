# The Stability of Variola Virus Propagated in Embryonated Eggs

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary laboratory propagation and stability paper for variola strains
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1943
- Author: J. B. Nelson
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Egg propagation, rabbit-testis passage, rabbit-skin testing, and comparative variola/vaccinia behavior study |
| Subject / sample | Chinese variola strain from glycerinated crusts and Minnesota variola strain from a 1938 smallpox case; embryonated eggs and rabbits |
| Exposure or intervention | Serial passage in embryonated eggs; attempted rabbit-testis maintenance; rabbit-skin and egg-membrane tests after passage |
| Comparator / control | Variola strain behavior compared with vaccinia behavior in eggs and rabbits; Chinese and Minnesota variola strains compared with each other |
| Endpoint | Chorioallantoic membrane reaction, embryo death or survival, virus recovery in eggs, rabbit-testis persistence, rabbit-skin reaction, and transformation into vaccinia-like behavior |
| Result | Variola and vaccinia remained behaviorally distinct in eggs. Vaccinia produced more rapid necrosis and embryo death; variola produced thicker membrane reactions with little or no necrosis and embryos often remained active. The Minnesota strain remained stable after many egg transfers. The Chinese strain could be maintained in rabbit testis without transformation into vaccinia; the Minnesota strain was less maintainable in rabbit testis. |
| Main limitation | The study addresses laboratory propagation and stability, not clinical smallpox causation or natural transmission. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that the tested variola strains could be propagated in embryonated eggs while retaining distinct behavior from vaccinia.
- It can answer that extended passage did not convert the tested variola strains into vaccinia-like strains under the reported conditions.
- It can answer strain-specific differences in rabbit-testis maintenance.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot answer ordinary human variola transmission.
- It cannot prove that egg-propagated variola reproduces human smallpox.
- It cannot settle the whole variola-vaccinia relationship historically.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is converting a stability and propagation result into a complete causation claim. The paper strengthens the laboratory-isolation/propagation rung, not the clinical or natural-route rung.

## Vault conclusion
Nelson 1943 supports variola propagation and stability in embryonated eggs, while preserving variola/vaccinia distinctions under the tested conditions.
