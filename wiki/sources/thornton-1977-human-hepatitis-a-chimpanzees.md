# Studies on human hepatitis A virus in chimpanzees (Thornton, Tsiquaye, Zuckerman 1977)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: chimpanzee inoculation record for [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]]
- Journal/year: *British Journal of Experimental Pathology*, 1977
- Source basis in hand: full text from PMC2041211 page images (7 digitized pages, scanned article with no text layer; content read page-by-page from the images). Tables I and III of the printed article are not present as separate image plates; the digitized set carries Table II (shedding timing) and Table IV (23-chimpanzee serology).

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental inoculation of seronegative chimpanzees with multiple independent human hepatitis A sources, with uninoculated cage-room controls |
| Subject / sample | 23 chimpanzees seronegative for hepatitis A antibody before inoculation (Table IV lists each animal's pre-inoculation status); 4 additional uninoculated control chimpanzees |
| Intervention or exposure | Inoculation with infective faecal extracts containing hepatitis A antigen from three sources: the MS-1 strain, first-passage material from chimpanzee George, and a strain from a naturally occurring German outbreak |
| Comparator / control | Four uninoculated chimpanzees kept through the observation period; hepatitis-B serology (HBs antigen and anti-HBs) followed in each animal to exclude type-B infection (none found in chimpanzee Ch 3, the worked example in the text) |
| Endpoint | Hepatitis development; serum transaminase (ALT) rise; days from inoculation to first antibody by immune electron microscopy; highest antibody titre; onset and duration of 27-nm particle shedding in faeces relative to biochemical onset (day 0); CsCl buoyant density of recovered particles |
| Result | Hepatitis developed in 18 of 23 inoculated animals. Faecal 27-nm virus-like particles were recovered from 2 animals, appearing from day 9 (one animal) and days 12–14 (others) relative to onset, excretion continuing 9–19 days with maxima at days 17–19; particle density in CsCl was 1.31–1.43 g/ml, mostly 1.34 g/ml. Antibody appeared during the incubation period while particles were still being excreted and roughly coincided with the biochemical liver damage. The four uninoculated controls remained well throughout, with no biochemical or serological evidence of hepatitis. A very close or identical morphologic and serologic relationship was demonstrated between human and chimpanzee-recovered virus. |
| Main limitation | Only 2 of 18 infected animals shed detectable particles, so shedding is demonstrated but not quantifiable as a per-animal rate; per-source attack rates are spread across tables rather than summarised; no sham-inoculation arm (controls were uninoculated, not vehicle-inoculated); the hepatitis endpoint is biochemical/serological rather than histological in the extracted text |

## What the experiment can answer
- Whether three independent human HAV sources (laboratory MS-1, chimpanzee-passaged, and a natural outbreak strain) each infect seronegative chimpanzees, with consistent incubation timing, particle density, and serology — a multi-source consistency rung with an explicit uninoculated control group and hepatitis-B exclusion.
- When antibody appears relative to shedding: during incubation, while 27-nm particles are still in faeces — which warns that serology is not a post-recovery-only endpoint.

## What the experiment cannot answer
- Ordinary human-to-human transmission of hepatitis A; the chimpanzee model with faecal-extract inoculation does not test the natural route in humans.
- Whether the 27-nm particles are themselves the infectious agent: no infectivity titration of purified particles and no particle-negative control inoculum were run.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Multi-source infection consistency strengthens the animal rung, but the rungs above it (ordinary human transmission, human disease causation) are not addressed by this design. Citing this paper as proof that "hepatitis A virus causes human hepatitis" extends a chimpanzee inoculation record past its endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
Full-text supported at the chimpanzee-inoculation rung: 23 seronegative chimpanzees inoculated with three distinct human HAV sources developed hepatitis (18/23) with transaminase elevation (~day 19), faecal 27-nm particle shedding in 2 animals (days 9–19, density mostly 1.34 g/ml), and antibody during incubation, while four uninoculated controls stayed well and hepatitis-B markers were excluded. What this supports is cross-species infection consistency under inoculation, not natural-route human causation.
