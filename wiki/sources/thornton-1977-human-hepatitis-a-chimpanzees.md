# Studies on human hepatitis A virus in chimpanzees (Thornton, Tsiquaye, Zuckerman 1977)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: chimpanzee inoculation record for [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]] — one of the named acquisition gaps, now at abstract level (free full text exists at PMC2041211 for a later pass)
- Journal/year: *British Journal of Experimental Pathology*, 1977
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text not extracted in this pass

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental inoculation of seronegative chimpanzees with multiple independent human HAV sources |
| Subject / sample | Several chimpanzees seronegative for hepatitis A by IEM and radioimmunoassay |
| Intervention or exposure | Inoculation with infective faecal extracts from three sources: the MS-1 strain, first-passage material from chimpanzee George, and a strain from a naturally occurring German outbreak |
| Comparator / control | Within-experiment timing comparisons; no sham-inoculated arm stated in the abstract |
| Endpoint | Serum transaminase elevation; fecal virus particle timing and duration; CsCl buoyant density; antibody by IEM and RIA |
| Result | Elevated transaminases appeared about 19 days after inoculation. Fecal particles appeared as early as day 9 in one animal and days 12–14 in others, with excretion continuing 9–19 days and maxima at days 17–19. CsCl density of fecal particles was 1.31–1.43 g/ml, mostly at 1.34 g/ml. Antibody was detected during the incubation period while particles were still being excreted and roughly coinciding with biochemical liver damage. A very close or identical morphologic and serologic relationship was demonstrated between human and chimpanzee-recovered virus. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; animal numbers and per-source infection rates not visible; no sham arm stated; hepatitis endpoint is biochemical in the abstract. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That three independent human HAV sources (laboratory, chimp-passaged, and outbreak) each produced infection in seronegative chimpanzees with consistent timing, density, and serology — a multi-source consistency rung.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Per-source infection rates and controls; histology; whether shedding particles were titrated for infectivity.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Multi-source infection consistency strengthens the animal rung but the rungs above it (ordinary human transmission) remain unextracted; antibody appearing during shedding also warns that serology is not a post-shedding endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: seronegative chimpanzees inoculated with three distinct human HAV sources developed transaminase elevation (~day 19), fecal particle excretion (days 9–19, peak 1.34 g/ml), and antibody during incubation while still shedding — a multi-source consistency record for the animal rung.
