# A Papillomavirus DNA from a Cervical Carcinoma and Its Prevalence in Cancer Biopsy Samples

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary molecular-cloning and tumor-DNA detection paper for HPV16 in cervical carcinoma
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 1983
- Authors: Matthias Durst, Lutz Gissmann, Hans Ikenberg, and Harald zur Hausen
- Source basis in hand: full-text Europe PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Molecular cloning plus Southern blot prevalence survey of HPV-related DNA in genital tumor biopsies |
| Subject / sample | One invasive cervical carcinoma biopsy used to clone HPV16-like DNA; additional cervical cancer, vulval cancer, penile cancer, cervical carcinoma in situ, cervical dysplasia, and condyloma acuminatum biopsy DNA from different geographic regions |
| Exposure or intervention | DNA extraction, restriction enzyme digestion, molecular cloning in lambda phage and pBR322, and low- and high-stringency hybridization using HPV probes |
| Comparator / control | Other characterized HPV types, high- versus low-stringency hybridization, benign genital warts, dysplasias, carcinoma in situ, and malignant tumors by site and geography |
| Endpoint | Detectable HPV16-homologous DNA and restriction/hybridization pattern in biopsy DNA |
| Result | HPV16 DNA was cloned from one cervical carcinoma. High-stringency HPV16 signals were reported in 11 of 18 German cervical cancers, 8 of 23 Kenya/Brazil cervical cancers, 2 of 7 vulval cancers, 1 of 4 penile cancers, 2 of 9 cervical carcinomas in situ, 2 of 20 cervical dysplasias, and 2 of 33 condylomata acuminata. The two condyloma positives also carried HPV6 or HPV11 DNA. |
| Main limitation | This was a molecular detection and association study, not an experimental infection, transformation, prospective progression, or prevention study. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that an HPV16-designated DNA was cloned from a cervical carcinoma biopsy.
- It can answer that HPV16-homologous DNA was detected disproportionately in malignant genital tumor samples compared with most benign genital papillomas in the tested set.
- It can answer that HPV16 detection varied by tumor type and geography in the tested biopsies.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that HPV16 caused the cervical cancers.
- It cannot establish natural infection-to-cancer sequence, necessity, sufficiency, cofactors, or population-level risk.
- It cannot show that HPV16 integration, persistence, or gene expression drove malignant transformation.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if HPV16 DNA detection in tumor biopsies is treated as direct proof of cervical-cancer causation. The source supports an important molecular-association rung, but not infection chronology, mechanism, or causal sufficiency.

## Vault conclusion
This source supports a narrow claim: Durst et al. 1983 cloned HPV16 DNA from a cervical carcinoma and found HPV16-homologous DNA frequently in tested cervical cancer samples, while leaving causation, progression, and mechanism unresolved.
