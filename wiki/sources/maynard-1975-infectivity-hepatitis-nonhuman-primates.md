# Infectivity studies of hepatitis A and B in non-human primates (Maynard et al. 1975)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: nonhuman-primate passage/challenge record touching both the hepatitis A and hepatitis B ladders' animal rungs
- Journal/year: *Developments in Biological Standardization*, 1975
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract; full text not obtained

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Serial passage and cross-challenge studies in nonhuman primates |
| Subject / sample | *Saguinus mystax* marmosets; chimpanzees |
| Intervention or exposure | Hepatitis A: primary inoculation with human serum containing MS-1 strain, then serial marmoset subpassages; intravenous inoculation of chimpanzees with particle-containing stool filtrates from a common-source Arizona epidemic; partially purified CsCl-banded particles. Hepatitis B: inoculation of chimpanzees with sera containing HBsAg of varying subspecificities; cross-challenge with alternate subspecificity sets |
| Comparator / control | Cross-challenge design for hepatitis B (d/y-w/r subspecificity system); acquisition of antibody against 17-nm particles measured by IEM in each passage |
| Endpoint | Hepatitis induction; IEM antibody against virus-like particles; homologous antibody subspecificity after experimental infection |
| Result | Hepatitis A was induced in *S. mystax* marmosets by MS-1 serum and in three further subpassages, each with demonstrable IEM antibody against 17-nm particles from both human volunteer stools and epidemic stools. Particle-containing epidemic stool filtrates induced hepatitis in chimpanzees after intravenous inoculation. Partially purified CsCl-banded particles induced hepatitis in marmosets. For hepatitis B, sera containing HBsAg induced hepatitis in chimpanzees lacking anti-HBs, with induced antibody homologous to the inoculum's antigen subspecificities; cross-challenge indicated animals infected with one subspecificity set did not re-develop hepatitis with the alternate set. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; no animal numbers; conference-proceedings format; route for the hepatitis A chimpanzee work limited to intravenous in this record; hepatitis and particle-antibody endpoints are composite. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That MS-1-associated material serially passaged in marmosets retained infectivity with IEM-detectable serologic response at each passage — a passage-stability rung.
- That epidemic stool filtrates induced hepatitis in chimpanzees by the intravenous route.
- That HBsAg-positive sera induced hepatitis in anti-HBs-negative chimpanzees with homologous subspecificity responses and cross-protection between subspecificity sets — a hepatitis B specificity rung.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Animal numbers, hepatitis severity grading, and controls for each arm.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Passage and challenge in marmosets/chimpanzees sit at the animal rungs of both ladders; they do not substitute for the human detection rung ([[sources/feinstone-kapikian-purcell-1973-hepatitis-a-iem]]) or ordinary human transmission.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: hepatitis A material passaged in marmosets stayed infectious with IEM-linked serology, epidemic stool filtrates hepatitis-infected chimpanzees intravenously, and HBsAg sera hepatitis-infected susceptible chimpanzees with subspecificity-specific immune responses and cross-challenge boundaries.
