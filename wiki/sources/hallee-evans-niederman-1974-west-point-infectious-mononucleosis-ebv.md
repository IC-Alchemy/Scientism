# Infectious Mononucleosis at the United States Military Academy

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary prospective cohort paper for EBV serostatus, EBV seroconversion, EBV-specific IgM, and clinical IM in a defined young-adult population
- Journal/year: *Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine*, 1974
- Authors: T. James Hallee, Alfred S. Evans, James C. Niederman, Charles M. Brooks, and John H. Voegtly
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Prospective cohort study with repeated serology and clinical surveillance |
| Subject / sample | Entering 1969 class of 1401 U.S. Military Academy cadets followed over four years; 890 had EBV antibody on entry and 511 lacked detectable antibody, with 437 initially susceptible cadets followed through the first year |
| Exposure or intervention | Repeated serum collection, EBV antibody testing by indirect immunofluorescence, EBV-specific IgM testing, heterophile antibody testing, immunoglobulin testing, and clinical surveillance for IM-like illness |
| Comparator / control | Cadets with EBV antibody on entry versus cadets lacking EBV antibody; exposed susceptible roommates of IM cases versus susceptible cadets not exposed to known clinical IM roommates; pre-illness versus illness and post-illness sera |
| Endpoint | EBV seroconversion, heterophile-positive clinical infectious mononucleosis, suggestive IM, no known mono-like illness, EBV-specific IgM, total immunoglobulin changes, and roommate infection rate |
| Result | Of 437 cadets without EBV antibody on entry, 201 seroconverted over the study period; 53 developed definite clinical IM, so 26.4% of EBV infections were manifested by heterophile-positive clinical IM. No clinical IM occurred in 890 healthy cadets who possessed EBV antibody on entry, except two already ill at first bleeding. In the first year, 54 of 437 susceptible cadets seroconverted; 15 had clinical IM, 12 suggestive IM, and 27 no known illness. EBV-specific IgM appeared during acute illness and was absent before illness and one year later. Susceptible roommates of known clinical cases did not have higher EBV infection rates than susceptible cadets not so exposed. |
| Main limitation | The study is observational and serologic. It does not use purified EBV challenge, does not identify exact transmission route, and clinical surveillance could miss mild or atypical disease. The population was young, male, military, and not representative of all age groups. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that EBV seroconversion prospectively preceded or accompanied clinical IM in susceptible cadets.
- It can answer that pre-existing EBV antibody was associated with absence of later clinical IM in this cohort.
- It can answer that most EBV infections in this cohort were not heterophile-positive clinical IM.
- It can answer that close roommate exposure to recognized clinical IM did not measurably increase EBV infection in this study.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove purified-virus sufficiency for IM.
- It cannot specify the ordinary transmission route or dose.
- It cannot make all mononucleosis-like illness EBV-caused.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This is a strong prospective timing and susceptibility paper. The leap begins if prospective EBV seroconversion is treated as a complete transmission-route or sufficient-cause experiment, or if EBV infection is equated with clinical IM in all hosts.

## Vault conclusion
Hallee et al. 1974 supports a narrow claim: in a four-year West Point cohort, clinical infectious mononucleosis occurred in cadets who lacked EBV antibody and then seroconverted, while most EBV infections were inapparent or non-definite and recognized clinical cases did not produce a higher roommate infection rate under the study's measurements.
