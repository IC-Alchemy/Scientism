# Safety and Efficacy of CR6261 in an Influenza A H1N1 Healthy Human Challenge Model

## Source type
- Role in this vault: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled human influenza challenge trial; for this vault the virus-only placebo arm is the sham-controlled exposure-to-disease row; fills GAP 1 in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] and rung 7 (controlled human challenge) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]
- Journal/year: *Clinical Infectious Diseases*, 2021;73(11):e4260-e4268
- Authors: Alison Han, Lindsay Czajkowski, Luz Angela Rosas, Adriana Cervantes-Medina, Yongli Xiao, Monica Gouzoulis, Keith Lumbard, Sally Hunsberger, Susan Reed, Rani Athota, Holly Ann Baus, Amy Lwin, Jerald Sadoff, Jeffery K. Taubenberger, Matthew J. Memoli
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article (PMC8664469)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled monoclonal-antibody trial inside the validated NIAID H1N1pdm09 human challenge model; 91 participants randomized after challenge 1:1; NIH Clinical Center isolation, minimum 10 days |
| Subject / sample | 408 screened, 104 enrolled, 91 randomized and challenged; healthy nonsmoking adults 18-45, BMI 18-35, serum HAI <=1:10 within 60 days of enrollment, no influenza vaccine that season; enrolled March 2015 - February 2018 |
| Intervention or exposure | All 91 participants received 10^7 TCID50 of wild-type A/California/04/2009(H1N1) passage-6 GMP challenge virus intranasally via MAD Nasal atomizer. Then, quote: "Participants were randomized after challenge 1:1, double-blinded, to receive CR6261 monoclonal antibody or placebo 24 hours after influenza challenge. Participants received 50 mg/kg of CR6261 or placebo (5% dextrose in water) as a single intravenous (IV) infusion." (49 CR6261, 42 placebo) |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes**, as a placebo infusion arm (not a non-inoculated group). Quote above: placebo was "5% dextrose in water" IV, double-blind. Both arms carry the identical challenge exposure, so the placebo arm is a randomized virus-only disease-incidence group: MMID 69% (29/42), confirmed influenza infection 88% (37/42), any symptoms 93% (39/42), any shedding 74% (31/42) |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: "The primary efficacy outcome was area under the curve (AUC) of viral RNA detection over time" (daily nasal-wash qRT-PCR). Secondary endpoints: MMID ("presence of at least 1 symptom of influenza plus detectable shedding"), confirmed influenza infection ("symptoms plus a 4-fold rise in convalescent HAI titer or MMID"), symptom number/duration, FLU-PRO patient-reported outcome scores, shedding incidence and duration |
| Result | CR6261 had no significant effect on the primary endpoint (median AUC 48.6 vs 25.5 log[copies/mL]x days, P=.315) nor on MMID (53% vs 69%, P=.137), confirmed infection (73% vs 88%, P=.114), or shedding (67% vs 74%, P=.646). Any symptoms were reduced by CR6261 (76% vs 93%, P=.045) with similar severity among those who developed symptoms. Baseline serum neuraminidase-inhibition titer, not treatment, was the only predictor of reduced MMID, confirmed infection, and shedding duration. Influenza disease was reproduced in ~69-88% of virus-only placebo recipients |
| Main limitation | The placebo arm controls the antibody infusion, not inoculation; there is no non-inoculated group, so placebo-arm illness attributable to the challenge virus rests on the within-trial contrast with the treated arm plus the model validation. Single strain, single high dose; young low-antibody adults; efficacy in this model may not transfer to natural exposure |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue GAP 1 (sham-controlled human challenge with purified inoculum): this trial supplies the sham-controlled element for influenza A(H1N1)pdm09 - randomized placebo control, defined purified inoculum, and named clinical+virological endpoints. The dose-finding companion is [[sources/memoli-2015-h1n1pdm09-human-challenge-model-validation]].
- Ladder rung 7 (controlled human challenge) of [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]; new row in [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]].
- Note the boundary: exposure was intranasal inoculation, so GAP 2 (natural-route transmission) is not touched.

## What the experiment can answer
- That the H1N1pdm09 challenge exposure produces defined influenza disease in ~69% (MMID) and infection in ~88% (confirmed) of placebo-treated seronegative adults under blinded randomized conditions, replicating the attack rate of the validation study.
- That a single high-dose anti-HA-stalk monoclonal antibody (CR6261) 24 h post-exposure did not significantly reduce viral RNA load, shedding, MMID, or confirmed infection, and reduced symptom incidence but not severity.
- That baseline anti-NA serum titer outperformed treatment and baseline HAI titer as a predictor of lower disease incidence and shorter shedding.

## What it cannot answer
- Natural-route transmission, aerosol dynamics, or community attack rates; all exposure was by atomizer inoculation in isolation.
- Whether CR6261-class anti-stalk antibodies fail generally; this is one antibody, one dose, one time point, one strain.
- Fever and medically attended influenza presentations; disease was by design mild to moderate upper-respiratory illness.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- The authors generalize from one failed anti-stalk antibody to vaccine strategy: "anti-stalk immunity alone, whether naturally occurring or artificially induced, may not be enough to abrogate or prevent an influenza infection." That is a single-agent, single-timepoint result, not a class-effect proof.
- Challenge-model efficacy figures are sometimes quoted as natural-exposure efficacy; this experiment tests inoculation under isolation, not community exposure.
- Placebo-arm rates describe seronegative screened adults and should not be read as population attack rates.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the sham-controlled-human-challenge rung for A(H1N1)pdm09: under randomized double-blind placebo control, the defined GMP inoculum reproducibly produced shedding plus clinical influenza-like disease (69% MMID, 88% confirmed infection in the virus-only arm). Natural-route transmission and ordinary population causation remain separate rungs ([[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]]).
