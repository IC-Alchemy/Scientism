# Latent Varicella-Zoster Virus Is Located Predominantly in Neurons in Human Trigeminal Ganglia

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary cellular-localization paper for latent VZV DNA in human trigeminal ganglia
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 1998
- Authors: P. G. E. Kennedy, E. Grinfeld, and J. W. Gow
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Human autopsy trigeminal ganglia study using PCR, in situ hybridization, and PCR in situ amplification |
| Subject / sample | 30 individuals including HIV-positive, neonatal, and immunocompetent adult cases |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural latent VZV context |
| Comparator / control | Neonatal/negative controls and neuronal versus non-neuronal localization |
| Endpoint | Cellular location of VZV DNA |
| Result | VZV DNA was detected mainly in neuronal nuclei, with occasional non-neuronal cells. |
| Main limitation | DNA presence does not prove active latency program or future reactivation in a particular person. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that latent VZV DNA localizes predominantly to neurons in human trigeminal ganglia under the reported methods.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot identify reactivation triggers or prove future shingles.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is turning DNA localization into a complete clinical reactivation model.

## Vault conclusion
Kennedy et al. 1998 supports a VZV neuronal latency-localization rung.
