# Cloning of Monomeric Human Papillomavirus Type 16 DNA Integrated within Cell DNA from a Cervical Carcinoma

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary HPV16 integration and viral-cell junction cloning paper from cervical carcinoma material
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1986
- Authors: Toshihiko Matsukura, Tadahito Kanda, Akemi Furuno, Hiroyuki Yoshikawa, Takashi Kawana, and Kunito Yoshiike
- Source basis in hand: full-text Europe PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Molecular cloning, restriction analysis, and junction sequencing of HPV16 DNA integrated with flanking cellular DNA from a cervical carcinoma |
| Subject / sample | Cervical carcinoma 219 DNA containing HPV16; cloned HPV16 DNA and flanking cell DNA fragments; cloned human repetitive DNA probe BLUR8 |
| Exposure or intervention | Southern blotting, restriction digestion, lambda cloning from HindIII-digested carcinoma DNA, subcloning, and sequencing around viral-cell DNA junctions |
| Comparator / control | Standard cloned HPV16 DNA map, restriction enzyme patterns, human repetitive DNA hybridization, and comparison to published HPV16 sequence |
| Endpoint | Presence and structure of monomeric integrated HPV16 DNA, viral-cell DNA junctions, retained/deleted viral regions, and sequence changes around the junctions |
| Result | The authors cloned monomeric HPV16 DNA with flanking cell DNA from cervical carcinoma material. Junction sequencing showed deletion between HPV16 nucleotides 1,284 and 4,471, including most of E1 and all of E2, with HPV DNA covalently connected to unrelated cell DNA. E6 and E7 were retained. The paper also reported a sequence difference that made the HPV16 E1 gene appear uninterrupted. |
| Main limitation | This is a structural integration study in one carcinoma. The paper states that the transforming capacity of HPV16/18 still needed demonstration, and that the state and function of HPV genomes in cancer cells required further study. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that HPV16 DNA integrated with flanking cellular DNA could be cloned from one cervical carcinoma.
- It can answer that the cloned integrated HPV16 DNA retained E6/E7 and had a deletion disrupting much of E1 and all of E2.
- It can answer that viral-cell junction sequences were directly demonstrated in this sample.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove that HPV16 integration caused the carcinoma.
- It cannot show that all cervical cancers contain this structure.
- It cannot show natural infection chronology, persistence requirements, cofactors, or transformation sufficiency.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The leap begins if one carcinoma's integrated HPV16 structure is treated as proof that HPV16 integration is necessary or sufficient for cervical cancer. The paper supplies a strong structure/junction endpoint, not a causal-progression endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
This source supports a narrow claim: Matsukura et al. 1986 cloned integrated HPV16-cell DNA junctions from one cervical carcinoma and mapped retained/deleted viral regions, while leaving oncogenic causality and generality unresolved.
