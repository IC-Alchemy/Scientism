# Studies on Measles II Symptomatology and Pathology in Monkeys Experimentally Infected

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary symptom/pathology endpoint paper for the Blake-Trask monkey measles model
- Journal/year: *Journal of Experimental Medicine*, 1921
- Authors: Francis G. Blake and James D. Trask, Jr.
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Clinical course and histopathology study in experimentally infected monkeys |
| Subject / sample | Experimentally infected monkeys from the companion transmission study |
| Exposure or intervention | Prior inoculation on respiratory mucous membranes or by passage material as described in the companion paper |
| Comparator / control | Descriptive comparison to human measles; not a sham-controlled pathology series |
| Endpoint | Incubation period, onset, enanthem, exanthem, conjunctivitis, fever, leukopenia, diarrhea, rhinitis/bronchitis absence, skin and mucosal histology |
| Result | Monkeys inoculated on respiratory mucous membranes usually had a six to ten day incubation period, with most at seven days. They developed listlessness, conjunctivitis, labial mucosal spots or enanthem, maculopapular exanthem, and consistent leukopenia. Fever varied widely, and rhinitis/bronchitis symptoms were absent. Histology showed vascular and pericapillary cellular lesions in skin and mucosal tissues. |
| Main limitation | This paper characterizes an induced monkey syndrome. It does not independently establish the source agent, and it cannot prove that all human measles pathology follows the same experimental route. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer what clinical and pathological endpoints Blake and Trask used to call the monkey reaction measles-like.
- It can answer that leukopenia was a more consistent endpoint than fever in this model.
- It can answer that the model differed from human measles in important details, especially absence of rhinitis and bronchitis.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot identify the causative particle.
- It cannot prove natural-route human transmission.
- It cannot stand alone without the companion inoculation and passage paper.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating symptom resemblance as causal proof by itself. In this vault, the paper supplies endpoint definitions and pathology, while the companion paper supplies the challenge and passage evidence.

## Vault conclusion
Blake and Trask's pathology paper is useful because it names the endpoints behind the monkey model. It strengthens the model description but should not be used as a standalone proof of measles-virus causation.
