# The Infectious Myth Busted Part 6: The Germ Duel

## TL;DR
This raw source is a polemical essay that uses self-experimentation stories, challenge rhetoric, and selected failed transmission studies to argue against infectious-disease causation claims. It raises questions worth checking against the cited studies about study design and burden of proof, but it also jumps between diseases, routes of exposure, and evidentiary standards.

## Source type
- Tier 3 argumentative essay.
- Broad in scope, covering multiple diseases and historical episodes.
- More focused on rhetorical burden-shifting and challenge narratives than on one tightly bounded historical experiment.

## What the source claims
- ViroLIEgy argues that many famous infection claims are inadequately demonstrated.
- The page treats negative self-experiments and failed transmission attempts as evidence against broader contagion claims.
- It also argues that defenders of germ theory improperly shift the burden of proof onto critics.

## What evidence or observation is involved
- The raw page cites challenge anecdotes, volunteer exposure studies, and self-experimentation reports.
- It uses those episodes to question whether certain organisms, under those conditions, reliably caused the expected disease.
- The cited episodes vary substantially by pathogen, host condition, exposure route, and endpoint.

## What question the evidence is capable of answering
- Such episodes can sometimes show that a specific transmission route, dose, or challenge design failed under the stated conditions.
- They can also reveal uncertainty about mechanism or about how reproducible a given model was.
- They are not automatically capable of disproving all infectious causation across different organisms and conditions.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This source is useful mainly as a map of burden-of-proof arguments and negative-challenge anecdotes that warrant closer examination.
- Its strongest contribution is methodological: failed transmission in one setup should not be inflated into a universal conclusion without careful comparison of endpoints and exposure conditions.
- The page should remain explicitly attributed and unresolved.
