# Plaque Differentiation and Replication of Virulent and Attenuated Strains of Measles Virus

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary tissue-culture replication and plaque assay paper for measles-virus strains
- Journal/year: *Journal of Bacteriology*, 1964
- Author: Fred Rapp
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC PDF

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | In vitro plaque assay, cytopathology, immunofluorescence, inhibitor, and growth-curve study |
| Subject / sample | Virulent Edmonston measles-virus strain, attenuated Edmonston strain, a fresh measles-virus isolate, commercial vaccine lots, and BSC-1 African green monkey kidney cells |
| Exposure or intervention | Inoculation of BSC-1 monolayers and cover-glass cultures; neutral-red plaque assay; serum plaque reduction; inhibitor exposure; harvest of intracellular and extracellular virus |
| Comparator / control | Virulent versus attenuated strains; acute versus convalescent human serum; pre- versus post-inoculation monkey serum; DNA-virus inhibitors and antibiotic controls |
| Endpoint | Plaque size and count, multinucleated giant-cell formation, immunofluorescent antigen, plaque reduction by immune sera, intracellular/extracellular infectious-virus titers, and inhibitor sensitivity |
| Result | Virulent measles virus produced larger plaques than attenuated virus in BSC-1 cells. Both produced multinucleated giant cells. Immune human or monkey sera inhibited plaque formation while acute/preinoculation sera did not. Intracellular virus appeared before extracellular release; virulent virus yielded roughly equal intra- and extracellular virus at peak, while attenuated virus remained mostly cell-associated. DNA-virus inhibitors did not block plaque formation in the tested conditions. |
| Main limitation | The study characterizes already identified measles-virus strains in cell culture. It is not a human clinical-causation or natural-transmission experiment. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that measles-virus strains replicate in BSC-1 cells with measurable plaque and growth-curve endpoints.
- It can answer that virulent and attenuated Edmonston strains differed in plaque size, release, and timing in this cell system.
- It can answer that immune sera, not acute/preimmune sera, reduced plaque formation under the tested conditions.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove the original clinical etiology of measles by itself.
- It cannot answer whether attenuated-virus cell-culture behavior directly predicts human transmission behavior.
- It cannot reconstruct ordinary human respiratory spread.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using a clean cell-culture replication system to imply that every earlier causation rung is settled. Rapp 1964 strengthens the replication and assay rung, but it presupposes the measles-virus identity established by earlier isolation and serology work.

## Vault conclusion
Rapp 1964 supports a measles-virus replication and plaque-assay rung. It should be used to describe culture behavior, not as a standalone proof of ordinary measles causation.
