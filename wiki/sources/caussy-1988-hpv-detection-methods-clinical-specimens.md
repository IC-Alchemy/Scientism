# Evaluation of methods for detecting HPV DNA sequences in clinical specimens (Caussy et al. 1988)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early method-boundary paper — direct evidence that HPV "positivity" is method-dependent
- Journal/year: *Journal of Clinical Microbiology*, 1988
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC266259)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Cross-sectional method-comparison study on the same specimens under three hybridization methods |
| Subject / sample | 26 condylomatous lesions, 24 invasive cancer cell specimens, and 33 cervices without evidence of disease |
| Intervention or exposure | Southern blot hybridization, in situ filter hybridization, and in situ tissue hybridization for HPV types 6, 11, 16, 18 |
| Comparator / control | Disease-free cervices as comparison specimens; each method against the others on identical material |
| Endpoint | HPV DNA detection rate by method and disease category; method specificity estimates |
| Result | Condylomatous lesions: 89% (23/26) positive by one or more methods (82% Southern, 62% in situ filter, 72% in situ tissue). Invasive cancer: 83% (19/23) positive by one or more methods, but only 30% positive by in situ tissue hybridization versus 89% by filter in situ and 70% by Southern. Control cervices: 13% positive by one or more assays. In situ tissue hybridization showed specificity of 72% for condylomas and 30% for invasive cancer cells. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; small per-group samples; "one or more methods" positivity is itself a composite endpoint; the 13% control positivity raises questions about assay specificity or latent infection that the abstract cannot resolve. |

## What the abstract can answer
- That HPV detection rates in identical clinical material varied enormously with the hybridization method — from 30% to 89% in invasive cancer — in 1988-era assays.
- That reported HPV prevalence in any study is conditional on the detection method, an interpretive boundary that applies to the whole 1980s HPV literature the vault audits.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Specimen processing details; how discordant results were adjudicated; the basis for the specificity estimates.

## Later inferential-leap risk
Both directions: 1980s negative studies using weak methods cannot be cited as showing HPV absence without naming the method; 1980s positive studies using in-situ-tissue methods cannot be cited as showing high prevalence without the specificity boundary. This is the same assay-boundary discipline the vault applies to modern PCR-negative papers.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: in a 1988 three-method comparison on identical specimens, invasive-cancer HPV positivity ranged from 30% to 89% by method, and 13% of disease-free cervices were positive by at least one assay — HPV detection is method-dependent.
