# MERS-CoV Causes Transient Lower Respiratory Tract Infection in Rhesus Macaques

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary animal-challenge, pathology, tissue-localization, and viral-replication paper for MERS-CoV
- Journal/year: *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 2013
- Authors: E. de Wit, T. Prescott, A. Baseler, T. Bushmaker, T. Thomas, M. G. Scott, F. Feldmann, A. Haddock, H. Feldmann, and V. J. Munster
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Experimental rhesus macaque challenge with clinical observation, swabs and bronchoalveolar lavage, qRT-PCR, tissue viral RNA, subgenomic mRNA, electron microscopy, histopathology, in situ hybridization, and immunohistochemistry |
| Subject / sample | Six rhesus macaques, four male and two female, 6-10 years old and 5-10 kg |
| Exposure or intervention | MERS-CoV isolate HCoV-EMC/2012 propagated in VeroE6 cells; six macaques received 7 x 10^6 TCID50 by combined intratracheal, ocular, oral, and intranasal routes |
| Comparator / control | Serial pre/post sampling and tissue localization; no sham-inoculated macaque group is presented as the direct comparator in this paper |
| Endpoint | Clinical signs, hematology, nasal/oropharyngeal/urogenital/rectal swabs, bronchoalveolar lavage viral RNA, tissue viral RNA, subgenomic mRNA, coronavirus particles in pneumocytes, lung lesions, and viral RNA/antigen localization |
| Result | The macaques developed transient clinical and hematologic changes. Nasal swabs were positive in all animals on days 1 and 3, BAL was positive in five of six animals on day 1, and viral RNA was found in respiratory tissues. Lung lesions included interstitial pneumonia, edema, fibrin, syncytia, type II pneumocyte hyperplasia, and hyaline membranes. Coronavirus particles, viral RNA, and antigen localized to pneumocytes, and subgenomic mRNA supported active lower-respiratory replication. Kidney and bladder were negative in the tested set. |
| Main limitation | The experiment used a high-dose combined artificial exposure route in six macaques, produced transient rather than fatal disease, and did not include a sham-inoculated macaque comparator. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that high-dose combined-route HCoV-EMC/2012 exposure produced transient lower-respiratory infection and lung pathology in rhesus macaques.
- It can answer that viral RNA, antigen, and replication markers localized to respiratory tissues, especially pneumocytes, under the tested conditions.
- It can answer that renal tropism was not supported in the tested kidney and bladder samples despite the index human case having renal failure.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove ordinary human natural-route causation, route-specific efficiency, or real-world infectious dose.
- It cannot prove that rhesus macaques reproduce severe or fatal human MERS.
- It cannot isolate symptom attribution against a sham-inoculated group.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is translating a high-dose combined-route macaque model into ordinary human respiratory transmission or into the entire human MERS syndrome. The paper supplies an important animal pathogenicity rung, but its endpoint is model-specific lower-respiratory infection.

## Vault conclusion
de Wit et al. 2013 supports a primary MERS-CoV animal-challenge rung: MERS-CoV can replicate in the lower respiratory tract and produce transient lung disease in rhesus macaques under artificial combined-route exposure. It does not settle ordinary human route, dose, or full clinical causation.
