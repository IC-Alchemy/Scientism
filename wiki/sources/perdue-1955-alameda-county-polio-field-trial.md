## Study type

Local operations and follow-up report for the southern-Alameda-County component of the 1954 nationwide poliomyelitis-vaccine field trials (National Foundation for Infantile Paralysis; areas proposed by state health departments; vaccine/placebo code held only at the Evaluation Center in Michigan). Southern Alameda County "was the only area in California to take part in the 1954 nationwide field trials." This is a component report, not the master analysis: "The final results of the nationwide trials became known April 12, 1955, and need no repeating." The paper contains no local randomization protocol detail beyond what is quoted below.

## Subjects

"By June 10, 1954, 7,166 children had received three injections each-half of them with vaccine, half with placebo" (3,583 per injected arm) in second grade; first and third grade children served as uninoculated controls, and this report gives no count for the uninoculated grades. Clinic staff: 100 local physicians injecting, ~600 volunteers; clinics May 5-6, May 12-13, June 9-10, 1954, in 90 schools. Vaccine shipment arrived "just two and a half hours before injections were to begin," after the National Foundation had decided at the last moment against California participation.

## Exposure / intervention

"Children in the second grade of school would-be injected with aqueous vaccine containing killed virus of the three known types. First and third grade children would serve as uninoculated controls." Injection schedule: first on day zero, second on day seven, third (booster) on day thirty-five. About 2% of inoculated children had blood specimens drawn for antibody studies before and after the series (national plan).

## Comparator / control

Two comparators. Sham arm: YES - a placebo injection; composition not stated in this report.

> "7,166 children had received three injections each-half of them with vaccine, half with placebo-all identified simply by a code, the key to which was known only at the Evaluation Center in Michigan."

> "California, in particular, insisted that a sound trial would necessarily include use of a placebo, and insisted, further, that satisfactory answers be obtained to the questions regarding safety of the vaccine before the state would agree to participate."

Second comparator: uninoculated first- and third-grade children (observed-control arm of the national design, quoted above).

## Endpoint (named exactly)

"Cases confirmed as poliomyelitis" among children in the study age group during follow-up May 10 through December 31, 1954. The report names the follow-up machinery but does not reproduce the diagnostic criteria themselves: "Uniform diagnostic criteria were to be applied, and prompt reporting was a necessity. Muscle grading examinations were to be arranged for children in the study ages who contracted poliomyelitis"; blood antibody titrations and stool specimens for virus isolation in any family with a case and a study-age child; reporting to the Evaluation Center with epidemiologic histories; follow-up shared with the State Department of Public Health. This is a clinical-disease endpoint with laboratory support in case families.

## Result

> "Results of the follow-up now reveal that a total of ten cases confirmed as poliomyelitis occurred during this time among children in the study age group in southern Alameda County. Seven of these ten cases were among children who did not receive any injections. The remaining three cases all occurred in children who had received the placebo."

Arm-level case counts: vaccinated 0/3,583; placebo 3 (denominator 3,583); uninoculated controls 7 (denominator not given in this report). Attack rates across the uninoculated arm therefore cannot be computed from this report, and the report computes none. No vaccine-arm poliomyelitis case and no severity/muscle-grading distribution is reported locally.

## Main limitation

This is a county operations report, not the trial's master analysis: 10 local cases total make arm contrasts extremely fragile; the uninoculated-control denominator is missing; placebo composition, randomization mechanics, and the "uniform diagnostic criteria" are not stated here; the national aggregate results are referenced but not reproduced. The master primary publications - Francis et al., AJPH 1955;45(5 Pt 2):1-63 summary report (PMID 14361811, Europe PMC idconv: "Identifier not found in PMC"), Dublin TD, JAMA 1955;158(15):1258-1265, and Francis T, JAMA 1955;158(15):1266-1270 - were probed this run (PMC idconv, Wayback CDX for ajph.aphapublications.org HighWire/doi/full/doi/pdf patterns, archive.org): no reachable full text, only 302-redirect snapshots. This component report is the only 1954-field-trial primary in hand, and it is full-text-grade for what it locally reports.

## Inferential leaps recorded

- "Inoculations of vaccine during the field trials in all probability prevented some cases of paralytic poliomyelitis among children in southern Alameda County" - inferred from 0 vaccinated vs 3 placebo vs 7 uninjected cases with no denominators, no attack-rate comparison, and no statistical treatment anywhere in the report.
- "Public health authorities in many areas were willing to accept without argument a trial originally designed on an unsound basis. Save for the insistence from some states, no placebo trials would have been conducted, and the results could not have been so conclusive" - participant-side design criticism recorded as-is; the report is a primary document for the placebo-versus-observed-control controversy around the 1954 trials.
- "Initial planning of the study was hasty and not sufficiently representative of medical and administrative thinking" and "rather detailed field trial plans had been formulated before some of these questions could be answered, and before all the necessary research data had been gathered" - the county authors' own account that the trial design preceded completed safety/potency research.
- "It seems clear that the studies of Jonas Salk did certainly justify large-scale testing" - asserted, not demonstrated within this report.

## What this wiki currently concludes

Source-reported: in the southern-Alameda-County component of the 1954 field trials, 7,166 second-grade children were injected three times with either Salk aqueous killed trivalent vaccine or a placebo (code at the Michigan Evaluation Center), first and third graders served uninoculated, and follow-up to December 31, 1954 recorded 10 confirmed poliomyelitis cases in study-age children - 7 uninjected, 3 placebo, 0 vaccinated. Vault status: this is direct experimental intervention evidence with a confirmed clinical-disease endpoint, consistent with an infectious-agent hypothesis for poliomyelitis, but the counts come from a single county report with a missing control denominator and no computed rates; the vault holds no full text of the trial's master report and therefore does not carry the trial-level efficacy estimate (about 60-90% against paralytic disease as usually quoted) as an experimentally supported claim here. It answers whether this component area recorded vaccine-arm disease (it recorded none), not national efficacy, long-term safety, or all-cause outcomes.
