# Development of AIDS in a chimpanzee infected with HIV-1 (Novembre et al. 1997)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: comparator paper for the AIDS→HIV row — it prevents overreading the chimpanzee "infection without disease" boundary as an absolute
- Journal/year: *Journal of Virology*, 1997
- Source basis in hand: PubMed abstract only; full text not obtained (free at PMC191562)

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Long-term single-animal observational record of an experimentally infected chimpanzee, with transmission pass to a second animal |
| Subject / sample | Chimpanzee C499 (infected over 10 years with three different HIV-1 isolates); chimpanzee C455 (uninfected, transfused with C499's blood) |
| Intervention or exposure | Original infection with three HIV-1 isolates; subsequent whole-blood transfusion from C499 to C455 |
| Comparator / control | C499's own earlier infection course; C455 as transmission recipient |
| Endpoint | CD4+ cell counts, plasma viral loads, viral cytopathicity, envelope V1–V2 divergence, opportunistic infection, blood dyscrasias |
| Result | Over the final three years C499 showed CD4+ decline and increasing plasma viral loads. A virus (HIV-1JC), cytopathic for chimpanzee PBMCs, emerged with ≥16% amino-acid divergence from all inoculating viruses, suggesting a large quasispecies. The animal had an opportunistic infection and blood dyscrasias — AIDS by the study's characterization. Transfusion of C499's blood into uninfected C455 induced rapid, sustained CD4 decline with high viral loads. The authors conclude HIV-1 can induce AIDS in chimpanzees and that long-term passage can yield more pathogenic virus. |
| Main limitation | Abstract-level record; single index animal; the animal received three isolates over years (a mixed-inoculum history); n = 1 transmission pass; AIDS development after >10 years limits generalization about typical host response. |

## What the abstract can answer
- Whether the chimpanzee model categorically cannot reproduce AIDS: no — one chimpanzee developed CD4 decline, rising viral load, an emergent cytopathic divergent virus, and opportunistic infection after more than a decade.
- Whether disease emergence was associated with viral evolution: the endpoint data associate it with a divergent, cytopathic quasispecies.

## What full text would still need to establish
- Full clinical and pathologic criteria used for the AIDS designation; timeline detail; the three isolates' identities and dates.

## Later inferential-leap risk
This is the paper that blocks the "chimpanzees never get AIDS from HIV" overreach of the model-boundary papers ([[sources/alter-1984-htlv-iii-chimpanzees-plasma]], [[sources/fultz-1987-persistent-chimpanzee-hiv-infection]]). The mirror overreach — treating one animal after 10+ years and mixed inocula as a normal-course demonstration — should also be avoided; it is a boundary-of-boundary record.

## Vault conclusion
Abstract-level: one chimpanzee infected with HIV-1 for over ten years developed CD4 decline, rising viral load, an emergent cytopathic divergent virus, and opportunistic infection, and transmitted CD4-declining infection by transfusion — AIDS development is documented in the chimpanzee model, late and after viral evolution.
