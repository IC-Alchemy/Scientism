# Herpes Simplex Virus Type 1 Shedding in Tears and Nasal and Oral Mucosa of Healthy Adults

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary prospective intensive HSV-1 orofacial shedding study
- Journal/year: *Sexually Transmitted Diseases*, 2016
- Authors: M. Ramchandani and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Intensive prospective shedding study |
| Subject / sample | Eight immunocompetent HSV-1-seropositive adults with cold-sore history |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural shedding; 12 orofacial sites sampled for about five weeks |
| Comparator / control | Site and symptom-status comparisons; PCR and culture context |
| Endpoint | HSV-1 DNA detection by PCR and culture comparison |
| Result | HSV-1 was detected on 77 of 291 days; all participants were positive at least once, mostly at oral sites and often asymptomatically. |
| Main limitation | Small cohort; PCR DNA does not prove infectious virions at every positive time point. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that HSV-1 reactivation/shedding was frequent and often asymptomatic in this small intensively sampled group.
- It can answer site distribution under the sampling protocol.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot estimate transmission probability per contact.
- It cannot prove infectious virus for every PCR-positive swab.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating HSV DNA shedding as measured infectiousness or generalizing from eight participants to all HSV-1 carriers.

## Vault conclusion
Ramchandani et al. 2016 supports a modern HSV-1 asymptomatic orofacial shedding rung, with a clear PCR-versus-infectiousness boundary.
