# Outbreaks of Hand, Foot, and Mouth Disease by Enterovirus 71

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary outbreak report linking EV71 isolation/typing with HFMD outbreaks and central-nervous-system complication counts
- Journal/year: *Archives of Disease in Childhood*, 1980
- Authors: Y. Ishimaru, S. Nakano, K. Yamaoka, and S. Takami
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Observational clinical-virological outbreak report |
| Subject / sample | Japanese HFMD outbreaks in 1973 and 1978 |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural EV71-associated outbreak infection |
| Comparator / control | Virus typing and cross-neutralization against prototype EV71 BrCr strain |
| Endpoint | Clinical HFMD, virus isolation/typing, cross-neutralization, and frequency of CNS complications |
| Result | EV71 was isolated in both outbreaks. CNS disorders were reported in 24% of patients in 1973 and 8% in 1978. Cross-neutralization did not show antigenic difference from the BrCr prototype strain under the reported tests. |
| Main limitation | The study is observational outbreak evidence. Neurologic association is not a controlled pathogenesis experiment and does not establish why particular patients developed CNS disease. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that EV71 was associated with HFMD outbreaks in which CNS complications were frequent.
- It can answer that the tested isolates were not antigenically distinct from the BrCr prototype by the reported cross-neutralization endpoint.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove the exact route of neuroinvasion or the mechanism of neurologic injury.
- It cannot prove that every neurologic disorder in the outbreak was directly virus-mediated.
- It cannot define universal EV71 severity or risk across all outbreaks.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is equating EV71 detection in outbreaks with proof that all neurologic injury was directly caused by the virus through a specified mechanism. The paper supports an outbreak association and typing rung, not full mechanism.

## Vault conclusion
Ishimaru et al. 1980 supports an EV71 HFMD outbreak rung with CNS-complication endpoints. It should be used to distinguish EV71-associated HFMD from Coxsackie A16-associated HFMD, while keeping neurologic causation and mechanism separate.
