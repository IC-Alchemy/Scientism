# FUTURE II 2007 Quadrivalent HPV Vaccine RCT (High-Grade Cervical Lesions)

## Source type
- Role in this vault: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled vaccine trial whose primary endpoint is the histopathological disease endpoint itself (HPV-16/18 DNA-confirmed cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2/3, adenocarcinoma in situ, or cervical cancer); fills queue item 10 / GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate) in [[synthesis/source-verification-queue]] and supplies the primary-paper anchor for the intervention rung of [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]].
- Trial name: Females United to Unilaterally Reduce Endo/Ectocervical Disease (FUTURE) II.
- Journal/year: *New England Journal of Medicine*, 2007;356(19):1915-1927.
- Authorship: The FUTURE II Study Group. **Name note:** the queue shortlist labeled this paper "Garland et al. 2007"; Garland et al. (*NEJM* 2007;356:1928-1943) is the companion FUTURE I trial (anogenital-disease endpoint), not this paper. Same name-error pattern as the item-8 "Cutland" reconciliation.
- Source basis in hand: full-text article (HighWire HTML archived by the Wayback Machine, 2007 snapshots of content.nejm.org/cgi/content/full/356/19/1915; 121 KB capture with Methods, Results, Discussion, and references). Read end to end.
- Funding/design disclosure: the trial "was designed, managed, and analyzed by Merck in conjunction with external academic investigators and members of the external data and safety monitoring board."

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial; enrollment June 2002 - May 2003 at 90 sites in 13 countries; ongoing at report with an average 3 years of follow-up after dose 1 (data cut June 15, 2006); group-sequential design with a prespecified interim analysis that was "part of the application for vaccine licensure" (FDA approval June 8, 2006) |
| Subject / sample | 12,707 women screened; 12,167 (96%) randomized 1:1 (6087 vaccine / 6080 placebo); ages 15-26; eligibility: not pregnant, no abnormal Papanicolaou smear, lifetime number of sex partners no more than four, effective contraception through month 7; baseline Pap abnormal 11.8% vs 11.1% between arms |
| Intervention or exposure | Three intramuscular doses at day 1, month 2, month 6 of quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 L1 virus-like-particle vaccine with amorphous aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate (AAHS) adjuvant (Gardasil, Merck) |
| Comparator / control | Sham arm: **yes, but not inert** - the placebo is aluminum-containing. Quote: "The quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 virus-like-particle vaccine with amorphous aluminum hydroxyphosphate sulfate adjuvant (Gardasil, Merck) and a visually indistinguishable aluminum-containing placebo have been described previously." So the comparison is adjuvant-vaccine vs adjuvant-alone, double-blinded by visual indistinguishability. It is not a saline/excipient-only placebo; adjuvant-specific systemic effects cannot be detected against this comparator |
| Endpoint | Named exactly: primary composite end point = "cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2 or 3, adenocarcinoma in situ, or invasive carcinoma of the cervix, with the detection of DNA from HPV-16, HPV-18, or both in one or more of three adjacent sections of the same lesion." Assignment by blinded consensus diagnosis of at least two of four pathologists under WHO histologic criteria; colposcopists instructed to biopsy all discrete abnormal areas (sensitivity) and HPV DNA in tissue required (specificity). Secondary/exploratory: type-specific seroconversion and month-24 seropositivity in an immunogenicity substudy; adverse events |
| Populations analyzed | (a) per-protocol susceptible: HPV-16/18 PCR- and serology-negative at enrollment and through month 7, all doses within 1 year, no protocol violations (10,565 = 87% of randomized; 5305 vs 5260); (b) unrestricted susceptible: negative at enrollment only (11,508 = 95%); (c) intention-to-treat: all randomized |
| Result | Per-protocol susceptible: 1 vs 42 composite-end-point cases, vaccine efficacy 98% (95.89% CI 86-100). Unrestricted susceptible: 3 vs 62, VE 95%. ITT, HPV-16/18-related: 83 vs 148, VE 44% (95% CI 26-58); the authors state "Vaccination did not appear to alter the course of cervical lesions related to HPV-16 or HPV-18 or of infection present at the time of randomization" (98% of the added ITT cases arose from infection prevalent at baseline). ITT, any HPV type: 219 vs 266, VE 17% (95% CI 1-31). Both-type-negative at enrollment (4693 vs 4703): 95 vs 130 cases, ~27% reduction (95% CI 4-44). Interim analysis: 0 vs 21, VE 100% (97.96% CI 76-100). "None of the women had invasive cervical cancer" in either arm. Immunogenicity substudy (n=1512 vaccinated): >99% seroconversion; month-24 seropositivity HPV-6 96%, HPV-11 97%, HPV-16 99%, HPV-18 68%, while HPV-18-lesion efficacy remained 100% through follow-up in the unrestricted susceptible population. Injection-site adverse events 84.4% vs 77.9% (pain risk difference 6.5 points, 95% CI 1.4-11.7); serious adverse events similar between arms; one placebo hypersensitivity discontinuation |
| Main limitation | The authors' own and structural: (1) Merck designed, managed, and analyzed the trial and the interim analysis doubled as the licensure application; (2) average follow-up ~3 years, "durability of vaccine-induced protection beyond 5 years is unknown"; (3) the primary analysis is per-protocol in a susceptible population (87% of randomized), which cannot speak to women already infected at vaccination - and the ITT analysis shows vaccination does not alter prevalent infection or lesions; (4) CIN2 histology is "less reproducible and spontaneous regression is more common than for grade 3" (the authors concede this); (5) invasive cancer was never an endpoint and no cancer occurred ("it is ethically unacceptable to use invasive cancer as the end point"); (6) women with HIV or immunosuppression were excluded; (7) the placebo contains AAHS, so systemic safety comparisons are adjuvant-matched, not against an inert comparator |

## Which gap and ladder rung this fills
- Queue item 10 / GAP 4 (intervention with disease endpoint, not surrogate) for the HPV -> cervical-cancer chain: the primary endpoint is the lesion itself (blinded histopathology + type attribution by HPV DNA in tissue), not an antibody titer. Immunogenicity is reported as secondary.
- Endpoint boundary preserved: CIN2/3+AIS is a histopathological precursor lesion, not invasive cancer. It is the accepted regulatory disease endpoint for prophylactic HPV trials (the paper notes an FDA advisory committee recommended powering to CIN2/3), but the vault records it as precursor-lesion evidence, not a cancer-incidence result: zero cancers occurred in either arm.
- This is the first primary-paper anchor of the HPV intervention rung of [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]]; until now that rung was started only by the secondary synthesis [[sources/cidrap-2026-hpv-vaccine-evidence-review]].
- GAP 1-3 and GAP 5 are untouched by this trial.

## What the experiment can answer
- That, in women 15-26 without HPV-16/18 infection at baseline and through month 7, three doses of quadrivalent VLP vaccine reduced HPV-16/18 DNA-confirmed CIN2/3, AIS, and cervical cancer by 98% versus an aluminum-containing placebo over ~3 years of blinded follow-up.
- That protection is type-restricted and baseline-status-dependent: against high-grade lesions from any HPV type in all randomized women, efficacy was 17%; vaccination did not change the course of infection or lesions already present at vaccination.
- That the vaccine's reactogenicity at the injection site exceeded adjuvant-only placebo by ~6.5 points, with systemic and serious adverse-event profiles similar between arms.

## What it cannot answer
- Whether the vaccine prevents invasive cervical cancer: no cancer occurred in either arm; cancer prevention is an extrapolation from precursor-lesion reduction.
- Therapeutic effect on existing HPV infection or lesions: the ITT analysis shows none.
- Population-level effectiveness (coverage, screening interaction, type replacement) and durability beyond 5 years.
- Safety relative to a truly inert placebo: the comparator contains the same adjuvant.
- Natural transmission dynamics or indirect effects: not measured.

## Later inferential-leap risk
- "98% effective" quotes without the susceptible-population qualifier (per-protocol, HPV-16/18-naive) against the 17% any-type ITT figure erase the population difference - the same figure-splitting risk recorded on [[concepts/surrogate-endpoints]].
- "Prevents cervical cancer" quotes rest on precursor lesions; the authors themselves argue CIN3/AIS "are likely to persist and may become invasive without treatment" - a prognostic claim from outside this experiment.
- "Placebo-controlled" quotes without naming the aluminum-containing comparator overstate what the safety arms can show; see the resolved row in [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]] (composition now verified from this full text).
- Cross-type protection sentences in the Discussion (e.g., HPV-18-seropositive women appearing protected against HPV-16 disease) are explicitly nonsignificant subgroup observations and must not be quoted as findings.

## Vault conclusion
Supported at the HPV intervention rung, with boundaries: a phase 3 randomized double-blind trial found that quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 vaccination reduced HPV-16/18 DNA-confirmed high-grade cervical lesions (98% per-protocol susceptible; 44% ITT type-specific; 17% ITT any type) versus an aluminum-containing placebo over ~3 years, with no cancer cases in either arm and no effect on infection or lesions already present. Cancer prevention, durability, population impact, and inert-placebo safety comparisons are separate, unproven rungs for this trial.
