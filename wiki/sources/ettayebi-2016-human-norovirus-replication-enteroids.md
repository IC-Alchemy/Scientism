# Replication of Human Noroviruses in Stem Cell-Derived Human Enteroids

## Source type
- Role in this vault: primary in vitro human intestinal epithelial replication-system paper for human noroviruses
- Journal/year: *Science*, 2016
- Authors: K. Ettayebi and colleagues
- Source basis in hand: full-text PMC article

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | In vitro culture/replication-system study |
| Subject / sample | Human intestinal enteroid monolayers and multiple human norovirus strains |
| Exposure or intervention | Inoculation of enteroids under bile and host-factor conditions |
| Comparator / control | Inactivated virus, serum neutralization, and permissive/nonpermissive culture conditions |
| Endpoint | Viral RNA increase and replication markers |
| Result | Multiple human norovirus strains replicated in stem-cell-derived human enteroids, with strain-dependent bile requirements; replication was blocked by inactivation or neutralization. |
| Main limitation | Specialized in vitro system; not a clinical disease model or transmission study. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that human noroviruses can replicate in a human intestinal epithelial culture model under defined conditions.
- It can answer that replication requirements differ across strains and conditions.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot prove symptom causation, infectious dose, or transmission dynamics in humans.
- It cannot replace challenge or outbreak evidence.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is treating cell replication as equivalent to clinical pathogenicity. This is a culture/replication rung, not a disease-reproduction endpoint.

## Vault conclusion
Ettayebi et al. 2016 supports a major human norovirus replication-system rung. It should be used for culture feasibility and replication biology, not as standalone clinical causation proof.
