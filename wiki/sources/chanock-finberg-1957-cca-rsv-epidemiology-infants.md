# Recovery from Infants with Respiratory Illness of a Virus Related to Chimpanzee Coryza Agent II

## Source type
- Role in this vault: early epidemiologic and serologic association paper for CCA-related/RSV infection in infants and young children
- Journal/year: *American Journal of Hygiene*, 1957
- Authors: R. Chanock and L. Finberg
- Source basis in hand: full-text scan used in this pass

## Evidence fields
| Field | Extraction |
|---|---|
| Study type | Epidemiologic and serologic association study |
| Subject / sample | Children under 4 with bronchopneumonia or bronchiolitis plus outpatient and hospital controls |
| Exposure or intervention | Natural infection inferred from virus isolation or antibody rise |
| Comparator / control | Outpatient and hospital nonrespiratory controls |
| Endpoint | Virus isolation and complement-fixation or neutralizing antibody rise |
| Result | Hospitalized bronchiolitis/pneumonia association was not demonstrated because ward controls had high infection rates; outpatient bronchopneumonia showed higher infection than outpatient controls, but the outpatient case set was small. |
| Main limitation | Small outpatient sample, imperfect control matching, and high nosocomial background infection limited causal attribution. |

## What the experiment can answer
- It can answer that infection markers were detected in some infants and young children with respiratory illness.
- It can answer that hospital control infection rates complicated the association.

## What it cannot answer
- It cannot cleanly prove RSV causes bronchiolitis from the hospitalized comparison.
- It cannot settle ordinary transmission or disease mechanism.

## Later inferential-leap risk
The risk is using early epidemiologic association as a direct RSV-causes-bronchiolitis proof despite the paper's own control and nosocomial-infection boundaries.

## Vault conclusion
Chanock and Finberg 1957 supports early RSV/CCA-related infection epidemiology and serology, but its bronchiolitis attribution is control-limited.
