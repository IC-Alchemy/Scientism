# Ladder Standard vs Revisionist Virus-Causation Critique

## What they share
Both positions reject uncritical acceptance of virus-disease attribution narratives. Both agree that many foundational experiments answer narrower questions than the later stories built on them, and both treat endpoint ambiguity, weak controls, and artificial exposure routes as real problems rather than footnotes.

## Where they diverge
| Dimension | This vault's ladder standard | Revisionist critique (ViroLIEgy-line sources) |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Which specific rung — detection, isolation, serology, animal challenge, human challenge, transmission, intervention — does a named experiment answer? | Whether the foundational experiments support virus causation at all; negative or ambiguous results are read as undermining the framework. |
| Handling of positive results | Accepted as answering their named rung, no more (e.g., isolation answers isolation, not natural-route causation). | Frequently treated as methodologically invalid (contaminated culture, unpurified samples) and therefore as answering nothing. |
| Handling of negative results | Preserved as route, host, or endpoint boundaries without generalizing ([[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]]). | Treated as evidence against causation in general, extending beyond the tested condition. |
| Standard of proof | Rung-by-rung; a claim is supported only at the rung its experiments reached, with replication the strongest marker. | Frequently an all-or-nothing demand (complete purification, sham controls everywhere), the failure of which is counted against the whole framework. |
| Endpoint discipline | Endpoint names are mandatory; PCR positivity, serology, culture effect, and disease are never interchangeable. | Valuable where it motivates skepticism of PCR-only or serology-only claims; overextended where it denies that culture-plus-challenge-plus-re-isolation chains answer anything. |
| View of consensus | Irrelevant as evidence; only named experiments count. | Treated as evidence of institutional fraud, which this vault does not adopt. |

## What question each position can answer
- The ladder standard can answer, per rung, what a named experiment showed and what it cannot be stretched to show — including where the mainstream narrative itself overreaches ([[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]]).
- The revisionist critique can answer where mainstream accounts flatten negatives and endpoint differences; its commentary sources are dispute-mapping material, not experiment-grade support under this vault's standard.
- Neither position is supported by the other's failure modes: ladder overreach (endpoint compression) and revisionist overreach (generalizing negatives, all-or-nothing purity demands) are symmetric inferential errors.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- The dispute is real and should be mapped, not flattened: the revisionist critique correctly identifies endpoint compression in mainstream retellings, and the ladder standard correctly limits what the critique's own negative-generalizations can claim.
- Tier 3 revisionist sources can seed questions and identify ambiguous episodes but cannot establish factual conclusions (iron rule 3).
- On every disease row audited so far, the honest state is partial: some rungs strong, some boundary-limited, some unreconstructed — not the clean closure the mainstream narrative implies, and not the total failure the revisionist narrative implies.
