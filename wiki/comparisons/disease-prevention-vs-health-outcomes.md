# Disease Prevention vs Health Outcomes

## What they share
Both framings assume that trial design and outcome measurement matter when judging whether an intervention is beneficial. The disagreement is about what level of outcome is needed to support a stronger claim.

## Where they diverge
| Dimension | Disease prevention | Health outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Core question | Does the intervention reduce the targeted disease or named event? | Does the intervention improve broader health status? |
| Typical endpoint | Infection, diagnosis, recurrence, antibody response, or other narrower measure | Mortality, hospitalization, severe disease, disability, symptom burden, or quality of life |
| Comparator issue | May still be studied with active comparators | Usually still requires clear endpoint naming and careful comparator interpretation |
| Main risk | Over-reading a narrower endpoint as total benefit | Treating absence of broad outcome data as if it were already resolved |

## Which sources take which side
- [[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]] frames this as a core methodological distinction for the vault.
- [[sources/vaccine-makers-do-not-use-placebos-to-test-the-safety-of-their-vaccines-for-kids]] argues that control-group design can affect how strongly safety conclusions should be stated.
- [[sources/profitable-sacred-cows-that-are-clearly-wrong-but-profitable-continue-getting-fu]] raises a weaker, more speculative concern about numerator and denominator quality in public-health datasets.

## What question the underlying studies can answer
- If a study measures only disease prevention, it can support that narrower claim within the limits of its design.
- If a study measures broader clinical endpoints, it can support broader health claims to the extent those endpoints were directly observed.
- If a commentary moves from one level to the other without showing the bridge, that is a candidate [[concepts/inferential-leap]].

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This distinction is methodologically important.
- It should be stated with the actual endpoint names rather than with generic phrases such as "worked" or "safe."
- Control-group design and endpoint choice should be analyzed together, not separately.
