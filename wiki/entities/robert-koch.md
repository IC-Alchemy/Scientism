# Robert Koch

## What it is
[[entities/robert-koch]] is a historical figure tied to laboratory standards for identifying pathogens and establishing transmission. In this vault he is also a focal point for the dispute over how much later germ-theory history compresses multiple methodological issues into a simpler success narrative.

## Why it matters
The page matters because it anchors the vault's interest in how claims of causation are experimentally established and later generalized.

## What the sources say
- [[sources/kochs-famous-paper]] shows Koch emphasizing pure culture, microscopy, host selection, tissue evidence, and the distinction between pathogenicity and infectiousness.
- [[sources/the-germ-hypothesis-part-2]] argues that later claims about Koch's proof outran what his experiments and later concessions established.
- [[sources/experimental-inhalation-anthrax-in-the-chimpanzee]] is relevant because anthrax challenge work is one of the experimental terrains often used in broader causation arguments associated with Koch's legacy.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- Koch is central here less as a symbol than as a methodological benchmark.
- [[sources/kochs-famous-paper]] can be used for the narrower claim that Koch's text contains technically specific methodological concerns around culture, microscopy, host selection, tissue evidence, pathogenicity, and infectiousness.
- Claims about simplified textbook retellings remain unresolved until actual textbook or secondary-history sources are ingested.
- The current sources do not yet justify either a full validation or a full dismissal of Koch-centered germ-theory history; the dispute remains source-specific.

## Related source pages in this vault

The central dispute wired below concerns the status of Koch's postulates: [[sources/kochs-famous-paper]] records Koch's own methodological standards, [[sources/fouchier-2003-kochs-postulates-sars-macaques]] records a modern claim that the postulates were fulfilled for SARS, and [[sources/rivers-1937-viruses-and-kochs-postulates]] records the argument that the postulates cannot be applied literally to viruses. Supporting and critical views are linked side by side.

### Experimental record
- [[sources/kochs-famous-paper]] - the vault's historical text attributed to Koch (1881), recorded by its own page as a Tier 1 primary-source style document with caution about translation and excerpting. The page records Koch's standards for pure culture, microscopy, host selection, tissue evidence, and the pathogenicity/infectiousness distinction, and notes that no specific experiment/result pair has been extracted from it yet.
- [[sources/rivers-1937-viruses-and-kochs-postulates]] - 1937 Tier 1 methodological paper by Thomas Rivers arguing that viral causation does not require literal fulfillment of Koch's bacteriological postulates but still requires proof of causal relevance beyond association.
- [[sources/fouchier-2003-kochs-postulates-sars-macaques]] - 2003 Tier 1 abstract-level Nature correspondence in which the Erasmus group states that experimental infection of cynomolgus macaques with SARS-associated coronavirus reproduced disease, framed by the authors as Koch's postulates fulfilled. Its page records that this phrasing is author framing and that endpoint detail is bounded by the companion Kuiken full-text reconstruction.
- [[sources/fredericks-relman-1996-sequence-based-identification-microbial-pathogens]] - Tier 2 methodological review proposing sequence-based criteria for linking microbial nucleic-acid evidence to disease while warning that a sequence hit alone is not causal proof; a molecular-era reconsideration of Koch's postulates.
- [[sources/experimental-inhalation-anthrax-in-the-chimpanzee]] - 1959 Tier 1 primary paper on aerosol anthrax challenge in chimpanzees, currently held only as a citation-pointer stub; its page records that dose, comparator, endpoint, and result are not yet extracted.
- [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] - vault synthesis page, not itself a source, for separating detection, isolation, transmission, challenge, molecular, clinical, and intervention evidence; it treats Rivers 1937 as the key historical bridge from Koch's postulates to modern viral-causation standards.

### Commentary and critique

## Open questions
- The vault's Koch text page records that no specific experiment/result pair has been extracted from it, and the anthrax challenge paper remains an unextracted stub, so Koch's own anthrax and tuberculosis experiments are not yet experimentally supported in this vault.
- No Tier 1 or Tier 2 source has yet been ingested on how later textbooks actually retell Koch; the simplified-retelling question recorded above stays unresolved.
