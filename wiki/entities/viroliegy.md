# ViroLIEgy

## Why this page matters
ViroLIEgy is a recurring source of raw material in this vault. Its articles often preserve quotations and research leads, but they also use strong polemical framing and make broader claims than the quoted evidence clearly supports.

## What the sources say
- [[sources/diagnosis-smallpox-viroliegy]] argues that diagnostic ambiguity in rash diseases undermines retrospective certainty about smallpox.
- [[sources/edward-jenners-smallpox-paper-1798]] uses Jenner excerpts to argue that later vaccination narratives simplify the original record.
- [[sources/louis-pasteurs-unethical-rabies-fraud]] and [[sources/from-pasteur-to-panic-fear-fraud-and-the-fight-for-historical-truth]] frame Pasteur and modern medicine in explicitly adversarial terms.
- [[sources/the-infectious-myth-busted-part-6-the-germ-duel]] uses negative challenge anecdotes to contest broader infection claims.
- [[sources/viroliegy-101-the-scientific-method]] frames ViroLIEgy as offering a general methodology critique, not just case-by-case historical revision.
- [[sources/the-infectious-myth-busted-part-1-the-rosenau-spanish-flu-experiments-1918]] uses one historical influenza experiment series to support a broader anti-infectiousness argument.
- [[sources/an-inquiry-into-the-logical-basis-of-the-germ-theory]] uses historical dissent quotations to argue that germ theory fails on logical grounds.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- ViroLIEgy is useful as a Tier 3 source of questions, quotations, and dispute structure.
- It should not be treated as sufficient on its own for factual closure on historical or medical claims.
- When ViroLIEgy cites a concrete experiment, the next step in this vault should usually be to ingest the underlying source directly.
