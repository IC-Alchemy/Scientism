# Mike Stone

## Why this page matters
Mike Stone appears in this vault as a recurring modern critic of germ-theory history, especially around Koch, Pasteur, rabies, and virology.

## What the sources say
- [[sources/the-germ-hypothesis-part-2]] argues that Koch's work is often credited with proving more than the historical experiments justify.
- [[sources/from-pasteur-to-panic-fear-fraud-and-the-fight-for-historical-truth]] presents Stone as a broader critic of modern infectious-disease narratives and medical authority.
- [[sources/viroliegy-101-the-scientific-method]] presents Stone as a critic of modern virology on methodological grounds, especially around hypotheses, variables, controls, and reproducibility.
- [[sources/the-infectious-myth-busted-part-1-the-rosenau-spanish-flu-experiments-1918]] presents Stone as using negative historical transmission experiments to challenge later infectious-disease doctrine.
- [[sources/an-inquiry-into-the-logical-basis-of-the-germ-theory]] presents Stone as a compiler and interpreter of historical anti-germ-theory arguments about logic and causation.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- Stone is important here as a map-maker of disputed claims, not yet as an authority whose conclusions have been independently confirmed in this vault.
- His pages are useful when they name specific experiments, endpoints, and historical episodes that can be checked directly.
- Stronger conclusions about the truth of his historical interpretation remain unresolved.
