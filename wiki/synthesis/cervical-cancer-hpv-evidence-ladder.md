# Cervical Cancer to Human Papillomavirus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make screening, vaccination, treatment, or prevention recommendations.

## Current answer
The cervical cancer -> HPV row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The current full-text set supports molecular tumor-DNA detection, HPV type distinction, and physical-state/integration rungs:

- [[sources/durst-gissmann-ikenberg-zur-hausen-1983-hpv16-cervical-carcinoma]] supports HPV16 cloning from cervical carcinoma material and frequent HPV16-homologous DNA detection in tested cervical cancers.
- [[sources/boshart-gissmann-ikenberg-1984-hpv18-genital-cancer-biopsies]] supports HPV18 cloning from cervical carcinoma material, detection in some genital cancers and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines, and integration-compatible physical-state findings.
- [[sources/durst-kleinheinz-hotz-gissmann-1985-physical-state-hpv16-genital-tumours]] supports HPV16 episomal-versus-integrated physical-state differences in a small selected set of benign and malignant genital tumors while explicitly leaving causal direction open.
- [[sources/matsukura-kanda-yoshikawa-1986-hpv16-integrated-cervical-carcinoma]] supports direct cloning and sequencing of HPV16-cell DNA junctions in one cervical carcinoma.
- [[sources/cullen-reid-campion-lorincz-1991-physical-state-hpv-cervical-neoplasm]] supports a larger physical-state boundary: integration was much more frequent in invasive cancers than in CIN or latent infection, but not universal in invasive cancer.

This is a strong molecular association and integration ladder, now with a replicated intervention-evidence rung anchored by two primary papers - [[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]] (cervical CIN2+ composite) and [[sources/future-i-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-anogenital-diseases-rct]] (external anogenital + cervical composites, added 2026-11-11) - and started at synthesis level by [[sources/cidrap-2026-hpv-vaccine-evidence-review]]. The current full-text set does not yet reconstruct prospective incident infection, persistence, CIN2+/CIN3 progression, or the 1999 "necessary cause" paper at full-text source-page level.

| Necessity claim | Walboomers et al. 1999 full text not obtained in this pass. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass. | A title or abstract-level "necessary cause" claim cannot be promoted without full methods, assays, exclusions, and residual-negative cases. |
| Sufficiency and cofactors | Not reconstructed here. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass. | HPV DNA detection and integration cannot prove that HPV alone is sufficient for invasive cancer. |
| Intervention evidence | Primary papers: [[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]] (FUTURE II, phase 3 RCT, 12,167 women 15-26; cervical composite endpoint CIN2/3, AIS, or cervical cancer with HPV-16/18 DNA in the lesion, blinded consensus pathology; VE 98% per-protocol susceptible, 44% ITT type-specific, 17% ITT any type, zero cancers in either arm) and [[sources/future-i-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-anogenital-diseases-rct]] (FUTURE I, phase 3 RCT, 5,455 women 16-24, independent populations; coprimary composites: anogenital warts + VIN/VaIN grades 1-3 or cancer, and CIN1-3 + AIS or cancer, each with vaccine-type HPV DNA in the lesion; per-protocol 100% (0 vs 60) external and 100% (0 vs 65) cervical; unrestricted susceptible 95%/98%; ITT vaccine-type 73%/55%; ITT any-type 34%/20%). CIDRAP: RCT-level CIN2+ reduction for vaccine-matched types at ages 15-25 (RR 0.39, 0.29-0.53) with no reduction at 25+; pooled observational cervical-cancer (RR 0.34) and CIN3+ (RR 0.37) reductions with a strong younger-initiation gradient; Cochrane-certainty findings of no elevated SAE, GBS, CRPS, or CFS/ME risk and one mixed POTS outcome. | Neither arm accrued cancers (FUTURE II zero in both arms; FUTURE I zero vaccine-type cancers, one non-vaccine-type vulvar cancer in the vaccine arm) - cancer prevention is extrapolation from precursor lesions; follow-up ~3 years with durability unknown; the placebo is AAHS-matched, so inert-comparator safety effects are untestable in either trial; no therapeutic effect on prevalent infection/lesions (FUTURE I: "no clear evidence that vaccination altered the course of disease or infection present before administration of the first dose"); populations are healthy young women with restricted partner counts; CIDRAP: over half the new observational studies had severe-to-critical risk of bias; the rung says nothing about necessity, sufficiency, or cofactors. |

## Current manuscript rule for cervical cancer and HPV
Cervical cancer should be presented as an association-heavy molecular ladder, not as a simple infection challenge model:

- Durst et al. 1983 answers an HPV16 tumor-DNA cloning and prevalence question.
- Boshart et al. 1984 answers an HPV18 tumor-DNA/cell-line detection and physical-state question.
- Durst et al. 1985 answers a small selected HPV16 episomal-versus-integrated physical-state question and explicitly leaves causal direction open.
- Matsukura et al. 1986 answers a direct viral-cell junction structure question in one carcinoma.
- Cullen et al. 1991 answers a broader cross-sectional integration-frequency question while preserving the fact that some invasive cancers had only episomal HPV DNA.
- FUTURE II (2007) answers a prophylactic-intervention question for quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 vaccination in HPV-16/18-naive young women against the CIN2/3+AIS+cancer composite (98% per-protocol) and must be cited with its boundaries: aluminum-containing placebo (not inert), no cancer cases in either arm, ~3-year follow-up, no effect on lesions present at vaccination.
- FUTURE I (2007) replicates the same design in an independent 5,455-woman population and adds the external anogenital disease composite (warts, VIN/VaIN) as a coprimary endpoint (100% per-protocol; 34% ITT any-type) with the same boundaries: aluminum-containing placebo (not inert), zero vaccine-type cancers, ~3-year follow-up, no effect on prevalent disease; its "widespread vaccination" and dosing-flexibility generalizations are recorded as inferential leaps on the source page.
- The CIDRAP 2026 review answers a prophylactic-intervention question (dose count and age at initiation against infection, premalignant, cancer, and safety endpoints) and must be cited with its rung boundaries: RCT premalignant endpoints, observational cancer outcomes, unresolved one-dose margin.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade HPV/cervical cancer from a candidate row into a started tumor-DNA and integration evidence ladder with a started intervention rung. It should not state that necessity, sufficiency, prospective persistence, or cofactor structure has been reconstructed here.
