# Cold Sores to HSV-1 Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, exposure-management, or sexual-health recommendations.

## Current answer
The cold sores -> HSV-1 row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder:

- [[sources/spruance-1984-pathogenesis-herpes-simplex-labialis-oral-excretion]] supports infectious HSV recovery from oral-cavity specimens in recurrent labialis subjects.
- [[sources/kriesel-1994-pcr-uv-induced-herpes-labialis]] supports experimental UV-induced labialis with PCR/culture oral-secretion endpoints and a negative pre-lesion oral-shedding finding.
- [[sources/ramchandani-2016-hsv1-shedding-tears-nasal-oral-mucosa]] supports frequent, often asymptomatic HSV-1 DNA shedding across orofacial sites in a small intensive sampling study.

The current full-text set supports HSV-1 recovery and shedding in recurrent labialis contexts. It does not by itself settle exact recurrence triggers or person-to-person transmission probability.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Infectious oral recovery | [[sources/spruance-1984-pathogenesis-herpes-simplex-labialis-oral-excretion]] | HSV can be isolated from oral-cavity specimens in some recurrent labialis subjects, sometimes outside obvious lesion contamination. | Transmission probability or recurrence triggers. |
| Experimental recurrence model | [[sources/kriesel-1994-pcr-uv-induced-herpes-labialis]] | PCR detects oral HSV more often than culture in a UV-induced labialis model; pre-lesion oral shedding was not increased in that setup. | Natural recurrence across populations or lesion pathogenesis from oral secretion alone. |
| Asymptomatic shedding | [[sources/ramchandani-2016-hsv1-shedding-tears-nasal-oral-mucosa]] | HSV-1 DNA shedding can be frequent and asymptomatic at orofacial sites in a small sampled group. | Infectiousness of every PCR-positive specimen or per-contact transmission risk. |

## Current manuscript rule for cold sores and HSV-1
HSV-1 should be presented as a recurrence and shedding ladder: infectious oral recovery, UV-reactivation detection, and intensive PCR shedding. The manuscript may say the current full-text set supports HSV-1 association with recurrent labialis and asymptomatic oral shedding. It should also say that shedding endpoints do not directly measure transmission probability or explain every recurrence trigger.
