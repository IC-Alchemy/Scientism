# Hepatitis C to Hepatitis C Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make exposure, testing, blood-donation, treatment, or prevention recommendations.

## Current answer
The hepatitis C row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text set in this pass supports four major rungs:

- [[sources/choo-kuo-weiner-1989-cdna-clone-non-a-non-b-hcv-genome]] supports molecular identification of HCV-associated cDNA clones and antigen/antibody reactivity in non-A non-B hepatitis material.
- [[sources/kuo-choo-alter-1989-assay-circulating-antibodies-hcv]] supports recombinant C100-3 antibody-assay validation against chimpanzee-proven infectious sera, matched donor-recipient cases, and chronic NANBH cohorts.
- [[sources/alter-purcell-shih-1989-hcv-antibody-transfusion-recipients]] supports prospective transfusion-recipient anti-HCV seroconversion and donor linkage in selected acute and chronic non-A non-B hepatitis cases.
- [[sources/yanagi-purcell-emerson-bukh-1997-hcv-infectious-cdna-clone-chimpanzee]] supports infectious molecular-clone replication after direct intrahepatic transfection of a chimpanzee, while preserving noninfectious clone results.

This is a stronger ladder than mere association, but it is not a complete natural-history reconstruction. The current full-text set supports molecular identification, antibody-assay validation, prospective transfusion serology/donor linkage, and molecular-clone infectivity. It does not yet reconstruct the 1984 non-A/non-B chimpanzee passage paper, the 1999 transmissible-virion clone paper, ordinary non-transfusion transmission, or all chronic-disease mechanisms.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current HCV evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-A non-B hepatitis transmission/passaging | Abstract-level record: [[sources/tabor-1984-non-a-non-b-two-agents-chimpanzees]]. | Two human-derived inocula each transmitted and passaged non-A/non-B hepatitis to chimpanzees, with hepatocyte cytoplasmic tubules tracking one agent but not the other. | Abstract-level: animal numbers and hepatitis grading unextracted; agent identity to later-cloned HCV is not established by this record. |
| Molecular identification | [[sources/choo-kuo-weiner-1989-cdna-clone-non-a-non-b-hcv-genome]] | Infectious NANBH material contained exogenous RNA linked to cDNA clones and a recombinant antigen recognized by NANBH patient and NANBH-infected chimpanzee sera, but not by the reported normal donor or HAV/HBV chimpanzee controls. | Molecular identification and antigen association are not identical to infectious full-genome proof or clinical causation. |
| Prospective transfusion serology | [[sources/alter-purcell-shih-1989-hcv-antibody-transfusion-recipients]] | Anti-HCV seroconversion tracked selected prospectively followed transfusion-associated NANBH cases, and anti-HCV-positive donors were found in most fully evaluable linked transmissions. | Antibody seroconversion does not directly prove infectious clone, culture, or every non-A non-B hepatitis case. |
| Antibody assay validation | [[sources/kuo-choo-alter-1989-assay-circulating-antibodies-hcv]] | Anti-HCV to recombinant C100-3 was found in six of seven chimpanzee-proven infectious NANBH sera, all ten chronic post-transfusion NANBH recipients tested seroconverted, and nine of ten linked transfusion cases had at least one anti-HCV-positive donor. | Antibody detection is indirect, delayed, weaker in acute resolving cases, and not equivalent to infectious virus isolation or universal NANBH attribution. |
| Infectious molecular clone | [[sources/yanagi-purcell-emerson-bukh-1997-hcv-infectious-cdna-clone-chimpanzee]] | RNA transcripts from one consensus full-length H77 clone initiated HCV replication after direct intrahepatic chimpanzee transfection; two other full-length clones did not. | Direct liver transfection is not ordinary human transmission, and one clone does not prove all genotypes or routes. |
| Transmissible molecular-clone-derived virions | Abstract-level record: [[sources/hong-1999-transmissible-hcv-molecular-clone]]. | Consensus-repaired H77 clone RNA initiated HCV infection with hepatitis in a chimpanzee after intrahepatic transfection, and its serum transmitted infection to a second naive animal. | Abstract-level with n = 1 per step; intrahepatic transfection is artificial; kinetics and histology need full text. |
| Ordinary non-transfusion transmission and chronic disease mechanisms | Not reconstructed here. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass alone. | Molecular and transfusion rungs cannot be silently substituted for all route and outcome questions. |

## Current manuscript rule for hepatitis C
Hepatitis C should be presented as a multi-rung molecular and transfusion-linked case:

- Choo et al. 1989 answers a molecular-identification and antigen-association question.
- Kuo et al. 1989 answers a recombinant-antigen antibody-assay validation and matched donor-recipient association question.
- Alter et al. 1989 answers a prospective transfusion-recipient serology and donor-linkage question.
- Yanagi et al. 1997 answers an infectious molecular-clone question in a chimpanzee under direct intrahepatic transfection, while preserving failed/noninfectious clone findings.
- The 1984 pre-HCV chimpanzee passage paper and 1999 transmissible-virion molecular clone paper now have abstract-level source pages ([[sources/tabor-1984-non-a-non-b-two-agents-chimpanzees]], [[sources/hong-1999-transmissible-hcv-molecular-clone]]) but remain full-text acquisition gaps at manuscript strength.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade HCV from a candidate row into a started molecular-identification, antibody-assay, prospective-serology, and molecular-clone challenge example. It should not claim that ordinary non-transfusion transmission or all chronic-disease mechanisms have been reconstructed here.
