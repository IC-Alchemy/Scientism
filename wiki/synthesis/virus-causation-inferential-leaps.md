# Virus Causation Inferential Leaps

> Warning: This page is provisional. It analyzes inferential-leap patterns from the current candidate-paper list, not from full source-page ingests of every paper.

## Why this page exists
The current [[synthesis/virus-disease-attribution-audit-worklist]] identifies candidate foundational papers for several disease-virus attribution claims. The next question is not only whether those papers exist, but what they actually establish and how later narratives may overextend them.

In this vault, scientism often appears when a narrower laboratory or animal result is treated as if it directly settled a broader causal, natural-history, or policy question without reconstructing the missing bridge.

The method page [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] now makes that bridge explicit by separating detection, culture, serology, animal challenge, human challenge, transmission-chain, intervention, and replicated convergence evidence.

## Core analytic rule
For each candidate paper, ask two separate questions:

1. What did the experiment actually do?
2. What larger causation claim was later built on top of it?

If the second question outruns the first, the gap should be marked as an inferential leap rather than silently absorbed into a settled narrative.

## Recurrent inferential-leap patterns
### 1. Artificial-route challenge becomes natural-route causation
An experiment may show that biological material can produce illness when placed into a body by an artificial route. That does not automatically prove that the same process explains ordinary transmission in the wild.

This matters because direct inoculation can bypass the barriers that a natural exposure route would have to cross.

### 2. Detection or isolation becomes full causal proof
A paper may show that a virus-like agent is present, recoverable, cultivable, or detectable in patients. That is stronger than commentary, but it is not identical to proving that the agent alone is sufficient to explain the disease pattern in ordinary conditions.

### 3. Animal-model disease becomes human natural history
An animal challenge model may show that a candidate agent can produce pathology in a susceptible model organism. The leap occurs when that result is treated as if it settled the whole human causal story, including route, dose, susceptibility, and background cofactors.

### 4. Association becomes necessity, sufficiency, or exclusivity
A repeated association can be important, but the leap begins when a detection or association result is translated into a claim that the agent is the necessary cause, the sufficient cause, or the sole meaningful cause without reconstructing the missing steps.

### 5. Laboratory tractability becomes real-world explanatory closure
Once an agent can be cultured, typed, sequenced, or propagated, the field may act as if the causal question is over. But tractability in the lab is not the same thing as a complete demonstration of ordinary disease causation in populations.

## Polio as a worked example
The current shortlist for poliomyelitis includes early monkey-inoculation papers and later tissue-culture isolation work:

- *EXPERIMENTAL EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS.* (1910)
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: THE ACTIVITY OF THE VIRUS* (1910)
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: A MODE OF SPONTANEOUS INFECTION* (1910)
- *TRANSMISSION EXPERIMENTS WITH THE VIRUS OF POLIOMYELITIS...* (1911)
- *Cultivation of the Lansing Strain...* (1949)
- *Studies on the cultivation of poliomyelitis viruses in tissue culture...* (1951)

### What the current evidence list appears capable of answering
- Whether biological material from poliomyelitis cases could produce paralytic disease in monkeys under the experimental conditions used.
- Whether the agent could later be cultivated or isolated in tissue culture and identified from patient material.

### The likely inferential leap
The leap comes if highly artificial inoculation routes are treated as if they directly prove ordinary natural causation in humans.

The user's stated concern is exactly this boundary: if repeated ordinary-route or naturalistic transmission attempts failed, but intracerebral or similarly invasive inoculation produced paralysis, then the narrower result would be that direct placement of infectious material into privileged tissue can produce paralytic disease in that model. The broader claim that natural poliomyelitis epidemics were therefore explained by the same route, mechanism, and agent would still require additional evidence.

### What is still missing before this vault can state that strongly
- A broader source-page reconstruction of the early polio experiments, including the actual inoculation route used in each paper. One primary boundary paper is now in hand: [[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]].
- A direct list of negative or failed natural-route transmission experiments beyond the Sabin-Olitsky tissue-recovery study rather than a secondhand summary of them.
- A boundary between experiments that produced disease only after invasive placement and experiments that succeeded by routes plausibly analogous to ordinary exposure.
- A clear endpoint distinction between paralysis in an experimental animal and the broader claim that epidemic human poliomyelitis had been causally explained.

### Current vault conclusion on the polio example
The vault can already flag the *type* of inferential leap: artificial-route challenge does not equal demonstrated natural-route causation.

The vault now also has one direct primary paper showing that a 1938 intranasal monkey study tracked tissue recovery over time and still treated host-to-host transmission as a live problem rather than reporting a simple transmission proof. What the vault does **not** yet have in hand is a reconstructed primary-paper record sufficient to say, in its own voice, that scientists tried "every possible natural means" or that "hundreds of failed experiments" were ignored. Those stronger claims should remain attributed or provisional until the failed-experiment record is directly assembled.

## Other likely leap patterns in the current shortlist
### AIDS -> HIV
The early AIDS shortlist is strongest on isolation, repeat detection, and association in affected groups. The likely leap is from repeated isolation in AIDS-spectrum patients to the full causal claim that HIV alone explains the syndrome in all relevant contexts without separately rebuilding the bridge from association, immune-cell tropism, and cohort outcomes to general causation.

### Influenza -> influenza virus
The classic 1933 paper and later transmission studies are stronger than mere speculation, but the leap can occur when a filterable agent in patient material plus animal transmission is treated as if it settled every later claim about ordinary human transmission dynamics, seasonality, population susceptibility, and all influenza-like illness.

The vault now also has a direct primary negative-result boundary paper from before virus identification: [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]]. Rosenau's extracted 1919 report preserves repeated human challenge failures across sprayed secretions, direct nose-to-nose and throat-to-throat transfer, blood injection, filtered-material injection, and intimate ward exposure. That makes two separate overreach patterns visible at once:

- later certainty narratives can glide past the fact that a primary outbreak-era volunteer series repeatedly failed under the tested conditions
- counter-narratives can turn those same failures into a blanket claim that influenza is noninfectious, even though Rosenau explicitly warned against drawing positive conclusions from the negative results

For this vault, the narrower defensible point is that the early transmission record included unresolved human challenge failures that any stronger influenza-causation story must account for. The stronger claim that those failures disproved infectious spread is not supported by Rosenau's own framing.

### COVID-19 -> SARS-CoV-2
The early COVID papers are strongest on outbreak association, genomic identification, culture, and animal challenge. The leap begins when these results are treated as if they automatically settle every downstream question about disease heterogeneity, causal sufficiency, measurement, or policy.

The current endpoint-specific audit is [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]]. Its main correction is that the Killingley human challenge study should not be treated as a controlled human causation experiment: it was open-label, non-randomized, and lacked a sham-inoculated control group. The animal challenge papers may support model-specific pathogenicity when they include pathology, culture, re-isolation, or mock controls, but they do not by themselves prove controlled human clinical causation for the syndrome COVID-19.

For this vault, PCR and RT-qPCR should be recorded as nucleic-acid detection endpoints, not as standalone proof of infectious virus, clinical diagnosis, or disease causation.

### Cervical cancer -> HPV
The HPV shortlist is strong on repeated detection, subtype focus, and integration findings. The inferential danger is translating strong association and near-ubiquity findings directly into a simple one-factor causal story without clearly separating necessity, sufficiency, cofactors, and time course.

### Mumps, rubella, and chickenpox
The newly logged mumps, rubella, and chickenpox rows show a different pattern from the polio example. Several candidate papers are stronger than pure detection because they include animal inoculation, human experimental exposure, culture, serology, or fatal-case pathology. The leap risk is not simply "detection equals causation"; it is more specific:

- mumps papers need route and control boundaries preserved, especially where parotid-gland inoculation or induced infection is treated as if it fully reconstructs ordinary transmission
- rubella papers need acute-rubella, experimental-rubella, and congenital-rubella-syndrome endpoints kept separate
- varicella papers need chickenpox lesion/isolation evidence separated from later zoster recurrence and latency claims

For this vault, these rows should be source-ingested as endpoint bundles rather than as one broad "virus causes disease" conclusion.

### Hepatitis A, B, and C
The hepatitis rows make the endpoint ladder unusually visible:

- hepatitis A starts with virus-like stool antigen detection, nonhuman-primate infectivity, particle characterization, and later culture propagation
- hepatitis B moves from clinical/immunologic syndrome separation and Australia-antigen particle detection into chimpanzee transmission and molecular-clone challenge
- hepatitis C moves from non-A/non-B transfusion and chimpanzee transmission into molecular identification, antibody assays, and infectious molecular-clone work

The inferential leap to watch is compression of that ladder. Detection, serology, animal infectivity, and molecular-clone challenge are not interchangeable endpoints. A paper showing antibody association or particle detection does not answer the same question as a paper showing cloned viral RNA can initiate infection in a chimpanzee.

### EBV, Burkitt lymphoma, infectious mononucleosis, and HHV-8 Kaposi sarcoma
The EBV and HHV-8 rows are association-heavy in a way that needs special handling. Prospective EBV seroconversion evidence for infectious mononucleosis is not the same endpoint as EBV DNA or particles in Burkitt lymphoma tissue or cultured cells. Likewise, HHV-8 lesion-specific DNA and serology in Kaposi sarcoma are stronger than commentary, but they still need careful separation from claims about necessity, sufficiency, cofactors, immune status, and tumor initiation.

The scientism risk here is treating repeated molecular presence as causal closure. The more precise vault formulation is: molecular detection and prospective serology can strengthen an attribution chain, but they must still be tied to the exact disease endpoint being claimed.

## Scientism relevance
This is a scientism problem when the prestige of the experiment, the field, or the established narrative hides the gap between what the experiment actually established and what later culture says has been proven.

The key move is not fraud alone. It is methodological overreach that becomes socially invisible once a research program is institutionally stabilized.
