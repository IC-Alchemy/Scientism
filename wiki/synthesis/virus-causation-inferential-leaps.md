# Virus Causation Inferential Leaps

> Warning: This page is provisional. It analyzes inferential-leap patterns from the current candidate-paper list, not from full source-page ingests of every paper.

## Why this page exists
The current [[synthesis/virus-disease-attribution-audit-worklist]] identifies candidate foundational papers for several disease-virus attribution claims. The next question is not only whether those papers exist, but what they actually establish and how later narratives may overextend them.

In this vault, scientism often appears when a narrower laboratory or animal result is treated as if it directly settled a broader causal, natural-history, or policy question without reconstructing the missing bridge.

The method page [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] now makes that bridge explicit by separating detection, culture, serology, animal challenge, human challenge, transmission-chain, intervention, and replicated convergence evidence.

## Core analytic rule
For each candidate paper, ask two separate questions:

1. What did the experiment actually do?
2. What larger causation claim was later built on top of it?

If the second question outruns the first, the gap should be marked as an inferential leap rather than silently absorbed into a settled narrative.

## Recurrent inferential-leap patterns
### 1. Artificial-route challenge becomes natural-route causation
An experiment may show that biological material can produce illness when placed into a body by an artificial route. That does not automatically prove that the same process explains ordinary transmission in the wild.

This matters because direct inoculation can bypass the barriers that a natural exposure route would have to cross.

### 2. Detection or isolation becomes full causal proof
A paper may show that a virus-like agent is present, recoverable, cultivable, or detectable in patients. That is stronger than commentary, but it is not identical to proving that the agent alone is sufficient to explain the disease pattern in ordinary conditions.

### 3. Animal-model disease becomes human natural history
An animal challenge model may show that a candidate agent can produce pathology in a susceptible model organism. The leap occurs when that result is treated as if it settled the whole human causal story, including route, dose, susceptibility, and background cofactors.

### 4. Association becomes necessity, sufficiency, or exclusivity
A repeated association can be important, but the leap begins when a detection or association result is translated into a claim that the agent is the necessary cause, the sufficient cause, or the sole meaningful cause without reconstructing the missing steps.

### 5. Laboratory tractability becomes real-world explanatory closure
Once an agent can be cultured, typed, sequenced, or propagated, the field may act as if the causal question is over. But tractability in the lab is not the same thing as a complete demonstration of ordinary disease causation in populations.

## Polio as a worked example
The expanded polio ladder is now [[synthesis/poliomyelitis-poliovirus-evidence-ladder]]. It should be treated as the working control page for this disease pair.

The current shortlist for poliomyelitis includes early monkey-inoculation papers and later tissue-culture isolation work:

- *EXPERIMENTAL EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS.* (1910)
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: THE ACTIVITY OF THE VIRUS* (1910)
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: A MODE OF SPONTANEOUS INFECTION* (1910)
- *TRANSMISSION EXPERIMENTS WITH THE VIRUS OF POLIOMYELITIS...* (1911)
- *Cultivation of the Lansing Strain...* (1949)
- *Studies on the cultivation of poliomyelitis viruses in tissue culture...* (1951)

### What the current evidence list appears capable of answering
- Whether biological material from poliomyelitis cases could produce paralytic disease in monkeys under the experimental conditions used.
- Whether the agent could later be cultivated or isolated in tissue culture and identified from patient material.

### The likely inferential leap
The leap comes if highly artificial inoculation routes are treated as if they directly prove ordinary natural causation in humans.

The user's stated concern is exactly this boundary: if repeated ordinary-route or naturalistic transmission attempts failed, but intracerebral or similarly invasive inoculation produced paralysis, then the narrower result would be that direct placement of infectious material into privileged tissue can produce paralytic disease in that model. The broader claim that natural poliomyelitis epidemics were therefore explained by the same route, mechanism, and agent would still require additional evidence.

### What is still missing before this vault can state that strongly
- A broader source-page reconstruction of the early polio experiments, including the actual inoculation route used in each paper. One primary boundary paper is now in hand: [[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]].
- A direct list of negative or failed natural-route transmission experiments beyond the Sabin-Olitsky tissue-recovery study rather than a secondhand summary of them.
- A boundary between experiments that produced disease only after invasive placement and experiments that succeeded by routes plausibly analogous to ordinary exposure.
- A clear endpoint distinction between paralysis in an experimental animal and the broader claim that epidemic human poliomyelitis had been causally explained.

### Current vault conclusion on the polio example
The vault can already flag the *type* of inferential leap: artificial-route challenge, tissue recovery, and tissue-culture cultivation do not equal demonstrated ordinary natural-route causation.

The vault now also has one direct primary paper showing that a 1938 intranasal monkey study tracked tissue recovery over time and still treated host-to-host transmission as a live problem rather than reporting a simple transmission proof. The 1949 Enders-Weller-Robbins paper adds a tissue-culture cultivation rung for the Lansing strain. What the vault does **not** yet have in hand is a reconstructed primary-paper record sufficient to say, in its own voice, that scientists tried "every possible natural means" or that "hundreds of failed experiments" were ignored. Those stronger claims should remain attributed or provisional until the failed-experiment record is directly assembled.

## Other likely leap patterns in the current shortlist
### AIDS -> HIV
The early AIDS shortlist is strongest on isolation, repeat detection, and association in affected groups. The likely leap is from repeated isolation in AIDS-spectrum patients to the full causal claim that HIV alone explains the syndrome in all relevant contexts without separately rebuilding the bridge from association, immune-cell tropism, and cohort outcomes to general causation.

### Influenza -> influenza virus
The expanded influenza ladder is now [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]]. It should be treated as the working control page for this disease pair.

The classic 1933 paper and later transmission studies are stronger than mere speculation, but the leap can occur when a filterable agent in patient material plus animal transmission is treated as if it settled every later claim about ordinary human transmission dynamics, seasonality, population susceptibility, and all influenza-like illness.

The vault now has three direct primary anchors: [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]], [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]], and [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]]. Rosenau's extracted 1919 report preserves repeated human challenge failures across sprayed secretions, direct nose-to-nose and throat-to-throat transfer, blood injection, filtered-material injection, and intimate ward exposure. Smith, Andrewes, and Laidlaw later showed that filtered material from some early influenza cases produced a reproducible ferret disease under experimental conditions. Shope and Francis showed that human influenza-virus strains infected young swine by intranasal inoculation and transferred weakly by swine contact. That makes three separate overreach patterns visible at once:

- later certainty narratives can glide past the fact that a primary outbreak-era volunteer series repeatedly failed under the tested conditions
- animal-passage and swine-susceptibility results can be retold as if they directly settle ordinary human transmission
- counter-narratives can turn those same failures into a blanket claim that influenza is noninfectious, even though Rosenau explicitly warned against drawing positive conclusions from the negative results

For this vault, the narrower defensible point is that the early transmission record included unresolved human challenge failures and the later virus-identification record added animal-model rungs. Neither side should be collapsed into the whole causation question.

### Measles -> measles virus
The measles row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/measles-measles-virus-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors show why route and sample material cannot be collapsed. [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]] and [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] support failed blood-inoculation endpoints in rhesus monkeys and human volunteers. [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] supports a different rung: nasopharyngeal washings, including filtered washings, produced a measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys under artificial respiratory-mucosal inoculation conditions. [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] defines the syndrome endpoints and also records model differences from human measles. [[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] supports tissue-culture isolation and serology, while explicitly preserving the remaining challenge-and-recovery proof gap. [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] supports later plaque assay and replication kinetics for identified strains.

The inferential danger cuts both ways. Negative blood-inoculation studies should not be retold as a failure of respiratory-material transmission or tissue-culture isolation. Positive monkey inoculation and culture findings should not be retold as if they directly settled ordinary human respiratory transmission, purified-virus challenge, or every clinical measles endpoint. The exact sample, route, host, and endpoint have to be named.

### Smallpox -> variola virus
The smallpox row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/smallpox-variola-virus-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors show why host and route cannot be collapsed. [[sources/brinckerhoff-tyzzer-1906-experimental-variola-monkeys]] supports artificial variola inoculation in nonhuman primates and records route-dependent differences between skin, mucous-membrane, and tracheal experiments. [[sources/nelson-1939-variola-virus-nasal-instillation-mice]] and [[sources/nelson-1940-variola-vaccinia-survival-mouse-lungs]] support mouse lung-recovery and immunity-boundary endpoints without producing ordinary smallpox in mice. [[sources/nelson-1943-stability-variola-virus-embryonated-eggs]] and [[sources/oya-1956-vaccinia-variola-one-day-hen-egg-growth]] support propagation and stability in egg systems. [[sources/noble-rich-1969-smallpox-contact-aerosol-macaca-irus]] supports experimental contact and aerosol transmission in *Macaca irus*, while [[sources/noble-1970-simian-reservoir-smallpox-monkeys]] blocks the leap from experimental monkey susceptibility to a wild simian reservoir.

The inferential danger again cuts both ways. Mouse nasal-route failure should not be retold as a failure of human variola causation. Monkey contact/aerosol transmission should not be retold as if it fully reconstructs human smallpox epidemiology. Egg growth should not be treated as clinical disease reproduction. The exact host, route, inoculum, and endpoint have to be named.

### SARS -> SARS-CoV
The SARS row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/sars-sars-cov-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors show a strong but still endpoint-bound chain. [[sources/peiris-2003-coronavirus-possible-cause-sars]] supports patient-series association, culture isolation from two patients, coronavirus-compatible EM, RT-PCR, paired serology, and negative controls. [[sources/drosten-2003-novel-coronavirus-sars-patients]] supports independent identification, random-amplification sequence, SARS-CoV-specific PCR assays, specimen viral loads, and early serology. [[sources/kuiken-2003-newly-discovered-coronavirus-primary-cause-sars]] supports multicentre association plus cynomolgus macaque challenge, lung pathology, viral localization, RT-PCR, and partial re-isolation.

The inferential danger is collapsing these rungs into a single unqualified proof sentence. Detection, culture, serology, multicentre comparison, and macaque challenge are different endpoints. The Kuiken macaque paper is the major experimental rung, but it used four macaques, high-dose combined intratracheal/intranasal/conjunctival inoculation, day-6 necropsy, and no sham-inoculated macaque comparator. That supports model-specific pathogenicity and re-isolation, not automatic proof of ordinary human route, dose, or every SARS-like illness mechanism.

### MERS -> MERS-CoV
The MERS row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/mers-mers-cov-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors show three different rungs. [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] supports index-case isolation, culture, RT-PCR, sequencing, patient-serum reactivity, and control-serum negativity from one fatal pneumonia case. [[sources/dewit-2013-mers-cov-rhesus-macaque-lower-respiratory-infection]] supports high-dose combined-route rhesus-macaque challenge with transient lower-respiratory infection, viral localization, and lung pathology. [[sources/adney-2014-mers-cov-dromedary-camel-shedding]] supports dromedary-camel upper-respiratory replication and nasal shedding after high-dose combined-route inoculation.

The inferential danger is treating those three rungs as one endpoint. Single-case human isolation is not controlled human causation. Rhesus-macaque lower-respiratory disease is not ordinary human route or dose. Camel shedding ecology is not human clinical MERS. The current full-text set supports a meaningful attribution ladder, but each rung must keep its host, route, sample, and endpoint visible.

### COVID-19 -> SARS-CoV-2
The early COVID papers are strongest on outbreak association, genomic identification, culture, and animal challenge. The leap begins when these results are treated as if they automatically settle every downstream question about disease heterogeneity, causal sufficiency, measurement, or policy.

The current endpoint-specific audit is [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]]. Its main correction is that the Killingley human challenge study should not be treated as a controlled human causation experiment: it was open-label, non-randomized, and lacked a sham-inoculated control group. The animal challenge papers may support model-specific pathogenicity when they include pathology, culture, re-isolation, or mock controls, but they do not by themselves prove controlled human clinical causation for the syndrome COVID-19.

For this vault, PCR and RT-qPCR should be recorded as nucleic-acid detection endpoints, not as standalone proof of infectious virus, clinical diagnosis, or disease causation.

### Cervical cancer -> HPV
The cervical cancer row now has a dedicated HPV ladder page: [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors are molecular and structural rather than challenge-style causation experiments. [[sources/durst-gissmann-ikenberg-zur-hausen-1983-hpv16-cervical-carcinoma]] supports HPV16 cloning and tumor-DNA detection in tested genital cancers. [[sources/boshart-gissmann-ikenberg-1984-hpv18-genital-cancer-biopsies]] supports HPV18 cloning, detection in some genital cancers and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines, and integration-compatible physical-state findings. [[sources/durst-kleinheinz-hotz-gissmann-1985-physical-state-hpv16-genital-tumours]] supports a selected benign-versus-malignant HPV16 physical-state contrast and explicitly leaves open whether integration is causal or consequential. [[sources/matsukura-kanda-yoshikawa-1986-hpv16-integrated-cervical-carcinoma]] supports direct viral-cell junction structure in one carcinoma. [[sources/cullen-reid-campion-lorincz-1991-physical-state-hpv-cervical-neoplasm]] supports the larger boundary that integration was much more frequent in invasive cancers than CIN/latent infection, while also showing invasive cancers with only episomal HPV DNA.

The inferential danger is translating strong tumor-DNA association and integration findings directly into a simple one-factor causal story. Necessity, sufficiency, persistence, invasive progression, cofactors, and intervention effects are separate endpoints. The 1999 "necessary cause" paper and a prospective progression cohort remain acquisition targets before the manuscript can strengthen those rungs.

### Mumps, rubella, and chickenpox
The mumps row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/mumps-mumps-virus-evidence-ladder]]. It should be treated as the working control page for this disease pair.

The current mumps full-text anchors are [[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]], [[sources/leymaster-ward-1947-direct-isolation-mumps-virus-chick-embryos]], and [[sources/henle-henle-wendell-rosenberg-1948-induced-mumps-infections-virus-isolation]]. Together they show a ladder that is stronger than pure detection but still endpoint-bound: monkey parotid-duct inoculation, chick-embryo isolation, complement-fixation identification, induced human exposure, serologic response, and saliva virus-isolation before or without clinical signs.

The mumps leap risk is therefore precise. Johnson and Goodpasture's direct inoculation of saliva into rhesus-monkey parotid ducts should not be retold as ordinary human transmission. Leymaster and Ward's chick-embryo isolation should not be retold as clinical sufficiency. Henle and colleagues' induced human exposure is stronger human evidence, but it lacked a sham control and used laboratory-prepared, intense exposure. The defensible point is that mumps has a multi-rung evidence ladder, not that any single rung proves the whole causal and transmission story.

The rubella row now also has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/rubella-rubella-virus-evidence-ladder]]. The current full-text anchors are [[sources/parkman-buescher-artenstein-1962-recovery-rubella-virus-army-recruits]], [[sources/weller-neva-1962-propagation-cytopathic-agents-rubella-like-illness]], and [[sources/sigurdardottir-givan-rozee-rhodes-1963-rubella-toronto-monkey-transmission]]. They support acute-case recovery, tissue-culture propagation, paired-serum neutralization, and grivet-monkey inoculation, while leaving congenital-rubella and experimental-human rubella papers as full-text acquisition gaps in this pass.

The rubella leap risk is endpoint substitution. Acute throat-washing recovery is not fetal disease proof. Human amnion culture propagation is not ordinary transmission. A two-monkey artificial-route response is not a population transmission model. Congenital rubella syndrome needs its own primary-source ladder rather than being inferred from acute-rubella isolation alone.

Chickenpox now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/chickenpox-varicella-zoster-virus-evidence-ladder]]. The current full-text anchors are [[sources/weller-1953-serial-propagation-varicella-herpes-zoster-agents]], [[sources/cheatham-dolan-dower-weller-1956-fatal-varicella-necropsy-virus-isolation]], and [[sources/weller-witton-bell-1958-varicella-zoster-isolation-propagation-culture]]. They support lesion-fluid culture, fatal-case tissue/blood recovery, and varicella/zoster cultural similarity.

The chickenpox leap risk is folding zoster latency into lesion culture. The fact that varicella-derived and zoster-derived agents look similar in culture is relevant identity evidence, but it is not direct observation of latent virus in ganglia reactivating into a later shingles case. Ordinary chickenpox transmission is also a separate endpoint from vesicle-fluid culture.

For this vault, these rows should be source-ingested as endpoint bundles rather than as one broad "virus causes disease" conclusion.

### Hand, foot, and mouth disease -> Coxsackievirus A16 / enterovirus 71
The HFMD row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/hand-foot-mouth-disease-enteroviruses-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors show why HFMD should not be treated as one virus, one endpoint, or one severity claim. [[sources/alsop-flewett-foster-1960-hand-foot-mouth-disease-birmingham]] supports Coxsackie A16 isolation from one hand-vesicle sample in an early outbreak. [[sources/brown-oleary-1974-fluorescent-antibody-hfmd]] supports Coxsackie A16 antibody responses in cases and contacts. [[sources/ishimaru-1980-hand-foot-mouth-enterovirus-71-cns-complications]] supports EV71 association with HFMD outbreaks that had frequent CNS complications. [[sources/li-2014-tree-shrew-coxsackievirus-a16-experimental-infection]] supports a Coxsackie A16 animal-model rung in tree shrews.

The inferential danger is endpoint substitution. Coxsackie A16 lesion isolation is not EV71 neurologic pathogenesis. Coxsackie A16 serology is not transmission direction. EV71 outbreak association is not a mechanism of CNS injury. Tree-shrew systemic infection is not ordinary pediatric HFMD lesion tropism. HFMD should be analyzed as a syndrome with multiple enterovirus-linked rungs.

### Rotavirus and norovirus gastroenteritis
The rotavirus row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/rotavirus-gastroenteritis-evidence-ladder]]. The current anchors show pediatric mucosal antigen localization through [[sources/davidson-1975-rotavirus-immunofluorescence-duodenal-mucosa]], hospitalized-child association through [[sources/mccormack-1982-clinical-features-rotavirus-gastroenteritis]], adult oral challenge through [[sources/kapikian-1983-oral-human-rotavirus-volunteers]], and culture/serotyping through [[sources/wyatt-1983-direct-isolation-human-rotaviruses-cell-culture-serotypes]].

The norovirus row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/norovirus-gastroenteritis-outbreaks-evidence-ladder]]. The current anchors show Norwalk-agent particle/serology through [[sources/kapikian-1972-immune-electron-microscopy-norwalk-agent]], adult human challenge and shedding through [[sources/atmar-2008-norwalk-virus-shedding-experimental-human-infection]], outbreak investigation through [[sources/arness-2000-norwalk-like-gastroenteritis-army-trainees]], and enteroid replication through [[sources/ettayebi-2016-human-norovirus-replication-enteroids]].

### Bronchiolitis -> respiratory syncytial virus
The RSV/bronchiolitis row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/bronchiolitis-rsv-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors include early CCA-related virus recovery through [[sources/chanock-roizman-myers-1957-cca-rsv-isolation-characterization]], early epidemiology and serology through [[sources/chanock-finberg-1957-cca-rsv-epidemiology-infants]], infant bronchiolitis diagnostic association through [[sources/cradock-watson-1971-rapid-diagnosis-rsv-immunofluorescence]], cotton-rat pathology through [[sources/prince-1978-pathogenesis-rsv-infection-cotton-rats]], and adult route-specific inoculation through [[sources/hall-1981-infectivity-rsv-various-routes-inoculation]].

The inferential danger is importing one respiratory endpoint into another. Early sparse isolation is not bronchiolitis causation. Adult nasal or ocular inoculation is not infant lower-airway disease. The RSV row is strong only when the rungs remain distinct.

### HSV and VZV reactivation rows
The cold-sore, genital-herpes, and shingles rows now have dedicated ladder pages: [[synthesis/cold-sores-hsv1-evidence-ladder]], [[synthesis/genital-herpes-hsv-evidence-ladder]], and [[synthesis/shingles-vzv-reactivation-evidence-ladder]].

The inferential danger is treating recovery or detection as the same endpoint as transmission or reactivation mechanism. HSV-1 oral shedding is not per-contact transmission probability. Genital HSV PCR shedding is not infectiousness for every positive swab. A case-level genital transmission chain is not a population rate estimate. VZV ganglionic DNA, transcripts, and active ganglionitis support a latency/reactivation ladder, but they do not by themselves explain why a particular shingles episode occurs.

### Hepatitis A, B, and C
The hepatitis rows make the endpoint ladder unusually visible:

- hepatitis A starts with virus-like stool antigen detection, nonhuman-primate infectivity, particle characterization, and later culture propagation
- hepatitis B moves from clinical/immunologic syndrome separation and Australia-antigen particle detection into chimpanzee transmission and molecular-clone challenge
- hepatitis C moves from non-A/non-B transfusion and chimpanzee transmission into molecular identification, antibody assays, and infectious molecular-clone work

The inferential leap to watch is compression of that ladder. Detection, serology, animal infectivity, and molecular-clone challenge are not interchangeable endpoints. A paper showing antibody association or particle detection does not answer the same question as a paper showing cloned viral RNA can initiate infection in a chimpanzee.

The hepatitis A row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]]. [[sources/provost-ittensohn-villarejos-1973-cr326-hepatitis-a-neutralization]] supports a CR326 marmoset-infectivity and human-serology neutralization bridge. [[sources/provost-wolanski-miller-1975-cr326-hepatitis-a-dimensions]] supports CR326 particle morphology, density, infected-liver localization, stability, and hepatitis A serologic identity. [[sources/provost-hilleman-1979-human-hepatitis-a-cell-culture]] supports noncytopathic in vitro propagation in marmoset liver explants and FRhK6 cells, with marmoset infectivity confirmation.

The hepatitis A leap risk is using this CR326 chain to silently import other rungs. Human acute-stool immune electron microscopy and chimpanzee challenge/tissue-shedding papers remain acquisition gaps here. Marmoset neutralization, marmoset particle localization, and cell-culture propagation should not be retold as if they already reconstruct ordinary human transmission, clinical severity, or every acute-case detection endpoint.

The hepatitis B row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-b-hepatitis-b-virus-evidence-ladder]]. [[sources/krugman-giles-hammond-1967-two-types-infectious-hepatitis]] supports the pre-HAV/HBV syndrome-split rung by distinguishing MS-1/IH-like and MS-2/SH-like hepatitis patterns in Willowbrook children through serum exposure, oral exposure, contact spread, SGOT/thymol endpoints, and rechallenge immunity. [[sources/acs-sells-purcell-1987-hepatitis-b-virus-transfected-hepg2-chimpanzees]] reports that cell-culture medium from HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2-derived cells produced HBV antigenemia, antibody response, elevated liver enzymes, biopsy hepatitis, and recoverable matching viral DNA in two intravenously inoculated chimpanzees. Together they support syndrome-split and high-rung molecular-clone/cell-produced virion infectivity claims. They should not be retold as if the vault has already reconstructed Australia-antigen particle detection, early chimpanzee passage, or ordinary human route ecology.

The hepatitis C row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-c-hepatitis-c-virus-evidence-ladder]]. [[sources/choo-kuo-weiner-1989-cdna-clone-non-a-non-b-hcv-genome]] supports molecular identification of HCV-associated cDNA and antigen reactivity in blood-borne non-A non-B hepatitis material. [[sources/kuo-choo-alter-1989-assay-circulating-antibodies-hcv]] supports recombinant C100-3 anti-HCV assay validation against chimpanzee-proven infectious sera, matched donor-recipient cases, and chronic NANBH cohorts. [[sources/alter-purcell-shih-1989-hcv-antibody-transfusion-recipients]] supports prospective transfusion-recipient anti-HCV seroconversion and donor linkage in selected cases. [[sources/yanagi-purcell-emerson-bukh-1997-hcv-infectious-cdna-clone-chimpanzee]] supports one direct intrahepatic chimpanzee molecular-clone infectivity endpoint while preserving two noninfectious full-length clone results. The leap risk is treating partial cDNA identification, antibody association, transfusion linkage, and intrahepatic molecular-clone infectivity as one interchangeable endpoint, or as proof that ordinary non-transfusion transmission and all chronic disease mechanisms have already been reconstructed here.

### Rabies and route-bound causation
The rabies row now has a dedicated ladder page: [[synthesis/rabies-rabies-virus-evidence-ladder]].

The current full-text anchors are experimental and model-specific. [[sources/goodpasture-1925-rabies-neural-transmission-rabbits-negri-bodies]] supports rabbit masseter-muscle inoculation and neural-spread/pathology inference. [[sources/dawson-1941-chick-embryo-adapted-rabies-virus]] supports chick-embryo propagation and altered pathogenicity after passage. [[sources/matsumoto-1963-electron-microscope-rabies-virus-mouse-brain]] supports particle and matrix morphology in intracerebrally infected mouse brain. [[sources/hummeler-koprowski-wiktor-1967-rabies-virus-tissue-culture]] supports BHK/21 fixed-virus replication correlated with fluorescent-antibody staining and infectivity. ### EBV, Burkitt lymphoma, infectious mononucleosis, and HHV-8 Kaposi sarcoma
The EBV and HHV-8 rows are association-heavy in a way that needs special handling. Prospective EBV seroconversion evidence for infectious mononucleosis is not the same endpoint as EBV DNA or particles in Burkitt lymphoma tissue or cultured cells. Likewise, HHV-8 lesion-specific DNA and serology in Kaposi sarcoma are stronger than commentary, but they still need careful separation from claims about necessity, sufficiency, cofactors, immune status, and tumor initiation.

The Kaposi sarcoma row now has a dedicated HHV-8 ladder page: [[synthesis/kaposi-sarcoma-hhv8-evidence-ladder]].

The current HHV-8 full-text anchors are molecular and characterization papers rather than challenge-style tumor-causation experiments. [[sources/chang-cesarman-pessin-1994-herpesvirus-like-dna-aids-kaposi]] supports discovery of novel herpesvirus-like DNA sequences in AIDS-associated KS tissue using representational difference analysis, Southern blotting, PCR, and sequencing. [[sources/moore-chang-1995-herpesvirus-like-dna-kaposi-hiv-nonhiv]] supports cross-status detection of the same marker in AIDS-associated KS, classic KS, and KS in HIV-seronegative homosexual men. [[sources/oneill-henson-ghorbani-1996-herpesvirus-like-sequences-kaposi-lesions]] supports lesion-concentrated PCR detection in a small AIDS-KS set with matched normal tissue negative under the reported PCR conditions. [[sources/moore-gao-dominguez-1996-primary-characterization-kshv]] supports viral-genome characterization as a new human gamma-2 herpesvirus, infected-cell-line endpoints, transient transfer attempts, and limited infected-cell-line serology.

The inferential danger is translating strong lesion association and viral characterization directly into a simple tumor-causation story. Lesion PCR is not infectious-virus recovery. Infected lymphoma cell lines are not KS tumor induction. Higher antibody titers in KS patients are not necessity or sufficiency. The broader seroepidemiology paper by Gao et al. 1996 and the prospective blood/progression papers remain acquisition targets before timing and population-level rungs can be strengthened.

The Burkitt lymphoma row now has its own EBV ladder page: [[synthesis/burkitt-lymphoma-ebv-evidence-ladder]].

The current EBV/Burkitt full-text anchors are cell-line and tumor-tissue association papers, not challenge-style cancer-causation experiments. [[sources/henle-henle-1965-persistent-viral-infection-burkitt-cell-line]] supports viral-interference and interferon-like behavior in the EB2 Burkitt-derived cell line, compatible with persistent infection by an unidentified agent. [[sources/epstein-henle-achong-barr-1965-morphological-biological-virus-burkitt-lymphoblasts]] supports recurring virus-like particles in EB1 and EB2 cultures while preserving negative chick-embryo, newborn-rodent, and tissue-culture recovery attempts. [[sources/nonoyama-huang-pagano-1973-ebv-dna-burkitt-npc]] supports EBV DNA detection in most tested African Burkitt lymphoma biopsies but also records one typical Burkitt lymphoma below detection and EBV DNA in some other African tumors.

The inferential danger is translating cell-line particles or tumor DNA into a simple sufficient-cause story. Particle morphology is not tumor initiation. Tumor-tissue DNA is not temporal proof. A high detection fraction is not identical to necessity, and the current pass does not reconstruct malignant-cell localization, ordinary transmission, malaria cofactors, or intervention evidence.

The infectious mononucleosis row now has its own EBV ladder page: [[synthesis/infectious-mononucleosis-ebv-evidence-ladder]].

Unlike the tumor rows, EBV/IM has useful prospective timing evidence. [[sources/klein-pearson-henle-1968-ebv-membrane-immunofluorescence-im]] supports an early immunofluorescence and IM-serum antibody rung, not clinical causation by itself. [[sources/nye-lambert-1973-ebv-antibody-cases-contacts-infectious-mononucleosis]] supports EBV antibody presence or development in Paul-Bunnell-positive cases and EBV infection markers in a subset of contacts, while also showing that some Paul-Bunnell-negative mono-like illnesses were associated with CMV or remained unexplained. [[sources/hallee-evans-niederman-1974-west-point-infectious-mononucleosis-ebv]] is the strongest current timing anchor: clinical IM occurred in initially EBV-antibody-negative cadets who seroconverted, while most EBV infections were not heterophile-positive clinical IM.

The EBV/IM leap risk is therefore different from EBV/Burkitt lymphoma. The stronger prospective serology should not be flattened into "EBV infection always equals clinical IM" or "all mono-like illness is EBV." Nor should it be treated as route-specific transmission proof; the West Point roommate analysis did not show higher infection rates among susceptible roommates of known cases.

The scientism risk here is treating repeated molecular presence as causal closure. The more precise vault formulation is: molecular detection and prospective serology can strengthen an attribution chain, but they must still be tied to the exact disease endpoint being claimed.

## Scientism relevance
This is a scientism problem when the prestige of the experiment, the field, or the established narrative hides the gap between what the experiment actually established and what later culture says has been proven.

The key move is not fraud alone. It is methodological overreach that becomes socially invisible once a research program is institutionally stabilized.
