# Measles to Measles Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make vaccination, exposure-management, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The measles -> measles virus row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text set in this pass supports a mixed pattern: early blood-inoculation attempts failed, respiratory-material monkey inoculation produced a reproducible measles-like syndrome, tissue culture later recovered cytopathogenic agents from patient blood or throat washings, and later plaque assays characterized measles-virus replication.

- [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]] supports a failed blood-inoculation boundary in rhesus monkeys.
- [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] supports a failed blood-inoculation boundary in human volunteers.
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] supports artificial respiratory-material monkey challenge and passage endpoints.
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] supports the clinical and pathology endpoints used to identify the induced monkey syndrome.
- [[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] supports tissue-culture isolation, cytopathic effect, serial passage, neutralization, and complement-fixation rungs.
- [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] supports plaque assay and replication kinetics for identified measles-virus strains.
- [[sources/ristori-1964-chile-measles-vaccine-controlled-trial]] supports a controlled vaccination rung (run 18): a WHO-sponsored double-blind trial of the Enders Edmonston B live vaccine, 530 children 8 months-3 years, inert sterile-tissue-culture-medium placebo arm quoted, ~90% complement-fixation conversion in vaccinees and none in controls, 2 clinically typical measles cases only in the placebo arm during the 21-30-day blinded window, and an unplanned six-month epidemic follow-up of a subsample (0/100 vaccine-only vs 14/85 placebo, 1 control death). The designed endpoints are reactions and serology; the clinical-disease component is incidental, subsample-only, and clinically diagnosed - recorded as a mixed-grade rung, not a laboratory-confirmed efficacy endpoint.
- [[sources/bolotovskij-zetilova-1968-leningrad16-schwarz-measles-placebo-trial]] supports the disease-endpoint half of the controlled vaccination rung (run 26): a randomized blinded trial in 1,190 Moscow children 1-6 years - Leningrad-16 (409) vs Schwarz (393) vs sterile-tissue-culture-fluid placebo (388), code numbers, daily physician observation x28 days - whose 12-month epidemiological-effectiveness phase found clinically diagnosed measles among children exposed in the collectives at 577.7/1,000 exposed placebo children vs 56.6 (Schwarz) and 47.1 (Leningrad-16), both vaccinated groups significantly different from placebo and not from each other, with 100% HI seroconversion in seronegative vaccinees and zero in placebo. The disease endpoint is unconfirmed clinical diagnosis in exposed subsets without printed denominators or intervals - boundaries recorded on the source page.
- [[sources/morley-1964-imesi-measles-vaccine-reactogenicity-trial]] supports the controlled reactogenicity/serology row of the same rung (run 26): the Imesi (Western Nigeria) randomized double-blind trial - Edmonston B + gamma-globulin (131) vs Schwarz (130) vs pooled inert-material controls (173, subdivided into 60 with and 113 without the same GG injection), daily paediatrician observation days 6-12, HI serology on every fifth child (31/31 seronegative vaccinees converted, 0/23 controls) - together with the village record of the 1961-62 epidemic (26 child deaths in five months). No disease endpoint was measured in any arm; controls were vaccinated at four weeks. Filed explicitly as surrogate-endpoint evidence, NOT a GAP 4 row.
- [[sources/takahashi-2026-osaka-measles-secondary-transmission]] supports a natural-exposure human transmission rung (run 24): retrospective surveillance analysis of the Osaka Prefecture 2018-2019 outbreak, 105 PCR/IgM-confirmed index cases, 9,846 monitored close contacts, 72 laboratory-confirmed secondary cases; SAR 0.73% overall, 1.19% from unvaccinated index cases vs 0.20%/0.13% from one/two-or-more-dose index cases (aOR 0.22/0.25), and 0.93% classical vs 0.12% modified measles (aOR 0.12). The secondary-case definition is symptom plus PCR/IgM (asymptomatic contact infections invisible) and contact-side vaccination histories were unavailable - boundaries recorded on the source page.

This is not a simple "failed" or "proved" row. The evidence is strongest when endpoints are kept separate: blood inoculation failed in the extracted 1919 studies; nasopharyngeal material succeeded in the Blake-Trask monkey model; culture and serology were substantially strengthened in 1954; cell-culture replication was clearer by 1964; natural-exposure human transmission is now quantified from outbreak surveillance (run 24) with its design limits attached; culture-passaged human challenge remains a distinct rung not reconstructed here.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failed blood inoculation | [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]]; [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] | Blood from early measles cases did not produce recognizable measles in the tested monkeys or human volunteers. | Does not refute respiratory secretion transmission or later tissue-culture evidence. |
| Respiratory-material animal challenge | [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] | Nasopharyngeal washings, including filtered washings, could induce a measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys; tissue emulsions could passage activity across monkeys. | Artificial inoculation is not ordinary community transmission or purified-virus challenge. |
| Experimental syndrome definition | [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] | Names the monkey endpoints: incubation, conjunctivitis, enanthem, exanthem, leukopenia, pathology, and absent rhinitis/bronchitis. | Symptom resemblance alone is not etiologic proof. |
| Tissue-culture isolation and serology | [[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] | Patient blood/throat washings yielded serially passaged cytopathogenic agents in renal-cell cultures, neutralized by convalescent serum and reactive in complement-fixation tests. | The authors still treated monkey and human disease production with culture-passaged material as essential remaining experiments. |
| Plaque assay and replication | [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] | Measles-virus strains replicated in BSC-1 cells with measurable plaque size, giant-cell, antigen, and growth-curve endpoints. | Characterizes known strains; does not by itself prove original clinical causation or natural transmission. |
| Natural-exposure human transmission quantified (GAP 3) | [[sources/takahashi-2026-osaka-measles-secondary-transmission]] | Close contact with laboratory-confirmed index cases during a defined infectious period produced laboratory-confirmed secondary infections in monitored contacts: SAR 0.73% overall (72/9,846), 1.19% from unvaccinated vs 0.20%/0.13% from one-dose/two-dose index cases (aOR 0.22/0.25), 0.93% classical vs 0.12% modified measles (aOR 0.12), with a contact-count gradient (6.67% at 1-9 contacts, 0.50% at >=100). | Retrospective surveillance design, not serial sampling; secondary cases are symptom + PCR/IgM (asymptomatic infections invisible); contact-side vaccination histories unavailable (confounding direction stated by the authors); exposure intensity unmeasured (category, not dose); no culture-passaged human challenge. |
| Controlled vaccination (GAP 4) | [[sources/ristori-1964-chile-measles-vaccine-controlled-trial]] (mixed-grade); [[sources/bolotovskij-zetilova-1968-leningrad16-schwarz-measles-placebo-trial]] (designed disease endpoint); [[sources/morley-1964-imesi-measles-vaccine-reactogenicity-trial]] (surrogate only) | Chile: blinded randomized placebo-controlled Edmonston B trial, ~90% seroconversion vs zero controls, 2 blinded-window measles cases only in placebo, unplanned six-month subsample 0/100 vs 14/85. Moscow: blinded randomized placebo-controlled L-16 vs Schwarz trial with a designed 12-month phase - clinically diagnosed measles among exposed children at 577.7/1,000 placebo vs 56.6/47.1 vaccinated, no placebo seroconversions. Imesi: blinded randomized inert-material-controlled comparison of Edmonston B+GG vs Schwarz on reactions and serology only (31/31 vs 0/23 conversions). | Chile: designed endpoints were reactions and serology; disease comparisons incidental or unplanned subsample with clinical diagnosis only. Moscow: disease endpoint is unconfirmed clinical diagnosis on exposed subsets without printed denominators, intervals, or lab confirmation; no all-cause outcome. Imesi: no disease endpoint measured in any arm (window 4 weeks, controls then vaccinated); two blinded-window deaths in one vaccine group with uncertain attribution. None of the three rows shows an all-cause health outcome. |

## Current manuscript rule for measles and measles virus
Measles should be presented as an evidence ladder with explicit route and endpoint boundaries:

- Sellards 1919 answers blood-inoculation failure questions in humans and monkeys.
- Blake and Trask 1921 answers respiratory-material monkey challenge and passage questions.
- The Blake-Trask pathology companion answers what counted as a measles-like endpoint in the monkey model.
- Enders and Peebles 1954 answers tissue-culture isolation, cytopathic effect, serial passage, and serology questions, while explicitly leaving host-reproduction experiments as unfinished.
- Rapp 1964 answers plaque-assay and replication-kinetics questions for identified strains.
- Ristori et al. 1964 answers what a controlled live-vaccine trial in endemic Chile showed about immunogenicity and clinically observed measles under a blinded placebo arm - while keeping its surrogate designed endpoints separate from its incidental and unplanned disease-endpoint observations.
- Bolotovskij and Zetilova 1968 answers what a placebo-controlled trial with a designed 12-month phase showed about clinically diagnosed measles among exposed children in Moscow collectives (roughly tenfold less in both vaccinated arms) - while keeping the unconfirmed clinical diagnosis, missing exposed-subset denominators, and no-all-cause boundary explicit.
- Morley et al. 1964 answers what the Imesi randomized double-blind trial showed about Edmonston B+GG vs Schwarz reactions and seroconversion with inert-material and GG-matched controls - and what the village record showed about measles burden - while keeping the absence of any disease endpoint in the trial itself explicit.
- Takahashi et al. 2026 answers what quantified natural-exposure transmission looked like in an elimination-setting outbreak, including the index-vaccination-status gradient, while keeping the symptom-plus-test secondary definition and the missing contact-side vaccination data explicit.

The manuscript may upgrade measles from candidate status to a started ladder. It should not flatten failed blood inoculation, positive respiratory-material monkey inoculation, tissue-culture isolation, quantified surveillance-based transmission, and ordinary human transmission into one claim.
