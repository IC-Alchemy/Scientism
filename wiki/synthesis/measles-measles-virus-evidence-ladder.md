# Measles to Measles Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make vaccination, exposure-management, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The measles -> measles virus row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text set in this pass supports a mixed pattern: early blood-inoculation attempts failed, respiratory-material monkey inoculation produced a reproducible measles-like syndrome, tissue culture later recovered cytopathogenic agents from patient blood or throat washings, and later plaque assays characterized measles-virus replication.

- [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]] supports a failed blood-inoculation boundary in rhesus monkeys.
- [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] supports a failed blood-inoculation boundary in human volunteers.
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] supports artificial respiratory-material monkey challenge and passage endpoints.
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] supports the clinical and pathology endpoints used to identify the induced monkey syndrome.
- [[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] supports tissue-culture isolation, cytopathic effect, serial passage, neutralization, and complement-fixation rungs.
- [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] supports plaque assay and replication kinetics for identified measles-virus strains.

This is not a simple "failed" or "proved" row. The evidence is strongest when endpoints are kept separate: blood inoculation failed in the extracted 1919 studies; nasopharyngeal material succeeded in the Blake-Trask monkey model; culture and serology were substantially strengthened in 1954; cell-culture replication was clearer by 1964. Ordinary human respiratory transmission and culture-passaged human challenge remain distinct rungs not reconstructed here.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failed blood inoculation | [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]]; [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] | Blood from early measles cases did not produce recognizable measles in the tested monkeys or human volunteers. | Does not refute respiratory secretion transmission or later tissue-culture evidence. |
| Respiratory-material animal challenge | [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] | Nasopharyngeal washings, including filtered washings, could induce a measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys; tissue emulsions could passage activity across monkeys. | Artificial inoculation is not ordinary community transmission or purified-virus challenge. |
| Experimental syndrome definition | [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] | Names the monkey endpoints: incubation, conjunctivitis, enanthem, exanthem, leukopenia, pathology, and absent rhinitis/bronchitis. | Symptom resemblance alone is not etiologic proof. |
| Tissue-culture isolation and serology | [[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] | Patient blood/throat washings yielded serially passaged cytopathogenic agents in renal-cell cultures, neutralized by convalescent serum and reactive in complement-fixation tests. | The authors still treated monkey and human disease production with culture-passaged material as essential remaining experiments. |
| Plaque assay and replication | [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] | Measles-virus strains replicated in BSC-1 cells with measurable plaque size, giant-cell, antigen, and growth-curve endpoints. | Characterizes known strains; does not by itself prove original clinical causation or natural transmission. |

## Current manuscript rule for measles and measles virus
Measles should be presented as an evidence ladder with explicit route and endpoint boundaries:

- Sellards 1919 answers blood-inoculation failure questions in humans and monkeys.
- Blake and Trask 1921 answers respiratory-material monkey challenge and passage questions.
- The Blake-Trask pathology companion answers what counted as a measles-like endpoint in the monkey model.
- Enders and Peebles 1954 answers tissue-culture isolation, cytopathic effect, serial passage, and serology questions, while explicitly leaving host-reproduction experiments as unfinished.
- Rapp 1964 answers plaque-assay and replication-kinetics questions for identified strains.

The manuscript may upgrade measles from candidate status to a started ladder. It should not flatten failed blood inoculation, positive respiratory-material monkey inoculation, tissue-culture isolation, and ordinary human transmission into one claim.
