# Specific Vaccine Overall Health Outcome Evidence

## Short answer
The clearest evidence found so far is [[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]]. It supports a narrow, experiment-grade claim: in young children in eastern Gambia, random assignment to a nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine reduced all-cause admissions and all-cause mortality compared with placebo.

This is not evidence that "all vaccines" or "the whole childhood vaccination schedule" improves overall health. It is evidence that one specific vaccine, in one setting, improved broader health endpoints.

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research audit, not medical advice.

## What evidence has been found so far

### Iteration 1 - existing vault state
The prior synthesis [[synthesis/vaccinated-children-more-healthy-question]] concluded that the audited pediatric vaccine records did not answer whether vaccinated children as a class are more healthy than unvaccinated children. The endpoint problem remains real: many vaccine trials measure antibody response, target-disease episodes, short-term reactogenicity, or one schedule versus another schedule.

### Iteration 2 - strongest favorable direct trial found
[[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]] is the strongest favorable direct study located in this pass.

Study type: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

Subject type: 17,437 children in eastern Gambia, age 6 to 51 weeks.

Intervention: three doses of nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine.

Comparator: placebo powder identical in appearance, given in the same routine-vaccine context.

Endpoint: radiological pneumonia, invasive pneumococcal disease, all-cause admissions, all-cause mortality, and short-term serious adverse events.

Result: per-protocol all-cause admissions were 1,065 in the vaccine group versus 1,216 in the placebo group, a 15% reduction. Per-protocol all-cause mortality was 330 deaths in the vaccine group versus 389 in the placebo group, a 16% reduction with 95% CI 3 to 28. Intention-to-treat mortality was also lower, with 14% efficacy and 95% CI 2 to 24.

Main limitation: this is a PCV9-specific result in young Gambian children in a high-burden setting. It does not answer the total childhood schedule question or a general claim about every vaccine.

### Iteration 3 - comparison with other all-cause mortality candidates
BCG and measles-vaccine nonspecific-effect studies are relevant but less clean for the specific proof standard requested here.

- Aaby et al. 2011 randomized low-birth-weight infants to early versus delayed BCG. Infant mortality was lower but not statistically significant in the primary analysis; the neonatal subgroup signal was stronger. This is a useful lead, not as clean as the PCV9 trial for a single-study overall-health claim.
- Biering-Sorensen et al. 2017 randomized low-weight neonates to early BCG-Denmark versus local-policy control. The individual trial showed a nonsignificant neonatal mortality reduction, while a meta-analysis of three BCG-Denmark trials reported a 38% neonatal mortality reduction. This is important but open-label and compares early BCG with delayed/no early BCG, not blinded placebo.
- Aaby et al. 2010 tested early two-dose measles vaccine timing. The overall mortality reduction did not reach statistical significance, though some subgroup analyses were favorable.
- Higgins et al. 2016 systematically reviewed BCG, DTP, and measles-containing vaccines and all-cause mortality. It is useful for mapping the evidence but mixes trials with observational studies and identifies high risk of bias in much of the observational evidence.

### Iteration 4 - a second full-text-grade placebo-controlled vaccine trial, but disease-endpoint only
[[sources/vesikari-2006-pentavalent-rotavirus-rest-rct]] (REST trial, NEJM 2006) is the second experiment-grade placebo-controlled vaccine trial now in the vault: ~68,000 infants, visibly indistinguishable placebo, lab-confirmed G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis endpoints (74% efficacy any severity, 98% severe, per-protocol first season; hospitalization/ED reduction 94.5% for rotavirus-attributable disease).

It does not join the all-cause proof above: all-cause mortality was 24 vaccine vs 20 placebo deaths with no efficacy analysis reported, and most gastroenteritis hospitalizations were not rotavirus-attributable (all-cause gastroenteritis hospitalization reduction 58.9%). REST is target-disease evidence; the overall-health-outcome rung for this vaccine remains untested.

### Iteration 5 - a third disease-endpoint RCT, now for HPV, with a non-inert placebo
[[sources/future-ii-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-cin2-rct]] (FUTURE II, NEJM 2007) is the third experiment-grade controlled vaccine trial in the vault and the first HPV one: 12,167 women 15-26, quadrivalent HPV-6/11/16/18 vaccine vs an aluminum-containing (AAHS) placebo, composite endpoint CIN2/3 + adenocarcinoma in situ + cervical cancer with HPV-16/18 DNA confirmed in the lesion. VE 98% per-protocol susceptible (1 vs 42 cases), 44% ITT type-specific, 17% ITT any type; no effect on infection or lesions already present; zero cervical cancers in either arm.

It does not join the all-cause proof either, on three grounds: the endpoint is a precursor lesion, not cancer (no cancer cases occurred); the comparator contains the same adjuvant as the vaccine arm, so it is not an inert placebo; and follow-up averaged ~3 years. FUTURE II is target-lesion evidence; the overall-health-outcome rung for HPV vaccination remains untested in this vault.

### Iteration 6 - two Covid-19 disease-endpoint RCTs with inert saline placebos, added 2026-11-10 (run 13)
[[sources/polack-2020-bnt162b2-covid-rct]] (BNT162b2, NEJM 2020) and [[sources/sadoff-2021-ad26cov2s-covid-rct]] (Ad26.COV2.S ENSEMBLE, NEJM 2021) are the fourth and fifth experiment-grade placebo-controlled vaccine trials in this vault, and the first for SARS-CoV-2: randomized, blinded, saline (inert) placebo arms, and primary endpoints that are the disease itself - FDA-criteria symptomatic illness with NAAT confirmation (Polack, 95.0%, 8 vs 162 cases) and centrally confirmed, adjudicated moderate to severe-critical Covid-19 (Sadoff, 66.9% >=14 days / 66.1% >=28 days, with severe-critical 76.7-85.4% and 5 Covid deaths all in the placebo arm).

They do not join the all-cause proof: mortality was a safety capture, not a powered endpoint (Polack: 2 vs 4 all-cause deaths, zero Covid deaths in either arm; Sadoff: 3 vs 16 all-cause deaths, all investigator-classified unrelated); the windows are short (Polack ~2 months median post-dose-2 follow-up; Sadoff median 58 days); and transmission and asymptomatic infection were not measured endpoints (Sadoff's preliminary 2,650-participant serology analysis and the authors' community-transmission sentence are recorded as inference on the source page). Covid-19 vaccination is target-disease evidence with numerically favorable, unpowered mortality splits; the overall-health-outcome rung for these vaccines remains untested in this vault.

### Iteration 7 - an RSV disease-endpoint RCT for a second respiratory pathogen, added 2026-11-10 (run 14)
[[sources/ison-2024-rsvpref3oa-two-season-rct]] (AReSVi-006, CID 2024;78(6):1732-1744, full text via PMC; NCT04886596) is the sixth experiment-grade placebo-controlled vaccine trial in this vault and the first for RSV: randomized, blinded, placebo-controlled phase 3 in >=60-year-olds (24,973 exposed, 17 countries), primary disease endpoints - committee-adjudicated, RT-PCR-confirmed RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease (67.2% [97.5% CI 48.2-80.0] over 2 seasons, 30 vs 139 cases; 82.6% in season 1, re-reporting the unreachable NEJM primary Papi et al. 2023) and severe RSV-LRTD (78.8%). It confirms the run-13 pattern: disease-specific benefit is demonstrable under blinded placebo-controlled conditions.

It does not join the all-cause proof either: hospitalization efficacy "could not be evaluated" (1 vs 5 events), >=80-year and frail subgroups are uninformative (too few cases), deaths and serious adverse events are balanced safety captures (~0.4-0.5% fatal SAEs, ~4% SAEs in every arm) rather than powered outcomes, protection wanes within 18 months, and no transmission or asymptomatic-infection endpoint was collected. The placebo composition is not named in the CID report (it sits in the unreachable NEJM primary) - recorded rather than assumed. RSV vaccination in older adults is target-disease evidence only; the overall-health-outcome rung remains untested in this vault.

### Iteration 8 - an influenza disease-endpoint RCT with an inert sham arm - and an intra-trial indirect-effect null, added 2026-11-10 (run 15)
[[sources/belshe-1998-laiv-children-culture-confirmed-influenza-rct]] (Belshe et al., NEJM 1998;338(20):1405-1412, full publisher PDF recovered from an Internet Archive capture of the bronze-OA nejm.org PDF) is the seventh experiment-grade placebo-controlled vaccine trial in this vault and the first for influenza: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter trial in 1,602 healthy children 15-71 months (2:1), intranasal cold-adapted trivalent vaccine versus an indistinguishable egg-allantoic-fluid placebo spray (sham arm quoted verbatim on the source page), and a primary endpoint that is the disease itself - first episode of culture-confirmed wild-type influenza from active surveillance and rhesus-monkey-kidney cultures with vaccine-shedding phenotyping (14/1,070 vs 95/532; efficacy 93%, 95% CI 88-96), plus 21% fewer febrile illnesses and 30% fewer febrile otitis media episodes (both P<0.001). The febrile-illness and otitis-media outcomes are medically attended disease reductions, but they are still target-disease-adjacent, not all-cause health outcomes.

Two additional facts earn this trial a distinct note in this synthesis. First, the vaccinated-sibling analysis inside the trial is a randomized-setting experimental negative for indirect protection: placebo recipients with a vaccinated sibling had the same 18% culture-positive influenza rate as placebo recipients overall (32/182) - "Clearly, having a vaccinated sibling was not protective" - so the trial's own data separate direct protection from herd effects in that season and setting. Second, the trial's gaps are instructive: zero wild-type A(H1N1) circulated (that component is untested against disease), surveillance was symptom-triggered (asymptomatic infection invisible), and there was no inactivated-vaccine arm - the "probably more efficacious" comparison rests on historical data, recorded as a leap. Mortality was not an endpoint at all; influenza vaccination here is target-disease evidence, and the overall-health-outcome rung for it remains untested in this vault.

### Iteration 9 - a pertussis disease-endpoint field-trial set with a true killed-bacteria sham arm, added 2026-11-10 (run 17)
[[sources/mrc-1951-whooping-cough-vaccine-field-trials]] (MRC Whooping-cough Immunization Committee, BMJ 1951;1(4719):1463-1471, complete scanned PDF read from PMC2069253) is the eighth experiment-grade sham-controlled vaccine study in this vault and the first for pertussis: ten field trials in five English areas, 7,558 children 6-18 months completing three inoculations (3,801 whole-cell pertussis vaccine vs 3,757 a four-species killed-bacterial "anticatarrhal" injection containing no H. pertussis, similar in turbidity, same bottle type, parents and observers blinded, allocation by pre-drawn random letter sheets stratified by age and sex), followed monthly for a mean 27 months; endpoint clinically diagnosed pertussis with 96.4% swab coverage and 57.7% culture confirmation, severity graded 1-5 from mothers' paroxysm diaries: 78% incidence reduction (149 vs 687 cases; 1.45 vs 6.72 per 1,000 child-months), home-exposure attack rates 18.2% vs 87.3%, mild-course fraction 73.2% vs 24.1%, complicated attacks 2 vs 28, zero deaths, batch potency spread 1:2.5-1:10.8 against near-constant control attack rates. Disease endpoint, not surrogate - but the endpoint is a single target disease: no all-cause or overall-health outcome was collected (other childhood infections were tallied only as group-similarity checks), the two-arm design cannot measure indirect (herd) effects, and the report's own potency ranking compares batches tested in different areas at different times. For the overall-health question this row adds a historical replicate of the target-disease pattern: a large relative reduction in the target syndrome with nothing measured about total health.

### Iteration 10 - an HPV replication row and a hepatitis B first row, both adjuvant-matched placebos, added 2026-11-11 (run 19)
[[sources/future-i-study-group-2007-quadrivalent-hpv-anogenital-diseases-rct]] (FUTURE I, NEJM 2007;356:1928-1943, complete 2007 Wayback HighWire HTML) replicates the FUTURE II design in an independent 5,455-woman population (16-24) and extends the endpoint set to external anogenital disease: coprimary composites of anogenital warts + VIN/VaIN 1-3 or cancer, and CIN 1-3 + AIS or cancer, each with vaccine-type HPV DNA in the lesion, against an aluminum-containing (AAHS-matched, non-inert) placebo - per-protocol 100% (0 vs 60) and 100% (0 vs 65), ITT any-type only 34%/20%. [[sources/coutinho-1983-heat-inactivated-hbv-vaccine-placebo-rct]] (BMJ 1983;286:1305-1308, complete 4-page scanned PMC PDF) adds a ninth pathogen to the disease-endpoint rung: randomised placebo-controlled double-blind trial in 800 high-risk homosexual men, three 3-ug heat-inactivated HBsAg doses vs 600 ug human albumin on identical 0.6 mg aluminium phosphate (adjuvant-matched, not inert), infections adjudicated by an independent committee blinded to anti-HBs before the code break: clinical ALT-confirmed hepatitis B 1.5% vs 7.7% (PE 80.7%), all serologically defined infections 4.8% vs 23.8% (PE 79.7%) at 21.5 months. Both rows are target-disease evidence with no all-cause or overall-health outcome; both placebos are adjuvant-matched rather than inert, so neither trial can bound systemic safety against a true placebo; the Coutinho "no serious side effects" sentence rests on n=800 over 21.5 months with no numbered SAE table. GAP 4's rung is now: PCV9 (all-cause), rotavirus, HPV (two rows), SARS-CoV-2 (two platforms), RSV, influenza, pertussis, measles, hepatitis B.

### Iteration 11 - a poliomyelitis field-trial component with a confirmed-disease endpoint, added 2026-11-11 (run 20)
[[sources/perdue-1955-alameda-county-polio-field-trial]] (Perdue & Malcolm, California Medicine 1955;83(3):233-236, complete scanned PMC PDF) is the tenth pathogen's entry in this file's disease-endpoint set and the first for polio: a local operations and follow-up report for the only California area in the 1954 nationwide Salk-vaccine field trials - 7,166 second-grade children injected three times (half vaccine, half placebo, "identified simply by a code, the key to which was known only at the Evaluation Center in Michigan"; placebo composition not stated in the report), first and third graders uninoculated controls, endpoint cases confirmed as poliomyelitis under uniform diagnostic criteria with muscle grading and stool virology, follow-up May 10-December 31, 1954: 10 confirmed cases - 7 uninjected, 3 placebo, 0 vaccinated.

### Iteration 12 - a hepatitis A first row and a pertussis second platform, both aluminum-adjuvant comparator arms, added 2026-11-11 (run 21)
[[sources/werzberger-1992-hepatitis-a-vaccine-rct]] (Werzberger et al., NEJM 1992;327:453-457, verified from the 2023 Wayback full-text HTML plus the complete 2012 scanned PDF) adds an eleventh pathogen: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled single-dose trial, 1,037 seronegative children 2-16 y in the Kiryas Joel, New York outbreak community (519/518), purified formalin-inactivated CR326F' cell-culture vaccine vs an aluminum-hydroxide diluent placebo ("300 ug of aluminum and thimerosal" - adjuvant-matched, not inert); adjudicated clinical hepatitis A endpoint (IgM + ALT >=2x ULN + defined signs/symptoms) counted from day 50: 0/498 vs 25/496, efficacy 100% (one-sided 95% CI lower bound 87.3), 7 vaccine-arm cases vs 3 placebo cases confined to days 5-18 (read as pre-injection incubating infections), seroconversion 304/305. [[sources/gustafsson-1996-acellular-pertussis-rct]] (Gustafsson et al., NEJM 1996;334:349-355, complete publisher PDF from a 2022 Wayback capture) replicates pertussis on the acellular platform: four-arm randomized double-blind trial, 9,829 Swedish infants (40.4% of eligible), three doses at 2/4/6 months of two-component acellular (2,566), five-component acellular (2,587), US whole-cell (2,102), or no-pertussis DT control (2,574); WHO 1991 primary endpoint: efficacy vs DT 58.9% / 85.2% / 48.3% over ~21-23.5 months, five-component 0.29 relative risk vs whole-cell with sustained efficacy while whole-cell declined over 720 days; whole-cell reactogenicity (23 crying >=3 h, 28 fevers >=40 C, 5 hypotonic-hyporesponsive episodes) against acellular safety matching DT; in-trial antibody inversion (higher titer, lower efficacy) recorded. Both rows are target-disease evidence with no all-cause or overall-health endpoint; both comparator arms contain aluminum adjuvant rather than inert material, so neither bounds systemic safety against a true placebo; the Werzberger trial stopped at its first interim analysis with the placebo arm vaccinated (mean follow-up ~103 days), and the Gustafsson transmission/control sentence is recorded as inference.

It does not join the all-cause proof: the counts come from one county with a missing uninoculated-control denominator and no rates computed; the master Francis 1955 AJPH analysis remains full-text-unreachable in this vault, so no national efficacy estimate is carried as experimentally supported here; the report's own leap ("in all probability prevented some cases of paralytic poliomyelitis") and its design criticism ("a trial originally designed on an unsound basis") are recorded on the source page. Polio vaccination is target-disease evidence at component-report grade; the overall-health-outcome rung remains untested in this vault. GAP 4's rung is now: PCV9 (all-cause), rotavirus, HPV (two rows), SARS-CoV-2 (two platforms), RSV, influenza, pertussis (two platforms), measles, hepatitis B, poliomyelitis, hepatitis A.

### Iteration 13 - a norovirus first row (the only challenge-exposure entry) and a rotavirus second platform, added 2026-11-11 (run 22)
[[sources/atmar-2011-norovirus-vlp-vaccine-challenge-rct]] (Atmar et al., NEJM 2011;365(23):2178-2187, complete NIH author manuscript at PMC3761795) adds a twelfth pathogen with a structurally different design: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled vaccine-under-challenge trial, 98 FUT2-functional adults 18-50 (50/48), two intranasal doses of a monophosphoryl-lipid-A/chitosan-adjuvanted Norwalk VLP vaccine vs an excipients-only placebo, then BOTH arms challenged orally with 48 RT-PCR units of homologous Norwalk virus; primary endpoint blinded-committee-adjudicated Norwalk gastroenteritis with modified-Vesikari severity: per-protocol 37% vs 69% (P=0.006), any-assay infection 61% vs 82% (P=0.05), severity 3.6 vs 5.5 (P=0.009). This is the vault's only intervention row whose exposure is itself a controlled experiment (GAP 1's sham design + GAP 4's disease endpoint), and its placebo is the audit's first adjuvant-UNmatched arm - the placebo lacked MPL/chitosan, the authors' own first limitation, so antigen-specific and nonspecific protection cannot be separated by this experiment. [[sources/ruiz-palacios-2006-rotarix-severe-rotavirus-rct]] (Ruiz-Palacios et al., NEJM 2006;354:11-22, complete 2006 HighWire Wayback HTML) replicates rotavirus on a second platform: 63,225 infants (efficacy cohort 20,169) across 11 Latin American countries and Finland, two oral doses of monovalent live G1P[8] Rotarix vs a same-constituents-minus-virus placebo (inert-class, quoted); severe rotavirus gastroenteritis 12 vs 77 = 84.7% efficacy (100% at Vesikari 19-20; G2P[4] non-significant at 41.0%, P=0.30), Brighton-adjudicated intussusception 6 vs 7 within 31 days of a dose, all-cause diarrhea hospitalization -42%, 99 deaths with a P=0.05 pneumonia-death imbalance investigated but unpowered. Both rows are target-disease evidence with no all-cause or overall-health endpoint; Rotarix's burden-inflation and strain-attribution sentences are recorded as leaps, and GSK Biologicals held the data and ran the analyses. GAP 4's rung is now: PCV9 (all-cause), rotavirus (two platforms), HPV (two rows), SARS-CoV-2 (two platforms), RSV, influenza, pertussis (two platforms), measles, hepatitis B, poliomyelitis, hepatitis A, norovirus.

## Current best-supported claim
Supported: PCV9, as tested by Cutts et al. 2005 in young children in eastern Gambia, reduced all-cause hospital admissions and all-cause mortality compared with placebo during trial follow-up.

Not supported by this source alone: vaccination as a class improves total health; the whole childhood schedule improves total health; every PCV formulation in every setting produces the same mortality effect.

## What would provide clear proof that a vaccine should be used because recipients are healthier
The cleanest proof would be a pre-registered, adequately powered randomized trial in the exact target population where the use decision will apply.

Minimum design:
- Randomize eligible participants to the specific vaccine versus inert placebo or no vaccine, with the same background care and the same non-study vaccines in both arms.
- Conceal allocation and blind participants, clinicians, outcome adjudicators, and analysts wherever feasible.
- Make the primary endpoint a broad health endpoint: all-cause mortality, all-cause serious adverse events, all-cause hospitalization, validated disability/function, or validated quality of life.
- Measure the target disease endpoint separately, so disease-specific benefit is not substituted for total net health.
- Pre-specify harm endpoints, withdrawals, emergency visits, medically attended adverse events, and serious adverse events.
- Use intention-to-treat analysis as primary and per-protocol analysis as secondary.
- Follow long enough to capture the plausible benefit and harm windows for that vaccine.
- Replicate the result in another population or setting before making a broad use claim.

The evidence would justify a use claim when the vaccine group has better overall health outcomes than the control group, the difference is large enough and precise enough to matter clinically, harms do not offset the benefit, and the result is replicated or otherwise robust in the population where the recommendation is being made.

## How the PCV9 trial fits that proof standard
The PCV9 Gambia trial meets several high-value criteria: randomization, blinding, placebo control, large sample size, broad endpoints, and both per-protocol and intention-to-treat analyses. It falls short of a universal proof standard because it is setting-specific, vaccine-specific, and tested as an addition to routine vaccination rather than as the whole schedule versus no schedule.
