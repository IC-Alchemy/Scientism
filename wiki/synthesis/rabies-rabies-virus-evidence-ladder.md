# Rabies to Rabies Virus Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make exposure-management, vaccination, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The rabies -> rabies virus row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The current full-text set supports artificial animal inoculation, neural-spread pathology, chick-embryo propagation, infected-brain particle morphology, tissue-culture replication, street/fixed strain differences, and route-boundary findings:

- [[sources/goodpasture-1925-rabies-neural-transmission-rabbits-negri-bodies]] supports masseter-muscle inoculation, rabbit clinical disease, and neural-spread/pathology endpoints.
- [[sources/dawson-1941-chick-embryo-adapted-rabies-virus]] supports serial chick-embryo propagation, embryo pathology, altered host pathogenicity, and survivor challenge-immunity endpoints.
- [[sources/matsumoto-1963-electron-microscope-rabies-virus-mouse-brain]] supports street-rabies particle and matrix morphology in intracerebrally infected mouse brain.
- [[sources/hummeler-koprowski-wiktor-1967-rabies-virus-tissue-culture]] supports fixed-rabies replication in BHK/21 cells with morphology, fluorescent-antibody, and infectivity endpoints.
- [[sources/miyamoto-matsumoto-1967-pathogenesis-street-fixed-rabies]] supports street-versus-fixed neuropathology differences in intracerebrally infected mice.
- [[sources/charlton-casey-1979-experimental-rabies-skunks-route-exposure]] and [[sources/shirakawa-2007-cats-fed-rabid-mouse-brains]] support oral, nasal, tracheal, intestinal, and feeding-route boundaries in selected animal systems.

This is a stronger experimental ladder than a mere detection row. It is still route- and model-bound. The current pass does not reconstruct ordinary bite transmission from saliva in a natural host, human clinical rabies, post-exposure prophylaxis evidence, or a modern molecular infectious-clone causation chain.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artificial animal inoculation and pathology | [[sources/goodpasture-1925-rabies-neural-transmission-rabbits-negri-bodies]] | Street-rabies brain material injected into rabbit masseter muscle could produce clinical rabies signs and CNS pathology compatible with neural spread. | Not ordinary bite transmission, natural dose, or human rabies causation. |
| Embryo propagation | [[sources/dawson-1941-chick-embryo-adapted-rabies-virus]] | Rabies-virus strains could be serially propagated in chick embryos and produce CNS pathology with Negri bodies. | Embryo adaptation is not natural transmission or vaccine proof by itself. |
| Infected-brain morphology | [[sources/matsumoto-1963-electron-microscope-rabies-virus-mouse-brain]] | Intracerebrally infected suckling mouse brain contained characteristic rod-like particles and matrix structures. | Particle visualization is not route-specific causation. |
| Tissue-culture replication | [[sources/hummeler-koprowski-wiktor-1967-rabies-virus-tissue-culture]] | Fixed rabies virus replicated in BHK/21 cells with correlated FA staining, infectivity rise, particle formation, and budding. | BHK/21 fixed-virus growth is not ordinary street-virus disease ecology. |
| Street-versus-fixed pathology | [[sources/miyamoto-matsumoto-1967-pathogenesis-street-fixed-rabies]] | Street and fixed rabies viruses differed in clinical timing, Negri-body formation, matrix morphology, and neuronal degeneration in mice. | Strain-specific intracerebral findings cannot be flattened into one generic rabies mechanism. |
| Route-boundary evidence | [[sources/charlton-casey-1979-experimental-rabies-skunks-route-exposure]]; [[sources/shirakawa-2007-cats-fed-rabid-mouse-brains]] | Intranasal exposure efficiently infected skunks; oral and intestinal exposure were inconsistent or negative in tested skunks, and oral feeding did not produce clinical rabies in tested cats while intramuscular inoculation did. | These findings do not refute bite/parenteral rabies causation and should not be generalized across all species and doses. |

## Current manuscript rule for rabies and rabies virus
Rabies should be presented as a multi-model experimental ladder:

- Goodpasture 1925 answers an artificial intramuscular neural-spread/pathology question.
- Dawson 1941 answers a chick-embryo propagation and altered-virulence question.
- Matsumoto 1963 answers an infected-brain morphology question.
- Hummeler et al. 1967 answers a fixed-virus tissue-culture replication question.
- Miyamoto and Matsumoto 1967 answers a street-versus-fixed neuropathology question.
- Charlton and Casey 1979 and Shirakawa et al. 2007 answer route-boundary questions.

The manuscript may upgrade rabies from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. It should not state that this pass has reconstructed ordinary bite transmission, human disease causation, post-exposure prophylaxis, or every natural-host route.
