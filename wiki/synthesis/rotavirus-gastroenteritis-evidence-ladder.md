# Viral Gastroenteritis to Rotavirus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, exposure-management, or vaccine recommendations.

## Current answer
The viral gastroenteritis -> rotavirus row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder:

- [[sources/davidson-1975-rotavirus-immunofluorescence-duodenal-mucosa]] supports pediatric intestinal mucosal antigen localization and early immune-response endpoints.
- [[sources/mccormack-1982-clinical-features-rotavirus-gastroenteritis]] supports hospitalized-child stool detection, clinical association, and control-negativity endpoints.
- [[sources/kapikian-1983-oral-human-rotavirus-volunteers]] supports adult human oral challenge, diarrhea, shedding, and serologic response endpoints.
- [[sources/wyatt-1983-direct-isolation-human-rotaviruses-cell-culture-serotypes]] supports human rotavirus culture/isolation and serotyping endpoints.
- [[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]] supports a prospective household natural-transmission rung for rotavirus: in the same 604-household Dutch cohort, a secondary AGE episode followed a rotavirus index case in 46% of households (per-person secondary attack rate 28%, 33/120), confirmed symptomatic transmission 18% (21/119), asymptomatic transmission 22% (12/55), and rotavirus severity exceeded norovirus (mean modified Vesikari 7.7 vs 6.2; severe episodes exclusively in infants <2 y).
- [[sources/vesikari-2006-pentavalent-rotavirus-rest-rct]] supports a disease-endpoint intervention row: in a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of ~68,000 infants, three oral doses of pentavalent human-bovine (WC3) reassortant vaccine reduced lab-confirmed G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis (74% any severity, 98% severe, per-protocol first season) and its medical-care use (hospitalization/ED reduction 94.5%), with no detected intussusception excess.
- [[sources/ruiz-palacios-2006-rotarix-severe-rotavirus-rct]] replicates that intervention rung on a second platform: in a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 63,225 infants (efficacy cohort 20,169) across 11 Latin American countries and Finland, two oral doses of monovalent live attenuated G1P[8] (Rotarix) reduced severe rotavirus gastroenteritis 84.7% vs the same-constituents-minus-virus placebo (100% at Vesikari 19-20), with no intussusception excess and a 42% all-cause diarrhea-hospitalization reduction.

The current full-text set supports a meaningful rotavirus attribution ladder, but pediatric tissue association, hospitalized-child stool detection, adult challenge, cell culture, and vaccine intervention are different rungs.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pediatric mucosal antigen | [[sources/davidson-1975-rotavirus-immunofluorescence-duodenal-mucosa]] | A shared viral antigen localized in duodenal villus epithelium in tested children with acute enteritis. | Population burden, all gastroenteritis causation, or natural transmission. |
| Clinical association | [[sources/mccormack-1982-clinical-features-rotavirus-gastroenteritis]] | Stool rotavirus was associated with hospitalized gastroenteritis in young children and absent from tested controls. | Causation for every case, community incidence, or clinical diagnosis from symptoms alone. |
| Human oral challenge | [[sources/kapikian-1983-oral-human-rotavirus-volunteers]] | Oral rotavirus could induce infection, shedding, serologic response, and diarrhea in some adult volunteers. | Infant disease burden, natural dose, or universal illness after exposure. |
| Culture/isolation | [[sources/wyatt-1983-direct-isolation-human-rotaviruses-cell-culture-serotypes]] | Some rotavirus-positive fecal specimens yielded cultivable human rotavirus and serotypes. | Clinical causality or natural infectiousness rate. |
| Prospective household transmission | [[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]] (run 23, queue item 45) | In an endemic-setting 604-household Dutch cohort with daily symptom diaries and AGE-triggered rtPCR (including sampled asymptomatic contacts), rotavirus produced a per-person secondary attack rate of 28% (33/120), confirmed symptomatic transmission of 18% (21/119), asymptomatic transmission of 22% (12/55), and more severe disease than norovirus in the same population. | Genomic confirmation of transmission direction (no sequencing), serial sampling of all members, and post-vaccination-era rates (the Netherlands had no rotavirus vaccination programme during the study). |
| Intervention (disease endpoint) | [[sources/vesikari-2006-pentavalent-rotavirus-rest-rct]]; replicated 2026-11-11 (run 22) by [[sources/ruiz-palacios-2006-rotarix-severe-rotavirus-rct]] | Blocking a G1-G4,P[8] reassortant vaccine reduced lab-confirmed G1-G4 rotavirus gastroenteritis by 74% (severe 98%) and medical-care use by 86-95% versus placebo in ~68,000 infants; blocking the monovalent live G1P[8] Rotarix reduced severe rotavirus gastroenteritis 84.7% (100% at Vesikari 19-20) versus a constituent-matched placebo in 63,225 Latin American/Finland infants, G1P[8] 90.8%, G2P[4] 41.0% non-significant. | Transmission dynamics, herd effects, all-cause health outcomes (REST 24 vs 20 deaths unanalyzed; Rotarix 56 vs 43 deaths P=0.20 with a P=0.05 pneumonia-death imbalance investigated but unpowered), duration past age 1, and the antigen-detection gap under the 42% all-cause reduction (authors' own undetected-rotavirus inference). |

## Current manuscript rule for rotavirus gastroenteritis
Rotavirus should be presented as a multi-rung attribution chain. The manuscript may say that the current full-text set supports intestinal antigen localization, pediatric hospital association, adult oral-challenge illness, culture/serotyping, prospective household transmission measurement (as of 2026-11-11, run 23, queue item 45 - [[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]], secondary attack rate 28% with symptomatic 18% and asymptomatic 22% transmission rates in an unvaccinated high-income population), and - as of 2026-11-11 (run 22) - a replicated placebo-controlled disease-endpoint intervention rung (pentavalent REST in the US/Finland plus monovalent Rotarix in Latin America/Finland, independent sponsors, platforms, and populations). It should also say that those rungs do not automatically settle every pediatric gastroenteritis case, natural transmission probability, or infant disease burden; that neither intervention trial showed an all-cause mortality benefit (REST 24 vs 20 unanalyzed deaths; Rotarix 56 vs 43, P=0.20, with a pneumonia-death imbalance 16 vs 6 P=0.05 investigated but not powered to resolve); and that Rotarix's G2P[4] row (no shared capsid antigen) was non-significant within the trial (41.0%, P=0.30), with the 45%/67% cross-protection claims coming from the Discussion and a pooled meta-analysis rather than this experiment.
