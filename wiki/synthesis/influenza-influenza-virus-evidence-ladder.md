# Influenza to Influenza Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make treatment, vaccination, or exposure recommendations.

## Current answer
The influenza row can now be upgraded from a Rosenau-only negative-result warning to a structured ladder, but only partly. The full-text primary set supports these different kinds of endpoint:

- Rosenau 1919 supports a pre-virus human transmission-failure boundary in clinically diagnosed outbreak influenza.
- Smith, Andrewes, and Laidlaw 1933 supports human-case filtrate producing a transmissible ferret disease under experimental conditions.
- Shope and Francis 1936 supports cross-species swine susceptibility and a contact-transfer boundary for human influenza-virus strains in swine.
- Memoli 2015 and Han 2021 (2026-11-10 verification run) support a modern controlled human challenge rung for A(H1N1)pdm09: purified GMP wild-type inoculum, dose-dependent shedding plus clinical disease, and a randomized placebo-controlled trial whose virus-only arm reached 69% MMID and 88% confirmed infection.
- Kasel and Couch 1969 (2026-11-10 run-7 reconciliation of queue item 1) support the earliest full-text controlled-challenge anchor: A/Equi-2/Miami/63 inoculum produced febrile influenza-like illness in 13/15 seronegative adults with shedding in 15/15, and a simultaneous 19-person unimmunized control group showed markedly more illness, shedding, and seroconversion on the A2/Hong Kong/68 challenge (9/19 vs 1/15 illness); the paper has no sham-inoculation arm and used a very large primary dose, both recorded on the source page.
- Cowling 2009 (2026-11-10 verification run, full text recovered from archived publisher HTML) supports a controlled natural-route household transmission rung: cluster-randomized facemask/hand-hygiene trial in Hong Kong households with RT-PCR-confirmed influenza in contacts as the primary endpoint - no significant reduction as randomized, significant reduction only in the within-36-hours subgroup (aOR 0.33).
- Aiello 2012 and Suess 2012 (2026-11-10 run-10, queue items 15/16) replicate the controlled natural-route rung in two further randomized trials with named RT-PCR endpoints: a US residence-hall trial (masks before illness; cumulative ILI RR 0.78 P=0.10, confirmed-influenza RR 0.57 P=0.15, both null as randomized) and a Berlin household trial across pandemic and post-pandemic seasons (control-arm SAR 23%; intervention p=0.18 as randomized; significance confined to the <36-h subgroup, pandemic-strain subgroup, and per-protocol analyses). Three of three natural-route replications now agree: null as randomized, significant only after conditioning. As of run 16 (queue item 28) a fourth trial, in a different setting, joins them: [[sources/stebbins-2011-school-hand-sanitizer-cough-hygiene-rct]] - the Pittsburgh Influenza Prevention Project cluster-randomized 10-school trial (2007-2008 season, 3,360 K-5 students, no sham arm) whose primary endpoint (absence with ILI lab-confirmed influenza A or B by RT-PCR) was NULL as randomized (adjusted IRR 0.81, 0.54-1.23, p=0.33); the influenza-A reduction (IRR 0.48, 0.26-0.87) was a protocol-specified ancillary outcome, influenza B was non-significantly higher in intervention schools (IRR 1.45, 0.79-2.67), and the authors record that reasons for absence were ascertainable for only 34% of episodes, leaving the study "under-powered for most outcomes". Four of four: significant findings in this rung appear only in subgroups, ancillary outcomes, and per-protocol analyses.
- Lowen, Mubareka, Steel, and Palese 2007 (2026-11-10 run-8, queue item 13) supports a controlled animal natural-route transmission rung: guinea pigs infected across a wire-grid barrier they cannot physically cross, at rates tracking humidity and temperature (0/4 at 30 degrees C, 3-4/4 at 20-35% RH), with plaque-assay and HI endpoints and no disease endpoint in the model.
- Kim, Breiman, et al. 2012 (2026-11-10 run-8, queue item 12) supports the vault's first prospective household cohort rung (GAP 3): Kenya PBIDS households with RT-PCR-confirmed pH1N1 index patients had more timed ILI than matched comparison households (RR 1.8 and 2.6), with the self-reported-ILI secondary endpoint recorded as the authors' own main limitation.
- Sauter, Kleynhans, et al. 2026 (2026-11-10 run-11, queue item 18) replicates the GAP 3 cohort rung with a stronger endpoint: the South Africa PHIRST cohort (327 households, 1,518 analyzed, twice-weekly nasopharyngeal swabs of every member regardless of symptoms, 2016-2018, unvaccinated) found 34% of members with >=1 PCR-defined influenza episode per year, infection risk rising with reconstructed household viral-load exposure (HR 1.64-1.96 per 10-Ct-unit) and community prevalence (HR 1.69-2.01 per 0.01), and pre-season HAI >=1:40 tracking 50-70% lower risk for A(H1N1)pdm09, A(H3N2), and B/Victoria (none for B/Yamagata). Exposure intensity and episode timing are model reconstructions from serial Ct curves - recorded as such on the source page.
- Tsang, Wang, Fang, ..., Cauchemez, Cowling 2023 (2026-11-10 run-12, queue item 19) adds the model-reconstruction layer to the GAP 3 rung: a Bayesian individual-based household transmission model fitted to the Hong Kong 2009 H1N1pdm09 household-cohort data (727 households, 2,515 members; infection = >=4-fold HAI rise and/or positive PCR; almost all preseason HAI <10) estimates 36% of cases asymptomatic (CrI 32-40%) and relative infectiousness of asymptomatic vs symptomatic cases 0.57 (95% CrI 0.11-1.54; posterior P(asymptomatic less infectious) = 0.82). The paper's own power analysis gives 0.48 at the estimated effect size, and its Methods state that "transmission events could not be directly observed" - the asymptomatic-infectiousness figure is a posterior estimate, not an observed count (recorded on the source page).
- Hayden, Gubareva, Monto, et al. 2000 (2026-11-11 run 25, queue item 48) supports a second GAP 4 platform with a transmission-relevant endpoint: family-randomized inhaled zanamivir (index treated 5 days, contacts prophylaxed 10 days) vs identical-inhaler placebo reduced families with at least one contact developing symptomatic laboratory-confirmed influenza from 19% to 4% (P<0.001; 79% protection), with no resistant variants - the first randomized household-transmission reduction measured in this vault's influenza set.
- Galbraith, Oxford, Schild, and Watson 1969 (2026-11-11 run 25, queue item 50) is the null half of the antiviral pair: whole-family amantadine prophylaxis (100 mg/12 h, oil-base placebo, 10 days) in 29 British GP practices during the 1969 A2/Hong Kong/68 epidemic produced no reduction in contact clinical influenza (15.5% vs 16.3%, P=0.9) or serologic infection (47.7% vs 33.3%, treated numerically higher, ns), with the epidemic strain drug-sensitive in vitro - the platform-discordant replication that keeps the rung honest.

This is not a global proof or refutation. The current vault can say that the later influenza-virus literature answered questions Rosenau's 1919 study could not answer, but it cannot erase Rosenau's negative human challenge results or treat animal passage as identical to ordinary human transmission.

## Current manuscript rule for influenza
Influenza should be presented as a layered case:

- Rosenau's negative human challenge experiments matter and should not be dismissed as meaningless.
- Smith, Andrewes, and Laidlaw answer a different question: whether a filterable agent from influenza cases could produce and pass a ferret disease.
- Shope and Francis answer another different question: whether human influenza-virus strains could infect swine and how strongly they transmitted by swine contact.
- Memoli 2015 and Han 2021 answer an inoculation question: whether purified wild-type A(H1N1)pdm09 delivered intranasally produces shedding plus influenza-like illness at a reproducible rate, including under a randomized placebo-controlled contrast. They do not test natural transmission.
- Cowling 2009 answers a natural-route intervention question: whether facemask and hand-hygiene interventions reduce RT-PCR-confirmed household transmission when applied with real-world delay. As randomized the answer was no; only the within-36-hours subgroup was significant. That is a different question from inoculation-route disease reproduction. Aiello 2012 and Suess 2012 (run 10) replicate the same answer in two further randomized populations - both null as randomized, significant only in secondary or conditioned analyses - which moves the natural-route rung from a single-study result to a replicated one. Stebbins 2011 (run 16) replicates it a third time, in schools, with the same structure: null primary, significance only in a protocol-specified ancillary outcome (influenza A) alongside a non-significant opposite-direction influenza-B excess.
- Lowen 2007 answers a route-separation question in an animal model: whether virus shed by inoculated donors infects recipients that cannot be touched, under controlled humidity and temperature. It is infection-marker evidence only; the model shows no disease.
- Kim 2012 answers a natural-history question: whether timed illness clusters around a virologically confirmed index case in households under continuous surveillance. Its secondary endpoint is self-reported ILI, so it shows timed symptom clustering, not confirmed secondary infections.
- Hayden 2000 and Galbraith 1969 answer a prophylaxis-intervention question at the household step: whether a randomized antiviral regimen given to families with a new index illness reduces symptomatic laboratory-confirmed influenza in contacts. Zanamivir reduced it 79%; amantadine 31 years earlier did not reduce it at all (null as randomized). The two rows share the family-randomized design and clinical-plus-serologic endpoints and disagree on result - carried as platform discordance, not averaged.
- None of these rungs alone is the same as ordinary human natural-route causation.

The manuscript may therefore strengthen the influenza section from a single negative-result paragraph into an evidence-ladder discussion. It should not say that influenza is noninfectious, and it should not say that ferret or swine experiments by themselves settle ordinary human causation.
