# Influenza to Influenza Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make treatment, vaccination, or exposure recommendations.

## Current answer
The influenza row can now be upgraded from a Rosenau-only negative-result warning to a structured ladder, but only partly. The full-text primary set supports these different kinds of endpoint:

- Rosenau 1919 supports a pre-virus human transmission-failure boundary in clinically diagnosed outbreak influenza.
- Smith, Andrewes, and Laidlaw 1933 supports human-case filtrate producing a transmissible ferret disease under experimental conditions.
- Shope and Francis 1936 supports cross-species swine susceptibility and a contact-transfer boundary for human influenza-virus strains in swine.
- Memoli 2015 and Han 2021 (2026-11-10 verification run) support a modern controlled human challenge rung for A(H1N1)pdm09: purified GMP wild-type inoculum, dose-dependent shedding plus clinical disease, and a randomized placebo-controlled trial whose virus-only arm reached 69% MMID and 88% confirmed infection.
- Kasel and Couch 1969 (2026-11-10 run-7 reconciliation of queue item 1) support the earliest full-text controlled-challenge anchor: A/Equi-2/Miami/63 inoculum produced febrile influenza-like illness in 13/15 seronegative adults with shedding in 15/15, and a simultaneous 19-person unimmunized control group showed markedly more illness, shedding, and seroconversion on the A2/Hong Kong/68 challenge (9/19 vs 1/15 illness); the paper has no sham-inoculation arm and used a very large primary dose, both recorded on the source page.
- Cowling 2009 (2026-11-10 verification run, full text recovered from archived publisher HTML) supports a controlled natural-route household transmission rung: cluster-randomized facemask/hand-hygiene trial in Hong Kong households with RT-PCR-confirmed influenza in contacts as the primary endpoint - no significant reduction as randomized, significant reduction only in the within-36-hours subgroup (aOR 0.33).

This is not a global proof or refutation. The current vault can say that the later influenza-virus literature answered questions Rosenau's 1919 study could not answer, but it cannot erase Rosenau's negative human challenge results or treat animal passage as identical to ordinary human transmission.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current influenza evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical syndrome and pre-virus transmission problem | [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]] | Rosenau reported repeated negative human exposure and close-contact attempts under the tested 1918-1919 outbreak conditions. | It cannot prove influenza is noninfectious, and it cannot test influenza-virus identification because it predates that framework. |
| Human-case filtrate to animal disease | [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]] | Filtered washings from some early human influenza cases produced a reproducible ferret disease. | It cannot prove ordinary human-to-human causation or clinical sufficiency in humans. |
| Ferret passage and animal contact | [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]] | The ferret disease was serially passaged by nasal material and reportedly transmitted by co-housing. | It cannot substitute for a controlled human transmission study. |
| Tissue recovery | [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]]; [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]] | Infective material was recovered from sick ferret nasal passages and from swine lung lesions under the tested conditions. | It cannot show that every clinical influenza case has the same causal pathway. |
| Serology / neutralization | [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]]; [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]] | Immune or convalescent sera neutralized experimental endpoints in ferrets, mice, or swine-derived material. | It cannot alone prove clinical causation; full Francis-Magill 1935 extraction remains a gap. |
| Cross-species susceptibility and contact boundary | [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]] | Young swine were infected by intranasal human influenza virus; contact transfer was weak, occurring in one of four exposed swine and without clinical illness. | It cannot establish ordinary human transmission or airborne spread. |
| Controlled human challenge, inoculum route (rung 7) | [[sources/kasel-couch-1969-experimental-infection-man-horses-influenza]]; [[sources/memoli-2015-h1n1pdm09-human-challenge-model-validation]]; [[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]] | Kasel & Couch 1969 (historical): 10^8.75 TCID50 A/Equi-2/Miami/63 in 15 seronegative adults produced febrile influenza-like illness in 13 and shedding in 15/15; on simultaneous A2/Hong Kong/68 challenge (10^3.2 TCID50), equine-primed subjects had 1/15 illness vs 9/19 unimmunized controls (P<0.06), reduced isolate distribution (P<0.05), and protection tracking heterologous antibody tier (infection 4/6, 2/5, 0/4 across tiers). No sham-inoculation arm; large primary dose; "indistinguishable from natural influenza" is textbook comparison, not a natural-case arm. Modern: dose-dependent viral shedding plus clinical influenza-like disease (MMID) in seronegative adults after intranasal GMP wild-type A(H1N1)pdm09: 69% MMID at 10^7 TCID50 (dose-finding), reproduced at 69% MMID / 88% confirmed infection in the randomized placebo arm. This is a positive modern human-inoculation boundary: purified wild-type virus is sufficient to produce influenza-like disease under these conditions. | It cannot answer natural-route transmission (no recipient subjects were exposed), population attack rates, or whether PCR shedding equals infectiousness; exposure was atomizer or spray inoculation in isolation; the 1969 paper has no sham arm and its illness classification rests on clinical-judgment comparison. |
| Natural-route household transmission, controlled (GAP 2) | [[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]] | In Hong Kong households with a rapid-test-positive index patient, household-level randomization to lifestyle control, enhanced hand hygiene, or facemasks+hand hygiene with an RT-PCR endpoint in contacts: 60/794 contacts (8%) infected, no significant difference between groups as randomized; prespecified within-36-hours subgroup showed facemask+hygiene aOR 0.33 (0.13-0.87) vs control. Natural-route transmission was measured under randomized conditions for the first time in this vault's influenza set. | Subgroup timing was not randomized within arm; adherence was low and control-group contamination documented; the bundled interventions cannot decompose transmission routes; interpandemic strains and mild illness only. |
| Ordinary human natural-route causation | Not yet reconstructed here. | We do not know from the current full-text set. | It cannot be inferred directly from pre-virus negative challenge, ferret passage, swine inoculation, or challenge-model inoculation alone. |

## Current manuscript rule for influenza
Influenza should be presented as a layered case:

- Rosenau's negative human challenge experiments matter and should not be dismissed as meaningless.
- Smith, Andrewes, and Laidlaw answer a different question: whether a filterable agent from influenza cases could produce and pass a ferret disease.
- Shope and Francis answer another different question: whether human influenza-virus strains could infect swine and how strongly they transmitted by swine contact.
- Memoli 2015 and Han 2021 answer an inoculation question: whether purified wild-type A(H1N1)pdm09 delivered intranasally produces shedding plus influenza-like illness at a reproducible rate, including under a randomized placebo-controlled contrast. They do not test natural transmission.
- Cowling 2009 answers a natural-route intervention question: whether facemask and hand-hygiene interventions reduce RT-PCR-confirmed household transmission when applied with real-world delay. As randomized the answer was no; only the within-36-hours subgroup was significant. That is a different question from inoculation-route disease reproduction.
- None of these rungs alone is the same as ordinary human natural-route causation.

The manuscript may therefore strengthen the influenza section from a single negative-result paragraph into an evidence-ladder discussion. It should not say that influenza is noninfectious, and it should not say that ferret or swine experiments by themselves settle ordinary human causation.
