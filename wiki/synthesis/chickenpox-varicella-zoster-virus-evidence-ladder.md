# Chickenpox to Varicella-Zoster Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make exposure, diagnostic, vaccination, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The chickenpox row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text primary set in this pass supports three main endpoint groups:

- Weller 1953 supports lesion-fluid culture and serial propagation of cytopathogenic agents from varicella and herpes zoster.
- Cheatham, Dolan, Dower, and Weller 1956 supports fatal-varicella pathology and recovery of virus from vesicle fluid, blood, and lung in severe cases.
- Weller, Witton, and Bell 1958 supports broader culture comparison showing that varicella-derived and zoster-derived strains could be serially propagated and could not be distinguished by the cultural attributes tested.

This is not yet a complete VZV natural-history chain. The current full-text set supports culture, tissue recovery, fatal-case pathology, and varicella/zoster cultural similarity. It does not directly establish ordinary chickenpox transmission, congenital disease, or the latency/reactivation sequence that explains shingles.

## Current manuscript rule for chickenpox/VZV
Chickenpox/VZV should be presented as a layered case:

- Weller 1953 answers a lesion-fluid culture and serial-propagation question.
- Cheatham et al. 1956 answer a fatal-case pathology and tissue-recovery question.
- Weller, Witton, and Bell 1958 answer a culture-comparison question between varicella-derived and zoster-derived strains.
- The zoster latency/reactivation story belongs to a separate ladder and cannot be treated as settled by culture similarity alone.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade chickenpox/VZV from a candidate row into a culture-and-pathology evidence-ladder example. It should not collapse chickenpox lesion culture, shingles reactivation, ganglionic latency, and ordinary transmission into one claim.
