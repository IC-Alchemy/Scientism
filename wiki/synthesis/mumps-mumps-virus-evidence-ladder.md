# Mumps to Mumps Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make exposure, diagnostic, vaccination, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The mumps row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text primary set in this pass supports three distinct rungs:

- Johnson and Goodpasture 1934 supports early-case saliva producing a reproducible rhesus-monkey parotitis endpoint after direct parotid-duct inoculation.
- Leymaster and Ward 1947 supports direct laboratory isolation of mumps virus from acute mumps saliva by chick-embryo amniotic-sac inoculation, with hemagglutination, complement-fixation, and selected monkey-confirmation endpoints.
- Henle and colleagues 1948 supports induced human exposure followed by clinical, serologic, and saliva virus-isolation endpoints, while also preserving the no-sham-control and intense-exposure limitations.

This is a stronger ladder than pure detection, but it is not a license to compress all endpoints. Animal parotid-duct inoculation, chick-embryo isolation, serologic response, induced human exposure, saliva shedding, and ordinary community transmission are different claims.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current mumps evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinical syndrome and case material | [[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]]; [[sources/leymaster-ward-1947-direct-isolation-mumps-virus-chick-embryos]] | These papers began from clinically diagnosed epidemic parotitis or acute mumps cases and sampled saliva early in disease. | Clinical diagnosis alone cannot prove viral causation, and one Leymaster-Ward clinical case was not laboratory-confirmed by isolation or paired serology. |
| Animal parotid inoculation | [[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]] | Early-case saliva produced a rhesus-monkey parotitis endpoint when inoculated directly through Stensen's duct; normal saliva did not in the small control set. | Direct parotid-duct inoculation is not ordinary human exposure or natural transmission. |
| Passage, filterability, and lesion recovery | [[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]] | The experimental agent was passaged through monkey parotid tissue, filtered gland emulsions remained virulent in reported tests, and parotid histopathology was described. | This does not show a natural route or human-to-human spread. |
| Chick-embryo cultivation / isolation | [[sources/leymaster-ward-1947-direct-isolation-mumps-virus-chick-embryos]] | Acute mumps saliva yielded mumps-virus isolates in chick embryos by hemagglutination, complement fixation, passage, and selected monkey confirmation. | Isolation/cultivation is not the same endpoint as clinical causation or transmission. |
| Human induced exposure | [[sources/henle-henle-wendell-rosenberg-1948-induced-mumps-infections-virus-isolation]] | Susceptible children exposed to chick-embryo-passaged mumps virus developed clinical salivary-gland involvement, orchitis, or inapparent serologic infection under the tested protocols. | There was no sham-exposed control, and the exposure was laboratory-prepared and intense rather than ordinary community exposure. |
| Saliva shedding and timing | [[sources/henle-henle-wendell-rosenberg-1948-induced-mumps-infections-virus-isolation]] | Virus was isolated from saliva before clinical salivary-gland signs in clinical cases and from several inapparent infections. | It does not prove onward transmission route; a later cottage outbreak mentioned by the authors was not definitively traced. |
| Ordinary community natural-route causation | Not yet reconstructed here. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass alone. | It cannot be inferred without preserving the distinctions above and adding appropriate transmission, timing, and replication evidence. |

## Current manuscript rule for mumps
Mumps should be presented as a layered case:

- Johnson and Goodpasture answer a monkey parotid-duct challenge question, not ordinary human transmission.
- Leymaster and Ward answer a chick-embryo isolation and identification question, not clinical sufficiency.
- Henle and colleagues answer an induced human exposure plus saliva-isolation question, but without sham controls and with intense laboratory exposure.
- The source set supports a meaningful mumps-virus evidence ladder while still requiring ordinary community transmission and natural-route claims to be stated carefully.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade mumps from a candidate row into an evidence-ladder example. It should not say that mumps causation is globally refuted, and it should not say that a parotid-duct animal experiment or chick-embryo isolation alone settles ordinary human causation.
