# Poliomyelitis to Poliovirus Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make treatment, vaccination, or exposure recommendations.

## Current answer
The polio file can now be upgraded from a general inferential-leap warning to a structured evidence ladder, but only partly. Three core primary full texts have been located or are represented directly enough for endpoint-level handling: Flexner and Lewis 1910, Sabin and Olitsky 1938, and Enders, Weller, and Robbins 1949. The JAMA route/activity notes and the 1951 American Journal of Hygiene direct-isolation paper remain acquisition gaps for manuscript-strength claims.

Under the vault standard, the currently supportable polio conclusion is narrow:

- early monkey work supports an animal-challenge and tissue-localization ladder, not a complete ordinary human natural-route proof
- Sabin and Olitsky 1938 supports a route/tissue-recovery boundary, not a clean host-to-host transmission proof
- Enders, Weller, and Robbins 1949 supports tissue-culture cultivation of the Lansing strain, not direct patient isolation or natural causation
- direct patient isolation and serologic identification remain unresolved here until the 1951 full text is obtained

## Current manuscript rule for polio
Polio should be presented as an evidence-ladder case, not as either settled proof or global refutation. The manuscript may say that the current full-text set supports specific laboratory and animal-model endpoints, while the direct bridge to ordinary natural-route human causation is not yet fully reconstructed in this vault.

It should not say that "only intracerebral inoculation worked," that "hundreds of natural-route experiments failed," or that tissue culture proves human causation, unless those claims are backed by full-text primary extraction.
