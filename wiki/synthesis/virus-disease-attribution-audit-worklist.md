# Virus-Disease Attribution Audit Worklist

> Warning: This page is a research queue, not a proof page. It lists conventional virus-disease attribution claims that still need direct experiment and endpoint review under this vault's evidence standard.

## Scope note
This page tracks diseases that are commonly described as being caused by a specific virus or by a narrow virus family. The list is intentionally practical rather than exhaustive. Each line names an attribution claim that can later be tested against named experiments, challenge studies, transmission studies, isolation workflows, pathology studies, or other direct evidence.

## Parallel Lens search status
Four independent Lens search tracks were run for the priority set:

- AIDS and poliomyelitis
- Rabies and smallpox
- Measles and influenza
- COVID-19 and cervical cancer

These are candidate foundational papers, not yet full source ingests. Several clusters, especially measles and smallpox, still need tighter follow-up because very early literature was noisy or sparse in Lens.

## Candidate foundational papers
### AIDS -> HIV
- *Isolation of a T-lymphotropic retrovirus from a patient at risk for acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS).* (1983; Lens `021-618-611-127-032`; DOI `10.1126/science.6189183`)  
  Why it matters: early isolation paper from a patient in the AIDS clinical spectrum.  
  Capable question: whether a retrovirus could be isolated from a patient in the AIDS clinical spectrum.
- *Isolation of human T-cell leukemia virus in acquired immune deficiency syndrome (AIDS)* (1983; Lens `034-765-396-169-716`; DOI `10.1126/science.6601823`)  
  Why it matters: early Gallo-group isolation paper linking an HTLV-like retroviral signal to AIDS cases.  
  Capable question: whether retroviral material consistent with human T-lymphotropic retroviruses was detectable in AIDS patients.
- *Frequent detection and isolation of cytopathic retroviruses (HTLV-III) from patients with AIDS and at risk for AIDS* (1984; Lens `066-072-407-275-813`; DOI `10.1126/science.6200936`)  
  Why it matters: repeated recovery in affected and at-risk groups, not a single index case.  
  Capable question: whether the virus could be detected and isolated reproducibly in AIDS patients and related risk groups.
- *Isolation of lymphocytopathic retroviruses from San Francisco patients with AIDS* (1984; Lens `065-781-855-861-627`; DOI `10.1126/science.6206563`)  
  Why it matters: independent isolation from a different cohort.  
  Capable question: whether a lymphocytopathic retrovirus could be independently isolated from AIDS patients.

### Poliomyelitis -> poliovirus
- *EXPERIMENTAL EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS.* (1910; Lens `014-221-781-959-342`; DOI `10.1084/jem.12.2.227`)  
  Why it matters: classic monkey transmission paper central to infectious-agent arguments.  
  Capable question: whether material from poliomyelitis cases could transmit paralytic disease experimentally to monkeys.
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: THE ACTIVITY OF THE VIRUS* (1910; Lens `026-136-629-233-351`; DOI `10.1001/jama.1910.92550270001001p`)  
  Why it matters: early direct study on infective activity in monkey experiments.  
  Capable question: whether the causal agent behaved as an active transmissible virus in experimental animal material.
- *EPIDEMIC POLIOMYELITIS IN MONKEYS: A MODE OF SPONTANEOUS INFECTION* (1910; Lens `048-435-772-700-782`; DOI `10.1001/jama.1910.92550330001001l`)  
  Why it matters: extends the monkey work toward route-of-infection questions.  
  Capable question: what infection route might plausibly transmit the agent in monkeys.
- *TRANSMISSION EXPERIMENTS WITH THE VIRUS OF POLIOMYELITIS: FINDING THE VIRUS IN THE NASOPHARYNGEAL MUCOSA OF MONKEYS RECOVERED FROM THE ACUTE STAGE* (1911; Lens `122-959-655-489-251`; DOI `10.1001/jama.1911.02560070027009`)  
  Why it matters: transmission and tissue-localization paper.  
  Capable question: whether virus could be recovered from relevant tissues after experimental infection.
- *Cultivation of the Lansing Strain of Poliomyelitis Virus in Cultures of Various Human Embryonic Tissues* (1949; Lens `007-991-138-404-767`; DOI `10.1126/science.109.2822.85`)  
  Why it matters: landmark tissue-culture cultivation paper.  
  Capable question: whether poliovirus could be grown in human embryonic tissue culture outside whole-animal passage.
- *Studies on the cultivation of poliomyelitis viruses in tissue culture. V. The direct isolation and serologic identification of virus strains in tissue culture from patients with nonparalytic and paralytic poliomyelitis.* (1951; Lens `033-311-796-760-393`; DOI `10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a119486`)  
  Why it matters: direct patient isolation plus serologic identification.  
  Capable question: whether virus strains could be directly isolated and identified from human poliomyelitis cases.

### Rabies -> rabies virus
- *A Study of Rabies, with Reference to a Neural Transmission of the Virus in Rabbits, and the Structure and Significance of Negri Bodies.* (1925; Lens `065-785-826-094-172`)  
  Why it matters: early pathology-plus-animal-transmission paper.  
  Capable question: whether infectious material from rabies cases could produce characteristic disease and pathology in rabbits.
- *A study of chick-embryo-adapted rabies virus* (1941; Lens `050-708-035-813-739`)  
  Why it matters: controlled propagation in an experimental system.  
  Capable question: whether the rabies agent can be adapted and maintained in chick-embryo systems.
- *ELECTRON MICROSCOPE STUDIES OF RABIES VIRUS IN MOUSE BRAIN.* (1963; Lens `012-402-479-683-357`; DOI `10.1083/jcb.19.3.565`)  
  Why it matters: direct visualization paper from infected tissue.  
  Capable question: whether infected mouse brain contains a morphologically identifiable viral particle associated with rabies infection.
- *Structure and Development of Rabies Virus in Tissue Culture* (1967; Lens `101-921-805-935-961`; DOI `10.1128/jvi.1.1.152-170.1967`)  
  Why it matters: later landmark for tissue-culture growth and morphology.  
  Capable question: whether rabies virus can be propagated in tissue culture and what developmental features characterize it there.
- *Comparative studies between pathogenesis of street and fixed rabies infection.* (1967; Lens `009-123-897-916-352`; DOI `10.1084/jem.125.3.447`)  
  Why it matters: compares naturally circulating and laboratory-fixed strains.  
  Capable question: how different rabies-virus strains behave in infection models and which features are conserved.

### Smallpox -> variola virus
- *Variola and vaccinia, their manifestations and inter-relations in the lower animals: A comparative study* (1894; Lens `175-432-042-252-560`; DOI `10.1002/path.1700020402`)  
  Why it matters: early comparative pathology and inoculation-era study.  
  Capable question: whether material associated with variola and vaccinia yields distinguishable but related animal manifestations.
- *THE BEHAVIOR OF POX VIRUSES IN THE RESPIRATORY TRACT : II. THE RESPONSE OF MICE TO THE NASAL INSTILLATION OF VARIOLA VIRUS.* (1939; Lens `171-797-541-816-865`; DOI `10.1084/jem.70.1.107`)  
  Why it matters: direct animal challenge paper using variola virus.  
  Capable question: whether intranasal exposure to variola virus produces infection or characteristic responses in mice.
- *THE BEHAVIOR OF POX VIRUSES IN THE RESPIRATORY TRACT III. THE SURVIVAL OF VARIOLA AND VACCINIA VIRUSES IN THE LUNGS OF MICE PREVIOUSLY INFECTED WITH VARIOLA* (1940; Lens `036-680-937-145-857`; DOI `10.1084/jem.71.5.653`)  
  Why it matters: follow-on experiment comparing variola and vaccinia in infected mouse lungs.  
  Capable question: whether variola persists in lung tissue after infection and how that behavior compares with vaccinia.
- *THE STABILITY OF VARIOLA VIRUS PROPAGATED IN EMBRYONATED EGGS.* (1943; Lens `056-265-493-401-174`; DOI `10.1084/jem.78.4.231`)  
  Why it matters: controlled propagation in embryonated eggs.  
  Capable question: whether variola virus can be propagated in eggs and remain stable enough for experimental study.
- *Growth of vaccinia and variola viruses in the one-day-old fertile hen's egg.* (1956; Lens `016-442-403-407-956`; DOI `10.7883/yoken1952.9.293`)  
  Why it matters: comparative growth study in a defined host system.  
  Capable question: whether variola virus grows reproducibly in fertile hen's eggs and how that growth compares with vaccinia.

### Measles -> measles virus
- *EXPERIMENTAL MEASLES* (1905; Lens `086-986-279-890-972`)  
  Why it matters: early direct experimental measles hit, but Lens metadata was sparse.  
  Capable question: whether material from measles cases could reproduce measles-like illness under the reported inoculation conditions.
- *Propagation in tissue cultures of cytopathogenic agents from patients with measles.* (1954; Lens `033-756-913-336-05X`; DOI `10.3181/00379727-86-21073`)  
  Why it matters: clearest measles isolation and culture paper surfaced in Lens.  
  Capable question: whether patient material from measles cases contains a transmissible cytopathogenic agent that can be isolated and propagated in tissue culture.
- *PLAQUE DIFFERENTIATION AND REPLICATION OF VIRULENT AND ATTENUATED STRAINS OF MEASLES VIRUS* (1964; Lens `105-657-235-447-127`; DOI `10.1128/jb.88.5.1448-1458.1964`)  
  Why it matters: later direct virology paper on replication behavior of measles strains.  
  Capable question: whether identified measles-virus strains replicate in culture in distinct, measurable ways.

### Influenza -> influenza virus
- *A VIRUS OBTAINED FROM INFLUENZA PATIENTS* (1933; Lens `049-221-578-928-620`; DOI `10.1016/S0140-6736(00)78541-2`)  
  Why it matters: classic Smith-Andrewes-Laidlaw isolation paper.  
  Capable question: whether material from human influenza cases contains a filterable transmissible agent that can infect an animal host.
- *THE SUSCEPTIBILITY OF SWINE TO THE VIRUS OF HUMAN INFLUENZA* (1936; Lens `058-059-817-342-787`; DOI `10.1084/jem.64.5.791`)  
  Why it matters: direct cross-species infection study.  
  Capable question: whether the human influenza virus can infect swine under experimental conditions.
- *Transmission of Epidemic Influenza Virus in Mice by Contact.* (1940; Lens `073-449-665-480-802`; DOI `10.1128/jb.39.3.229-241.1940`)  
  Why it matters: direct contact-transmission experiment.  
  Capable question: whether epidemic influenza virus can be transmitted by contact between animals under controlled conditions.
- *Studies on air-borne virus infections: I. Experimental technique and preliminary observations on influenza and infectious ectromelia.* (1943; Lens `015-655-000-968-196`; DOI `10.1017/S0022172400035828`)  
  Why it matters: airborne-transmission methodology and experimental observations.  
  Capable question: whether influenza infection can be produced through airborne exposure under the reported setup.

### COVID-19 -> SARS-CoV-2
- *A Novel Coronavirus from Patients with Pneumonia in China, 2019* (2020; Lens `011-720-951-980-353`; DOI `10.1056/NEJMoa2001017`)  
  Why it matters: core early patient paper directly reporting a novel coronavirus in outbreak pneumonia cases.  
  Capable question: whether a novel coronavirus was present in sampled outbreak patients, with genome-level identification tied to the early clinical cluster.
- *A pneumonia outbreak associated with a new coronavirus of probable bat origin* (2020; Lens `034-136-902-364-353`; DOI `10.1038/s41586-020-2012-7`)  
  Why it matters: landmark early identification and isolation paper.  
  Capable question: whether outbreak patients carried a distinct coronavirus and whether the isolate showed properties consistent with the observed respiratory outbreak.
- *Isolation and characterization of SARS-CoV-2 from the first US COVID-19 patient* (2020; Lens `039-186-707-153-785`; DOI `10.1101/2020.03.02.972935`)  
  Why it matters: direct isolation and culture paper rather than a review or summary.  
  Capable question: whether live SARS-CoV-2 could be isolated from a diagnosed COVID-19 patient and characterized in vitro.
- *Respiratory disease in rhesus macaques inoculated with SARS-CoV-2* (2020; Lens `113-694-184-552-930`; DOI `10.1038/s41586-020-2324-7`)  
  Why it matters: early animal inoculation study.  
  Capable question: whether inoculation with SARS-CoV-2 can produce respiratory disease and pathology in a susceptible animal model.

See [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]] for the endpoint-level correction: the current COVID paper set should be separated into virus characterization, animal-model pathogenicity, and controlled human clinical causation. The Killingley human challenge paper is not a controlled causation experiment because it lacks a sham-inoculated control group.

### Cervical cancer -> HPV
- *A papillomavirus DNA from a cervical carcinoma and its prevalence in cancer biopsy samples from different geographic regions* (1983; Lens `055-365-522-303-154`; DOI `10.1073/pnas.80.12.3812`)  
  Why it matters: early molecular detection paper from cervical carcinoma tissue.  
  Capable question: whether papillomaviral DNA is detectable in cervical carcinoma samples and across biopsy sets from multiple regions.
- *A new type of papillomavirus DNA, its presence in genital cancer biopsies and in cell lines derived from cervical cancer* (1984; Lens `030-532-626-172-345`; DOI `10.1002/j.1460-2075.1984.tb01944.x`)  
  Why it matters: classic early paper linking specific HPV types to cervical cancer material.  
  Capable question: whether a specific HPV DNA type is present in genital cancer biopsies and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines.
- *The physical state of human papillomavirus type 16 DNA in benign and malignant genital tumours* (1985; Lens `028-647-868-750-812`; DOI `10.1099/0022-1317-66-7-1515`)  
  Why it matters: examines viral DNA state in benign versus malignant lesions.  
  Capable question: whether HPV16 DNA differs in physical state between benign and malignant genital tumors.
- *Cloning of monomeric human papillomavirus type 16 DNA integrated within cell DNA from a cervical carcinoma* (1986; Lens `063-737-835-170-967`; DOI `10.1128/JVI.58.3.979-982.1986`)  
  Why it matters: direct integration-focused paper from cervical carcinoma material.  
  Capable question: whether HPV16 DNA can be found integrated into host cell DNA in cervical carcinoma tissue.
- *Human papillomavirus is a necessary cause of invasive cervical cancer worldwide* (1999; Lens `007-564-623-889-570`; DOI `10.1002/(SICI)1096-9896(199909)189:1<12::AID-PATH431>3.0.CO;2-F`)  
  Why it matters: landmark large-scale pathology and epidemiology paper commonly cited for the attribution claim.  
  Capable question: whether HPV DNA is found in the overwhelming majority of invasive cervical cancer cases worldwide.

## Search quality and cautions
- Early measles and smallpox literature was the noisiest in Lens and needs follow-up before any full synthesis page treats the exact historical chain as stable.
- The AIDS shortlist supports isolation, detection, and association questions more directly than every stronger later causation formulation.
- The cervical-cancer shortlist is strong on detection, integration, and necessity-style claims, but those are not identical to proving sufficiency by themselves.
- One Lens search track hit `HTTP 429` after several confirmation lookups, so later confirmatory searches may need to be staggered.

## Inferential-leap analysis
The current cross-cutting analysis page is [[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]]. Its core warning is that foundational papers are often treated as proving more than their direct experimental setup can answer, especially when artificial inoculation, detection, isolation, or animal-model results are retold as if they fully settled ordinary human causation.

The companion negative-result page is [[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]]. It tracks where the vault currently has failed or non-confirmatory experimental leads and where that record still has to be assembled.

## Audit method
For each attribution claim, the vault should try to name:

- study type
- sample or subject type
- intervention or exposure
- comparator or control
- endpoint
- result
- main limitation

If the available sources do not name those fields clearly, the claim should remain open or unresolved rather than being treated as supported.
