# Burkitt Lymphoma to Epstein-Barr Virus Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, screening, or prevention recommendations.

## Current answer
The Burkitt lymphoma -> EBV row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The current full-text set supports three distinct endpoints:

- [[sources/henle-henle-1965-persistent-viral-infection-burkitt-cell-line]] supports an indirect viral-interference and interferon-like endpoint in the EB2 Burkitt-derived cell line.
- [[sources/epstein-henle-achong-barr-1965-morphological-biological-virus-burkitt-lymphoblasts]] supports recurring virus-like particle morphology in EB1 and EB2 cultures, while preserving negative animal and tissue-culture recovery attempts.
- [[sources/nonoyama-huang-pagano-1973-ebv-dna-burkitt-npc]] supports EBV DNA detection in most tested African Burkitt lymphoma biopsy tissues by cRNA-DNA hybridization.

This is an association-heavy tumor ladder. It is stronger than commentary because it has direct cell-culture, microscopy, biological-test, and tumor-tissue DNA endpoints. It is not a complete tumor-causation ladder. The current full-text set does not prove necessity, sufficiency, ordinary EBV transmission, timing of infection before tumor initiation, malignant-cell localization in every case, or cofactor structure.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Derived-cell persistent-infection behavior | [[sources/henle-henle-1965-persistent-viral-infection-burkitt-cell-line]] | EB2 cells and EB2-conditioned media showed viral-interference and interferon-like behavior compatible with persistent infection by an unidentified agent. | It does not identify EBV by itself or prove tumor causation. |
| Cultured-cell particle morphology | [[sources/epstein-henle-achong-barr-1965-morphological-biological-virus-burkitt-lymphoblasts]] | EB1 and EB2 lymphoblast cultures repeatedly contained morphologically similar virus-like particles in about 1-2% of cells. | Particle morphology in derived cultures is not proof of primary tumor causation. |
| Biological recovery boundary | [[sources/epstein-henle-achong-barr-1965-morphological-biological-virus-burkitt-lymphoblasts]] | Whole or disrupted lymphoblast inocula did not produce reported pathology in chick embryos, newborn hamsters, or newborn mice and did not produce detectable changes in nine tissue-culture systems. | These negative tests do not disprove EBV association, but they block a simple "visible particle equals recovered infectious tumor virus" narrative. |
| Tumor-tissue EBV DNA | [[sources/nonoyama-huang-pagano-1973-ebv-dna-burkitt-npc]] | EBV DNA was detected in 22 of 23 African Burkitt lymphoma biopsies under the reported hybridization method. | Detection does not prove timing, malignant-cell localization in every sample, necessity, sufficiency, or tumor initiation. |
| Specificity and comparison tumors | [[sources/nonoyama-huang-pagano-1973-ebv-dna-burkitt-npc]] | EBV DNA was also detected in 18 of 23 nasopharyngeal carcinomas and 5 of 24 other African tumors, while one typical Burkitt lymphoma was below detection. | Presence in other tumors and one negative Burkitt specimen complicate any simple one-virus, one-tumor claim. |
| Prospective timing, intervention, and cofactors | Not reconstructed here. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass. | The current set does not settle whether EBV infection precedes tumor initiation, whether EBV is required in all Burkitt lymphoma, or how malaria and other cofactors enter the causal model. |

## Current manuscript rule for Burkitt lymphoma and EBV
Burkitt lymphoma should be presented as a tumor-association ladder, not as a simple challenge or transmission model:

- Henle and Henle 1965 answers a derived-cell viral-interference question.
- Epstein et al. 1965 answers a cultured-cell morphology and negative biological-recovery question.
- Nonoyama et al. 1973 answers a tumor-tissue EBV DNA detection question.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade EBV/Burkitt lymphoma from candidate status into a started evidence ladder. It should not state that EBV necessity, EBV sufficiency, tumor initiation, ordinary transmission, or cofactor structure has been reconstructed here.
