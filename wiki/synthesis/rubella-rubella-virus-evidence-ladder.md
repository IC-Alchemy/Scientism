# Rubella to Rubella Virus Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make exposure, pregnancy, vaccination, diagnostic, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The rubella row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder for acute rubella. The full-text primary set in this pass supports three main rungs:

- Parkman, Buescher, and Artenstein 1962 supports recovery of a rubella-associated interfering agent from acute throat washings, contrasted with scarlet-fever cases, plus paired-serum neutralization.
- Weller and Neva 1962 supports tissue-culture propagation of cytopathic agents from blood or urine of rubella-like cases, with parallel culture controls and paired-serum response.
- Sigurdardottir and colleagues 1963 supports Toronto recovery of a similar interference agent from typical rubella cases, convalescent-serum neutralization, and mild grivet-monkey illness after combined intraperitoneal/intranasal inoculation.

This is not yet a full rubella-causation dossier. The current source pages are strongest for acute-rubella recovery, propagation, and serology. Congenital rubella syndrome and experimental human rubella remain acquisition gaps in this pass.

| Rung | Current rubella evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |

| Comparison / control boundary | [[sources/parkman-buescher-artenstein-1962-recovery-rubella-virus-army-recruits]] | The Fort Dix study contrasted rubella with contemporaneous scarlet-fever patients and atypical exanthem cases. | It does not eliminate all other exanthem etiologies or prove sufficiency. |
| Tissue-culture propagation | [[sources/weller-neva-1962-propagation-cytopathic-agents-rubella-like-illness]] | Agents from urine or blood were serially propagated in primary human amnion cultures with parallel controls. | Long culture observation and passage are laboratory endpoints, not ordinary clinical causation. |
| Serology / neutralization | All three source pages | Paired sera from rubella cases often showed increased neutralizing activity against the recovered agents; comparison sera in Weller-Neva did not show the same pattern. | Serology does not alone prove infection route, dose, or full clinical causation. |
| Animal inoculation | [[sources/sigurdardottir-givan-rozee-rhodes-1963-rubella-toronto-monkey-transmission]] | A passaged Toronto isolate produced mild rash and lymphadenopathy in two grivet monkeys after combined intraperitoneal/intranasal inoculation. | Two-animal artificial-route response is not ordinary human transmission. |
| Congenital rubella syndrome | Full text not yet extracted in this pass. | We do not know at manuscript strength from the current full-text set. | Acute rubella recovery and monkey inoculation cannot be silently substituted for fetal disease proof. |
| Ordinary human natural-route causation | Not yet reconstructed here. | We do not know at manuscript strength from this pass alone. | It requires direct route, timing, transmission, or exposure evidence beyond the current isolation and serology rungs. |

## Current manuscript rule for rubella
Rubella should be presented as a layered case:

- Parkman and colleagues answer an acute throat-washing recovery and scarlet-fever comparison question.
- Weller and Neva answer a tissue-culture propagation and paired-serology question.
- Sigurdardottir and colleagues answer a Toronto recovery, neutralization, and grivet-monkey inoculation question.
- Congenital rubella syndrome and experimental human rubella should remain marked as acquisition gaps until full-text primary extraction is complete.

The manuscript may therefore upgrade rubella from a candidate row into an acute-rubella evidence-ladder example. It should not collapse acute-virus recovery, monkey inoculation, fetal disease, and ordinary community transmission into one claim.
