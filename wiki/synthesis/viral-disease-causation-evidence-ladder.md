# Viral Disease Causation Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a methodological synthesis, not medical advice and not a proof page for any single virus-disease pair.

## Current answer
The current vault evidence does not support a blanket statement that viral disease causation has either been globally proven or globally refuted. The better answer is disease-specific and endpoint-specific.

For each claimed viral disease, the vault should ask which rung of evidence is actually in hand and what question that rung can answer.

## Why a ladder is needed
The existing [[synthesis/virus-disease-attribution-audit-worklist]] shows that many diseases have candidate foundational papers, but candidate status is not the same as completed evidence review. The central risk is endpoint compression: a source may show detection, culture, serology, animal disease, or intervention effect, while later summaries retell the result as simple causal closure.

[[sources/rivers-1937-viruses-and-kochs-postulates]] is the key historical bridge. Rivers argued that viruses do not have to satisfy Koch's bacteriology rules literally, but he still required more than mere association: the virus must be shown to be causally involved rather than incidental or coexisting.

[[sources/fredericks-relman-1996-sequence-based-identification-microbial-pathogens]] extends the same caution into molecular evidence. Sequence evidence can be useful, but a sequence hit is not the same as disease causation unless it is tied to controls, localization, timing, biological plausibility, and the disease endpoint.

[[sources/jefferson-2022-hierarchical-framework-transmission-causality-respiratory-viruses]] applies the same problem to respiratory-virus transmission: weak detection evidence and strong transmission evidence should not be treated as interchangeable.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Evidence type | What it can answer | What it cannot answer by itself |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Clinical syndrome description | Whether a named disease pattern was observed. | Whether a virus caused it. |
| 2 | Microscopy, antigen, PCR, RT-qPCR, or sequencing detection | Whether viral material or a viral signal was detected in sampled material. | Whether the detected material was infectious, sufficient, or causal. |
| 3 | Culture, propagation, or isolation workflow | Whether an agent can be propagated or characterized under laboratory conditions. | Whether it caused the original disease in ordinary conditions. |
| 4 | Serology or immune response | Whether the host mounted an immune response associated with the agent. | Whether the agent was necessary, sufficient, or non-incidental. |
| 5 | Animal challenge or experimental exposure | Whether the agent or material can produce disease-like endpoints in a specified host by a specified route. | Whether ordinary human natural-route causation is settled. |
| 6 | Re-isolation or recovery from the experimental host | Whether the agent can be recovered after experimental infection. | Whether this recapitulates natural disease ecology. |
| 7 | Controlled human challenge | Whether exposure under a specified protocol can produce defined clinical or laboratory endpoints. | Whether population-level disease patterns, cofactors, and all real-world transmission are explained. |
| 8 | Prospective cohort, outbreak, or transmission-chain evidence | Whether exposure, infection markers, and disease endpoints align over time in humans. | Whether all alternative causes, cofactors, or measurement artifacts are excluded. |
| 9 | Intervention evidence | Whether blocking, removing, or modifying the agent changes a disease endpoint. | Whether the whole causal story is one-factor, sufficient, or universal. |
| 10 | Replicated cross-method convergence | Whether multiple independent methods point to the same causal model. | It still does not justify ignoring endpoint limits or unfavorable findings. |

- COVID-19 currently has an endpoint-specific audit in [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]]. The key boundary is that PCR and RT-qPCR are detection endpoints, animal inoculation is model-specific pathogenicity, and the audited human challenge evidence is uncontrolled challenge-model evidence rather than sham-controlled clinical causation proof. As of run 12 (queue item 20) the audit also carries the vault's first SARS-CoV-2 natural-route human-transmission row: [[sources/kelly-2022-sars-cov-2-household-transmission-cohort]] (longitudinal household cohort, near-daily nasal swabs of contacts regardless of symptoms; 43% of contacts PCR-infected within the observation window, 81% of transmissions within 5 days of case onset) - a rung-8 cohort measurement with the from-the-index-case assumption and the observational vaccination contrast recorded as boundaries; it does not supply sham-controlled clinical causation evidence, and no SARS-CoV-2 sham-inoculated challenge exists in the corpus. As of run 15 (queue item 25) the audited challenge evidence itself is upgraded from inventory-level to full-text-grade: [[sources/killingley-2022-sars-cov-2-human-challenge-model-validation]] (Killingley et al., Nat Med 2022;28:1031-1041 - first SARS-CoV-2 human challenge study, 36 seronegative adults 18-29, 10 TCID50 cGMP D614G inoculum, open-label non-randomized, no sham arm) is the third pathogen's challenge-model-validation rung, mirroring Memoli 2015 for influenza and DeVincenzo 2010 for RSV; named quantifiable-PCR infection endpoint met in 18/34 (53%, CI 35-70), viable virus to day 12, symptoms mild-to-moderate only, and no correlation between viral load and symptom scores - the uncontrolled status is unchanged and is what keeps the clinical-causation rung unresolved. As of run 13 (queue items 21/22) the COVID chain also carries GAP 4 intervention evidence with disease endpoints: [[sources/polack-2020-bnt162b2-covid-rct]] (Polack et al., NEJM 2020;383:2603-2615 - BNT162b2 mRNA, 43,448 injected, saline placebo, FDA-criteria symptomatic NAAT-confirmed Covid-19 primary endpoint, 95.0% over a roughly two-month window) and [[sources/sadoff-2021-ad26cov2s-covid-rct]] (Sadoff et al., NEJM 2021;384:2187-2201 - Ad26.COV2.S adenovector, single dose, double-blind saline placebo, adjudicated moderate to severe-critical Covid-19 primary endpoint, 66.9% >=14 d / 66.1% >=28 d at median 58-day follow-up, preserved in the B.1.351-predominant South African stratum). Both are intervention evidence consistent with an infectious-agent hypothesis for the syndrome, but they measure only symptomatic PCR-confirmed disease in short windows - infection, transmission, and mortality remain unmeasured rungs, and the Sadoff authors' community-transmission sentence is recorded on the source page as inference, not experiment. As of run 17 (queue item 31) the audit's natural-route cohort row is replicated with directly measured exposure: [[sources/kleynhans-2023-sarscov2-household-proximity-null]] (Kleynhans et al., eLife 2023;12:e84753 - SA-S-HTS contact substudy, Klerksdorp and Soweto, Oct 2020-Sept 2021; 88 index households, 252 contacts; wearable RF proximity sensors logging every face-to-face <1.5 m contact at 20-second resolution for two weeks; nasal swabs on three visits per week plus entry/exit serology): no contact parameter associated with acquisition (aOR 1.0-1.1; sharing a room with the index OR 0.94) while index minimum Ct<30 (aOR 10.4-16.8 vs Ct>35) and female contact sex (aOR ~2.4) did, 153/252 contacts (60.7%) infected; the airborne-from-a-null interpretation and the face-to-face-only measurement boundary are recorded on the source page.
- Influenza now has a full-text controlled-human-challenge rung (rung 7) with a 1969 historical anchor and modern confirmation: [[sources/kasel-couch-1969-experimental-infection-man-horses-influenza]] (2026-11-10 run-7 reconciliation of queue item 1: A/Equi-2 inoculum, 13/15 febrile illness, 15/15 shedding, simultaneous 19-control contrast on the heterologous challenge, no sham arm) with its clinical companion [[sources/couch-1969-nature-equine-influenza-syndrome-man]] (run-9 verification of the run-7 Nature lead: same 15-man trial titrated at 10^6.7 TCID50, plus a March 1965 10^5.3-TCID50 pilot with 5-6/6 infected and zero illness - a dose-associated illness contrast; single-arm, prisoner volunteers, no sham arm) through [[sources/memoli-2015-h1n1pdm09-human-challenge-model-validation]] (dose-finding, no sham arm, 69% MMID at 10^7 TCID50) and [[sources/han-2021-cr6261-placebo-controlled-h1n1pdm09-challenge]] (randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial; virus-only arm 69% MMID, 88% confirmed infection). The 1969 Nature letter is also the vault's primary experimental demonstration that an animal-origin (equine) influenza A virus produced febrile influenza-like illness in inoculated seronegative men - an inoculation boundary datum, not evidence of natural cross-species transfer; its closing pandemic-emergence suggestion is recorded on the source page as an inferential leap. The boundary stays sharp: inoculation-route disease reproduction is not natural-route transmission, which for influenza remains unreconstructed, and PCR-detected shedding is not demonstrated infectiousness. As of run 15 (queue item 26) the influenza chain also carries a GAP 4 intervention rung with a disease endpoint: [[sources/belshe-1998-laiv-children-culture-confirmed-influenza-rct]] (Belshe et al., NEJM 1998;338:1405-1412 - randomized double-blind placebo-controlled multicenter trial, 1,602 healthy children 15-71 months, 2:1, indistinguishable egg-allantoic-fluid placebo spray quoted; primary endpoint first episode of culture-confirmed wild-type influenza from rhesus-monkey-kidney cultures with vaccine-shedding phenotyping; 14/1,070 vs 95/532, efficacy 93% (88-96); 21% fewer febrile illnesses and 30% fewer febrile otitis media episodes; zero H1N1 circulated, so that component is untested against disease). The same trial holds a rare experimental negative for indirect protection: placebo children with a vaccinated sibling had the same 18% culture-positive rate as placebo overall (32/182), "Clearly, having a vaccinated sibling was not protective" - recorded as an intra-trial natural-route boundary observation.
- Influenza now also has a controlled natural-route household-transmission rung (2026-11-10 verification run) through [[sources/cowling-2009-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]]: cluster-randomized household trial with RT-PCR-confirmed contact infection as the endpoint; no significant intervention effect as randomized, significant only in the within-36-hours subgroup. This is the first natural-route randomized measurement in the vault's influenza set; it still does not by itself establish ordinary-route causation. As of run 10 it is replicated twice: [[sources/aiello-2012-facemasks-hand-hygiene-young-adults-rct]] (US residence halls, masks before illness, time-to-event RT-PCR endpoint, cumulative RR 0.78 ILI / 0.57 confirmed, both null as randomized) and [[sources/suess-2012-berlin-household-facemask-hand-hygiene-rct]] (Berlin households, pandemic and post-pandemic seasons, serial qRT-PCR of all members days 2-8, control SAR 23%, p=0.18 as randomized, significant only in the <36-h subgroup and per-protocol analyses). As of run 16 (queue item 28) the rung carries a fourth, school-based trial: [[sources/stebbins-2011-school-hand-sanitizer-cough-hygiene-rct]] (Pittsburgh Influenza Prevention Project, cluster-randomized K-5, 2007-2008 season, 10 schools 5v5, 3,360 students, no sham arm) - primary endpoint (absence with ILI lab-confirmed influenza A or B by RT-PCR) NULL as randomized (adjusted IRR 0.81, 0.54-1.23, p=0.33), with the influenza-A reduction (IRR 0.48, 0.26-0.87) a protocol-specified ancillary outcome, influenza B non-significantly higher in intervention (IRR 1.45), and the authors' own under-powering from a 34% case-ascertainment funnel recorded. The replicated rung-level result is now 5/5, extending beyond influenza on 2026-11-11 (run 20, queue item 38): [[sources/macintyre-2009-household-mask-cluster-rct]] (MacIntyre, Cauchemez, Dwyer, Ferguson et al., Emerg Infect Dis 2009;15(2):233-241 - prospective cluster-randomized three-arm Sydney household trial, 290 exposed adults/145 families, surgical 3M masks vs P2/N95-spec vs no masks, identical infection-control pamphlets to all arms, no sham arm) - primary endpoints ILI and multiplex-RT-PCR-confirmed infection across eight virus groups with symptom-triggered adult swabbing; ITT null on both (lab-confirmed numerically inverted: control 3/100 vs masks 14/186, RR 2.51 p=0.19) under <50% self-reported adherence, with the significant adherent-use Cox result (HR 0.26-0.32) a non-randomized contrast the authors themselves refuse to read causally. This fifth row is multi-virus rather than influenza-specific, so the randomized-null pattern now spans four influenza-endpoint trials and one pooled-respiratory-virus trial; significant findings still appear only in non-randomized adherence/subgroup/ancillary contrasts. Natural-route respiratory transmission is measurable under randomized conditions, and the tested nonpharmaceutical bundles show no significant effect as randomized on any trial's primary endpoint.
- Influenza now has a route-separated controlled animal natural-route transmission rung (2026-11-10 run-8, queue item 13) through [[sources/lowen-2007-guinea-pig-humidity-temperature-transmission]]: guinea-pig recipients infected across a wire-grid barrier they cannot physically cross, under controlled humidity/temperature (3-4/4 at 20-35% RH at 20 degrees C, 0/4 at 80% RH, 0/4 at 30 degrees C), with plaque-assay and HI endpoints, no sham arm, and no disease endpoint - the model is asymptomatic, so this rung can never support illness claims. As of run 18 (queue item 33) the rung is replicated for a second pathogen: [[sources/kutter-2021-sarscov2-sarscov-ferret-airborne-transmission]] (Kutter, de Meulder, Bestebroer, Lexmond, Mulders, Richard, Fouchier, Herfst, Nat Commun 2021;12:1653 - donor and indirect-recipient ferrets in separate cages connected only by a 118-cm hard duct with four 90-degree turns, 100 L/min upward airflow, and steel grids blocking spill-over of food/feces/large particles, in class III isolators at BSL-3+): with pandemic A/H1N1 influenza as a same-set-up positive control (4/4 recipients), SARS-CoV-2 transmitted to 2/4 recipients (RNA from 3 dpe, infectious virus recovered, seroconversion at 17 dpe, the two negative recipients not seroconverting) and SARS-CoV to 4/4; no sham-inoculated animals, no disease endpoint for the asymptomatic SARS-CoV-2 model, and the authors' own route boundary quoted on the source page ("do not discriminate between transmission via small aerosols, large droplets and fomites"; >10 µm particles present at the recipient inlet).
- Influenza now has the vault's first full-text prospective household cohort rung (GAP 3; 2026-11-10 run-8, queue item 12) through [[sources/kim-2012-kenya-household-pdm09-transmission-cohort]]: Kenya PBIDS households with RT-PCR-confirmed pH1N1 index patients versus matched comparison households, timed secondary ILI excess (RR 1.8 and 2.6) with the self-reported secondary endpoint recorded as the authors' own limitation - timed symptom clustering, not virologically confirmed secondary infections. As of run 11 (queue item 18) the rung is replicated with a stronger endpoint: [[sources/sauter-2026-phirst-influenza-household-transmission]] (South Africa PHIRST, 327 households, twice-weekly PCR of every member regardless of symptoms, 2016-2018) observed 34% of members infected per year, with acquisition risk tracking reconstructed household viral-load exposure (HR 1.64-1.96 per 10-Ct-unit) and community prevalence (HR 1.69-2.01 per 0.01), and pre-season HAI >=1:40 tracking 50-70% lower risk for three of four subtypes - exposure intensity and timing remain model reconstructions from Ct curves, recorded on the source page. As of run 12 (queue item 19) the rung gains a third, model-reconstruction row: [[sources/tsang-2023-hong-kong-household-asymptomatic-transmission-reconstruction]] (Bayesian individual-based model over the Hong Kong 2009 H1N1pdm09 household cohort, 727 households/2,515 members, infection = >=4-fold HAI rise and/or PCR in a seronegative pandemic-naive population) - 36% of cases asymptomatic (CrI 32-40%), relative infectiousness of asymptomatic vs symptomatic cases 0.57 (CrI 0.11-1.54, posterior P=0.82), with the paper's own power of 0.48 at that estimate and "transmission events could not be directly observed" (Methods) recorded as boundaries. RSV holds the same rung through [[sources/munywoki-2014-rsv-infant-source-household-cohort]] (run-11, queue item 17): Kilifi households under 3-4-day PCR surveillance, 63.6% of infants infected in one season, 53.6% of infant infections within-household (G-gene-identical in 10/10 sequenced pairs), infection endpoint not disease. As of run 14 (queue item 24) that row is replicated in a high-income setting: [[sources/cox-2025-cascadia-rsv-household-transmission]] (CASCADIA, Seattle/Portland 2022-23, 1,009 households / 3,058 people, weekly swabs regardless of symptoms) - 37/369 contacts (10.0%) infected within 14 days of a distinct index case, 89% of index cases children <=12, index viral detection >=1 week aRR 5.62 (2.62-12.06), whole-genome sequencing corroborating timing-based attribution (mean 4 nt in <=14-day same-household pairs vs 137 nt beyond; still not per-pair directionality). As of run 14 (queue item 23) the RSV chain also carries GAP 4 intervention evidence with disease endpoints: [[sources/ison-2024-rsvpref3oa-two-season-rct]] (AReSVi-006, NCT04886596, randomized placebo-controlled phase 3, >=60 y, 24,973 exposed - one AS01E-adjuvanted RSVPreF3 OA dose reduced adjudicated RT-PCR-confirmed RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease by 67.2% (97.5% CI 48.2-80.0; 30 vs 139 cases) over 2 seasons and severe RSV-LRTD by 78.8%, season-1 82.6% re-reporting the unreachable NEJM primary; hospitalization unpowered at 1 vs 5 events, >=80 y and frail subgroups uninformative, no all-cause endpoint collected) - the full text is the CID 2024 two-season report (PMC11175669) because the NEJM primary remains publisher-walled. As of run 12 (queue item 20) a third pathogen holds the rung: SARS-CoV-2, through [[sources/kelly-2022-sars-cov-2-household-transmission-cohort]] (San Francisco Bay Area longitudinal household cohort, Sept 2020-Jan 2022, near-daily self-collected nasal swabs of 74 contacts regardless of symptoms) - 32/74 (43%) contacts PCR-infected, 81% of transmissions within 5 days of case onset, median serial interval 3 days, no significant cumulative-incidence difference by primary-case vaccination status (41% vs 45%, p=0.60); the assumed all-infections-from-the-primary-case step is quoted on the source page. As of run 16 (queue item 29) a fourth pathogen group holds the rung in its serologic form: seasonal human coronaviruses, through [[sources/schmidt-1986-seattle-families-hcov-229e-oc43-antibody-rises]] (Seattle Virus Watch VI substudy - 10 families, 44 persons, 419 sera at 3 samplings/year 1975-1979, whole-virus ELISA for 229E and OC43): 92 significant antibody rises (ELISA ratio >=1.8; 56 OC43, 36 229E) over 128 person-years = 0.7/person-year with children ~1/person-year (~3x adults), same-virus reinfections always >=8 months apart, frequent concurrent same-family rises, and the paper's own boundary that the four-month bleed intervals made illness linkage impossible - 36% of adult and 40% of child rises fell in illness-free intervals, so this row carries infection markers and timing but no disease-timing endpoint, and authors themselves flag possible related-virus or chronic-infection explanations for rises without illness. As of run 17 (queue item 31) the SARS-CoV-2 row is replicated with the vault's only instrument-measured exposure: [[sources/kleynhans-2023-sarscov2-household-proximity-null]] (Kleynhans et al., eLife 2023;12:e84753) - nested contact substudy of the case-ascertained SA-S-HTS household transmission study (Klerksdorp and Soweto, 2020-2021): 252 contacts of 88 rRT-PCR-confirmed index cases wore RF proximity sensors (<1.5 m face-to-face, 20-second intervals, two weeks) and were swabbed at three visits per week regardless of symptoms; 60.7% of contacts acquired infection and every measured contact parameter was null on mixed-effects regression (aOR 1.0-1.1; same-room OR 0.94, 0.47-1.88), while index viral load (min Ct<30, aOR 10.4-16.8 vs Ct>35) and female contact sex (aOR ~2.4) carried the risk - exposure measured, not reconstructed, with the pre-onset gap, face-to-face-only sensing, and unobserved within-household directionality recorded by the authors themselves. As of run 17 (queue item 30) pertussis becomes the fifth pathogen with a GAP 4 disease-endpoint intervention row: [[sources/mrc-1951-whooping-cough-vaccine-field-trials]] (MRC Whooping-cough Immunization Committee, BMJ 1951;1(4719):1463-1471, scanned PMC2069253 PDF read in full after solving the PMC proof-of-work cookie) - ten controlled field trials, 7,558 children 6-18 months completing three inoculations (3,801 whole-cell pertussis vaccine vs 3,757 a pertussis-free four-species killed-bacterial "anticatarrhal" sham, parents and observers blinded, allocation by pre-drawn random letter sheets stratified by age and sex), monthly nurse follow-up ~27 months; endpoint clinically diagnosed pertussis with 96.4% swab coverage, 57.7% culture confirmation, and paroxysm-diary severity grades 1-5: 149 vs 687 cases = 1.45 vs 6.72 per 1,000 child-months (78% reduction), home-exposure attack rates 18.2% vs 87.3%, mild-course fraction 73.2% vs 24.1%, complicated attacks 2 vs 28, zero deaths, batch potency spread 1:2.5-1:10.8 against near-constant control rates (6.48-7.04); the committee's own negative 1942-44 Oxford trials and the mid-trial moment when it covered the sham letters after seeing excess control-arm cases are recorded on the source page as transparency boundaries; no all-cause endpoint, and a two-arm design says nothing about indirect protection. As of run 18 (queue item 32) measles becomes the sixth pathogen with a GAP 4 row: [[sources/ristori-1964-chile-measles-vaccine-controlled-trial]] (Ristori, Boccardo, Miranda, Borgoño, Bull WHO 1964;30:763-768, scanned PMC2555087 PDF read in full) - a WHO-sponsored double-blind randomized five-group trial in 530 Santiago children 8 months-3 years (July-December 1962), Edmonston B live vaccine 2500 TCID50 subcutaneously vs an inert sterile-tissue-culture-medium placebo (sham arm YES, quoted), designed endpoints reactions + complement-fixation seroconversion (~90% of vaccinees, zero control conversions), 2 clinically typical measles cases occurring only in the placebo arm during the 21-30-day blinded window, and an unplanned six-month epidemic revisit of a subsample: 0/100 vaccine-only vs 14/85 placebo with one control death - the disease-endpoint component is incidental and subsample-only with clinical diagnosis, recorded as a mixed-grade rung on [[synthesis/measles-measles-virus-evidence-ladder]]. adjudicated clinical hepatitis A endpoint = IgM anti-HAV + ALT >=2x ULN + defined signs/symptoms counted from day 50: 0/498 vaccine vs 25/496 placebo, efficacy 100% (one-sided 95% CI lower bound 87.3), early cases 7 vaccine vs 3 placebo confined to days 5-18 read as pre-injection incubating infections, seroconversion 304/305, no serious adverse reactions; trial stopped at its first interim analysis with the placebo arm vaccinated - the ~103-day window, the missing subclinical-infection measurement, and the "should replace immune globulin" recommendation with no globulin arm are recorded on the source page) and [[sources/gustafsson-1996-acellular-pertussis-rct]] (Gustafsson et al., NEJM 1996;334:349-355, complete publisher PDF from a 2022 Wayback capture - four-arm randomized double-blind Swedish infant trial, 9,829 of 24,336 eligible children, three doses at 2/4/6 months of two-component or five-component acellular DTP, US-licensed whole-cell DTP, or a no-pertussis DT control (pertussis-target sham arm, quoted); WHO 1991 primary endpoint >=21 days paroxysmal cough with culture, >=100% PT/FHA serologic rise, or documented household contact: 58.9% (two-component) / 85.2% (five-component) / 48.3% (whole-cell) efficacy vs DT over ~21-23.5 months, five-component 0.29 relative risk vs whole-cell (0.21-0.40) and sustained efficacy over 720 days while whole-cell declined; whole-cell reactogenicity (23 crying >=3 h, 28 fevers >=40 C, 5 hypotonic-hyporesponsive episodes) against acellular safety matching DT; the antibody inversion (two-component higher titers, lower efficacy) and the 59.6% non-participation recorded). Two papers = two more disease-endpoint intervention rows (hepatitis A's first; pertussis's second platform); neither measures transmission, subclinical infection, or all-cause outcomes, and the Gustafsson community-control leap is recorded as inference. As of run 23 (queue item 44) hand-foot-and-mouth disease/enterovirus 71 becomes a GAP 4 pathogen with a platform-replicated rung: [[sources/zhu-2014-sinovac-ev71-hfmd-rct]] (Zhu F et al., NEJM 2014;370:818-828, complete publisher PDF from the 2014-08-31 Wayback nejm.org/doi/pdf capture - randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3, 10,077 children 6-35 months in Jiangsu, Vero-cell inactivated C4 vaccine 400 U vs an aluminum-hydroxide placebo quoted (adjuvant-matched, not inert), DSMB-adjudicated endpoint EV71-associated HFMD/herpangina confirmed by two consecutive EV71-rtPCR positives or isolation + VP1 sequencing: efficacy 94.8% (87.2-97.9) ITT, hospitalization 0 vs 24, neurologic-severe HFMD 0 vs 8, while efficacy against coxsackievirus A16 was null (6.6%, CI -9.2-20.1) and against all-cause HFMD/herpangina only 19.8% because EV71 caused only 99 of 1,950 observed cases; sponsor Sinovac Biotech excluded from data analysis per the paper, and the authors' own duration-overestimation and no-other-pathogen-testing boundaries recorded) and [[sources/li-2014-imb-ev71-hfmd-rct]] (Li R et al., NEJM 2014;370:829-837, same capture route - randomized double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3, 12,000 children 6-71 months in Guangxi, human-diploid-cell KMB17 inactivated C4 vaccine 100 U vs an aluminum-hydroxide placebo quoted, qRT-PCR-confirmed EV71-HFMD endpoint from 2 weeks post-dose-2 to 1 year: 4 vs 151 cases = 97.4% (92.9-99.0), both severe cases and the one EV71 death in the placebo arm, CA16 11.1% (-30.8-39.6) null, all-cause clinically diagnosed HFMD 48.5%; state-program funded, village-doctor contact every 2 weeks). ## Current unresolved assessment
Under this vault's evidence standard, "virus causes disease" should not be accepted or rejected as one global proposition. The phrase has to be expanded into a named disease, named virus, named study design, exposure route, comparator or control, endpoint, result, and limitation.

The current evidence in hand supports a methodological conclusion: viral causation claims often rest on multiple rungs that are rhetorically compressed after the fact. That does not prove the claims false. It means each disease row needs a paper-by-paper ladder reconstruction before it can be marked supported, contradicted, or unresolved.
