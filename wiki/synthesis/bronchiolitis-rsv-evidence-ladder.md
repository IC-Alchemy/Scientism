# Bronchiolitis to Respiratory Syncytial Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, exposure-management, or infection-control recommendations.

## Current answer
The bronchiolitis -> RSV row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder:

- [[sources/chanock-roizman-myers-1957-cca-rsv-isolation-characterization]] supports early CCA-related virus isolation and culture characterization from infants with respiratory illness.
- [[sources/chanock-finberg-1957-cca-rsv-epidemiology-infants]] supports early epidemiologic and serologic association, while preserving control and nosocomial-infection limitations.
- [[sources/cradock-watson-1971-rapid-diagnosis-rsv-immunofluorescence]] supports RSV antigen detection and culture confirmation in hospitalized infant bronchiolitis cases.
- [[sources/prince-1978-pathogenesis-rsv-infection-cotton-rats]] supports cotton-rat respiratory replication, antigen localization, and bronchiolitis-like pathology.
- [[sources/hall-1981-infectivity-rsv-various-routes-inoculation]] supports adult nasal and ocular inoculation-route infectivity.
- [[sources/devincenzo-2010-rsv-human-challenge-model-validation]] (added 2026-11-10 verification run) supports a GMP wild-type RSV-A Memphis 37 adult challenge model with quantified inoculum, 77% infection by qPCR, and viral-load/disease correlation; it has no sham or placebo arm.
- [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]] (added 2026-11-10 verification run) supplies the randomized placebo-controlled element in the same challenge model: infected placebo arm documents disease incidence under blinded conditions, and active drug reduced viral load and symptom scores.
- [[sources/munywoki-2014-rsv-infant-source-household-cohort]] (added 2026-11-10 run-11, queue item 17) adds the first natural-route cohort rung: rural Kilifi households under 3-4-day PCR surveillance of every member - 84.1% of households infected, 63.6% of infants infected in one season, sources split 53.6% within-household (11/15 primary cases older school-attending children, G-gene homologous in 10/10 sequenced pairs), 32.1% outside, 14.3% coprimary-inconclusive. Infection endpoint, not a disease endpoint; source attribution is temporal-ordering inference, recorded as such.
- [[sources/cox-2025-cascadia-rsv-household-transmission]] (added 2026-11-10 run-14, queue item 24) replicates the household-cohort rung in a high-income country: CASCADIA Seattle/Portland, 1,009 multi-person households, 3,058 people 6 months-50 years, weekly swabs/surveys irrespective of symptoms - RSV in 10% of participants and 20% of households; secondary infection within 14 days of a distinct index case in 37/369 contacts (10.0%); 89% of index cases children <=12; index viral detection >=1 week carried aRR 5.62; whole-genome sequencing corroborates timing-based attribution (mean 4 nt within <=14-day same-household pairs vs 137 nt beyond). Infection endpoint, not disease; the leap from timed detections to directed transmission is recorded.
- [[sources/ison-2024-rsvpref3oa-two-season-rct]] (added 2026-11-10 run-14, queue item 23) adds the first intervention rung with disease endpoints: AReSVi-006 (NCT04886596), randomized placebo-controlled phase 3 in >=60-year-olds (24,973 exposed) - one AS01E-adjuvanted RSVPreF3 OA dose reduced adjudicated RT-PCR-confirmed RSV-related lower respiratory tract disease by 67.2% (97.5% CI 48.2-80.0; 30 vs 139 cases) over 2 seasons (82.6% in season 1, re-reporting the unreachable NEJM primary) and severe RSV-LRTD by 78.8%; hospitalization endpoints unpowered (1 vs 5 events); >=80-year and frail subgroups uninformative; no all-cause endpoint was collected.

The current full-text set supports a strong RSV attribution chain for respiratory infection and infant bronchiolitis association, plus a modern adult challenge chain, but infant bronchiolitis causation should not be imported from early sparse isolation, adult challenge, cotton-rat pathology, or quarantine inoculation alone.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early isolation/characterization | [[sources/chanock-roizman-myers-1957-cca-rsv-isolation-characterization]] | CCA-related syncytium-forming viruses were recoverable from some infants with respiratory illness. | Bronchiolitis causation, burden, or disease reproduction. |
| Early epidemiology/serology | [[sources/chanock-finberg-1957-cca-rsv-epidemiology-infants]] | Infection markers appeared in some respiratory-illness cases, but controls complicated the association. | Clean proof of hospitalized bronchiolitis causation. |
| Diagnostic bronchiolitis association | [[sources/cradock-watson-1971-rapid-diagnosis-rsv-immunofluorescence]] | RSV antigen detection aligned with culture confirmation in many hospitalized bronchiolitis infants. | Lower-airway causal sufficiency or all bronchiolitis mechanisms. |
| Animal pathology | [[sources/prince-1978-pathogenesis-rsv-infection-cotton-rats]] | RSV infected cotton-rat respiratory tissues and produced model-specific bronchiolitis-like pathology. | Direct infant-human causation. |
| Adult route inoculation | [[sources/hall-1981-infectivity-rsv-various-routes-inoculation]] | RSV infected adults by nasal and ocular instillation under the protocol used. | Infant bronchiolitis, household transmission probability, or severe lower-airway disease. |
| Controlled adult challenge, dose-finding (rung 7) | [[sources/devincenzo-2010-rsv-human-challenge-model-validation]] | GMP-purified wild-type RSV-A Memphis 37 inoculum infected 27/35 (77%) low-antibody adults by qPCR across a ~500-fold dose range, with viral load correlated to symptom scores, physical examination scores, and mucus weights; disease was self-limited and upper-respiratory. | It has no sham or placebo arm, so all rates describe inoculated persons only; it cannot answer natural-route transmission, infant bronchiolitis, or lower-airway disease. |
| Placebo-controlled challenge trial (rung 7) | [[sources/byrne-2026-s337395-placebo-controlled-rsv-challenge]] | In the Memphis 37b model, 114 inoculated adults randomized double-blind to S-337395 or placebo: 60 infected (53%); 30/300 mg reduced qRT-PCR viral load (64.87%/88.94%) and culture viral load, and 300 mg reduced symptom-score AUC (78.15%) vs placebo; no serious adverse events. | Placebo controls the drug, not inoculation; no non-inoculated group; healthy young adults, single RSV-A strain, 5-day quarantine dosing; no natural-exposure or severe-disease evidence. |
| Prospective household cohort, natural exposure (GAP 3, row 1) | [[sources/munywoki-2014-rsv-infant-source-household-cohort]] | 44 rural Kenyan households (451 members) with deep nasopharyngeal swabs every 3-4 days from all occupants regardless of symptoms across one full season (24 weeks; 15,396/17,985 expected samples, 85.6%): 84.1% of households and 38.4% of individuals had >=1 PCR-defined RSV episode; 28/44 infants (63.6%) infected; of 28 first infant episodes 15 (53.6%) acquired within the household, 9 (32.1%) outside, 4 (14.3%) coprimary-inconclusive; within-household primary cases were 11 older children (10 school-attending), 1 mother, 1 father, 2 other adults; primary-infant G-gene pairs identical in 10/10 successfully sequenced pairs; no field-team infections (a built-in outside-introducer control). Supplies exposure + infection marker + timing in the same people, with no inoculation or intervention. | No sham arm (natural cohort) and no comparison households; endpoint is PCR infection, not bronchiolitis or any disease outcome; source attribution is an inference from temporal ordering under 3-4-day sampling plus sequencing (5/15 pairs failed sequencing at high Ct); single season, single rural site; other adults sampled on only 64.3% of expected visits; cannot separate transmission routes or answer severity. |
| Prospective household cohort, natural exposure (GAP 3, row 2 - replication) | [[sources/cox-2025-cascadia-rsv-household-transmission]] | US replication in a different setting and season: CASCADIA, Seattle + Portland, June 2022-May 2023, 1,009 multi-person households / 3,058 people aged 6 months-50 years, weekly at-home nasal swabs and symptom surveys irrespective of symptoms plus illness-triggered swabs, serum prefusion IgG at baseline and semi-annually: RSV detected in 310/3,058 (10%) individuals within 200/1,009 (20%) households; 37/369 contacts (10.0%, CI 7.0-14.4) infected within 14 days of a distinct index case; 27/170 (16%) infected households produced a secondary case; median generation interval 7 days; index cases children <=12 in 24/27 (89%), secondary cases mostly adults (21/37); index viral detection >=1 week aRR 5.62 (2.62-12.06) while index Ct-based viral load and both parties' baseline titers showed no association; WGS of 120 specimens: same-household pairs <=14 days apart mean 4 nt difference (n=17 households, none cross-clade) vs 137 nt (n=2) >14 days apart. Replicates the Kenya row's finding that children introduce RSV into households, with genomic corroboration the Kenya row lacked at the whole-genome level. | No sham arm (natural cohort); endpoint is PCR infection, not disease; incomplete household enrollment (52% complete ascertainment) and asymmetric swabbing by symptoms (authors' own limitations); direction of transmission inferred from timing, not per-pair genomics; affluent skew; the contact-influenza-vaccination association (aRR 0.36) is observational and season-confounded; only 17 transmitting households contributed genomic pairs. |
| Randomized placebo-controlled vaccine trial, disease endpoints (GAP 4) | [[sources/ison-2024-rsvpref3oa-two-season-rct]] | AReSVi-006 (NCT04886596), phase 3 randomized placebo-controlled blinded trial, >=60 y, 24,973 exposed (12,470 vaccine / 12,503 placebo), 17 countries: one AS01E-adjuvanted RSVPreF3 OA dose before RSV season 1 reduced committee-adjudicated RT-PCR-confirmed RSV-LRTD over 2 seasons by 67.2% (97.5% CI 48.2-80.0; 30 vs 139 cases), severe RSV-LRTD 78.8% (52.6-92.0; 7 vs 48), RSV-ARI 52.7% (40.0-63.0); season 1 alone 82.6% (57.9-94.1; 7 vs 40) re-reporting the NEJM primary's numbers; efficacy declined to 56.1% in season 2 alone and revaccination at 1 year added no measurable benefit; RSV-A 80.5% vs RSV-B 59.7%. Supplies the intervention rung with disease endpoints in older adults. | No all-cause endpoint (deaths and SAEs are balanced safety captures, ~4% each); hospitalization unpowered (1 vs 5 events, "could not be evaluated"); >=80 y and frail estimates uninformative (too few cases); placebo composition not named in the CID report (sits in the unreachable NEJM primary); sponsor (GSK) designed and ran the trial with employees among authors; the subgroups are unadjusted descriptive analyses. |

## Current manuscript rule for RSV and bronchiolitis
RSV should be presented as a multi-rung chain: early CCA-related isolation, serology/epidemiology, diagnostic infant bronchiolitis association, animal lower-airway pathology, adult route-specific infectivity, the modern Memphis 37 adult challenge chain (dose-finding validation plus randomized placebo-controlled treatment), prospective household cohorts measuring natural-route infection spread and timing in ordinary households (rural Kenya 2012 and US 2022-23, now replicated across settings with genomic corroboration), and one randomized placebo-controlled trial showing a prefusion-F vaccine reduces adjudicated RT-PCR-confirmed RSV lower respiratory tract disease in older adults (GAP 4, disease endpoints). The manuscript may say that RSV has substantial endpoint-specific evidence for association with infant bronchiolitis, for experimental infection/pathology in other systems, for natural household transmission from child introducers, and - in older adults - for a licensed-class vaccine reducing PCR-confirmed lower-respiratory disease. It should also say that detection in nasopharyngeal secretions, adult inoculation, cotton-rat pathology, and quarantine challenge treatment are not interchangeable with direct infant bronchiolitis causation, that the household rows measure infection not bronchiolitis, and that the vaccine trial's endpoints are disease-specific with no all-cause outcome, unpowered hospitalization analysis, and uninformative >=80-year/frail subgroups.
