# Infectious Gastroenteritis Outbreaks to Norovirus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, exposure-management, or infection-control recommendations.

## Current answer
The infectious gastroenteritis outbreaks -> norovirus row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder:

- [[sources/kapikian-1972-immune-electron-microscopy-norwalk-agent]] supports the Norwalk-agent particle and serology discovery rung.
- [[sources/atmar-2008-norwalk-virus-shedding-experimental-human-infection]] supports controlled adult human oral challenge, gastroenteritis, shedding, and antibody response.
- [[sources/arness-2000-norwalk-like-gastroenteritis-army-trainees]] supports outbreak-level epidemiology, RT-PCR, EM, and bacterial/parasitic exclusion.
- [[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]] supports a prospective household natural-transmission rung: 604 randomly selected Dutch households (2,298 people) under 10 weeks of daily symptom diaries for every member with AGE-triggered stool sampling and rtPCR in cases plus sampled asymptomatic contacts - secondary attack rate 15% after a norovirus index case, microbiologically confirmed symptomatic transmission 10%, asymptomatic transmission 51% (52/102) of sampled contacts, with epidemiological-link attribution (GI/GII-level PCR, no sequencing) and the authors' own overestimation caveat recorded.
- [[sources/ettayebi-2016-human-norovirus-replication-enteroids]] supports human intestinal enteroid replication under defined culture conditions.
- [[sources/atmar-2011-norovirus-vlp-vaccine-challenge-rct]] supports an intervention row with a clinical disease endpoint under controlled challenge exposure.

The current full-text set supports norovirus as more than a PCR-only attribution, but particle visualization, human challenge, outbreak investigation, household natural-transmission measurement, enteroid replication, and intervention are different rungs.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prototype particle and serology | [[sources/kapikian-1972-immune-electron-microscopy-norwalk-agent]] | A 27 nm particle was associated with infectious Norwalk gastroenteritis material and host serologic response. | Full norovirus diversity, culture replication, or all outbreak ecology. |
| Human challenge and shedding | [[sources/atmar-2008-norwalk-virus-shedding-experimental-human-infection]] | Oral Norwalk virus exposure produced infection, defined gastroenteritis, RT-PCR shedding, and antibody response in susceptible adults. | Infectiousness of all PCR-positive shedding or all population severity. |
| Outbreak investigation | [[sources/arness-2000-norwalk-like-gastroenteritis-army-trainees]] | A trainee gastroenteritis outbreak was linked to Norwalk-like virus by stool RT-PCR/EM and negative tested bacterial/parasitic alternatives. | Exact contamination event or universal outbreak source dynamics. |
| Prospective household transmission | [[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]] (run 23, queue item 45) | In an endemic-setting household cohort with daily symptom diaries for every member and AGE-triggered rtPCR of cases plus sampled asymptomatic contacts, a secondary AGE episode followed a norovirus index case in 35% of households (per-person secondary attack rate 15%, 37/244), confirmed symptomatic transmission was 10% (25/241), and 51% (52/102) of sampled asymptomatic household members were norovirus-PCR-positive within a week of an index case - exposure, infection markers, and disease timing measured in the same people. | Direction and genomic confirmation of individual transmission events (GI/GII-level PCR, no sequencing), serial sampling of all members regardless of symptoms, year-round rates, and infectiousness of asymptomatic PCR detections. |
| Enteroid replication | [[sources/ettayebi-2016-human-norovirus-replication-enteroids]] | Human noroviruses can replicate in stem-cell-derived human enteroids under defined conditions. | Symptom causation or human transmission by itself. |
| Intervention (disease endpoint) | [[sources/atmar-2011-norovirus-vlp-vaccine-challenge-rct]] | Blocking the agent (Norwalk VLP vaccine, adjuvant-unmatched placebo quoted) reduced adjudicated Norwalk gastroenteritis (37% vs 69%, P=0.006) and any-assay infection (61% vs 82%, P=0.05) under an identical inpatient oral Norwalk challenge in FUT2-functional adults - intervention evidence consistent with the infectious-agent hypothesis. | Homologous GI.1 strain only; enriched maximally susceptible adults; natural exposure, transmission, heterologous protection, duration, and an adjuvant-matched comparator all untested. |

## Current manuscript rule for norovirus outbreaks
Norovirus should be presented as a multi-rung chain: prototype particle/serology, human challenge, outbreak investigation, prospective household natural-transmission measurement, enteroid replication, and - as of 2026-11-11 (run 22, queue item 42) - a placebo-controlled disease-endpoint intervention result ([[sources/atmar-2011-norovirus-vlp-vaccine-challenge-rct]]: two intranasal doses of an MPL/chitosan-adjuvanted Norwalk VLP vaccine, placebo arm excipients-only and inoculated with the same 48-RT-PCR-unit challenge; per-protocol gastroenteritis 37% vs 69%, P=0.006). The manuscript may say that the current full-text set supports norovirus attribution for infectious gastroenteritis under several endpoint types, now including a blocked-agent disease endpoint and - as of 2026-11-11 (run 23, queue item 45) - an endemic-setting prospective household cohort ([[sources/quee-2020-rotafam-household-gastroenteritis-cohort]]) placing natural household transmission on the same ladder. It should also say that PCR positivity, prolonged shedding, outbreak epidemiology, household secondary-episode clustering, cell replication, and challenge efficacy in an enriched homologous-challenge population cannot be treated as interchangeable proof; that the RotaFam transmission attribution is epidemiological-link plus same-genogroup PCR without sequencing (the authors' own overestimation caveat); and that the placebo lacked the vaccine's adjuvants (the authors' own first limitation).
