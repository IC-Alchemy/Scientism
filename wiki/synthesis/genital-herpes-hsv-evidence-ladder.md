# Genital Herpes to HSV Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, treatment, exposure-management, pregnancy, or sexual-health recommendations.

## Current answer
The genital herpes -> HSV-2 / HSV-1 row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder:

- [[sources/brown-1979-clinical-virologic-course-herpes-simplex-genitalis]] supports cultivable virus recovery and titer/excretion differences in genital lesions.
- [[sources/koelle-1992-asymptomatic-reactivation-women-first-genital-herpes]] supports culture-detected asymptomatic reactivation after first-episode genital herpes.
- [[sources/tronstein-2011-genital-shedding-hsv2-symptomatic-asymptomatic]] supports common HSV-2 DNA shedding in symptomatic and asymptomatic HSV-2-seropositive persons.
- [[sources/johnston-2022-viral-shedding-first-episode-genital-hsv1]] supports genital HSV-1 DNA shedding decline during the first year after first episode.
- [[sources/barton-1987-asymptomatic-shedding-transmission-genital-herpes]] supports a documented asymptomatic-shedding transmission case/cluster with molecular typing.

The current full-text set supports lesion culture, asymptomatic reactivation, PCR shedding, and at least case-level transmission evidence. It does not convert PCR-positive shedding into per-act infectious transmission probability.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lesion culture and titer | [[sources/brown-1979-clinical-virologic-course-herpes-simplex-genitalis]] | Cultivable HSV is recoverable from genital lesions and varies by initial/recurrent status and lesion condition. | Asymptomatic shedding burden or transmission rate. |
| Asymptomatic culture reactivation | [[sources/koelle-1992-asymptomatic-reactivation-women-first-genital-herpes]] | Asymptomatic genital HSV isolation occurs after first episodes and differs by HSV type/status. | Full PCR-detectable shedding burden or per-contact risk. |
| HSV-2 DNA shedding | [[sources/tronstein-2011-genital-shedding-hsv2-symptomatic-asymptomatic]] | HSV-2 DNA shedding is common even in people without recognized symptoms. | Infectiousness of every PCR-positive specimen. |
| Genital HSV-1 shedding | [[sources/johnston-2022-viral-shedding-first-episode-genital-hsv1]] | Genital HSV-1 shedding declines during the first year after first episode. | Lifetime transmission risk. |
| Transmission case | [[sources/barton-1987-asymptomatic-shedding-transmission-genital-herpes]] | Asymptomatic shedding can be implicated in a documented transmission chain. | Population transmission probability. |

## Current manuscript rule for genital herpes and HSV
Genital herpes should be presented as a lesion, shedding, recurrence, and transmission ladder. The manuscript may say that the current full-text set supports genital HSV lesion recovery, asymptomatic reactivation/shedding, and case-level transmission. It should also say that PCR shedding, culture shedding, and transmission probability are distinct endpoints.
