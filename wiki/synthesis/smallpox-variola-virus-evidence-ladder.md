# Smallpox to Variola Virus Evidence Ladder

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make vaccination, exposure-management, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The smallpox -> variola virus row can now move from candidate status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text set in this pass supports several distinct rungs: artificial variola inoculation in nonhuman primates, mouse nasal-route and lung-recovery boundaries, embryonated-egg propagation and stability, one-day fertile-egg growth, experimental monkey contact/aerosol transmission, and a negative simian-reservoir assessment.

- [[sources/brinckerhoff-tyzzer-1906-experimental-variola-monkeys]] supports early nonhuman-primate inoculation and route-boundary evidence.
- [[sources/nelson-1939-variola-virus-nasal-instillation-mice]] supports mouse nasal-route, lung-recovery, and host-range boundary evidence.
- [[sources/nelson-1940-variola-vaccinia-survival-mouse-lungs]] supports mouse lung-recovery and partial variola/vaccinia cross-protection boundaries.
- [[sources/nelson-1943-stability-variola-virus-embryonated-eggs]] supports egg propagation and stability of variola strains.
- [[sources/oya-1956-vaccinia-variola-one-day-hen-egg-growth]] supports comparative growth of variola and vaccinia in one-day fertile hen eggs.
- [[sources/noble-rich-1969-smallpox-contact-aerosol-macaca-irus]] supports contact and aerosol transmission in experimentally infected *Macaca irus*.
- [[sources/noble-1970-simian-reservoir-smallpox-monkeys]] supports simian host-range and reservoir-boundary evidence.

This is not a single proof sentence. The evidence is strongest when endpoints stay separate: lesion production after variolation, survival or recovery in mouse lung, growth in eggs, monkey contact/aerosol transmission, and absence of convincing simian-reservoir evidence answer different questions.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonhuman-primate artificial inoculation | [[sources/brinckerhoff-tyzzer-1906-experimental-variola-monkeys]] | Human variola material could produce local lesions, fever, and sometimes generalized exanthem in monkeys by deliberate inoculation; route mattered. | Ordinary human smallpox transmission or purified-virus causation. |
| Mouse respiratory-route boundary | [[sources/nelson-1939-variola-virus-nasal-instillation-mice]]; [[sources/nelson-1940-variola-vaccinia-survival-mouse-lungs]] | Nasal variola exposure in mice yielded lung recovery/pathology and altered reinfection kinetics, but not ordinary clinical smallpox. | Human disease causation or faithful mouse smallpox. |
| Egg propagation and stability | [[sources/nelson-1943-stability-variola-virus-embryonated-eggs]]; [[sources/oya-1956-vaccinia-variola-one-day-hen-egg-growth]] | Variola could be propagated in embryonated eggs and one-day fertile eggs while retaining distinguishable behavior from vaccinia in the tested systems. | Human disease reproduction, ordinary transmission, or complete variola-vaccinia historical identity. |
| Contact and aerosol animal transmission | [[sources/noble-rich-1969-smallpox-contact-aerosol-macaca-irus]] | Experimentally infected *Macaca irus* could transmit variola by contact and by an aerosol chamber arrangement. | Full human epidemiology or natural monkey-reservoir maintenance. |
| Reservoir boundary | [[sources/noble-1970-simian-reservoir-smallpox-monkeys]] | Tested monkey species and sera did not support a likely wild simian smallpox reservoir, despite experimental susceptibility in *Macaca irus*. | Every possible reservoir and human smallpox causation. |

## Current manuscript rule for smallpox and variola virus
Smallpox should be presented as an evidence ladder with explicit route, host, and endpoint boundaries:

- Brinckerhoff and Tyzzer 1906 answers artificial variolation and route-comparison questions in nonhuman primates.
- Nelson 1939 and 1940 answer mouse nasal-route, lung-recovery, and partial cross-protection questions.
- Nelson 1943 and Oya 1956 answer laboratory propagation and stability questions in egg systems.
- Noble and Rich 1969 answers experimental monkey contact/aerosol transmission questions.
- Noble 1970 answers simian-reservoir plausibility questions.

The manuscript may upgrade smallpox from candidate status to a started ladder. It should not flatten variolation lesions, egg growth, mouse lung recovery, monkey aerosol transmission, and human natural-route smallpox into one claim.
