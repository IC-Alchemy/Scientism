# Scientism, Methodological Overreach, and the Discipline of Evidence

## Abstract

This manuscript summarizes the current work of the Scientism notebook. The central finding is methodological, not doctrinal: the vault is not yet a proof system for a single grand conclusion about medicine, biology, virology, vaccines, AIDS, germ theory, or public health. It is a disciplined procedure for preventing overclaiming.

The working definition of scientism used here is the extension of scientific authority, methods, or results beyond what the underlying evidence strictly supports. That definition is preserved in [[concepts/scientism]]. It requires a constant separation between three things: what was observed, what the experiment was designed to test, and what later interpreters concluded from it. The vault's strongest conceptual tools are [[concepts/empirical-evidence]], [[concepts/inferential-leap]], [[concepts/clinical-endpoints]], [[concepts/placebo-controls]], [[concepts/surrogate-endpoints]], [[concepts/germ-theory]], [[concepts/virus-isolation]], and [[concepts/cell-culture]].

The notebook's most important conclusion is that evidence must be handled endpoint by endpoint. A PCR result is a nucleic-acid detection endpoint. A cell-culture effect is a laboratory endpoint. An animal challenge is an animal-model endpoint. A human challenge without a sham control is not the same thing as a blinded controlled human clinical causation trial. A disease-specific vaccine endpoint is not the same claim as overall health benefit. A short-term drug efficacy result is not a long-term safety result. A negative transmission experiment is not proof that transmission is impossible, but it is also not nothing.

Under this standard, the current vault supports several narrow conclusions. It supports that the Bridges et al. influenza vaccine trial was a randomized saline-placebo trial whose results differed sharply by season and endpoint, including unfavorable ILI-related utilization outcomes in the vaccine group during the mismatched 1997-1998 season [[sources/effectiveness-and-cost-benefit-of-influenza-vaccination-of-healthy-working-adults]]. It supports that the Cutts et al. PCV9 trial in Gambian children is strong direct evidence that one specific vaccine, in one high-burden setting, reduced all-cause admissions and all-cause mortality, while not proving that the full childhood vaccination schedule improves overall health in all contexts [[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]], [[synthesis/specific-vaccine-overall-health-outcome-evidence]], [[synthesis/vaccinated-children-more-healthy-question]]. It supports that the Gutknecht AIDS letter is a historically important challenge document, but not biomedical proof by itself; direct-study follow-up rejects the broad claim that recreational drugs or AZT alone can explain AIDS generally while preserving unresolved questions about mechanisms, long-term survival, assay performance, and treatment ethics [[sources/congressman-questions-aids-in-letter]], [[synthesis/gutknecht-letter-evidence-check]], [[synthesis/gutknecht-letter-questions-7-9]].

The vault also supports a broader methodological warning about virus-disease attribution. It does not support the blanket statement that viral causation has been globally proven, and it does not support the blanket statement that viral causation has been globally refuted. The correct unit of analysis is the disease-specific evidence ladder: syndrome description, detection, culture or propagation, serology, animal challenge, re-isolation, controlled human challenge, prospective transmission or cohort evidence, intervention evidence, and replicated cross-method convergence [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]. That ladder is the current center of the notebook's strict science-based method.

## Thesis

The work of this notebook can be stated as a rule:

> A scientific claim is only as strong as the study design, endpoint, comparator, result, limitation, and replication record that directly support it.

This rule is intentionally stricter than consensus, prestige, peer review, expert agreement, regulatory position, policy usefulness, or cultural familiarity. Authority can help locate sources. It cannot replace the experiment. The vault's mission statement says the same thing operationally: replication is the strongest marker of truth; experimental methods and results matter more than the author's personal interpretation; and no claim is supported unless the study type, subject, intervention or exposure, comparator, endpoint, result, and main limitation can be named.

This standard also applies symmetrically. Mainstream institutions can overclaim from narrow findings, but critics can overclaim from gaps, failures, anomalies, or weak study designs. The notebook therefore refuses two common shortcuts:

1. It refuses to treat official summaries, expert consensus, or institutional confidence as proof.
2. It also refuses to treat a methodological objection, a failed experiment, or a polemical essay as automatic disproof of an entire field.

The result is a research posture rather than a slogan. The pages most responsible for that posture are [[synthesis/scientism-research-program-overview]], [[synthesis/source-corpus-triage]], and [[synthesis/vault-maintenance-guide]].

## 1. The Notebook's Evidence Grammar

### 1.1 Scientism as overextension

In this vault, scientism does not mean "science is bad" or "scientific experiments are untrustworthy." It means that scientific authority, technical vocabulary, or experimental results are used to carry a conclusion that the underlying evidence does not strictly support [[concepts/scientism]].

The core blur is between:

- direct observation
- experimental design
- later interpretation

For example, a study may observe viral RNA in a sample. That observation may support a detection claim. It does not by itself prove infectious virus, disease causation, clinical diagnosis, transmission, or policy necessity. A trial may show fewer cases of a target disease. That can support a target-disease claim. It does not automatically prove better overall health, lower all-cause hospitalization, lower mortality, improved neurodevelopment, or long-term net benefit [[comparisons/disease-prevention-vs-health-outcomes]].

The current vault uses scientism as a diagnostic label for this overextension. It does not use the term as a substitute for source reading.

### 1.2 Empirical evidence is necessary but not self-interpreting

[[concepts/empirical-evidence]] defines empirical evidence as observation, measurement, or experiment. But the page's most important point is negative: empirical evidence is not self-interpreting. A measurement becomes relevant only after the method, endpoint, and limitation are understood.

The sequencing-method paper [[sources/insight-into-biases-and-sequencing-errors-of-illumina-miseq-platform]] is the cleanest current example. It is a primary methodology study using mock communities, single-species samples, library-preparation comparisons, primer choices, sequencing runs, trimming, error correction, and read-overlap handling. It can answer a technical question about MiSeq amplicon error profiles in the tested setup. It cannot answer whether a downstream disease-causation interpretation is true. That limited strength is exactly why it is useful: a strong experiment still has boundaries.

### 1.3 Inferential leaps are the central object of study

[[concepts/inferential-leap]] is the vault's main analytic tool. An inferential leap occurs when a source moves from a narrower observation or experiment to a broader conclusion without establishing the missing bridge.

The vault has found this pattern in both mainstream and dissenting directions:

- A vaccine trial measuring antibody response, short-term reactogenicity, or target-disease endpoints may be presented as proof of general health benefit [[synthesis/vaccinated-children-more-healthy-question]].
- A no-virus commentary may move from cell-culture criticism or isolation terminology to the claim that virology as a whole is pseudoscience [[sources/the-virus-cult-a-religion-built-on-fear-not-science]], [[sources/the-virus-paradigm-assumptions-lies-and-the-foundation-of-modern-medicine]].
- A negative influenza transmission experiment may be ignored by later certainty narratives, while later ferret and swine experiments may be overextended into ordinary human transmission proof [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]], [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]], [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]], [[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]].
- A human challenge model without a sham control may be treated as if it settled controlled human clinical causation [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]].

The method is not to assume the leap is false. The method is to name the bridge that is missing.

### 1.4 Replication is strongest, but this page is still underbuilt

The vault's stated standard gives replication the highest weight. But [[concepts/replication-crisis]] is still exploratory because the notebook has not yet ingested strong direct replication-project sources with field, method, endpoint, replication result, and limitation. This is an important limitation of the current notebook. Replication is the vault's preferred evidentiary ideal, but the replication-crisis page itself cannot yet carry field-wide quantitative claims.

That limitation should stay visible. A strict evidence standard must apply to its own slogans.

## 2. Trial Endpoints, Controls, and the Difference Between Disease Prevention and Health

### 2.1 Endpoints define the claim

The notebook repeatedly returns to endpoint discipline. [[concepts/clinical-endpoints]] defines clinical endpoints as outcomes that directly describe patient health: mortality, hospitalization, severe disease, symptom burden, disability, or quality of life. [[concepts/surrogate-endpoints]] covers narrower measures used as stand-ins: antibody response, infection counts, biomarkers, or other proxy outcomes.

The key rule is simple: the endpoint defines what the study can answer.

A trial that measures antibody titers can answer whether antibody titers changed under that protocol. A trial that measures all-cause mortality can answer a broader mortality question within its population and follow-up limits. A trial that measures ILI-related lost workdays can answer that work-utilization endpoint. It cannot answer rare harms, long-term disease patterns, or all-cause mortality unless those were measured.

### 2.2 Controls matter, but they do not solve everything

[[concepts/placebo-controls]] emphasizes that control groups are not a technical footnote. An inert placebo comparison can isolate some absolute treatment effects more directly than an active comparator. An active-comparator trial can still be valuable, but it answers a different question.

The pediatric vaccine audit [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]] illustrates this. It checked 18 PubMed-indexed trial records cited by a Children's Health Defense source. At the abstract and metadata level, many cited trials used active comparators, schedule comparisons, or dose comparisons. Some records were placebo-labeled but did not identify placebo composition in the abstract. The audit supports a narrow methodological point: comparator type must be named. It does not prove a general conclusion about all vaccine safety testing.

This is the vault's tone at its best: the critique is accepted only at the level directly supported.

### 2.3 The Bridges influenza vaccine trial as a model endpoint audit

[[sources/effectiveness-and-cost-benefit-of-influenza-vaccination-of-healthy-working-adults]] is one of the vault's strongest direct-study anchors. It reports a double-blind randomized saline-placebo trial of trivalent inactivated influenza vaccine in healthy working adults over two seasons.

The study can be decomposed:

| Field | Vault extraction |
| --- | --- |
| Study type | Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial |
| Population | Healthy full-time working adults aged 18 to 64 at Ford Motor Co. |
| Intervention | Trivalent inactivated influenza vaccine |
| Comparator | Sterile saline injection in identical syringes |
| Endpoints | Serologic influenza illness in a subset, ILI, ILI-related sick days, physician visits, work absenteeism, costs, short-term adverse effects |
| Key result | Matched and mismatched seasons differed; 1997-1998 had worse ILI-related utilization endpoints in the vaccine group, while 1998-1999 showed reductions in several influenza-related endpoints |
| Limitation | Two seasons, one employer population, subset serology, survey endpoints, no rare or long-term safety power |

The scientism problem here is not that the trial is weak. It is that its abstract-level framing can foreground conditional benefit while leaving an unfavorable randomized endpoint signal less visible. In the mismatched 1997-1998 season, vaccine recipients reported significantly more ILI-related sick days, lost workdays, and physician-visit work hours than placebo recipients. The proper conclusion is not "influenza vaccines are harmful in general." The proper conclusion is that this trial directly observed worse short-term utilization outcomes for the vaccine group in that season, and that any summary should preserve that fact.

### 2.4 The PCV9 Gambia trial as a narrow favorable health-outcome example

The strongest favorable vaccine-health evidence currently in the vault is [[sources/cutts-2005-pcv9-gambia-rct]]. It tested a nine-valent pneumococcal conjugate vaccine in 17,437 young children in eastern Gambia. It was randomized, double-blind, and placebo-controlled. It measured radiological pneumonia, invasive pneumococcal disease, all-cause admissions, all-cause mortality, and short-term serious adverse events.

The key result is broad enough to matter: per-protocol all-cause admissions were lower in the vaccine group, and all-cause mortality was also lower. The intention-to-treat mortality result was directionally similar. That makes this trial the vault's best current counterexample to the broad claim that no vaccine has direct evidence for improved broader health outcomes [[synthesis/specific-vaccine-overall-health-outcome-evidence]].

But the limitation is equally important. The trial tested one product in young Gambian children in a high-burden setting, added to a routine-vaccine context. It did not compare a completely vaccinated childhood schedule with no vaccination. It did not establish that all vaccines, all PCV formulations, all settings, or the entire childhood schedule improve overall health. The broad parental question remains unresolved in [[synthesis/vaccinated-children-more-healthy-question]].

This is the vault's preferred form of answer:

- Supported: PCV9 reduced all-cause admissions and all-cause mortality in the tested Gambian child population.
- Not supported by this source alone: vaccination as a class improves total health.
- Unresolved: whether the full childhood vaccination program makes vaccinated children more healthy overall than unvaccinated children.

## 3. Historical Germ Theory: Method, Narrative, and Overreach

### 3.1 Germ theory is not one claim

[[concepts/germ-theory]] treats germ theory as a historical research program, not as a slogan. The vault's current position is unresolved because the source base is mixed: a few primary historical or methodological anchors, several Tier 3 revisionist essays, and many open questions.

The most useful current historical anchor is [[sources/kochs-famous-paper]]. It is a primary-source style text attributed to Robert Koch, focused on method: pure culture, microscopy, staining, tissue examination, host choice, pathogenicity, infectiousness, and transmission testing. The vault does not treat it as proof that every later attribution made in Koch's name was valid. It treats it as evidence that Koch himself was concerned with methodological discipline.

That matters because some critiques target the later narrative rather than the original method. [[sources/the-germ-hypothesis-part-2]] argues that later celebrations of Koch overstate what his experiments proved. [[sources/an-inquiry-into-the-logical-basis-of-the-germ-theory]] argues that germ-theory reasoning confused association with causation and spread through authority. These sources are valuable as argument maps, but they are Tier 3 commentary and cannot settle the historical or biomedical claims themselves.

### 3.2 Rivers shows that viral causation was never just literal Koch postulates

[[sources/rivers-1937-viruses-and-kochs-postulates]] is a key methodological source. Rivers argued that Koch's bacteriological postulates could not be copied mechanically into virology, especially where viruses could not be grown on lifeless media. But Rivers did not replace causal proof with loose association. He warned against treating incidental or coexisting viruses as causal and emphasized timing, susceptible hosts, infectious material, controls, immunology, and avoidance of fortuitous association.

The lesson for the notebook is precise: the question is not whether a source uses the words "Koch's postulates." The question is whether the source shows causal relevance rather than mere presence.

### 3.3 Molecular methods need their own proof standard

[[sources/fredericks-relman-1996-sequence-based-identification-microbial-pathogens]] extends the same issue into the molecular era. The source is a methodological review, not a single disease experiment. It argues that sequence evidence can support causal inference when it is repeatedly found in diseased hosts, localized to diseased tissue, uncommon or absent in controls, biologically coherent, and reduced with disease resolution or effective intervention.

The important boundary is that a sequence hit is not disease causation. PCR, RT-qPCR, sequencing, and metagenomic findings should stay in a molecular-detection bucket until the disease endpoint, controls, localization, timing, and mechanism bridge are named.

[[sources/jefferson-2022-hierarchical-framework-transmission-causality-respiratory-viruses]] applies a related standard to respiratory-virus transmission. It is not a primary experiment, but it reinforces the vault's distinction between weak detection evidence and stronger transmission-causality evidence.

## 4. The Viral Disease Causation Ladder

### 4.1 Why the ladder exists

The central virology synthesis is [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]]. Its first sentence matters: the current vault evidence does not support a blanket statement that viral disease causation has either been globally proven or globally refuted.

The proper unit is a named disease-virus pair and a named endpoint. The ladder separates:

1. clinical syndrome description
2. microscopy, antigen, PCR, RT-qPCR, or sequencing detection
3. culture, propagation, or isolation workflow
4. serology or immune response
5. animal challenge or experimental exposure
6. re-isolation or recovery from the experimental host
7. controlled human challenge
8. prospective cohort, outbreak, or transmission-chain evidence
9. intervention evidence
10. replicated cross-method convergence

Each rung answers a different question. Collapsing rungs into one word - "cause" - is the scientism risk.

### 4.2 Detection is not causation

The SARS-CoV-2 audit [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]] demonstrates the rule. Early papers can support virus characterization from patient-associated material. Animal studies can support model-specific pathogenicity under artificial exposure. But PCR and RT-qPCR are detection endpoints. They do not stand alone as proof of infectious virus, clinical diagnosis, symptom cause, transmission, or disease causation.

The audit classifies the current SARS-CoV-2 paper set as:

- partially supported for virus characterization from patient-associated material
- partially supported for animal-model pathogenicity under artificial exposure
- unresolved for controlled, sham-tested human clinical causation

That is not a denial that the papers contain evidence. It is a refusal to compress distinct endpoints.

### 4.3 Human challenge without sham control is not controlled human clinical causation

The same SARS-CoV-2 audit treats the Killingley human challenge study as a useful lead but not a clean controlled clinical causation experiment. The study was open-label, non-randomized, and lacked a sham-inoculated control group. Under this vault's standard, that missing comparator matters. It weakens symptom attribution because there is no sham group to separate inoculum effects from procedure, expectation, quarantine, background symptoms, or nonspecific reporting [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]].

This is a general rule. A human challenge study is not automatically strong enough to answer every human clinical causation question. The design still matters.

### 4.4 Animal models are evidence inside their model

The SARS-CoV-2 audit does not discard animal studies. Mock-controlled animal challenge with pathology and re-isolation is stronger than a PCR-only association. But the conclusion must stay inside the model. High-dose intratracheal exposure, engineered receptor expression, and hamster or macaque respiratory pathology are not identical to ordinary human disease causation. They are model-specific pathogenicity evidence [[synthesis/sars-cov-2-covid-causation-audit]].

The same boundary appears in the polio and VZV pages. The Sabin-Olitsky poliomyelitis source is a rhesus-monkey intranasal instillation study with timed tissue-recovery endpoints, not a complete human natural-route transmission proof [[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]]. The shingles/VZV audit separates lesion-derived culture, serologic relatedness, ganglionic DNA/RNA detection, epidemiologic latency hypotheses, and zoster vaccine endpoint trials rather than treating them as one proof package [[synthesis/shingles-vzv-causation-and-vaccine-trial-audit]].

## 5. Negative Results and the Duty Not to Forget Failed Attempts

### 5.1 Negative results are neither final disproof nor disposable noise

[[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]] exists because a causation history is distorted if it records only successful or canonical experiments. But the register also warns that it is not a complete literature review and that negative results must be classified by type: failed detection, failed culture, failed transmission, failed challenge, failed intervention, or failed generalization.

This is another symmetric rule. A negative result does not automatically refute a causal model. But if later narratives ignore repeated negative attempts, the field's apparent certainty may be inflated.

### 5.2 Influenza and the evidence ladder problem

The influenza branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]]. That page replaces a single-source negative-result warning with a source-by-source structure that separates pre-virus human challenge, human-case filtrate, animal passage, tissue recovery, serology, swine contact transfer, and ordinary human natural-route causation.

[[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]] remains the key pre-virus negative-result anchor. The paper reports repeated human volunteer transmission attempts during the 1918-1919 influenza outbreak. The exposure methods included Pfeiffer bacillus cultures, mixed respiratory secretions, filtered material, direct nose-to-nose and throat-to-throat transfer, citrated whole blood, filtered upper-respiratory material injected subcutaneously, and close ward exposure. Across the specifically described series, Rosenau reported that volunteers did not become recognizably sick under the tested conditions.

The proper interpretation has two boundaries: the source directly supports the narrow claim that repeated challenge and close-contact procedures failed in the reported volunteer groups under those conditions, and it does not support a vault-level conclusion that influenza is noninfectious. Rosenau's own caution is essential. He warned against drawing positive conclusions from negative results and preserved uncertainty about susceptibility, immunity, diagnosis, timing, and unknown transmission factors. The source is therefore a model of disciplined uncertainty: the failures matter, but they do not authorize more than they show.

[[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]] answers a different question. It reports that filtered throat washings from some early human influenza cases produced a reproducible ferret disease under experimental conditions. The endpoints include ferret temperature response, nasal catarrh, serial passage by nasal-turbinate material, co-housing transmission in ferrets, tissue recovery from nasal passages, and neutralization by immune or convalescent serum. That is stronger than mere association with an outbreak, but it is still an animal-model and passage result, not a controlled human transmission result.

[[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]] adds a cross-species susceptibility rung and a contact-boundary rung. The paper reports that human influenza-virus strains PR8 and WS infected young swine after intranasal inoculation, produced mild respiratory illness and lung lesions, and yielded virus recoverable by mouse inoculation. It also reports that virus plus *H. influenzae suis* produced more severe illness in many swine, while contact transfer among swine occurred in only one of four exposed animals and was clinically inapparent, detected by neutralizing antibody.

The current manuscript conclusion is therefore endpoint-specific. Influenza-virus evidence in this pass supports animal challenge, animal passage, tissue recovery, and serology rungs. It also preserves a pre-virus human challenge failure and a weak swine-contact boundary. It does not yet reconstruct ordinary human natural-route causation from full-text primary sources. We do not know that broader claim at manuscript strength from the current influenza source set.

### 5.3 Poliomyelitis and the evidence ladder problem

The polio branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/poliomyelitis-poliovirus-evidence-ladder]]. That page is deliberately stricter than the older provisional warning. It separates clinical syndrome material, monkey inoculation, exposure route, tissue recovery, tissue-culture cultivation, direct patient isolation, and ordinary natural-route causation.

The current source status is mixed. Flexner and Lewis's 1910 *Journal of Experimental Medicine* paper has been located as a full-text primary target, but the vault still needs a clean source-page extraction before it should carry detailed manuscript claims. Three important JAMA items from 1910-1911 and the 1951 American Journal of Hygiene direct-isolation paper remain full-text acquisition gaps in this pass. Under the vault rule, those gaps are not rhetorical openings for stronger claims. They mean "we do not know yet" at manuscript strength.

[[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]] remains the current route-boundary anchor. The study used intranasal instillation of poliomyelitis virus in normal and convalescent immune rhesus monkeys and tracked recovery of infective virus from nasal mucosa, olfactory bulbs, and central nervous system tissues. The abstract-level extraction supports a narrow tissue-recovery statement: early recovery from sampled nasal mucosa and olfactory bulbs failed during the first 48 hours, later recovery appeared in small amounts, and immune animals did not yield recoverable virus from the named tissues at the reported time points. That is not the same endpoint as host-to-host transmission.

[[sources/enders-weller-robbins-1949-cultivation-lansing-strain-human-embryonic-tissues]] adds a different rung. It reports tissue-culture propagation of the Lansing strain in human embryonic tissues, including non-neural arm-leg tissue cultures, with identity checks by mouse disease, Lansing-specific antiserum neutralization, and selected rhesus-monkey inoculation. This strengthens the cultivation rung of the ladder. It does not answer ordinary human natural-route causation, and it does not replace the still-missing 1951 direct-isolation source.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is therefore endpoint-specific. The current full-text set supports laboratory and animal-model claims. It does not yet support a vault-level claim that ordinary natural-route human poliomyelitis causation has been fully reconstructed here. It also does not support the opposite overclaim that polio causation has been refuted. The right sentence is narrower: polio is a case where artificial challenge, route/tissue recovery, tissue culture, and natural causation must not be compressed into one word.

### 5.4 Mumps and the evidence ladder problem

The mumps branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/mumps-mumps-virus-evidence-ladder]]. It is a useful contrast with polio and influenza because the current full-text set includes not only animal challenge and isolation, but also a historical induced human exposure study.

[[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]] is the artificial-route anchor. It reports that saliva from four of six early human mumps cases produced acute non-suppurative parotitis in rhesus monkeys when placed directly into the parotid glands through Stensen's duct. The paper also reports that two normal-saliva control monkeys did not develop the delayed experimental parotitis pattern, that one strain was passaged through seven monkey generations, and that filtered monkey-gland emulsions remained virulent in five trials. That supports a monkey parotid-inoculation and passage rung. It does not prove ordinary human transmission.

[[sources/leymaster-ward-1947-direct-isolation-mumps-virus-chick-embryos]] adds a laboratory-isolation rung. The paper reports direct isolation of mumps virus from acute saliva in 8 of 9 clinically diagnosed mumps patients by amniotic-sac inoculation of chick embryos, with hemagglutination, complement-fixation identification, and selected monkey confirmation. It also preserves a useful diagnostic boundary: one clinically diagnosed case had negative isolation and no convalescent complement-fixation rise, so the clinical diagnosis was not laboratory-confirmed by that paper's endpoints.

[[sources/henle-henle-wendell-rosenberg-1948-induced-mumps-infections-virus-isolation]] adds a human induced-exposure rung. Fifteen susceptible children were exposed to fifth-amniotic-passage mumps virus by oral spray or atomized droplets. Six developed salivary-gland involvement, one developed orchitis without recognized salivary-gland involvement, and eight showed no signs of mumps but were classified as inapparent infections by serologic response. Virus was isolated from saliva in all salivary-gland cases before clinical signs, from the orchitis case before illness, and from six of eight inapparent infections. This is important human evidence, but it was not sham-controlled and used laboratory-prepared exposure rather than ordinary community exposure.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is therefore layered. Mumps-virus evidence in this pass supports animal parotid-duct challenge, chick-embryo isolation, serology, induced human exposure, and saliva-shedding rungs. It does not let the manuscript collapse direct parotid inoculation, laboratory isolation, induced exposure, and ordinary natural transmission into a single unexamined claim.

### 5.5 Rubella and the evidence ladder problem

The rubella branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/rubella-rubella-virus-evidence-ladder]]. The current pass strengthens acute-rubella source structure while keeping congenital-rubella and experimental-human rubella as acquisition gaps.

[[sources/parkman-buescher-artenstein-1962-recovery-rubella-virus-army-recruits]] reports recovery of a rubella-associated interfering agent from Fort Dix army recruits with clinically definite or probable rubella. The endpoint was not visible primary cytopathology, but interference with ECHO-11 cytopathology in grivet monkey kidney cultures. The comparison group matters: the agent was recovered from many definite or probable rubella cases, but rarely from contemporaneous scarlet-fever patients, and paired sera from most rubella patients showed rising neutralizing activity.

[[sources/weller-neva-1962-propagation-cytopathic-agents-rubella-like-illness]] adds a different culture rung. It reports cytopathic agents isolated from urine or blood of four rubella-like cases and serially propagated in primary human amnion cultures. The paper used parallel control cultures and compared paired sera from rubella cases with sera from several other viral illnesses. This supports propagation and serology, not ordinary transmission.

[[sources/sigurdardottir-givan-rozee-rhodes-1963-rubella-toronto-monkey-transmission]] adds a Toronto isolation and animal-inoculation rung. It reports recovery of an interference agent from 9 of 18 typical rubella specimens, convalescent-serum neutralization in four of five paired sera, and mild rash/lymphadenopathy in two grivet monkeys after combined intraperitoneal and intranasal inoculation. That is animal challenge evidence, but it is a small artificial-route model.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that acute rubella-virus recovery, culture propagation, paired-serology, and monkey-inoculation evidence are now represented. Congenital rubella syndrome and ordinary human transmission are not yet reconstructed from full-text primary sources here. They must not be inferred silently from the acute-rubella isolation rung.

### 5.6 Chickenpox/VZV and the evidence ladder problem

The chickenpox branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/chickenpox-varicella-zoster-virus-evidence-ladder]]. This pass deliberately separates direct chickenpox attribution from the later shingles latency/reactivation chain.

[[sources/weller-1953-serial-propagation-varicella-herpes-zoster-agents]] reports that vesicle fluids from 6 of 11 varicella cases produced focal cytopathogenic lesions with intranuclear inclusions in roller-tube human tissue cultures, and that two strains were maintained for 10 passages using infected tissue suspensions. The same paper reports similar cytopathogenic agents from two herpes zoster vesicle-fluid specimens. That supports lesion-fluid culture and serial propagation, not ordinary transmission or latency.

[[sources/cheatham-dolan-dower-weller-1956-fatal-varicella-necropsy-virus-isolation]] adds fatal-case pathology and tissue-recovery evidence. In two medically complicated fatal varicella cases, the authors described disseminated lesions with type A intranuclear inclusions and recovered virus by tissue culture from vesicle fluid and blood in both cases, and from lung in one case. These are strong systemic-recovery and pathology endpoints for severe cases, but they are not ordinary uncomplicated chickenpox.

[[sources/weller-witton-bell-1958-varicella-zoster-isolation-propagation-culture]] adds a larger culture-comparison rung. It reports serial propagation of 14 varicella-derived and 8 herpes-zoster-derived strains in human tissue culture and states that strains from the two syndromes could not be distinguished by the cultural attributes tested. That supports varicella-zoster cultural similarity. It does not directly prove that a later shingles episode is caused by reactivation of virus latent since chickenpox.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that chickenpox/VZV evidence in this pass supports lesion culture, fatal-case tissue recovery, and varicella/zoster culture similarity. It does not yet reconstruct ordinary chickenpox transmission, serologic identity from the companion 1958 paper, or the latency/reactivation sequence.

### 5.7 Hepatitis B and the evidence ladder problem

The hepatitis B branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-b-hepatitis-b-virus-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is deliberately partial: it obtained a pre-HAV/HBV syndrome-split source and a strong late molecular-clone chimpanzee challenge source, but not the intermediate full texts needed to reconstruct the whole historical ladder.

[[sources/krugman-giles-hammond-1967-two-types-infectious-hepatitis]] reports seven Willowbrook human infection trials using hepatitis-containing serum, oral exposure, intramuscular exposure, contact exposure, and later rechallenge. The paper distinguishes MS-1/IH-like and MS-2/SH-like patterns by incubation, SGOT duration, thymol turbidity, contact spread, and rechallenge immunity. This supports a syndrome-split and immunologic distinction rung. It does not identify HBV particles, culture a virus, sequence a virus, or prove purified-virus causation.

[[sources/acs-sells-purcell-1987-hepatitis-b-virus-transfected-hepg2-chimpanzees]] reports that medium from HBV-DNA-transfected Hep G2-derived cells contained particles that produced HBV infection markers and hepatitis endpoints in two chimpanzees after intravenous inoculation. The endpoints included HBsAg and HBeAg appearance, anti-HBc response, elevated alanine aminotransferase, serial liver-biopsy hepatitis, and recovered viral DNA matching the cloned/cell-produced input pattern by restriction analysis. That supports a high-rung claim: cell-produced HBV virions from cloned HBV DNA were infectious in this chimpanzee model.

The source does not settle the whole HBV chain. It used two chimpanzees, high-dose intravenous inoculation, and no sham-inoculated animal control. It does not answer ordinary human route, dose, transmission ecology, chronicity, or the 1970-1973 discovery sequence from Australia-antigen particles through earlier chimpanzee transmission. Those papers remain acquisition gaps here.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that HBV now has syndrome-split and molecular-clone/cell-produced virion infectivity rungs in the vault. The Australia-antigen and early chimpanzee-passage rungs should remain provisional until full-text primary extraction is completed.

### 5.8 Hepatitis C and the evidence ladder problem

The hepatitis C branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-c-hepatitis-c-virus-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is useful because it shows how a disease attribution can climb from pre-molecular non-A non-B hepatitis into molecular identification, antibody assays, prospective transfusion serology, and molecular-clone infectivity without making those endpoints interchangeable.

[[sources/choo-kuo-weiner-1989-cdna-clone-non-a-non-b-hcv-genome]] reports molecular identification of cDNA clones from infectious chimpanzee plasma linked to blood-borne non-A non-B hepatitis. The endpoints include non-host hybridization behavior, RNA sensitivity, infected-chimpanzee liver and plasma association, recombinant antigen reactivity, and serologic responses in NANBH patients and experimentally infected chimpanzees. This supports a molecular-identification rung. It does not by itself prove infectious full-genome causality or ordinary route transmission.

[[sources/kuo-choo-alter-1989-assay-circulating-antibodies-hcv]] adds the recombinant-antigen antibody-assay rung. The paper used C100-3 antigen to test chimpanzee-proven infectious NANBH sera, selected donor-recipient transfusion cases, controls, and chronic NANBH cohorts. Anti-HCV was detected in six of seven chimpanzee-proven infectious sera, all ten chronic post-transfusion NANBH recipients in the selected set seroconverted, and nine of ten matched transfusion cases had at least one anti-HCV-positive implicated donor. That is a strong serology-association result, but antibody detection is indirect and delayed and cannot be treated as equivalent to infectious virus isolation.

[[sources/alter-purcell-shih-1989-hcv-antibody-transfusion-recipients]] answers a prospective transfusion-recipient question. It reports anti-HCV seroconversion in all 15 selected chronic biopsy-supported transfusion-associated NANBH cases and in three of five acute resolving cases, with anti-HCV-positive donor samples found in most fully evaluable anti-HCV-positive recipient cases. This strengthens the donor-linkage and timing rung, while preserving selection, first-generation assay, and anti-HCV-negative acute-case limits.

[[sources/yanagi-purcell-emerson-bukh-1997-hcv-infectious-cdna-clone-chimpanzee]] answers a higher-rung molecular-clone question. Two initial full-length H77 clones, pH21 and pCV-H11, did not produce detectable replication after direct intrahepatic chimpanzee transfection. A later consensus clone, pCV-H77C, produced detectable and rising HCV RNA, and recovered sequences matched the input clone in tested regions. This supports one direct intrahepatic chimpanzee molecular-clone infectivity claim and also preserves a noninfectious-clone boundary.

The source set still has gaps. The 1984 pre-HCV chimpanzee transmission/passaging paper and the 1999 transmissible molecular-clone virion paper were not obtained in full text in this pass. Ordinary non-transfusion transmission and chronic-disease mechanisms are also not reconstructed here. The defensible manuscript conclusion is therefore narrow: HCV now has molecular-identification, antibody-assay, prospective transfusion-serology, and direct intrahepatic molecular-clone infectivity rungs in the vault. It should not be retold as if those rungs alone reconstruct every route, chronic outcome, or population-level claim.

### 5.9 Cervical cancer, HPV, and the evidence ladder problem

The cervical cancer branch now has its own HPV ladder page: [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]]. It differs from influenza, polio, and mumps because the central evidence in this pass is not transmission or challenge. It is tumor DNA, HPV type specificity, physical state, integration, and viral-cell junction structure.

[[sources/durst-gissmann-ikenberg-zur-hausen-1983-hpv16-cervical-carcinoma]] reports cloning HPV16 DNA from one cervical carcinoma and detecting HPV16-homologous DNA in subsets of cervical, vulval, and penile cancers. In the tested cervical-cancer sets, high-stringency HPV16 signals were found in 11 of 18 German cervical cancers and 8 of 23 Kenya/Brazil cervical cancers, while only 2 of 33 condylomata acuminata carried HPV16 DNA and both also carried HPV6 or HPV11. This supports an HPV16 tumor-DNA association rung, not a causal-progression or sufficiency rung.

[[sources/boshart-gissmann-ikenberg-1984-hpv18-genital-cancer-biopsies]] adds an HPV18 rung. It reports HPV18 cloning from cervical carcinoma material, HPV18 detection in some genital cancers and cervical-cancer-derived cell lines, absence from the sampled normal cervical tissue and benign lesions, and physical-state findings compatible with integration in some tumors. Again, the endpoint is molecular presence and structure, not experimental transformation of human cervix into cancer.

[[sources/durst-kleinheinz-hotz-gissmann-1985-physical-state-hpv16-genital-tumours]] sharpens the integration boundary. Six benign HPV16-positive genital tumors contained viral DNA as 8 kb circles. Four malignant tumors showed evidence of integration, and one cervical carcinoma had cloned virus/cell junction fragments. But the paper also states the crucial uncertainty: whether integration of HPV16/18 DNA is a causative event for malignant transformation or a consequence of tumor-cell DNA rearrangement remained open.

[[sources/matsukura-kanda-yoshikawa-1986-hpv16-integrated-cervical-carcinoma]] answers a direct viral-cell junction structure question in one carcinoma. The authors cloned HPV16 DNA with flanking cellular DNA and found a deletion spanning most of E1 and all of E2 while retaining E6/E7. This is a strong structural endpoint, but one carcinoma's integration structure does not establish necessity, sufficiency, or natural chronology.

[[sources/cullen-reid-campion-lorincz-1991-physical-state-hpv-cervical-neoplasm]] adds a larger cross-sectional boundary. Integration was documented in 56 of 69 invasive cancers, but 13 invasive cancers had only episomal HPV DNA. All 12 latent HPV infections and all 39 CIN1 specimens were episomal only, and only 3 of 61 CIN2-CIN3 specimens had mixed episomal/integrated HPV DNA. This supports an invasion-associated integration-frequency claim, while blocking the overclaim that detectable integration is universally required for invasive cancer.

The current manuscript conclusion is therefore strict. HPV/cervical cancer now has a started molecular and physical-state ladder: tumor-DNA detection, type specificity, integration-compatible patterns, and viral-cell junction structure. It does not yet have full-text source pages here for the 1999 "necessary cause" paper, prospective infection/persistence/progression cohorts, sufficiency, cofactor structure, or intervention endpoints. Those claims remain unresolved at manuscript strength.

### 5.10 Kaposi sarcoma, HHV-8, and the evidence ladder problem

The Kaposi sarcoma branch now has its own HHV-8 ladder page: [[synthesis/kaposi-sarcoma-hhv8-evidence-ladder]]. Like the HPV/cervical-cancer branch, this is primarily an association-heavy molecular and serologic ladder rather than a human challenge or animal tumor-induction ladder.

[[sources/chang-cesarman-pessin-1994-herpesvirus-like-dna-aids-kaposi]] reports discovery of novel herpesvirus-like DNA sequences in AIDS-associated Kaposi sarcoma tissue using representational difference analysis, Southern blotting, PCR, cloning, and sequencing. KS330233 was detected in 25 of 27 AIDS-KS tissues; the two negative specimens had technical limitations described by the paper. Six of 39 non-KS AIDS lymph node or lymphoma samples were positive, while the non-AIDS and other control groups tested were negative by the reported PCR endpoint. This supports molecular discovery and lesion association, not causal closure.

[[sources/moore-chang-1995-herpesvirus-like-dna-kaposi-hiv-nonhiv]] adds a cross-status lesion-detection rung. In a blinded PCR study, KS330233 was detected in 20 of 21 Kaposi sarcoma lesion samples: 10 of 11 AIDS-associated KS, 6 of 6 classic KS, and 4 of 4 KS cases in HIV-seronegative homosexual men. Three of 14 uninvolved-skin samples from KS patients were positive. This weakens the idea that the marker was only an AIDS-KS artifact, but it does not prove temporal precedence or tumor causation.

[[sources/oneill-henson-ghorbani-1996-herpesvirus-like-sequences-kaposi-lesions]] adds a small lesion-versus-normal tissue boundary. All four fresh KS biopsies and all three paraffin-embedded KS tissue samples were PCR-positive for KSHV-like sequences, while matched normal tissue and an HIV-seronegative control were negative under the reported PCR conditions. The paper is useful because it also preserves a negative boundary: viral particles had not been reported by electron microscopy in KS tissue samples at that time.

[[sources/moore-gao-dominguez-1996-primary-characterization-kshv]] raises the rung from sequence fragments to viral characterization. A 20.7 kb KS-derived genomic clone contained ORFs with gammaherpesvirus homology; infected lymphoma-derived cell lines carried KSHV genome; limited serology showed higher infected-cell-line IFA titers in AIDS-KS sera than in HIV/AIDS controls after EBV-reactive antibodies were adsorbed. The same paper also preserves the boundary that replication-competent virions were not identified in infected cell lines.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is therefore narrow. HHV-8/Kaposi sarcoma now has molecular discovery, lesion association, cross-status detection, viral characterization, infected-cell-line, and limited-serology rungs in the vault. It does not yet have full-text source pages here for broader seroepidemiology, prospective timing, ordinary transmission route, necessity, sufficiency, immune-cofactor structure, or direct tumor initiation.

### 5.11 Infectious mononucleosis, EBV, and the evidence ladder problem

The infectious mononucleosis branch now has its own EBV ladder page: [[synthesis/infectious-mononucleosis-ebv-evidence-ladder]]. This row is important because it has stronger prospective timing evidence than the tumor-virus rows, but still needs endpoint discipline.

[[sources/klein-pearson-henle-1968-ebv-membrane-immunofluorescence-im]] reports that infectious-mononucleosis-derived blastoid cultures with detectable EBV antigen shared membrane-immunofluorescence behavior with Burkitt tumor cell lines in the tested system. Serial IM sera generally moved from absent or insignificant membrane-immunofluorescence reactivity before illness or early in illness to stronger reactivity after onset. This supports an EBV-linked immunofluorescence and immune-response rung, not purified-virus clinical causation.

[[sources/nye-lambert-1973-ebv-antibody-cases-contacts-infectious-mononucleosis]] adds a general-population case/contact rung. All 147 Paul-Bunnell-positive IM patients possessed or developed EBV antibody, and 93 of 147 showed significant EBV antibody change. Among 306 followed contacts, 21 showed evidence of recent EBV infection or reinfection, but only five developed Paul-Bunnell-positive IM. The same paper preserves an important syndrome boundary: among 13 persistently Paul-Bunnell-negative glandular-fever-like cases, four showed recent EBV infection, two probable CMV mononucleosis, and several no recent EBV or CMV evidence.

[[sources/hallee-evans-niederman-1974-west-point-infectious-mononucleosis-ebv]] adds the strongest current prospective rung. In 1401 entering West Point cadets followed over four years, clinical IM occurred in initially EBV-antibody-negative cadets who seroconverted. Of 201 EBV infections among initially susceptible cadets, 53 were definite heterophile-positive clinical IM, 16 were suspected IM, and 132 had no known IM. No later clinical IM occurred in 890 healthy cadets who had EBV antibody on entry, except two already ill at first bleeding. But the paper also blocks a simple transmission story: susceptible roommates of known clinical IM cases did not have higher EBV infection rates than susceptible cadets not so exposed.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that EBV/infectious mononucleosis now has immunofluorescence, EBV antibody, case/contact serology, prospective seroconversion, EBV-specific IgM, and clinical IM rungs. It does not yet reconstruct purified-virus sufficiency, route-specific transmission, infectious dose, or all mononucleosis-like illness mechanisms.

### 5.12 Measles, measles virus, and the evidence ladder problem

The measles branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/measles-measles-virus-evidence-ladder]]. It is a useful case because the early primary record contains both negative and positive experimental rungs.

[[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]] and [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]] preserve the negative blood-inoculation boundary. In the extracted monkey series, blood from early measles cases did not produce recognizable measles-like illness in three rhesus monkeys, and two reinjected monkeys also remained negative. In the human-volunteer paper, blood from measles patients did not produce clinical measles in screened adult volunteers. Those papers answer a blood-route question. They do not settle respiratory transmission.

[[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]] answers a different question. Nasopharyngeal washings from early measles cases, including filtered washings in selected experiments, produced a measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys under intratracheal or mucosal inoculation conditions, and tissue emulsions could carry activity through monkey passages. [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]] defines the model endpoints: incubation, conjunctivitis, enanthem, exanthem, leukopenia, and tissue lesions, while also noting that fever was variable and rhinitis/bronchitis were absent.

[[sources/enders-peebles-1954-cytopathogenic-agents-measles-tissue-culture]] adds the tissue-culture rung. Patient blood or throat washings yielded cytopathogenic agents in human or simian renal-cell cultures; convalescent measles sera neutralized cytopathogenicity and reacted with infected-culture antigen. The paper is also important because it states its own remaining proof gap: producing measles in monkey and man with sufficiently passaged tissue-culture material and recovering the virus. [[sources/rapp-1964-plaque-differentiation-measles-virus]] later strengthens the culture-assay rung by measuring plaque formation, giant-cell cytopathic effect, antigen timing, and virulent-versus-attenuated strain replication in BSC-1 cells.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is strict. Measles now has failed blood-inoculation, respiratory-material monkey challenge, pathology definition, tissue-culture isolation, serology, and plaque-replication rungs. These should not be collapsed into a single proof sentence, and the negative blood-inoculation papers should not be overread as a failure of respiratory-material or tissue-culture evidence.

### 5.13 Smallpox, variola virus, and the evidence ladder problem

The smallpox branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/smallpox-variola-virus-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is useful because it includes positive animal transmission evidence and negative host/reservoir boundaries in the same disease row.

[[sources/brinckerhoff-tyzzer-1906-experimental-variola-monkeys]] is the early nonhuman-primate variolation anchor. It reports that human variola material produced local lesions, fever, lymph-node reaction, and sometimes generalized exanthem in macaques under deliberate inoculation. The route mattered: skin inoculation produced generalized exanthem far more often than mucous-membrane inoculation, and tracheal experiments included technical confounding from operation-wound infection. This supports artificial variolation and route-comparison endpoints, not ordinary human smallpox transmission.

[[sources/nelson-1939-variola-virus-nasal-instillation-mice]] and [[sources/nelson-1940-variola-vaccinia-survival-mouse-lungs]] add mouse host-boundary evidence. Nasal variola instillation in mice did not produce ordinary clinical smallpox or upper-airway lesions, though virus was recovered from lungs for several days and lung pathology developed. Prior variola exposure changed later lung recovery kinetics and partly reduced vaccinia severity, but it did not make mice a faithful smallpox model.

[[sources/nelson-1943-stability-variola-virus-embryonated-eggs]] and [[sources/oya-1956-vaccinia-variola-one-day-hen-egg-growth]] add laboratory propagation rungs. Variola could be propagated in embryonated eggs and one-day fertile hen eggs while remaining distinguishable from vaccinia under the tested endpoints. These papers support growth and stability in defined systems, not clinical disease reproduction.

[[sources/noble-rich-1969-smallpox-contact-aerosol-macaca-irus]] is the strongest current transmission rung for this row. Experimentally infected *Macaca irus* transmitted variola to other monkeys by contact and by an aerosol-chamber arrangement. But the paper also preserves model limits: the monkey disease was mild, its lesion distribution differed from human smallpox, and serial passage died out. [[sources/noble-1970-simian-reservoir-smallpox-monkeys]] then blocks a different leap by finding little support for naturally maintained simian smallpox in tested monkey species and sera.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is layered. Smallpox/variola now has animal variolation, mouse route-boundary, egg propagation, monkey contact/aerosol transmission, and simian-reservoir boundary rungs. These should not be collapsed into a single proof sentence, and the negative mouse/reservoir findings should not be overread as refuting human smallpox causation.

### 5.14 Hepatitis A, hepatitis A virus, and the evidence ladder problem

The hepatitis A branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is deliberately partial: it strengthens the CR326 marmoset, characterization, and cell-culture chain, while leaving canonical acute-stool IEM and chimpanzee papers as full-text acquisition gaps.

[[sources/provost-ittensohn-villarejos-1973-cr326-hepatitis-a-neutralization]] links marmoset-propagated CR326 to human hepatitis A by a neutralization endpoint. Convalescent sera from hepatitis A cases, including coded MS-1 hepatitis A subjects, neutralized CR326 infectivity in marmosets more than pre-illness sera, while hepatitis B cases did not show the same hepatitis-A-specific rise. Human immune globulin also neutralized CR326, and marmosets given neutralized virus remained susceptible to later challenge while infected animals were immune to reinfection. This supports a marmoset-infectivity and human-serology identity rung.

[[sources/provost-wolanski-miller-1975-cr326-hepatitis-a-dimensions]] adds particle characterization. Purified CR326 appeared as 27 nm spherical particles with main CsCl density around 1.34, was seen in infected marmoset liver cytoplasm, reacted with hepatitis A antisera by immune electron microscopy, and showed acid/ether/heat-stability boundaries consistent with an enterovirus-like agent. This supports morphology, tissue localization, density, and stability endpoints.

[[sources/provost-hilleman-1979-human-hepatitis-a-cell-culture]] adds the cell-culture rung. CR326 was propagated in marmoset liver explant cultures and FRhK6 fetal rhesus kidney cells without cytopathic effect; identity was supported by immunofluorescence blocking, serum neutralization, immune adherence, radioimmunoassay, immune electron microscopy, and marmoset inoculation. This is a strong propagation endpoint, but it remains nonhuman-primate-adapted and noncytopathic.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is narrow. Hepatitis A/HAV now has marmoset neutralization, particle characterization, tissue localization, and cell-culture propagation rungs in the vault. It does not yet have full-text source pages here for the 1973 Science acute-stool IEM detection paper or the 1975-1977 chimpanzee challenge/tissue-shedding papers. Those claims remain "we do not know from full text in hand."

### 5.15 SARS, SARS-CoV, and the evidence ladder problem

The SARS branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/sars-sars-cov-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is useful because the evidence chain includes both patient association/isolation and a high-rung nonhuman-primate challenge experiment, while still preserving route and comparator boundaries.

[[sources/peiris-2003-coronavirus-possible-cause-sars]] is the Hong Kong patient-series anchor. It reports coronavirus isolation from two SARS patients, coronavirus-compatible electron microscopy, a 646 bp coronavirus polymerase sequence, RT-PCR detection in nasopharyngeal and fecal samples, paired-serum antibody rises, and negative tested controls. This supports association, isolation, sequence, serology, and diagnostic endpoints.

[[sources/drosten-2003-novel-coronavirus-sars-patients]] is an independent virus-identification anchor. It reports random-amplification sequence fragments, linkage to a CDC coronavirus sequence, specific PCR assay development, viral RNA in multiple specimen types, high sputum RNA levels, and early serologic reactivity. It also preserves a useful method boundary: antibody testing used primary infected-culture antigen, so the authors noted that plaque-purified virus or recombinant antigen would be needed to exclude a co-isolated unknown antigen explanation fully.

[[sources/kuiken-2003-newly-discovered-coronavirus-primary-cause-sars]] supplies the strongest experimental rung. In a multicentre set, SARS-CoV infection was diagnosed in 329 of 436 SARS patients. Four cynomolgus macaques were then inoculated with 1 x 10^6 TCID50 SARS-CoV by combined intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival routes and necropsied on day 6. Three of four developed diffuse alveolar damage, virus was detected and localized in pneumonic lung, and virus was re-isolated from selected respiratory or tissue samples.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is strong but bounded. SARS/SARS-CoV now has patient association, culture/isolation, molecular detection, serology, multicentre comparison, macaque challenge, pathology, viral localization, and partial re-isolation rungs. It should not be collapsed into a route-free proof sentence. The macaque experiment supports model-specific pathogenicity under high-dose combined artificial exposure; it does not by itself define ordinary human natural-route dose, and it lacked a sham-inoculated macaque comparator.

### 5.16 MERS, MERS-CoV, and the evidence ladder problem

The MERS branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/mers-mers-cov-evidence-ladder]]. This pass is useful because it separates one index-case isolation paper from two different animal-model rungs: rhesus-macaque lower-respiratory disease and dromedary-camel shedding ecology.

[[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] is the index-case anchor. It reports recovery of a novel lineage C betacoronavirus from admission sputum of a fatal pneumonia case, cytopathic effect in Vero and LLC-MK2 cells, coronavirus RT-PCR products, a nearly full genome sequence, patient-serum reactivity to infected cells, and negative testing of 2400 historical hospital sera. The limitation is equally important: the paper concerns one patient, with bacterial isolates, no autopsy, no contact serology, and no experimental disease reproduction.

[[sources/dewit-2013-mers-cov-rhesus-macaque-lower-respiratory-infection]] supplies the nonhuman-primate challenge rung. Six rhesus macaques received 7 x 10^6 TCID50 HCoV-EMC/2012 by combined intratracheal, ocular, oral, and intranasal routes. The animals developed transient clinical and hematologic changes, respiratory viral RNA, subgenomic mRNA, pneumocyte localization, and lung lesions. That supports model-specific lower-respiratory infection, not ordinary human route, dose, severe disease, or sham-controlled symptom attribution.

[[sources/adney-2014-mers-cov-dromedary-camel-shedding]] supplies a reservoir-ecology rung. Three seronegative male dromedary camels inoculated by combined intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival routes developed mild rhinorrhea, shed infectious virus nasally, showed respiratory-epithelium antigen, and seroconverted. This supports camel upper-respiratory replication and shedding, but not human clinical MERS causation or route-specific camel-to-human transmission.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that MERS/MERS-CoV now has index-case isolation, culture, sequencing, serology, rhesus-macaque challenge, and camel-shedding rungs. It should not be collapsed into a single proof sentence. Human case isolation, macaque pathogenicity, and camel ecology answer different questions.

### 5.17 Hand, foot, and mouth disease, enteroviruses, and the evidence ladder problem

The HFMD branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/hand-foot-mouth-disease-enteroviruses-evidence-ladder]]. This pass matters because HFMD is a clinical syndrome with at least two important enterovirus attribution tracks in the current full-text set: Coxsackie A16 and EV71.

[[sources/alsop-flewett-foster-1960-hand-foot-mouth-disease-birmingham]] is an early outbreak and lesion-isolation anchor. It reports a mild Birmingham outbreak with oral, hand, and foot vesicles and Coxsackie A16 recovery from one hand-vesicle sample. This supports a lesion-material isolation endpoint, not universal outbreak attribution.

[[sources/brown-oleary-1974-fluorescent-antibody-hfmd]] adds a serology/contact rung. Coxsackie A16 fluorescent-antibody responses appeared in clinical cases and in some family or school contacts. This supports immune-response and subclinical-infection endpoints, but antibody positivity alone is not lesion-site viral replication or transmission direction.

[[sources/ishimaru-1980-hand-foot-mouth-enterovirus-71-cns-complications]] supplies the EV71 outbreak rung. It links EV71 isolation/typing with Japanese HFMD outbreaks and reports frequent CNS complications. That is a stronger endpoint than generic syndrome description, but it still does not define the mechanism of neuroinvasion or prove that every neurologic event was directly virus-mediated.

[[sources/li-2014-tree-shrew-coxsackievirus-a16-experimental-infection]] supplies a model-specific animal rung. Nasal-spray Coxsackie A16 exposure in tree shrews produced fever/inflammatory markers, viral RNA or antigen in multiple tissues, and fecal shedding, while typical human HFMD vesicles were not seen. That supports model infection and tissue distribution, not ordinary pediatric lesion pathogenesis.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that HFMD now has Coxsackie A16 lesion-isolation, Coxsackie A16 serology, EV71 outbreak/CNS association, and tree-shrew animal-model rungs. Those endpoints should not be compressed into a claim that every HFMD presentation, every neurologic complication, or every transmission route has been settled.

### 5.18 Rotavirus, norovirus, and gastroenteritis endpoint compression

The gastroenteritis rows now have two separate ladder pages: [[synthesis/rotavirus-gastroenteritis-evidence-ladder]] and [[synthesis/norovirus-gastroenteritis-outbreaks-evidence-ladder]]. They should remain separate because their evidentiary anchors differ.

For rotavirus, [[sources/davidson-1975-rotavirus-immunofluorescence-duodenal-mucosa]] supports pediatric duodenal villus antigen localization in acute enteritis, [[sources/mccormack-1982-clinical-features-rotavirus-gastroenteritis]] supports hospitalized-child stool detection and control negativity, [[sources/kapikian-1983-oral-human-rotavirus-volunteers]] supports adult oral-challenge infection, diarrhea, shedding, and serologic response, and [[sources/wyatt-1983-direct-isolation-human-rotaviruses-cell-culture-serotypes]] supports culture/isolation and serotyping from fecal specimens. The strongest causal rung is the human oral challenge, but it is an adult volunteer protocol rather than infant natural-transmission evidence.

For norovirus, [[sources/kapikian-1972-immune-electron-microscopy-norwalk-agent]] supports Norwalk-agent particle visualization and serology, [[sources/atmar-2008-norwalk-virus-shedding-experimental-human-infection]] supports adult oral challenge with defined gastroenteritis and shedding, [[sources/arness-2000-norwalk-like-gastroenteritis-army-trainees]] supports outbreak attribution by epidemiology and stool testing, and [[sources/ettayebi-2016-human-norovirus-replication-enteroids]] supports replication in human intestinal enteroids. Here the key boundary is that PCR-positive shedding and cell replication should not be treated as identical to infectious shedding or symptom causation.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that both rows have strong multi-rung attribution evidence, including human challenge anchors. The scientism risk is not absence of evidence; it is compressing different gastroenteritis endpoints into one broad "virus causes all such disease" sentence.

### 5.19 Bronchiolitis, RSV, and host-endpoint boundaries

The RSV branch now has its own ladder page: [[synthesis/bronchiolitis-rsv-evidence-ladder]]. This pass shows a strong respiratory-virus attribution chain, but it also shows why bronchiolitis needs endpoint discipline.

[[sources/chanock-roizman-myers-1957-cca-rsv-isolation-characterization]] supports early recovery and characterization of CCA-related syncytium-forming agents from infants with respiratory illness. [[sources/chanock-finberg-1957-cca-rsv-epidemiology-infants]] adds early infection-marker epidemiology but also records control and nosocomial-background limits. Those papers are important for naming the agent and early human association, not for direct bronchiolitis causation closure.

[[sources/cradock-watson-1971-rapid-diagnosis-rsv-immunofluorescence]] supplies a much stronger infant diagnostic rung: in hospitalized bronchiolitis infants, RSV immunofluorescence in nasopharyngeal secretions aligned closely with culture confirmation. But this remains a diagnostic association endpoint; it does not by itself prove lower-airway pathogenesis or sufficiency.

[[sources/prince-1978-pathogenesis-rsv-infection-cotton-rats]] supplies model pathology: cotton rats infected intranasally with RSV had recoverable virus, antigen localization, and mild proliferative bronchiolitis-like pathology. [[sources/hall-1981-infectivity-rsv-various-routes-inoculation]] supplies adult human route evidence: nasal and ocular instillation infected adults under the tested protocol. Neither endpoint is infant bronchiolitis itself.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is that RSV/bronchiolitis has early isolation, early serology/epidemiology, diagnostic infant association, cotton-rat pathology, and adult route-inoculation rungs. The evidence is substantial, but host, age, route, and endpoint boundaries should remain visible.

### 5.20 HSV, VZV, shedding, and reactivation endpoints

The last cleanup pass added three herpes-family ladder pages: [[synthesis/cold-sores-hsv1-evidence-ladder]], [[synthesis/genital-herpes-hsv-evidence-ladder]], and [[synthesis/shingles-vzv-reactivation-evidence-ladder]].

For cold sores, the current source pages support infectious HSV recovery from oral specimens, UV-reactivation PCR/culture findings, and frequent asymptomatic HSV-1 DNA shedding. For genital herpes, the current source pages support lesion culture, asymptomatic reactivation, HSV-2 daily DNA shedding, genital HSV-1 first-year shedding, and a case-level asymptomatic-shedding transmission chain. For shingles, the current source pages support varicella/zoster cultural similarity, ganglionic VZV detection, neuronal localization, latent transcription, and active ganglionitis.

The defensible manuscript conclusion is narrow: these rows now have evidence ladders, but recovery, PCR shedding, case-level transmission, ganglionic DNA, transcripts, and clinical reactivation triggers are different endpoints.

### 5.21 Failed-attempt categories differ by disease

The failed-attempt register shows that different disease pairs require different negative-result categories [[synthesis/failed-virus-causation-attempts-register]].

- Influenza has a primary pre-1933 human challenge failure source.
- Polio has a tissue-recovery and route-boundary source.
- Mumps has control, diagnosis, and study-design boundaries rather than a broad failed-causation series.
- Measles has primary blood-inoculation failures in humans and monkeys, plus positive artificial respiratory-material monkey inoculation and later tissue-culture evidence; the failure category is sample/route boundary.
- Smallpox has mouse respiratory-route and simian-reservoir boundaries beside positive nonhuman-primate inoculation and monkey contact/aerosol transmission evidence; the failure category is host/route/reservoir boundary.
- Rubella has acute-isolation and monkey-inoculation source pages, but congenital-rubella and experimental-human rubella remain acquisition gaps.
- Chickenpox/VZV has lesion-culture and fatal-case recovery source pages, but zoster latency/reactivation remains a separate unresolved chain.
- Hepatitis B has syndrome-split and molecular-clone chimpanzee challenge source pages, but the Australia-antigen particle and early chimpanzee-passage rungs remain acquisition gaps.
- Hepatitis C has molecular-identification, antibody-assay, transfusion-serology, and molecular-clone source pages, but the 1984 non-A non-B chimpanzee passage and 1999 transmissible-virion clone papers remain acquisition gaps.
- Hepatitis A has CR326 marmoset neutralization, particle-characterization, and cell-culture source pages, but acute-stool IEM and chimpanzee challenge/tissue-shedding rungs remain acquisition gaps.
- MERS has index-case isolation, macaque challenge, and camel-shedding source pages, but ordinary human natural-route causation and route-specific transmission remain separate endpoints.
- HFMD has Coxsackie A16 lesion-isolation and serology source pages plus EV71 outbreak/CNS and tree-shrew model source pages, but lesion recovery, serology, outbreak association, neurologic complications, and animal-model infection remain different endpoints.
- Rotavirus has pediatric mucosal, clinical-association, adult oral-challenge, and cell-culture source pages, but adult challenge and infant disease burden remain separate endpoints.
- Norovirus has prototype particle, adult challenge, outbreak, and enteroid-replication source pages, but PCR shedding, infectious shedding, outbreak source, and clinical disease remain separate endpoints.
- RSV has early isolation, serology/epidemiology, diagnostic bronchiolitis association, cotton-rat pathology, and adult route-inoculation source pages, but infant lower-airway causation is not identical to any one of those rungs.
- HSV/VZV rows now have shedding, lesion-culture, transmission-case, latency, and reactivation source pages, but PCR positivity, culture recovery, transmission probability, and reactivation trigger are separate endpoints.
- SARS has patient association, isolation, RT-PCR, serology, macaque challenge, pathology, viral localization, and partial re-isolation source pages; the boundary is artificial combined-route animal challenge without a sham-inoculated macaque group, not a failed SARS causation series.
- Cervical cancer/HPV has tumor-DNA, HPV type, integration, and viral-cell junction source pages, but prospective progression, necessity, sufficiency, cofactor, and intervention rungs remain unresolved here.
- Kaposi sarcoma/HHV-8 has molecular discovery, lesion PCR, cross-status detection, viral characterization, and limited serology source pages, but broader seroepidemiology, prospective timing, transmission, necessity, sufficiency, and tumor-initiation rungs remain unresolved here.
- Infectious mononucleosis/EBV has immunofluorescence, case/contact serology, prospective seroconversion, EBV-specific IgM, and clinical IM source pages, but purified-virus challenge, route-specific transmission, infectious dose, and all mono-like illness mechanisms remain unresolved here.
- Rabies has route and species-boundary candidates, especially oral, intestinal, and aerosol exposure, but those do not challenge bite-route rabies causation.
- AIDS/HIV does not have a Rosenau-style failed challenge record in the current vault; the relevant boundary categories are animal-model nonprogression, long-term nonprogression, and HIV-negative CD4/opportunistic-infection syndromes.
- Cervical cancer/HPV does not fit a failed-transmission model; the relevant categories are HPV-negative or apparently HPV-negative tumors, assay false negatives, and infection without progression.
- Kaposi sarcoma/HHV-8 does not fit a failed-transmission model in the current source set; the relevant categories are molecular association without causal closure, lesion DNA without infectious-virus recovery, infected-cell-line characterization without KS tumor induction, and serology without necessity or sufficiency.
- Infectious mononucleosis/EBV has stronger prospective timing evidence than many rows, but its boundary categories are infection without clinical IM, mono-like illness with non-EBV or unresolved etiologies, and contact/roommate results that do not define a simple route-specific transmission model.

This is why one global "failed causation" template is not scientific. The endpoint and failure mode must match the claim.

## 6. AIDS, HIV, AZT, and the Discipline of Historical Controversy

### 6.1 The Gutknecht letter as historical document, not biomedical evidence

[[sources/congressman-questions-aids-in-letter]] is a March 24, 1995 letter from Representative Gil Gutknecht to Anthony Fauci. The vault treats it as a Tier 1 historical document for what was being asked politically in 1995, not as Tier 1 biomedical evidence. It packages dissent claims about HIV/AIDS causation, transmission, long latency, HIV-negative AIDS, Kaposi's sarcoma, AZT toxicity, and alternative-hypothesis funding.

The letter is useful because it maps controversy. It is not sufficient because it asks questions, cites secondhand claims, and frames anomalies without reconstructing direct experiments.

### 6.2 Direct-study follow-up weakens the broad dissent claim

[[synthesis/gutknecht-letter-evidence-check]] and [[synthesis/gutknecht-letter-questions-7-9]] are among the notebook's most developed source audits. They do not close every HIV/AIDS question. But they do reject a broad version of the dissent claim that recreational drugs or AZT can explain AIDS generally.

The direct-study register includes non-recreational exposure contexts:

- transfusion-associated cohorts [[sources/donegan-1990-risk-of-aids-after-transfusion-of-hiv-positive-blood]], [[sources/ward-1989-natural-history-of-transfusion-associated-hiv-infection]]
- hemophilia and blood-product cohorts [[sources/darby-1989-aids-and-excess-mortality-in-uk-haemophiliacs]], [[sources/darby-1995-mortality-before-and-after-hiv-infection-in-uk-haemophiliacs]], [[sources/simmonds-1991-determinants-of-hiv-disease-progression-in-edinburgh-haemophilia-cohort]], [[sources/schinaia-1994-progression-to-aids-in-italian-hiv-positive-hemophiliacs]]
- pediatric and perinatal cohorts [[sources/european-collaborative-study-1991-children-born-to-women-with-hiv-1-infection]], [[sources/european-collaborative-study-1994-natural-history-of-vertically-acquired-hiv-1]], [[sources/abrams-1998-hiv-load-early-in-life-and-infant-disease-progression]]
- occupational seroconversion [[sources/cardo-1997-occupational-hiv-seroconversion-after-percutaneous-exposure]]

The chronology register includes AIDS-defining case records before AZT marketing approval, including early MMWR and clinical case records [[sources/cdc-1981-pneumocystis-pneumonia-los-angeles]], [[sources/cdc-1981-kaposis-sarcoma-and-pneumocystis-pneumonia]], [[sources/gottlieb-1981-pneumocystis-carinii-pneumonia-and-mucosal-candidiasis]], [[sources/cdc-1982-update-on-acquired-immune-deficiency-syndrome-united-states]], [[sources/cdc-1982-aids-among-patients-with-hemophilia-a]], plus the FDA 1987 Retrovir approval chronology [[sources/fda-1987-zidovudine-retrovir-approval-record]].

The vault conclusion is narrow: these records make a general AZT-origin explanation chronologically untenable and make a recreational-drug-only explanation inadequate for AIDS generally. They do not prove that cofactors never matter. They do not complete a full HIV causation dossier. They do not answer all questions about test specificity, immune pathogenesis, long-term nonprogression, Kaposi's sarcoma distribution, or funding history [[synthesis/gutknecht-letter-evidence-check]].

### 6.3 AZT trial evidence is mixed, not slogan-friendly

The AZT trial record is exactly the kind of case where the notebook's endpoint discipline matters. The early pivotal efficacy trial [[sources/fischl-1987-efficacy-of-azidothymidine-in-aids-and-arc]] was a double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 282 patients with AIDS or advanced AIDS-related complex. It reported fewer deaths and opportunistic infections in the zidovudine group over short follow-up. That supports a narrow short-term efficacy claim in a selected advanced-disease population.

But companion and later sources preserve toxicity, timing, resistance, and comparator limits. [[sources/richman-1987-toxicity-of-azidothymidine-in-aids-and-arc]] records hematologic toxicity from the same early trial context. [[sources/fischl-1992-safety-and-toxicity-of-zidovudine-in-actg-019]] records dose-related severe anemia and neutropenia in an asymptomatic trial context. [[sources/concorde-1994-immediate-versus-deferred-zidovudine]] complicates immediate-versus-deferred timing. Later comparator trials such as [[sources/delta-1996-zidovudine-combinations-versus-zidovudine-alone]] and [[sources/actg-175-1996-zidovudine-monotherapy-versus-combination-nucleosides]] show the shift away from simple monotherapy framing.

The vault's current conclusion is therefore not "AZT was safe and mandatory" or "AZT had no direct benefit." It is:

- some trials showed measurable benefit in defined populations and endpoints
- some trials showed meaningful toxicity
- timing, dose, comparator, resistance, and year matter
- broad ethical claims must name the population, comparator, dose, endpoint, toxicity signal, and historical treatment context

## 7. COVID-19 Protocols and the Risk of Policy-Speed Inference

[[synthesis/covid-early-intubation-noninvasive-ventilation-lens-summary]] tracks a different kind of scientism risk: protocol confidence during an emergency. The early COVID-19 respiratory-support dispute involved whether hospitals should avoid noninvasive respiratory support because of aerosol concerns and proceed earlier to intubation, or whether high-flow nasal cannula and noninvasive ventilation could prevent unnecessary invasive ventilation.

The retrieved evidence is mixed and mostly observational. Several meta-analyses found no clear mortality difference between early and late intubation. Some studies suggested a small or trivial mortality advantage for early intubation; others found no survival improvement or strong confounding by baseline severity. HFNC repeatedly appears as a reasonable strategy that may reduce intubation without clear mortality penalty. NIV is more conflicted, including registry evidence associating NIV with higher ICU mortality than other initial strategies.

This is not a settled protocol conclusion. It is a case study in how emergency practice can harden around incomplete evidence. The vault's proper conclusion is that early pandemic protocol choices should be reconstructed endpoint by endpoint: mortality, intubation rate, ICU length of stay, ventilation duration, nosocomial transmission, aerosol dispersion, and confounding by disease severity.

## 8. Commentary Sources: Useful Leads, Not Conclusions

The vault contains many Tier 3 sources. These are not useless. They often identify disputes, cite papers, preserve dissent rhetoric, and force useful questions. But they cannot carry factual conclusions alone.

Examples include:

- [[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]], the seed transcript that sets the research agenda but does not establish biomedical facts.
- [[sources/the-virus-cult-a-religion-built-on-fear-not-science]], a polemical no-virus essay that maps methodological objections but bundles technical, legal, historical, and policy claims.
- [[sources/the-virus-paradigm-assumptions-lies-and-the-foundation-of-modern-medicine]], a long-form advocacy text useful for locating claims about isolation, controls, FOI responses, and cell culture, but not direct proof.
- [[sources/an-inquiry-into-the-logical-basis-of-the-germ-theory]], a commentary source arguing that germ theory rests on invalid causal reasoning, useful as a map of historical objections but not sufficient to refute germ theory.
- [[sources/the-germ-hypothesis-part-2]], a Mike Stone essay useful because it points to specific historical experiments and standards, but still requiring primary-source checking.
- [[sources/vaccine-makers-do-not-use-placebos-to-test-the-safety-of-their-vaccines-for-kids]], which raised a real comparator issue but required the separate pediatric trial-record audit before the vault could state narrower findings.

The rule is: a Tier 3 source can open a question. It cannot close it.

## Conclusion

The Scientism notebook is not best understood as an anti-science notebook. It is an anti-overclaiming notebook.

Its central discipline is to force every claim back down to the study that could actually support it. What was the subject? What was done? What was the comparator? What endpoint was measured? What happened? What limitation remains? Has it replicated? What larger conclusion is being drawn afterward?

That procedure protects against institutional scientism and dissenting scientism alike. It prevents a PCR result from becoming a disease-causation proof by rhetoric. It prevents a negative challenge experiment from becoming a universal refutation by rhetoric. It prevents a disease-specific vaccine benefit from becoming overall-health proof by rhetoric. It prevents a toxicity signal from becoming a total condemnation by rhetoric. It prevents a historical document from becoming biomedical proof by rhetoric.

The notebook's voice should therefore remain strict, narrow, and unsentimental:

We know what the direct experiment can answer.

We do not know what no direct experiment has yet answered.

And when a source moves from one to the other without building the bridge, the vault should name the move as an inferential leap.
