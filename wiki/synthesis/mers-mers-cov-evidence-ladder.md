# MERS to MERS-CoV Evidence Ladder

> Warning: This page is a research synthesis, not medical advice. It does not make diagnosis, exposure-management, infection-control, or treatment recommendations.

## Current answer
The MERS -> MERS-CoV row can move from open status to a started evidence ladder. The full-text set in this pass supports three different rungs:

- [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] supports the index human case isolation, cell-culture cytopathic effect, coronavirus RT-PCR, sequencing, patient-serum reactivity, and control-serum negativity rung.
- [[sources/dewit-2013-mers-cov-rhesus-macaque-lower-respiratory-infection]] supports a rhesus-macaque challenge rung with transient lower-respiratory infection, viral replication markers, pneumocyte localization, and lung pathology.
- [[sources/adney-2014-mers-cov-dromedary-camel-shedding]] supports a dromedary-camel replication and shedding ecology rung, with mild disease, nasal shedding, respiratory-epithelium antigen, and seroconversion after combined-route exposure.

The strongest experimental disease rung in hand is still model-bound. The rhesus macaque paper used six macaques, high-dose combined intratracheal/ocular/oral/intranasal exposure, transient disease, and no sham-inoculated macaque comparator. The camel paper supports reservoir/shedding plausibility, not human clinical disease causation.

## Evidence ladder
| Rung | Current evidence in hand | What it can answer | What it cannot answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human case isolation and characterization | [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] | A novel coronavirus could be isolated and sequenced from the admission sputum of one fatal pneumonia case, with patient-serum reactivity and negative tested historical sera. | Causal sufficiency for the fatal syndrome, role of coinfections, ordinary transmission, or population-level disease spectrum. |
| Cell culture and molecular identification | [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] | The agent produced cytopathic effect on Vero and LLC-MK2 cells and yielded coronavirus RT-PCR products and a nearly full genome. | Disease reproduction or natural-route infection. |
| Serology | [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]] | The patient's late sera reacted with infected cells and 2400 tested control sera were negative. | Timing of infection, serologic response in contacts, or necessity/sufficiency across MERS cases. |
| Rhesus macaque challenge and pathology | [[sources/dewit-2013-mers-cov-rhesus-macaque-lower-respiratory-infection]] | High-dose combined-route MERS-CoV exposure produced transient lower-respiratory infection, viral replication markers, pneumocyte localization, and lung pathology in rhesus macaques. | Ordinary human route, dose, severe or fatal MERS, and sham-controlled symptom attribution. |
| Dromedary-camel replication and shedding | [[sources/adney-2014-mers-cov-dromedary-camel-shedding]] | Experimentally inoculated camels developed mild disease, shed infectious virus nasally, showed respiratory-epithelium antigen, and seroconverted. | Human clinical causation, route-specific camel infection efficiency, or direct camel-to-human transmission under ordinary conditions. |

## Current manuscript rule for MERS and MERS-CoV
MERS should be presented as a multi-rung evidence ladder:

- Zaki et al. 2012 answers the index-case isolation, culture, RT-PCR, genome, and limited serology questions.
- de Wit et al. 2013 answers a model-specific rhesus-macaque lower-respiratory challenge/pathology question.
- Adney et al. 2014 answers a dromedary upper-respiratory replication and shedding question.

The manuscript may say that the current full-text set supports MERS-CoV isolation from a fatal human pneumonia case, model-specific pathogenicity in rhesus macaques, and camel shedding ecology. It should also say that a single index case with bacterial coinfections, a high-dose combined-route macaque model, and a high-dose combined-route camel model do not by themselves settle ordinary human natural-route causation, route-specific transmission, or the complete MERS syndrome.
