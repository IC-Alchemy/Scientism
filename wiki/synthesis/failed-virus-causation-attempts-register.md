# Failed Virus-Causation Attempt Register

> [!warning] Warning
> This page is a register of currently assembled negative-result leads. It does not yet represent a complete literature review for every disease-virus pair in the audit set.

## Why this page exists
The current vault already tracks candidate foundational papers that are commonly treated as supporting virus-disease attribution claims. But a causation audit is incomplete if it records only the experiments later described as successful while ignoring failed, negative, or non-confirmatory attempts.

This matters because a field can look far more causally secure when the remembered story is built from a small number of positive or rhetorically successful experiments while unsuccessful attempts fade from view.

Use [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] to classify failed attempts as failed detection, failed culture, failed transmission, failed challenge, failed intervention, or failed generalization rather than treating all negative results as the same kind of evidence.

## Disease notes
### Poliomyelitis -> poliovirus
Current primary negative-result paper in hand:

- [[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]]

What this currently supports:
- The abstract-level record is no longer title-only. In normal monkeys, recoverable virus was absent from excised nasal mucosa and olfactory bulbs during the first 48 hours after intranasal instillation.
- At 72 hours, just before or with the first temperature rise, very small amounts were found in nasal mucosa and for the first time in olfactory bulbs.
- At 96 hours and later, virus remained detectable in olfactory bulbs while none was detected in nasal mucosa.
- In convalescent immune animals, none was recovered from nasal mucosa or central nervous system at the multiple time points named in the abstract.
- This is enough to show that the paper preserved a nontrivial transmission-route problem rather than presenting ordinary host-to-host transmission as already straightforwardly demonstrated.

What it does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that this paper itself demonstrated failed monkey-to-monkey transmission, because the abstract in hand is centered on tissue-recovery timing rather than a direct transmission series.
- A vault-level claim that natural-route transmission repeatedly failed across the whole literature.
- A vault-level claim that only intracerebral inoculation worked.
- A vault-level claim that "hundreds" of failed experiments were ignored.

Current methodological value:
- This is enough to justify a narrower scientism warning: later causal narratives should not treat this paper as if it had already provided a clean natural-route or host-to-host transmission demonstration when it instead frames transmission as a problem and reports a constrained tissue-recovery sequence rather than a simple transmission proof.

### Clinically diagnosed influenza / influenza-like illness transmission (pre-1933)
Current primary failure-oriented source in hand:

- [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]]
- [[synthesis/influenza-influenza-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What the primary report now supports:
- Nineteen volunteers received a large mixed Pfeiffer bacillus preparation sprayed into nose, eyes, and throat, and Rosenau reports that none took sick.
- Ten volunteers received mixed respiratory material from influenza cases by sprayed exposure; none took sick. Filtered material also produced no results in other volunteers.
- Another ten volunteers received 6 c.c. of similar material with only one hour and forty minutes between collection and exposure; none took sick.
- Nineteen volunteers received direct nose-to-nose and throat-to-throat transfer from donors in early disease; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers received pooled citrated whole blood from febrile influenza cases; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers received filtered upper-respiratory material injected subcutaneously; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers underwent intimate ward exposure with ten influenza patients each, including face-to-face breathing and direct coughing; none took sick in any way.
- Rosenau closes by warning that investigators must be very careful not to draw positive conclusions from such negative results and says they were not quite sure what they knew about the disease.
- The extracted report also notes similar negative results from Dr. McCoy's Goat Island series.

What this source is and is not a boundary for:
- It is a pre-1933 transmission-failure boundary paper in clinically diagnosed influenza or influenza-like illness.
- It is not a direct failed test of influenza-virus identification, because the report predates the 1933 identification framework and works from outbreak diagnosis and transmission attempts rather than later virus-isolation methods.

What remains missing:
- a broader primary reconstruction of the later Portsmouth or related follow-up series beyond the summary preserved in the current extraction
- a fuller historical comparison with later influenza transmission and agent-identification papers; the ladder page now starts this with [[sources/smith-andrewes-laidlaw-1933-virus-obtained-from-influenza-patients]] and [[sources/shope-francis-1936-susceptibility-of-swine-to-human-influenza-virus]]
- a clean explanation, from direct sources, of why these volunteer challenges failed under the tested conditions

Current methodological value:
- Influenza is now a primary-anchored case where negative results can be used in two opposite inferentially risky ways:
- later certainty narratives can underplay repeated negative human challenge findings from the primary report
- later animal-model successes can be used to imply that the earlier human challenge failures no longer need to be explained
- counter-narratives can overextend the same negative findings into a claim that influenza is not infectious at all, even though Rosenau explicitly warned against that move

### Mumps -> mumps virus
Current primary boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/johnson-goodpasture-1934-investigation-etiology-mumps]]
- [[sources/leymaster-ward-1947-direct-isolation-mumps-virus-chick-embryos]]
- [[sources/henle-henle-wendell-rosenberg-1948-induced-mumps-infections-virus-isolation]]
- [[synthesis/mumps-mumps-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Johnson and Goodpasture's normal-saliva control monkeys had only transient injection-related swelling after parotid-duct inoculation and did not develop the delayed parotitis, fever, or leukopenia seen with early-case mumps saliva.
- Leymaster and Ward reported one clinically diagnosed mumps case with negative virus isolation and no paired complement-fixation rise, treating the clinical diagnosis as laboratory-unconfirmed.
- Henle and colleagues reported induced human exposure followed by clinical or serologic infection and saliva virus isolation, but without a sham-exposed control group and without a definitive onward-transmission trace from later cottage cases.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that mumps-virus causation failed.
- A claim that ordinary community transmission is disproven.
- A claim that animal parotid-duct inoculation, chick-embryo isolation, or induced exposure are individually sufficient to settle the full causation ladder.

Current methodological value:
- Mumps is a good example of a disease row where boundary findings are not broad negative results. They instead prevent endpoint compression: artificial route, isolation, serology, induced exposure, and ordinary transmission must remain separate.

### Measles -> measles virus
Current primary boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/sellards-wentworth-1919-monkeys-blood-measles-patients]]
- [[sources/sellards-1919-man-blood-measles-patients]]
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-monkey-susceptibility]]
- [[sources/blake-trask-1921-measles-symptomatology-pathology-monkeys]]
- [[synthesis/measles-measles-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Sellards and Wentworth 1919 reported that blood from early measles cases did not produce recognizable measles-like illness in three rhesus monkeys, including two reinjected monkeys.
- Sellards 1919 reported that blood from measles patients did not produce clinical measles in screened adult human volunteers.
- Blake and Trask 1921 reported that nasopharyngeal washings, including filtered washings, could produce a measles-like syndrome in rhesus monkeys, and that tissue emulsions could passage activity across monkeys.
- The Blake-Trask pathology companion defines the experimental endpoints: incubation, conjunctivitis, enanthem, exanthem, leukopenia, and tissue lesions, while also recording that fever was variable and rhinitis/bronchitis were absent in the monkey model.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that measles-virus causation failed.
- A claim that ordinary human respiratory transmission is fully reconstructed.
- A claim that blood, nasopharyngeal secretions, tissue emulsions, and tissue-culture virus are interchangeable experimental materials.

Current methodological value:
- Measles is now a primary-anchored example where negative blood-inoculation findings and positive respiratory-material monkey findings must be preserved together. The failure category is material and route boundary, not general failure of measles attribution.

### Smallpox -> variola virus
Current primary boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/brinckerhoff-tyzzer-1906-experimental-variola-monkeys]]
- [[sources/nelson-1939-variola-virus-nasal-instillation-mice]]
- [[sources/nelson-1940-variola-vaccinia-survival-mouse-lungs]]
- [[sources/noble-rich-1969-smallpox-contact-aerosol-macaca-irus]]
- [[sources/noble-1970-simian-reservoir-smallpox-monkeys]]
- [[synthesis/smallpox-variola-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Brinckerhoff and Tyzzer 1906 reported that human variola material produced variola-inoculata-like local lesions and sometimes generalized exanthem in monkeys under artificial inoculation, with strong route boundaries: mucous-membrane inoculation produced generalized exanthem much less often than skin inoculation.
- Nelson 1939 reported that nasal variola instillation in mice did not produce ordinary clinical smallpox or upper-airway lesions, while variola could be recovered from lung tissue for several days with lung pathology.
- Nelson 1940 reported that prior variola exposure changed later mouse-lung recovery kinetics and gave incomplete protection against vaccinia disease.
- Noble and Rich 1969 reported contact and aerosol transmission in experimentally infected *Macaca irus*, but the model disease was mild, host-bound, and did not establish a wild monkey reservoir.
- Noble 1970 reported weak evidence for a simian reservoir: most monkey sera were negative, low-titer positives were suspect, and tested New World monkeys did not support Brazilian variola-minor infection under the small experimental set.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that variola causation failed.
- A claim that mouse nasal-route failure refutes human smallpox.
- A claim that monkey variolation, egg propagation, and monkey aerosol transmission are interchangeable endpoints.
- A claim that ordinary human transmission has been fully reconstructed from these sources.

Current methodological value:
- Smallpox is now a primary-anchored example where positive animal inoculation and transmission evidence must be kept beside host-range, route, and reservoir boundaries. The failure category is not general causal failure; it is mouse-host/route boundary and simian-reservoir boundary.

### Rabies -> rabies virus
Current full-text route-boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/charlton-casey-1979-experimental-rabies-skunks-route-exposure]]
- [[sources/shirakawa-2007-cats-fed-rabid-mouse-brains]]

Candidate route-boundary papers (source pages now created; Bell and Moore remains a pointer-only record):

- [[sources/bell-moore-1971-rabies-oral-carnivora]] — Bell and Moore, *Susceptibility of Carnivora to Rabies Virus Administered Orally* (1971; PMID `5104866`). No indexed abstract and no free full text exist, so the page is a pointer preserving the register's characterization (oral feeding of infected carcasses; infection failure in ferrets and cats under those conditions) as unverified at primary-text level. Acquisition lead.
- [[sources/charlton-casey-1979-experimental-rabies-skunks-route-exposure]] — already a full-text-backed page in this vault (the register's earlier "still needing full text" entry for Charlton 1979 was stale; the paper was extracted in the May pass).
- [[sources/davis-rudd-bowen-2007-aerosolized-rabies-bats-mice]] — Davis, Rudd, and Bowen 2007. Abstract-level. Aerosol exposure killed several mice and no bats; all bats seroconverted but showed poor protection against later intramuscular challenge — an aerosol/species boundary plus an antibody-versus-protection endpoint boundary.
- [[sources/shirakawa-2007-cats-fed-rabid-mouse-brains]] — already a full-text-backed page in this vault.
- [[sources/ramsden-johnston-1975-oral-infectivity-rabies-carnivora]] — Ramsden and Johnston 1975. Abstract-level comparator: feeding infected mice produced rabies deaths and neutralizing antibody in both red foxes and striped skunks, so oral infection did occur under those conditions — not a clean failed-attempt entry.

Current vault conclusion:
- Rabies no longer belongs in the "no paper in hand" bucket.
- The useful failure category is route and species boundary, especially oral, intestinal, and aerosol exposure.
- The full-text skunk and cat papers should not be overread as showing that rabies virus fails by bite or parenteral routes. They instead show that exposure route, species, strain, and endpoint matter.

### Hepatitis C -> hepatitis C virus
Current primary boundary source in hand:

- [[sources/yanagi-purcell-emerson-bukh-1997-hcv-infectious-cdna-clone-chimpanzee]]
- [[synthesis/hepatitis-c-hepatitis-c-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What this currently supports:
- Two initial full-length H77 cDNA clones, pH21 and pCV-H11, did not produce detectable HCV replication in the tested chimpanzee after direct intrahepatic RNA-transcript injection.
- A later consensus chimera, pCV-H77C, did produce detectable HCV RNA beginning at week 1, with increasing titers and recovered sequence matching the input clone in the tested regions.
- The negative result is therefore not a failure of HCV attribution in general. It is evidence that clone sequence details and construct viability mattered for the molecular-clone infectivity endpoint.

Current methodological value:
- This is a useful high-rung boundary because it prevents the manuscript from treating "full-length clone" as automatically equivalent to infectious virus. The exact construct, route, host, and replication endpoint have to be named.

### Hepatitis A -> hepatitis A virus
Current primary boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/provost-ittensohn-villarejos-1973-cr326-hepatitis-a-neutralization]]
- [[sources/provost-wolanski-miller-1975-cr326-hepatitis-a-dimensions]]
- [[sources/provost-hilleman-1979-human-hepatitis-a-cell-culture]]
- [[synthesis/hepatitis-a-hepatitis-a-virus-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Provost et al. 1973 linked marmoset-propagated CR326 to human hepatitis A by convalescent-serum and immune-globulin neutralization, while contrasting hepatitis B sera.
- Provost et al. 1975 characterized CR326 as 27 nm particles in infected marmoset material, with density, stability, liver cytoplasmic localization, and hepatitis A serologic identity endpoints.
- Provost and Hilleman 1979 propagated CR326 in marmoset liver explant cultures and FRhK6 cells, with no cytopathic effect and with serologic, antigen, IEM, and marmoset infectivity confirmation.
- Abstract-level pages now also exist for the former acquisition-gap papers: [[sources/feinstone-kapikian-purcell-1973-hepatitis-a-iem]] (acute-stool IEM detection), [[sources/dienstag-1975-experimental-chimpanzee-hepatitis-a]] (controlled chimpanzee challenge with filtrate-negative controls and a housing-exposure arm), [[sources/schulman-1976-hepatitis-a-antigen-liver-bile-stool]] (liver/bile/stool antigen), [[sources/thornton-1977-human-hepatitis-a-chimpanzees]] (multi-source infection consistency), and [[sources/maynard-1975-infectivity-hepatitis-nonhuman-primates]] (marmoset passage and chimpanzee intravenous challenge).

What this does **not** yet support:
- Full-text strength for the acute human detection rung: Feinstone/Kapikian/Purcell 1973 is abstract-level only.
- Full-text strength for the chimpanzee challenge/tissue-shedding rung: the JID/Oxford papers are abstract-level records, not extractions.
- A claim that noncytopathic cell culture is equivalent to human clinical disease reproduction.

Current methodological value:
- Hepatitis A is a source-completion warning. The CR326 chain is strong for marmoset neutralization, characterization, and culture, and the formerly missing rungs now have abstract-level records, but the manuscript must still say "we do not know from full text in hand" for the acute-stool IEM and chimpanzee rungs at full-text strength.

### AIDS -> HIV
The failed-attempt framing is harder here because the major canonical case is not a simple animal or volunteer natural-route transmission program like influenza or early polio.

Candidate boundary papers now have source pages (abstract-level unless noted):

- [[sources/alter-1984-htlv-iii-chimpanzees-plasma]] — Alter et al. 1984. Strong animal-model boundary paper. The endpoint was seroconversion, lymphadenopathy, T4/T8 changes, and impaired blastogenic responses after plasma exposure; the boundary result is infection and immune changes without opportunistic infections or AIDS syndrome in the reported animals.
- [[sources/cdc-1984-experimental-chimpanzee-lav-infection]] — CDC 1984 MMWR historical report, extracted from the CDC archive. It records recoverable LAV, p25 seroconversion, and T4/T8 trends in inoculated chimpanzees with "no clinical illness," and should be paired with the underlying primary papers.
- [[sources/fultz-1987-persistent-chimpanzee-hiv-infection]] — Fultz et al. 1987. Model-boundary candidate. The endpoint is persistent infection and broadening serologic response over two years rather than reproduction of AIDS as a clinical syndrome.
- [[sources/learmont-1992-long-term-symptomless-hiv-single-donor]] — Learmont et al. 1992. Strong progression-boundary paper. Long-term nonprogression (1/6 progressed vs 94/101 other transfusion recipients) in a transfusion-linked cluster, not a failed HIV challenge experiment.
- [[sources/smith-1993-icd-opportunistic-infections-no-hiv]] — Smith et al. 1993. Strong exclusivity-boundary paper. AIDS-defining-type immune phenomena without HIV evidence in 47 investigated cases, with no transmission to investigated contacts, but a distinct HIV-negative syndrome.
- [[sources/spira-1993-idiopathic-cd4-lymphocytopenia]] — Spira et al. 1993 (the register previously attributed this record to "Ho et al."; the first author is Thomas J. Spira). Strong exclusivity-boundary paper. Serology, culture, PCR, and isolation attempts found no HIV/HTLV or transmissible retroviral signal, and the immunologic profile differed from CD4-matched HIV infection.
- [[sources/novembre-1997-aids-chimpanzee-hiv-1]] — Novembre et al. 1997. Comparator paper, not a failed-attempt entry: one chimpanzee developed AIDS-like disease after more than ten years and viral evolution, which prevents overreading the chimpanzee boundary as absolute.

Current vault conclusion:
- The current search did not retrieve a clean primary "challenge failed to cause AIDS" paper comparable to Rosenau's influenza series.
- The better negative-result taxonomy involves animal-model nonprogression, long-term nonprogression, and HIV-negative CD4/opportunistic-infection boundary cases.

### SARS -> SARS-CoV
Current primary endpoint-boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/peiris-2003-coronavirus-possible-cause-sars]]
- [[sources/drosten-2003-novel-coronavirus-sars-patients]]
- [[sources/kuiken-2003-newly-discovered-coronavirus-primary-cause-sars]]
- [[synthesis/sars-sars-cov-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Peiris et al. 2003 linked a Hong Kong SARS patient series to a novel coronavirus by culture from two patients, EM morphology, a 646 bp coronavirus sequence, RT-PCR, paired serology, and negative tested controls.
- Drosten et al. 2003 independently identified a novel coronavirus in SARS patient material, built specific PCR assays, detected viral RNA in probable SARS patients and not healthy contacts in the tested set, and recorded early serologic response.
- Kuiken et al. 2003 linked multicentre patient testing to an experimental macaque challenge: 329 of 436 SARS patients were diagnosed with SARS-CoV infection, and four cynomolgus macaques exposed to SARS-CoV showed respiratory shedding, with three of four developing diffuse alveolar damage and some virus detection, localization, and re-isolation endpoints.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that the SARS causation ladder failed.
- A claim that high-dose combined intratracheal, intranasal, and conjunctival macaque exposure is the same endpoint as ordinary human natural-route transmission.
- A claim that culture, PCR, serology, and macaque challenge are interchangeable.

Current methodological value:
- SARS is now a primary-anchored case where positive evidence is strong but still has route and comparator boundaries. The failure category is not causal failure; it is endpoint compression risk, especially around translating a four-macaque artificial-route challenge without a sham-inoculated macaque group into ordinary human transmission closure.

### MERS -> MERS-CoV
Current primary endpoint-boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/zaki-2012-novel-coronavirus-pneumonia-saudi-arabia]]
- [[sources/dewit-2013-mers-cov-rhesus-macaque-lower-respiratory-infection]]
- [[sources/adney-2014-mers-cov-dromedary-camel-shedding]]
- [[synthesis/mers-mers-cov-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Zaki et al. 2012 isolated and characterized a novel lineage C betacoronavirus from the admission sputum of one fatal pneumonia case, with culture, RT-PCR, sequencing, patient-serology, and control-serum endpoints.
- de Wit et al. 2013 showed that high-dose combined-route MERS-CoV exposure produced transient lower-respiratory infection, viral replication markers, pneumocyte localization, and lung pathology in six rhesus macaques.
- Adney et al. 2014 showed that high-dose combined-route MERS-CoV exposure produced mild disease, upper-respiratory replication, nasal shedding, respiratory-epithelium antigen, and seroconversion in three dromedary camels.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that MERS-CoV causation failed.
- A claim that one index case with bacterial coinfections proves the full MERS syndrome.
- A claim that high-dose combined-route macaque or camel exposure is equivalent to ordinary human natural-route infection.
- A claim that camel shedding ecology by itself proves human clinical MERS.

Current methodological value:
- MERS is a primary-anchored endpoint-boundary row. The useful failure category is not a failed causation experiment; it is host, route, and endpoint compression across single-case isolation, nonhuman-primate challenge, and camel reservoir ecology.

### Hand, foot, and mouth disease -> Coxsackievirus A16 / enterovirus 71
Current primary endpoint-boundary sources in hand:

- [[sources/alsop-flewett-foster-1960-hand-foot-mouth-disease-birmingham]]
- [[sources/brown-oleary-1974-fluorescent-antibody-hfmd]]
- [[sources/ishimaru-1980-hand-foot-mouth-enterovirus-71-cns-complications]]
- [[sources/li-2014-tree-shrew-coxsackievirus-a16-experimental-infection]]
- [[synthesis/hand-foot-mouth-disease-enteroviruses-evidence-ladder]]

What these sources currently support:
- Alsop et al. 1960 supports Coxsackie A16 recovery from one hand-vesicle sample during an early HFMD outbreak.
- Brown and O'Leary 1974 supports Coxsackie A16 fluorescent-antibody responses in clinical cases and contacts, including subclinical infection markers.
- Ishimaru et al. 1980 supports EV71 isolation/typing in HFMD outbreaks with frequent CNS complications.
- Li et al. 2014 supports Coxsackie A16 systemic infection markers in tree shrews after nasal-spray inoculation with mock controls.

What this does **not** yet support:
- A claim that all HFMD is caused by Coxsackie A16 or EV71.
- A claim that EV71 detection explains the mechanism of every neurologic complication.
- A claim that antibody positivity alone proves acute individual causation.
- A claim that tree-shrew systemic infection reproduces ordinary pediatric HFMD lesion tropism.

Current methodological value:
- HFMD is a useful endpoint-substitution row. The failure category is not a failed causation experiment; it is the risk of collapsing lesion isolation, serology, EV71 outbreak association, CNS complications, and animal-model infection into one undifferentiated causation claim.

### Cervical cancer -> HPV
The HPV/cervical-cancer row now has a started ladder page:

- [[synthesis/cervical-cancer-hpv-evidence-ladder]]
- [[sources/durst-kleinheinz-hotz-gissmann-1985-physical-state-hpv16-genital-tumours]]
- [[sources/cullen-reid-campion-lorincz-1991-physical-state-hpv-cervical-neoplasm]]

Current primary boundary findings in hand:
- Durst et al. 1985 stated that whether HPV16/18 integration is a causative event for malignant transformation or a consequence of tumor-cell DNA rearrangement remained an open question.
- Cullen et al. 1991 found integration in 56 of 69 invasive cancers, but also found 13 invasive cancers with only episomal HPV DNA.
- Cullen et al. 1991 found only 3 mixed episomal/integrated cases among 61 CIN2-CIN3 specimens and no integration among latent/CIN1 specimens, which makes integration a late or invasion-associated marker in that set rather than a universal early event.

Candidate HPV-negative, assay-failure, and insufficiency papers now have source pages (abstract-level):

- [[sources/kaliff-2020-hpv-negative-tumors-swedish-cohort]] — retesting halved the HPV-negative fraction (14% to 7%), with residual negatives skewed toward adenocarcinoma and some p16-positive cases suspected of remaining false negatives.
- [[sources/rodriguez-carunchio-2015-hpv-negative-cervical-carcinoma]] — HC2-negative cases fell from 14/136 to 8/136 confirmed HPV-negative after three-assay PCR reanalysis; confirmed negatives clustered in adenocarcinomas with worse disease-free survival.
- [[sources/petry-2017-surgical-staging-false-hpv-negative]] — surgical staging showed 21 of 31 HC2-negative "cervical cancers" were not cervical cancer at all; one of 350 staging-confirmed primaries was HPV-DNA-negative by all tests.
- [[sources/arroyo-muhr-2020-deep-sequencing-pcr-negative]] — deep sequencing found HPV sequences in 169 of 392 PCR-negative cervical cancers, lifting cumulative HPV positivity above 92% in the 2,850-case Swedish series. (The register previously attributed this paper to "Lagheden et al."; the first author is Arroyo Mühr.)
- [[sources/arroyo-muhr-2020-sequencing-hpv-apparently-negative]] — RNA-enriched sequencing detected HPV in 48 of 92 PCR-negative cases, with splice junctions in 12 cases indicating transcriptionally active material.
- [[sources/caussy-1988-hpv-detection-methods-clinical-specimens]] — method-comparison boundary: HPV positivity in identical invasive-cancer specimens ranged from 30% to 89% by hybridization method.
- [[sources/claas-1989-hpv-pcr-paraffin-embedded-cervical]] — PCR recovered HPV in three of five DISH-negative cervical carcinomas (with matched-metastase concordance); two squamous carcinomas stayed negative for the four tested types.
- [[sources/mccance-1983-hpv-dna-cin]] — early partial-detection boundary: HPV6 DNA in 13 of 22 CIN-spectrum cases, predating HPV16/18 discovery in cervical material.
- [[sources/peto-2004-manchester-hpv-neoplasia-cohort]] — progression boundary: oncogenic HPV strongly predicted CIN3 (OR 17.2), yet only 28% of cytologically normal HPV-positive women developed CIN3 within 14 years.
- [[sources/skinner-2016-hpv-progression-clearance]] — insufficiency boundary: 79.3% of baseline HPV infections cleared in a vaccine-trial control arm, with CIN2+ risk concentrated in 6-month persistent oncogenic infections.

Current vault conclusion:
- Cervical cancer -> HPV does not fit a simple failed-transmission register.
- The useful failure categories are failed detection, diagnostic misclassification, true or apparent HPV-negative tumors, and infection without progression.
- The current full-text HPV source set adds a physical-state boundary: integration is frequent in invasive cancer but not universal, and causal direction remains unresolved from physical-state evidence alone.

### Kaposi sarcoma -> HHV-8
The HHV-8/Kaposi row now has a started ladder page:

- [[synthesis/kaposi-sarcoma-hhv8-evidence-ladder]]
- [[sources/chang-cesarman-pessin-1994-herpesvirus-like-dna-aids-kaposi]]
- [[sources/moore-chang-1995-herpesvirus-like-dna-kaposi-hiv-nonhiv]]
- [[sources/oneill-henson-ghorbani-1996-herpesvirus-like-sequences-kaposi-lesions]]
- [[sources/moore-gao-dominguez-1996-primary-characterization-kshv]]

Current primary boundary findings in hand:
- Chang et al. 1994 found KS330233 in 25 of 27 AIDS-KS tissues, but also found six positive non-KS AIDS lymph node or lymphoma samples.
- Moore and Chang 1995 found KSHV-like DNA in 20 of 21 KS lesions, but also found three positive uninvolved-skin samples from 14 KS patients and one initially positive control sample that became negative on repeat testing.
- O'Neill et al. 1996 found lesion-specific PCR positivity in a small series, but stated that viral particles had not been reported by electron microscopy in KS tissue samples at that time.
- Moore et al. 1996 characterized the agent and added cell-line/serology evidence, but did not identify replication-competent virions in infected cell lines.

Current vault conclusion:
- Kaposi sarcoma -> HHV-8 does not fit a simple failed-transmission register.
- The useful boundary categories are association-without-causal-closure, lesion DNA without infectious-virus recovery, infected-cell-line characterization without KS tumor induction, and serology without necessity or sufficiency.
- The current full-text HHV-8 set should be used to strengthen the association and characterization rungs while keeping transmission, prospective timing, and tumor-causation claims unresolved.

### Infectious mononucleosis -> EBV
The EBV/infectious-mononucleosis row now has a started ladder page:

- [[synthesis/infectious-mononucleosis-ebv-evidence-ladder]]
- [[sources/klein-pearson-henle-1968-ebv-membrane-immunofluorescence-im]]
- [[sources/nye-lambert-1973-ebv-antibody-cases-contacts-infectious-mononucleosis]]
- [[sources/hallee-evans-niederman-1974-west-point-infectious-mononucleosis-ebv]]

Current primary boundary findings in hand:
- Nye and Lambert 1973 found that among 13 persistently Paul-Bunnell-negative glandular-fever-like cases, four had recent EBV infection, two had probable CMV mononucleosis, and several remained without recent EBV or CMV evidence.
- Nye and Lambert 1973 found 21 EBV infection/reinfection markers among 306 followed contacts, but only five contacts developed Paul-Bunnell-positive IM.
- Hallee et al. 1974 found 201 EBV infections among initially susceptible West Point cadets over four years, but only 53 were definite heterophile-positive clinical IM.
- Hallee et al. 1974 found no higher EBV infection rate among susceptible roommates of known clinical IM cases than among susceptible cadets not so exposed.

Current vault conclusion:
- EBV/infectious mononucleosis has stronger prospective timing evidence than many tumor-virus rows.
- The useful boundary categories are infection without clinical IM, mononucleosis-like illness from non-EBV or unresolved causes, and roommate/contact findings that do not define a simple route-specific transmission model.
- These boundaries do not refute the EBV/IM association; they prevent equating EBV infection with every clinical mono-like syndrome or treating serology as purified-virus challenge evidence.

## Scientism relevance
This register supports a broader methodological claim: biology and medicine can become narratively selective when a small number of tractable or institutionally successful experiments become the remembered foundation while non-confirmatory attempts become background noise.

That does not prove the mainstream causal claim false by itself. It does show why a serious causation history must preserve:

- positive experiments
- failed experiments
- ambiguous experiments
- the mismatch between experimental route and natural route
- the mismatch between association and full causal closure

## Next step

2. the primary Rosenau influenza transmission report

The polio lead now has abstract-level primary detail, and the influenza lead now has extracted-report primary detail. Both tracks still need broader primary reconstruction before the vault can speak confidently about field-wide failure patterns.
