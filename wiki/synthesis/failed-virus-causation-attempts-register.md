# Failed Virus-Causation Attempt Register

> Warning: This page is a register of currently assembled negative-result leads. It does not yet represent a complete literature review for every disease-virus pair in the audit set.

## Why this page exists
The current vault already tracks candidate foundational papers that are commonly treated as supporting virus-disease attribution claims. But a causation audit is incomplete if it records only the experiments later described as successful while ignoring failed, negative, or non-confirmatory attempts.

This matters because a field can look far more causally secure when the remembered story is built from a small number of positive or rhetorically successful experiments while unsuccessful attempts fade from view.

Use [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] to classify failed attempts as failed detection, failed culture, failed transmission, failed challenge, failed intervention, or failed generalization rather than treating all negative results as the same kind of evidence.

## Disease notes
### Poliomyelitis -> poliovirus
Current primary negative-result paper in hand:

- [[sources/sabin-olitsky-1938-fate-of-nasally-instilled-poliomyelitis-virus]]

What this currently supports:
- The abstract-level record is no longer title-only. In normal monkeys, recoverable virus was absent from excised nasal mucosa and olfactory bulbs during the first 48 hours after intranasal instillation.
- At 72 hours, just before or with the first temperature rise, very small amounts were found in nasal mucosa and for the first time in olfactory bulbs.
- At 96 hours and later, virus remained detectable in olfactory bulbs while none was detected in nasal mucosa.
- In convalescent immune animals, none was recovered from nasal mucosa or central nervous system at the multiple time points named in the abstract.
- This is enough to show that the paper preserved a nontrivial transmission-route problem rather than presenting ordinary host-to-host transmission as already straightforwardly demonstrated.

What it does **not** yet support:
- A vault-level claim that this paper itself demonstrated failed monkey-to-monkey transmission, because the abstract in hand is centered on tissue-recovery timing rather than a direct transmission series.
- A vault-level claim that natural-route transmission repeatedly failed across the whole literature.
- A vault-level claim that only intracerebral inoculation worked.
- A vault-level claim that "hundreds" of failed experiments were ignored.

Current methodological value:
- This is enough to justify a narrower scientism warning: later causal narratives should not treat this paper as if it had already provided a clean natural-route or host-to-host transmission demonstration when it instead frames transmission as a problem and reports a constrained tissue-recovery sequence rather than a simple transmission proof.

### Clinically diagnosed influenza / influenza-like illness transmission (pre-1933)
Current primary failure-oriented source in hand:

- [[sources/rosenau-1919-experiments-to-determine-mode-of-spread-of-influenza]]

What the primary report now supports:
- Nineteen volunteers received a large mixed Pfeiffer bacillus preparation sprayed into nose, eyes, and throat, and Rosenau reports that none took sick.
- Ten volunteers received mixed respiratory material from influenza cases by sprayed exposure; none took sick. Filtered material also produced no results in other volunteers.
- Another ten volunteers received 6 c.c. of similar material with only one hour and forty minutes between collection and exposure; none took sick.
- Nineteen volunteers received direct nose-to-nose and throat-to-throat transfer from donors in early disease; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers received pooled citrated whole blood from febrile influenza cases; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers received filtered upper-respiratory material injected subcutaneously; none took sick in any way.
- Ten volunteers underwent intimate ward exposure with ten influenza patients each, including face-to-face breathing and direct coughing; none took sick in any way.
- Rosenau closes by warning that investigators must be very careful not to draw positive conclusions from such negative results and says they were not quite sure what they knew about the disease.
- The extracted report also notes similar negative results from Dr. McCoy's Goat Island series.

What this source is and is not a boundary for:
- It is a pre-1933 transmission-failure boundary paper in clinically diagnosed influenza or influenza-like illness.
- It is not a direct failed test of influenza-virus identification, because the report predates the 1933 identification framework and works from outbreak diagnosis and transmission attempts rather than later virus-isolation methods.

What remains missing:
- a broader primary reconstruction of the later Portsmouth or related follow-up series beyond the summary preserved in the current extraction
- a fuller historical comparison with later influenza transmission and agent-identification papers
- a clean explanation, from direct sources, of why these volunteer challenges failed under the tested conditions

Current methodological value:
- Influenza is now a primary-anchored case where negative results can be used in two opposite inferentially risky ways:
- later certainty narratives can underplay repeated negative human challenge findings from the primary report
- counter-narratives can overextend the same negative findings into a claim that influenza is not infectious at all, even though Rosenau explicitly warned against that move

### Measles, smallpox, and rabies
The first pass did not surface a clean negative-result landmark comparable to the polio transmission-problem paper or the Rosenau influenza lead.

- absence of a clear failed-attempt paper in hand is not evidence that no failed attempts existed
- it is evidence that the current vault has not yet reconstructed them

### AIDS -> HIV
The failed-attempt framing is harder here because the major canonical case is not a simple animal or volunteer natural-route transmission program like influenza or early polio.

Current vault conclusion:
- the current search pass did not retrieve a clean primary failed-attempt experiment
- the better negative-result taxonomy here may involve failed exclusivity claims, inconsistent progression, or mismatch between narrower virologic findings and broader syndrome-causation claims rather than a single challenge model

### COVID-19 -> SARS-CoV-2
The first pass surfaced at least one host-range limit paper:

- *Susceptibility of swine cells and domestic pigs to SARS-CoV-2* (2020; DOI `10.1080/22221751.2020.1831405`)

Current caution:
- host restriction in a specific animal is a useful negative or boundary result
- but it is not itself a failed causation experiment for COVID-19 in humans

### Cervical cancer -> HPV
This search track did not surface a clean failed-attempt paper. That is likely because the relevant negative-result question is not "failed transmission" but something like:

- failure to find HPV in a subset of lesions under older methods
- failure of a simple one-factor model to explain progression
- negative or weak association in particular lesion classes or populations

That requires a different search strategy from the one used for transmission-style diseases.

## Scientism relevance
This register supports a broader methodological claim: biology and medicine can become narratively selective when a small number of tractable or institutionally successful experiments become the remembered foundation while non-confirmatory attempts become background noise.

That does not prove the mainstream causal claim false by itself. It does show why a serious causation history must preserve:

- positive experiments
- failed experiments
- ambiguous experiments
- the mismatch between experimental route and natural route
- the mismatch between association and full causal closure

## Next step
The highest-value next step is a fuller paper-by-paper negative-result reconstruction for the two best current leads:

1. the Sabin and Olitsky 1938 poliomyelitis transmission-problem paper
2. the primary Rosenau influenza transmission report

The polio lead now has abstract-level primary detail, and the influenza lead now has extracted-report primary detail. Both tracks still need broader primary reconstruction before the vault can speak confidently about field-wide failure patterns.
