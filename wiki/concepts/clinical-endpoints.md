# Clinical Endpoints

## Definition
Clinical endpoints are outcomes that directly describe patient health, such as mortality, hospitalization, severe disease, symptom burden, disability, or quality of life.

## Why this matters in this vault
[[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]] repeatedly asks whether disease-prevention claims are being overstated into broader claims about health benefit.

[[sources/vaccine-makers-do-not-use-placebos-to-test-the-safety-of-their-vaccines-for-kids]] focuses more on control groups, but it also points back to a related question: which outcomes were actually measured in the cited trials. [[sources/pediatric-vaccine-trial-records-placebo-control-audit]] begins that trial-record check by naming abstract-level safety, immunogenicity, and disease endpoints for the cited records.

[[sources/effectiveness-and-cost-benefit-of-influenza-vaccination-of-healthy-working-adults]] is a direct trial example. It names clinical and work-utilization endpoints separately: influenzalike illness, physician visits, lost workdays, serologically confirmed influenza illness in a subset, and cost per vaccinated versus unvaccinated person.

## What question the underlying experiments can answer
- A trial that directly measures hospitalization or mortality can speak to those named outcomes within its design limits.
- A trial that measures only infection counts, antibody response, or laboratory markers cannot by itself answer every broader question about total patient benefit.
- A trial that measures ILI, physician visits, and lost workdays can answer those named short-term endpoints without automatically answering mortality, rare adverse events, or long-term overall-health outcomes.
- In the Bridges et al. influenza trial, the 1997-1998 clinical-utilization endpoints moved against the vaccine group: vaccine recipients reported significantly more ILI-related sick days, lost workdays, and physician-visit work hours than placebo recipients.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- Endpoint naming should be explicit.
- Broader benefit claims should preferentially rest on clinical endpoints when the claim itself is clinical.
- A mismatch between the endpoint measured and the conclusion asserted is one common form of [[concepts/inferential-leap]].
