# Concepts

The vault's working vocabulary: the standards used to judge claims, the laboratory methods whose interpretation is disputed, and the analytic frames applied to both mainstream and critical sources.

## Evidence standards

- [[concepts/scientism|Scientism]] — In this vault, scientism means extending scientific authority, methods, or results beyond what the underlying evidence strictly supports.
- [[concepts/empirical-evidence|Empirical Evidence]] — Empirical evidence consists of observed or measured data, but the evidentiary force of that data depends on method quality, endpoint clarity, and reproducibility.
- [[concepts/replication-crisis|Replication Crisis]] — Concept page anchored on direct replication projects: the psychology multi-lab project, the cancer-biology Amgen report, and the Nature researcher survey, with the evidentiary limits of each kept distinct.
- [[concepts/clinical-endpoints|Clinical Endpoints]] — Outcomes such as mortality, hospitalization, symptom burden, or quality of life that speak more directly to patient health than narrower proxy measures.
- [[concepts/surrogate-endpoints|Surrogate Endpoints]] — Trial outcomes that stand in for broader clinical benefit, such as infection counts, biomarkers, or immune response measures rather than overall health outcomes.
- [[concepts/placebo-controls|Placebo Controls]] — A concept page for distinguishing inert placebos from active comparators and for tracking what a control group can and cannot show.

## Laboratory methods under dispute

- [[concepts/cell-culture|Cell Culture]] — Cell culture is a laboratory method whose evidentiary meaning depends on controls, media conditions, and the specific endpoint being claimed.
- [[concepts/virus-isolation|Virus Isolation]] — Virus isolation is a disputed methodological term in this vault, especially where sources move from cell-culture findings or mixed samples to broader claims about causation.

## Historical and analytic framing

- [[concepts/germ-theory|Germ Theory]] — A disease framework centered on microorganisms, treated in this vault as both a historical research program and a site of disputed interpretation about what classic experiments actually proved.
- [[concepts/inferential-leap|Inferential Leap]] — An inferential leap is the move from a narrower observation or experiment to a broader claim that the evidence may not strictly justify.
