# Surrogate Endpoints

## Definition
Surrogate endpoints are measures used in trials as stand-ins for broader clinical benefit. Depending on context, they can include biomarkers, disease incidence, antibody response, or other narrower outcomes that do not by themselves settle questions about mortality, hospitalization, quality of life, or overall health benefit.

## Why this matters in this vault
[[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]] frames endpoint choice as central to judging whether a source is making a narrow disease-prevention claim or a broader claim about human benefit.

[[sources/vaccine-makers-do-not-use-placebos-to-test-the-safety-of-their-vaccines-for-kids]] raises a related but distinct question: whether trial controls and endpoint choices make it harder to estimate net clinical harm or benefit. The vault has not yet verified its cited trials directly.

## What these sources claim
- [[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]] argues that endpoint criticism should be central to this research program.
- [[sources/vaccine-makers-do-not-use-placebos-to-test-the-safety-of-their-vaccines-for-kids]] argues that many pediatric vaccine trials rely on active comparators or non-inert controls, which can complicate some safety comparisons.

## What question the underlying experiments can answer
- A trial using a disease-specific endpoint can answer whether the intervention changed that named endpoint within the study design.
- A trial using antibody or immunogenicity endpoints can answer whether the intervention changed that measured immune marker.
- Those same designs do not automatically answer whether the intervention improved overall health outcomes unless those outcomes were directly measured.
- Trial-specific conclusions remain unresolved until named trials list intervention, comparator, endpoint, result, and limitation.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- Endpoint choice is a legitimate methodological issue and should be named precisely.
- A surrogate endpoint is not automatically invalid.
- A broader health claim requires a clear bridge from the measured endpoint to the larger conclusion being asserted.
