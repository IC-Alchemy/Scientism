# Scientism

## Working definition
In this vault, scientism is not simply "valuing science." It names a pattern in which experimental results, scientific institutions, or technical vocabularies are used to justify broader conclusions than the evidence itself can carry.

## Core distinction
Scientism becomes visible when three layers are blurred:
- what was directly observed
- what the experiment was designed to test
- what later interpreters conclude from it

That distinction is central to [[concepts/empirical-evidence]] and [[concepts/inferential-leap]].

## How the current sources frame it
- [[sources/scientism-conversation-may-1-2026]] treats scientism as a research program centered on overreach, replication, and endpoint disputes.
- [[sources/the-virus-cult-a-religion-built-on-fear-not-science]] and [[sources/the-virus-paradigm-assumptions-lies-and-the-foundation-of-modern-medicine]] argue that virology turns contested methods into broad doctrine.
- [[sources/viroliegy-101-the-scientific-method]] is treated here as a Tier 3 methodological example arguing that scientific authority is often claimed without satisfying clear hypothesis, control, and reproducibility standards.
- [[sources/effectiveness-and-cost-benefit-of-influenza-vaccination-of-healthy-working-adults]] gives a Tier 1 trial example of the gap between endpoint results and author-level framing: the 1997-1998 vaccine group had significantly worse ILI-related sick-day, lost-workday, and physician-visit work-hour outcomes, while the abstract conclusion emphasizes conditional benefit in matched seasons and lack of overall economic benefit.

## What this wiki currently concludes
- Scientism is best tracked here as a methodological problem of over-claiming, not as a blanket rejection of science.
- A field can produce valid experiments and still support scientistic interpretations if later claims outrun the experiment's endpoint or scope.
- The concept is therefore downstream of method reading, not a substitute for it.
- The new method-focused source is treated as a prompt to watch for overclaiming in both directions: mainstream institutions can overclaim from experiments, and critics can overclaim from methodological criticism if they move too quickly from a design problem to a total dismissal of a field.
- A source can be strong scientific evidence and still present an ethically or rhetorically weak conclusion if it fails to foreground an unfavorable randomized endpoint signal.

## Contrasts with
- [[concepts/replication-crisis]]: replication problems concern whether a result reproduces at all
- [[concepts/inferential-leap]]: inferential leap concerns whether a result, even if real, is being stretched too far
- [[concepts/empirical-evidence]]: empirical evidence concerns what was actually measured or observed
