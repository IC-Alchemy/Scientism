# Inferential Leap

## Definition
An inferential leap occurs when a source moves from observed data or a specific experiment to a wider conclusion without establishing the missing evidentiary bridge.

## Typical pattern
1. A source reports an observation, signal, endpoint, or laboratory effect.
2. The source treats that result as if it directly answered a broader historical, medical, or philosophical question.
3. The gap between the two is left implicit.

## Current examples in this vault

- [[sources/the-virus-cult-a-religion-built-on-fear-not-science]] and [[sources/the-virus-paradigm-assumptions-lies-and-the-foundation-of-modern-medicine]] move from methodological criticisms of cell culture or isolation standards to broad claims about virology and public policy.
- [[synthesis/vaccinated-children-more-healthy-question]] documents the inverse public-health version of the same problem: evidence for target-disease prevention, antibody response, or selected safety endpoints can be communicated as if it proved that vaccinated children are more healthy overall.
- [[sources/scobey-poisoning-as-the-cause-of-poliomyelitis]] often moves from toxic syndromes that resemble poliomyelitis to broader claims about the general cause of polio epidemics.
- [[sources/congressman-questions-aids-in-letter]] packages anomalies, unanswered questions, and secondhand claims into a wider challenge to HIV/AIDS causation and policy.
- [[sources/the-infectious-myth-busted-part-1-the-rosenau-spanish-flu-experiments-1918]] moves from negative transmission results in specific 1918 influenza experiments to a general claim that influenza is not infectious at all.
- [[sources/an-inquiry-into-the-logical-basis-of-the-germ-theory]] is useful because it highlights the difference between observing microbes in disease and proving causation, while also making its own larger leap toward total rejection of germ theory.
- [[sources/viroliegy-101-the-scientific-method]] turns a methodological critique into a wider claim that whole fields such as virology are pseudoscientific.
- [[sources/insight-into-biases-and-sequencing-errors-of-illumina-miseq-platform]] is a useful counterexample: it makes narrower claims that are closely tied to the experiment actually performed.
- [[sources/effectiveness-and-cost-benefit-of-influenza-vaccination-of-healthy-working-adults]] is another useful counterexample. It reports season-specific trial endpoints and keeps a visible boundary between worse ILI-related utilization outcomes in the mismatched 1997-1998 season, reduced ILI/work-loss outcomes in the matched 1998-1999 season, and broader claims about economic benefit or general population health.
- [[synthesis/virus-causation-inferential-leaps]] extends this method to foundational virus-disease attribution papers, including the specific risk that artificial-route inoculation or isolation results are later treated as if they fully proved ordinary natural causation.
- [[synthesis/viral-disease-causation-evidence-ladder]] turns the virus-causation audit into an explicit ladder so detection, culture, serology, challenge, transmission, and intervention evidence are not collapsed into one causal claim.
- [[sources/speciation-direct-evidence-ai-chat]] applies the same distinction outside virology: an AI-chat exchange separating observations such as sequenced telomere-like remnants or measured chromosome counts from the historical narrative that random mutation caused speciation.

## Questions to ask
- What was directly observed?
- What was the explicit endpoint?
- What causal or historical conclusion is being added afterward?
- What additional experiment or evidence would be needed to justify that added conclusion?
- Is an unfavorable endpoint being softened, omitted, or reframed in the author conclusion?
- Is disease-specific benefit being described to the public as overall-health benefit?

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This is one of the central analytic tools of the vault.
- It applies both to mainstream scientific overreach and to counter-establishment critiques.
- The concept is especially important in disputed medical and historical domains where rhetorical certainty can outrun the experiment itself.
- Direct trial reports can reduce inferential ambiguity when they preserve the intervention, comparator, endpoint, result, and limitation in one place.
- This concept also applies in reverse: a source can understate the force of its own unfavorable endpoints by drawing a conclusion that foregrounds benefit while leaving a harm signal less visible.
- Consent-relevant medical messaging should preserve the endpoint boundary. A claim that a vaccine reduced a target disease is not the same claim as saying the childhood vaccination program improves overall child health.
- Viral disease causation claims should preserve the same endpoint boundary. A paper showing viral RNA, antibody response, animal-model disease, or culture growth does not answer the same question as a controlled human clinical causation study or an intervention study.
