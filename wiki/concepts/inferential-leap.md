# Inferential Leap

## Definition
An inferential leap occurs when a source moves from observed data or a specific experiment to a wider conclusion without establishing the missing evidentiary bridge.

## Typical pattern
1. A source reports an observation, signal, endpoint, or laboratory effect.
2. The source treats that result as if it directly answered a broader historical, medical, or philosophical question.
3. The gap between the two is left implicit.

## Current examples in this vault
- [[sources/speciation]] separates observed chromosome differences from stronger historical stories about what caused speciation.
- [[sources/evolution]] distinguishes present-day evidence from claims about deep-time causal history.
- [[sources/the-virus-cult-a-religion-built-on-fear-not-science]] and [[sources/the-virus-paradigm-assumptions-lies-and-the-foundation-of-modern-medicine]] move from methodological criticisms of cell culture or isolation standards to broad claims about virology and public policy.
- [[sources/insight-into-biases-and-sequencing-errors-of-illumina-miseq-platform]] is a useful counterexample: it makes narrower claims that are closely tied to the experiment actually performed.

## Questions to ask
- What was directly observed?
- What was the explicit endpoint?
- What causal or historical conclusion is being added afterward?
- What additional experiment or evidence would be needed to justify that added conclusion?

## What this wiki currently concludes
- This is one of the central analytic tools of the vault.
- It applies both to mainstream scientific overreach and to counter-establishment critiques.
- The concept is especially important in disputed medical and historical domains where rhetorical certainty can outrun the experiment itself.
